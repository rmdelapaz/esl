Words & Questions

Three kinds of precision. Build the exact form of a word (decide → decision → decisive → decisively). Say exactly how big a difference is (far cheaper, only slightly heavier, nowhere near as good). And ask questions in the right shape and tone for the moment (Could you tell me where the station is? Isn't it beautiful?).

6.1

One Word, a Whole Family — Word Formation

Class C17
📖 🗣️ New words — talking about words
  • base (root): the core word · success
  • prefix: a start · un-, re-, mis-
  • suffix: an ending · -ful, -tion, -ly
  • word family: all the words from one base
  • part of speech: noun, verb, adjective, adverb
  • stress: the strong syllable · phoTOgraphy

🗣️ Useful phrases for checking your own writing:

It needs a noun here.That slot wants an adjective.Add -ly for the adverb.The meaning is negative.What's the base word?Does the stress move?
Grammar box

un + success + ful + ly = unsuccessfully  ·  prefix = meaning · suffix = the job

The ending tells you the job:

Noun-tion / -sion · -ment · -ness · -ity · -er / -oreducation · agreement · kindness · ability · actor
Adjective-ful · -less · -able / -ible · -ive · -ous · -alhopeful · hopeless · reliable · creative · famous · natural
Adverb-lyquickly · easily · carefully (-ful has one l, -fully has two)
Verb-ize · -en · -ifymodernize · widen · simplify

Prefixes change the meaning, not the job:

Negativeun- · in- · im- (before m, p) · il- (before l) · ir- (before r) · dis- · non-unfair · incorrect · impossible · illegal · irregular · dishonest · non-smoker
Otherre- again · over- too much · under- too little · mis- wrongly · pre- beforerewrite · overwork · underpaid · misunderstand · preview

⚠️ Careful: invaluable = extremely valuable (not "worthless"). flammable = inflammable. Don't invent words: there is no "beautiless."

To fill a gap: (1) What job does the slot need? (2) Build the form, and check: positive or negative? singular or plural?

📖 ✏️ Exercise 5 — A comeback story. Complete it with the right form.

Last spring, Rosa and Sam opened a taco truck. Despite careful (1) (PREPARE), the first month was a complete (2) (FAIL). Customers found the menu (3) (CONFUSE), and the prices were (4) (REASON): far too high. The team felt (5) (DISAPPOINT), but they acted (6) (DECIDE). They made several key (7) (IMPROVE): a shorter menu and lower prices. Then they waited (8) (PATIENT). Today the truck is a real (9) (SUCCEED), and they have three (10) (EMPLOY).

✏️ Label each answer: N, V, ADJ or ADV.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Tutor: Your essay is strong, but look at this line: "The city should modern the railroads."

Student: Oh, that slot needs a verb, doesn't it? So… modernize the railroads.

Tutor: Exactly. And here you wrote "a decide leader." What job does that slot need?

Student: It's before a noun, so an adjective: a decisive leader. And if I meant the opposite, indecisive.

Tutor: Perfect. One more: "The project was a success, and we finished it success."

Student: That describes how we finished, so it's an adverb: successfully.

Tutor: You didn't learn any new words today. You just reshaped the ones you already had.

6.2

How Much Better? — Advanced Comparison

Class C18
📖 🗣️ New words — weighing options
  • value for money: worth the price
  • reliable: works well every time
  • a commute: the trip to work
  • the cost of living: rent, food, bills
  • a trend: a change that continues
  • a warranty: the store's promise to repair

🗣️ Useful phrases for comparing and recommending:

far / much better than…slightly cheaper than…not nearly as good as…nowhere near as…twice as expensive as…the same as…the more…, the bettergetting better and betterSo overall, I'd recommend…
Grammar box

How big is the difference? Put a word in front of the comparative:

Big differencefar / much / a lot / considerably / significantly + comparativeThis laptop is far more powerful than my old one.
Small differenceslightly / a bit / a little / marginally + comparativeThe blue jacket is slightly cheaper.
Much lessnot nearly as / nowhere near as + adjective + asThe movie was nowhere near as good as the book.
Almost asnot quite as + adjective + asThe bus isn't quite as fast as the train.

❌ very cheaper · very more expensive → ✅ much cheaper · far more expensive (very only with a plain adjective: very expensive)

Equalas + adjective / adverb + asHe works as hard as anyone.
Multiplestwice / three times / half as… asTheir house is three times as big as ours.
Same / similarthe same as · similar to · different fromYour phone is the same as mine.
Than or as?-er / more / less → than · as / the same → asbigger than · as big as
Two changes togetherThe + comparative…, the + comparative…The more you practice, the better you get. · The sooner, the better.
A trendcomparative and comparative · more and more + long adjectiveHer English is getting better and better. · Rent is getting more and more expensive.
Actionsmore quickly · more carefully · faster · harder · betterShe types more quickly than I do. (never "more faster")

📖 👂 Dialogue — read with a partner

A: So, which route do you recommend: the highway or the coast road?

B: The highway is far quicker, but honestly, it's nowhere near as pretty. The coast road is twice as long, though.

A: Hmm. The earlier we leave, the less traffic we'll hit, right?

B: Exactly. And the coast road gets more and more beautiful the farther south you go. It's slightly slower, but I think it's worth it.

A: And gas? Doesn't the long way cost a lot more?

B: Only a little more. It's not quite as cheap, but it's the same car, the same trip, and a much better view.

A: OK, you've convinced me. Let's take the coast road. The earlier we set off, the better!

6.3

Asking Skillfully — Indirect & Negative Questions

Class C19
📖 🗣️ New words — at the front desk
  • the front desk / reception
  • an inquiry: a question to a business
  • a membership: you pay to join
  • availability: free times or places
  • to confirm: to check it's true
  • an opener: Could you tell me…?

🗣️ Openers, from casual to careful:

Do you know…?Could you tell me…?Do you have any idea…?Would you mind telling me…?I was wondering if…I'd like to know…
Grammar box

Indirect questions: opener + statement word order. No do / does / did.

DirectIndirect
Where is the station?Could you tell me where the station is?
What time does it open?Do you know what time it opens?
How much did it cost?Could you tell me how much it cost?
Are they open on Sundays?Do you know if they're open on Sundays?
Does the bus stop here?I was wondering whether the bus stops here.

or not: whether or not they're open · if they're open or not (not "if or not")

Negative questions show what you already think or feel:

Agree with me!Isn't it beautiful? ↘
I'm surprisedDidn't you get my message? ↗
I think so: checkIsn't the office closed on Mondays? ↗
Suggest / persuadeDon't you think we should wait? ↘ · Wouldn't it be easier to fly? ↘

Answer by the facts: "Didn't you get my message?" I got it → Yes, I did. · I didn't → No, I didn't.

Subject question (no auxiliary)Object question (do / does / did)
Who saw you? (someone saw you)Who did you see? (you saw someone)
Who broke the window?What did she break?
What happened next?What does this mean?

🗣️ Sound polite: start a little higher, use your whole voice, and let an indirect question rise gently at the end ↗. A flat, low voice sounds bored, even with perfect grammar.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Staff: Good morning, Lakeview Community College. How can I help you?

Caller: Hi. I was wondering if you still had places in the evening computer class.

Staff: Let me check… Yes, we have four places left.

Caller: Great. Could you tell me when the class starts?

Staff: On October sixth, Tuesdays and Thursdays from six to eight.

Caller: And do you know how much it costs?

Staff: It's $180 for ten weeks. Didn't you see the price on our website?

Caller: No, I didn't. I couldn't find it. Would you mind telling me whether I need to take a test first?

Staff: No test for this class. Don't you think it would be easier to register online, though? It only takes five minutes.

Caller: Maybe, but isn't it better to pay in person? I'd like a receipt.

Staff: Of course. You can come in any weekday before five.
