Narrating & Looking Back

Tell stories in layers, not in a straight line. Imagine how things could have turned out differently. Then talk about wishes, small regrets and preferences: I wish I'd set an alarm. I'd rather walk. It's high time we fixed the printer.

At B2 you already have the grammar for most situations. Now you'll work on range and nuance: combining tenses to tell a richer story, choosing the exact form for what you mean, arguing a point, and adjusting how formal you sound. Mistakes are a normal part of trying something more ambitious.

1.1

Narrative Tenses — Telling the Past in Layers

Class C1
📖 🗣️ New words — telling a story
  • nervous
  • worried
  • excited

an anecdote = a short, true (or true-sounding) story about something that happened · a mix-up = a mistake where two things get confused · a near miss = something bad almost happened · relieved = happy because a worry is over · it turned out (that)… = in the end, we learned that…

🗣️ Sequencing words to make the order clear:

By the time…while / aswhenalreadyjustuntilby thenbefore / after

🗣️ Storytelling phrases:

You'll never guess what happened…All of a sudden,…To make things worse,…Luckily,…It turned out that…In the end,…
Grammar box

Four past tenses, four jobs in a story:

Past simplethe main events, in orderShe opened the door.
Past continuousbackground in progress · was / were + -ingThe rain was falling outside.
Past perfectan earlier event · had + past participleSomeone had broken the window.
Past perfect continuoushow long an activity had been going on · had been + -ingThey'd been arguing for hours.
had donea finished action, or a numberBy noon she'd written three emails.
had been doingthe activity and its duration, or the cause of a resultHer eyes were tired. She'd been reading all morning.
State verbsknow, be, have (own), want, like, believe stay simpleI'd known him for years. ❌ I'd been knowing him

while / as + a long background action (past continuous) · when + the short action that interrupts it (past simple): While I was cooking, the phone rang.

Use the past perfect when the order matters. With before and after, the past simple is often enough: After I finished, I left. ✅

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

A: You'll never guess what happened on Tuesday.

B: Go on. What?

A: I'd been working on a big report all afternoon, and I was just finishing the last page when the power went out.

B: Oh no! Had you saved it?

A: That's the thing. I hadn't saved it for about two hours.

B: So you'd lost everything?

A: I thought so! I was sitting there in the dark, feeling terrible, when my coworker came over. She'd been having the same problem for weeks, so she knew the trick.

B: What trick?

A: The program saves a copy every ten minutes. By the time the lights came back on, she'd already found my report. I was so relieved!

1.2

The Third Conditional — What Could Have Happened

Class C2
📖 🗣️ New words — imagining a different past

a turning point = a moment after which things change · a chain reaction = one event causes the next, and the next… · by chance = without a plan · in hindsight = looking back, with what I know now · a silver lining = something good in a bad situation · It's a good thing (that)… = I'm glad that…

🗣️ Phrase bank:

If I had known,…I would have…You could have…!We might have…If only I'd…If I hadn't…, I wouldn't be… nowThings would have been different if…I never would have…
Grammar box

The third conditional imagines the opposite of what really happened. It can't change now.

RealityI didn't leave early. I missed the train.
Third conditionalIf I had left early, I would have caught the train.
FormIf + had + past participle, … would have + past participle
Other orderI would have caught the train if I'd left early. (no comma)
would havecertainIf we'd left earlier, we would have caught it.
could haveit was possibleIf we'd left earlier, we could have caught it.
might havemaybe, not sureIf we'd left earlier, we might have caught it.

Can it still change? Yes → second conditional: If I had money, I'd travel. · No → third: If I'd had money, I'd have traveled.

Mixed: past → nowIf + had + pp, … would + base verbIf I'd taken that job, I would be rich now.
Mixed: always → pastIf + past simple / were, … would have + ppIf I were more careful, I wouldn't have lost my keys.

We say would've /ˈwʊdəv/, and it sounds like "would of." But we always write would have or would've. ❌ would of · ❌ If I would have known

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

A: I still can't believe we missed the concert. If we hadn't taken the highway, we would've gotten there in time.

B: Honestly, you could have checked the traffic before we left. I did say something felt wrong.

A: I know, I know. If you'd told me you were worried, I would've listened.

B: Well, if I were a bit more assertive, I probably would've insisted. So it's on both of us.

A: True. And if we'd left even ten minutes earlier, we might have made it anyway.

B: If only we had! Still, if we hadn't gotten lost, we wouldn't be sitting in this great little café right now. Silver lining.

1.3

Wishes, Regrets & Preferences — Wish, If Only, Would Rather, It's Time

Class C3
📖 🗣️ New words — wishes and complaints

overdue = it should have happened already · to put something off = to delay it · to get around to (doing) something = to finally find time for it · to be fed up with = to be tired of and annoyed by something · a habit = something you do often, without thinking · a complaint / a suggestion

🗣️ Phrase bank:

I wish I had…I wish I'd…If only…!I wish you wouldn't…I'd rather… than…I'd rather not.I'd rather you didn't…Would you rather…?It's time we…It's high time…It's about time…It's time to…
Grammar box

One trick: a past form can mean unreal or not yet.

Present wishwish / if only + past simple (or could, were)I wish I had more time. (I don't.)
Past regretwish / if only + past perfectI wish I had set an alarm. (I didn't.)
Annoyancewish + someone / something else + wouldI wish the printer would work!

❌ I wish I would exercise more. → ✅ I wish I exercised more. / I wish I could find the time.

I'd rather + base verbmy preferenceI'd rather walk than wait for the bus.
I'd rather + someone + past simplewhat I want someone else to do (now / later)I'd rather you didn't call after ten.
It's time + subject + past simpleit should happen nowIt's time we left.
It's high / about time + past simpleit's overdue (a little annoyed)It's high time they fixed the elevator.
It's time to + base verbno person namedIt's time to go.

hope = possible: I hope you pass! · wish = unreal: I wish I'd studied more.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Nadia: You look exhausted.

Tom: I am. I wish I hadn't stayed up so late finishing that report. If only I'd started it on Monday like you told me.

Nadia: Ha! It's high time you learned to plan ahead.

Tom: I know, I know. But honestly, I wish my manager wouldn't give me things at the last minute.

Nadia: Fair. So, lunch? There's that new Thai place.

Tom: To be honest, I'd rather just grab a sandwich and sit outside. I'd rather we didn't spend an hour waiting in line.

Nadia: Deal. And it's about time we caught up properly. I feel like I never see you.

Tom: Same. I wish I had more free weekends. OK, it's time we got going before the good tables fill up.

✏️ Tom says "I wish" three times. Which one is about the past? Which one is a complaint about someone else?
