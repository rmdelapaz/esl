Module 1 — Narrating & Looking Back: Answers

Answers to every Module 1 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (Story Reconstruction orders and model retells, Chain of Consequences model chain, dictations), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 1.1–1.3Classes C1–C39 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At B2, mark meaning first, then form, then range. An answer is correct if the time relationship is right (earlier / background / duration; real vs unreal; now vs past) and the form is right. Be generous about optional choices: the past perfect is optional after before / after; had worked and had been working are both fine with for + a period for many verbs; contractions (I'd, would've, 'd rather) are always fine; was after I wish I / If only it is fine in speech (were is safer in writing). American English is the model; British forms (got for gotten, travelled, organised, film, flat, flatmate, queue) are acceptable when a learner uses them. In open tasks, reward a correct richer form with a tick and a "B2!" in the margin.

1.1 · Narrative Tenses — Past Perfect & Continuous (C1)

Exercise 1 — The wrong train (Script 1.1-A)

Gist: The speaker got on the express train by mistake (they were reading and didn't hear the announcements).

was goinghad been lookingwas sittinghad gottenhad traveledhad been readinghadn't heardhad already started

Background (past continuous): 1, 3 · earlier (past perfect): 4, 5, 7, 8 · duration (past perfect continuous): 2, 6. Also accept: 4 had got (British) · 5 had travelled · contractions (I'd been looking). Wrong: 4 "got" (the story needs the earlier event) · 6 "was reading" (possible in another story, but the script has had been reading, which explains the result).

Exercise 2 — Choose the narrative tense
  1. had already started
  2. had been working
  3. was driving
  4. had been working (also: had worked: with for 30 years both are natural)
  5. hadn't met
  6. rang
  7. had been walking
  8. had eaten

Wrong: 1 "already started" (order unclear) · 2 "has been working" (present perfect inside a past story) · 5 "didn't meet" (acceptable in casual speech, but before that evening calls for the past perfect) · 6 "was ringing" (the ringing interrupts) · 8 "had been eating" (the cookies are all gone: a finished result = simple).

Exercise 3 — Simple or continuous?

1 had written (a number: six emails) · 2 had been reading (activity → result: tired eyes) · 3 had known (state verb) · 4 had been cooking (activity since lunchtime; the smell is the result) · 5 had painted (a number: three rooms) · 6 had been waiting (activity → result: wet).

Also accept 6 had waited with a note: correct grammar, but the continuous explains the result better. Never accept 3 "had been knowing."

Exercise 4 — Sequencing words

1 While (also: As) · 2 By the time · 3 just · 4 until · 5 when · 6 already.

Note on 1: When I was cooking dinner, my brother called is also possible and common in speech; while is the model for the long background action. Note on 2: When is grammatical but loses the "too late" meaning.

Exercise 5 — From flat to layered (open)

Model: By the time I got to work, the meeting had already started. I'd been sitting in traffic for over an hour, and my phone had died, so I couldn't call anyone. When I walked in, my boss was standing by the door, and she didn't look happy.

Check for: one past continuous (background), one past perfect (earlier), one past perfect continuous (duration), one sequencing word, and a story that still makes sense. Many good versions are possible.

Dialogue — the four tenses

Past simple: happened, went out, came over, knew, came back, was · past continuous: was just finishing, was sitting · past perfect: Had you saved, hadn't saved, you'd lost, she'd already found · past perfect continuous: I'd been working, She'd been having. Any one of each is correct.

Speaking — Story Reconstruction (Set D)

Story A · The Wedding Cake: C – F – A – H – B – G – E – D. Story B · Locked Out: E – B – G – A – H – C – F – D. Literacy-support short versions (★ strips): A = C · F · B · E · D; B = E · B · A · H · D.

Clues learners should notice: A-C "had been baking since five" is the scene-setter; A-D "By the time they arrived" must come last; B-A "As the door was closing" must follow B-G "ran out of the apartment"; B-H introduces Mrs. Ortiz before B-C uses "She."

Model retell (A, taxi driver): "I'd been driving since six that morning, and I was waiting at a red light when the van in front of me just died. A woman got out. She'd been driving for twenty minutes with a strange noise in the engine, and she was holding a huge wedding cake! By the time we'd pushed the van to the side of the road, she was nearly crying, so I drove her to the hotel. The guests had already sat down, but the cake was perfect, and I got the first slice." Model retell (B, Mrs. Ortiz): "I was watering my plants when I heard a door slam. The young man next door had been looking forward to his new job for weeks, and he'd just locked himself out! I'd been keeping a spare key for the building manager for years, so I called him. While we were waiting, I made Daniel coffee and a sandwich. He told me later his supervisor had been locked out herself the week before."

Self-check
Q1
"When I got to the airport, the plane ___." The plane left before I arrived.
A) had already taken off
B) took off
C) was taking off
Why: the past perfect shows the plane left before the story moment. B means I arrived and then it left; C means it was in the middle of leaving.
Q2
"She was exhausted because she ___ all night."
A) had been studying
B) studied
C) has been studying
Why: the past perfect continuous stresses the duration of an activity that explains a past result. C is present perfect: wrong time frame inside a past story.
Q3 · new
Which sentence is correct?
A) I had been knowing him for ten years…
B) I had known him for ten years when we finally met in person.
C) I was knowing him for ten years…
Why: know is a state verb, so it stays in the simple form even with a duration.
Errors you will hear most (1.1)

"When I arrived, the train already left." (no past perfect) · "Yesterday I had gone to the market." (past perfect for no reason) · "I had been knowing / was knowing her." · "By the time I arrived, the party has finished." (present perfect in a past story) · "She had been wrote three emails." · "While the phone rang, I was cooking." (while / when reversed) · "I was go to work." Correct the missing past perfect when the order is unclear; treat an unnecessary past perfect gently (it isn't wrong, just heavy).

1.2 · The Third Conditional — Past Hypotheticals (C2)

Exercise 1 — The picnic that wasn't (Script 1.2-A)

What really happened (any two): they didn't check the forecast · it rained all afternoon · they didn't bring an umbrella · they got very wet · they went into a café · they met their old friend Leo · they have lunch with him every month now.

  1. had checked … wouldn't have planned
  2. had brought … wouldn't have gotten
  3. hadn't gone … wouldn't have met
  4. hadn't met … wouldn't be

Why is 4 different? The result is about now (they are friends again now), so it is a mixed conditional: would + base verb. Also accept: 2 wouldn't have got (British); contractions (If we'd checked). Wrong: 4 "wouldn't have been friends now" (two pasts with now).

Exercise 2 — Put it in the third conditional
  1. had known … would have bought
  2. wouldn't have missed … had set
  3. had listened … wouldn't have gotten (British: got)
  4. would have won … hadn't made
  5. had asked … would have helped
  6. had been … could have bought

Also correct: contractions (I'd known, I'd have bought, would've); might have in 5 changes the meaning (less sure) but is grammatical. Wrong: "If I would have known" · "would of" · "If I knew" (second conditional) · 6 "could buy."

Exercise 4 — would, could or might?

1 would (certain) · 2 might (not sure) · 3 could (it was possible) · 4 could (criticism: it was possible and you didn't) · 5 might ("Who knows?" = uncertain).

Note on 3: would is also grammatical (it means "I definitely would have"), but the sentence says "It was possible," so could is the target. In 4, might have called exists as a complaint in British English ("You might have told me!"), but could is the everyday American choice.

Exercise 5 — Build a mixed conditional
  1. If I had learned to drive, I would be (more) independent now.
  2. If he were (was) more organized, he wouldn't have forgotten the meeting (yesterday).
  3. If Mia hadn't taken a free cooking class (two years ago), she wouldn't run / wouldn't be running a food truck today.
  4. If I hadn't stayed up until 2 a.m. (last night), I wouldn't be so tired now.
  5. If Sam weren't (wasn't) so shy, he would have spoken at yesterday's meeting.

Types: 1, 3, 4 = past cause → present result (a) · 2, 5 = present trait → past result (b). Other natural phrasings are fine if both time frames are right (e.g. 1 If I'd learned to drive, I wouldn't need rides from my friends now). Also accept learnt, organised. Wrong: "…I would have been independent now" · "If he had been more organized…" in 2 (grammatical as a third conditional, but loses the "always" meaning: mark with a note).

Dialogue — find the structures

could have criticism: you could have checked the traffic · might have: we might have made it anyway · mixed conditional about now: if we hadn't gotten lost, we wouldn't be sitting in this great little café right now. (Also in the dialogue: pure third conditionals If we hadn't taken the highway, we would've gotten there, If you'd told me…, I would've listened; and a type (b) mixed conditional if I were a bit more assertive, I probably would've insisted.)

Speaking — Chain of Consequences (Set E) · model chain

Start card 1: Monday, 7 a.m.: Ana's alarm didn't go off. → If Ana's alarm had gone off, she wouldn't have missed the bus. → If she hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't have walked through the park. → If she hadn't walked through the park, she wouldn't have smelled the tacos from a little food cart. → If she hadn't smelled the tacos, she wouldn't have stopped to buy some. → (ending card: …runs a food truck today) → If she hadn't stopped, she wouldn't have started talking to the cook, who was looking for a partner. → If he hadn't been looking for a partner, she might never have learned to make tacos. → If Ana hadn't learned to make tacos, she wouldn't be running her own food truck today!

Check each slip: if-half = had / hadn't + pp; result = would / could / might (not) have + pp; the last link = would (not) + base / be + -ing with now / today. The story can be as silly as the group likes.

Small turning points (open)

Check for: one correct third conditional, one mixed conditional (with now / today / still), one could have / might have. Model: Three years ago a coworker invited me to a salsa class. If she hadn't invited me, I would never have tried dancing. I might have joined a gym instead! If I hadn't gone, I wouldn't have so many new friends now. Don't mark the content, and don't ask for more personal detail.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is written correctly?
A) If I had seen it, I would have told you.
B) If I had seen it, I would of told you.
C) If I would have seen it, I told you.
Why: if + past perfect, then would have + past participle. B is a spelling error ("of" is how 've sounds); C puts would have in the if-clause.
Q2
"I didn't take the job in Berlin. If I ___ it, I ___ living in Germany now."
A) had taken … would be
B) had taken … would have been
C) took … would be
Why: a past cause (had taken) with a present result (would be… now) = mixed conditional. B uses a past result with now; C makes the cause present.
Q3 · new
Your friend didn't tell you about a big sale. You're annoyed.
A) You could have told me!
B) You could told me!
C) You could of told me!
Why: could have + past participle criticizes something that was possible but didn't happen. B is missing have; C is the "of" spelling error.
Errors you will hear most (1.2)

"If I would have known, I would help." · "If I knew (yesterday), I would tell you." · "If she hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't meet him." (second-conditional result for a past) · "If I had taken the job, I would have been rich now." · "I would of / could of…" (writing) · "If I had know…" (wrong participle) · "What you would have done?" Correct would in the if-clause and the missing have every time; treat mixed-conditional slips gently. They are a stretch at B2.

1.3 · Past Regrets — Wish, If Only, Would Rather & It's Time (C3)

Exercise 1 — The office kitchen (Script 1.3-A)
1234567
had brought · Pwould clean · Nhad · Nhadn't finished · Pgot · N'd rather · Nwe asked · N

Also accept: 1 'd brought · 5 bought (meaning OK) · 7 we went to / we talked to (meaning OK if the verb is past simple). Items 2, 5, 6, 7 are "now / future" even though 5 and 7 use a past form: that is the point of the lesson. Wrong: 5 "gets / get" · 7 "we ask."

Exercise 2 — Present wish or past regret?

1 earned (present) · 2 had taken (past regret) · 3 were (present; was is common in speech) · 4 hadn't told (past regret) · 5 could (present) · 6 hadn't missed (past regret) · 7 had brought (past regret: the moment of packing is over) · 8 were / was (present).

Wrong: 1 "would earn" · 5 "can" · 7 "brought" (sounds like a present wish about a general habit) · 8 "is."

Exercise 3 — Rewrite with I wish… / If only…
  1. I wish I had saved more money.
  2. I wish I hadn't eaten so much. (also: I wish I'd eaten less.)
  3. If only we had booked a table.
  4. I wish I hadn't forgotten her birthday. (also: I wish I'd remembered her birthday.)
  5. I wish my roommate would close the door.
  6. I wish it would stop raining.

1–4 must use wish / if only + past perfect; 5–6 use wish + would (annoyance, someone or something else). Also accept 5 I wish my roommate closed the door (a present wish, less annoyed). Contractions are fine. Wrong: "I wish I would have saved…" · 6 "I wish it stopped raining" (grammatical, but it sounds like a general wish, not an annoyed one: mark with a note).

Exercise 4 — would rather or it's time?

1 walk (same subject → base verb) · 2 didn't park (different subject → past simple) · 3 did · 4 went (also: left) · 5 called · 6 talk · 7 to start (no subject → to + base).

Wrong: 1 "to walk" · 2 "don't park / not park" · 3 "does / do" · 4 "go" · 7 "we started" is correct grammar if a learner adds a subject (It's time we started the meeting): accept it.

Exercise 5 — Find the mistake
  1. I wish I slept earlier. / I wish I could sleep earlier. / I wish I went to bed earlier. (no wish + would about yourself)
  2. It's time we left. (or: It's time to leave.)
  3. I'd rather you didn't go.
  4. I wish I had studied.
  5. I'd rather stay home tonight. (or: I'd prefer to stay…)
  6. I hope you pass your driving test! (possible → hope; also I hope you'll pass)
Dialogue — Tom's three wishes

About the past: I wish I hadn't stayed up so late (also If only I'd started it on Monday). A complaint about someone else: I wish my manager wouldn't give me things at the last minute. (The third, I wish I had more free weekends, is a present wish.)

Speaking — Would You Rather? & Wish List Meeting (open)

Listen for: I'd rather + base + than + base (not "than to"); I'd rather you + past simple in the follow-up; the meeting targets (2 present wishes, 1 regret with hadn't / had + pp, It's high time + past, I'd rather they + past, a polite disagreement). Model report (scenario A): "We wish the elevator worked properly. It's high time the owner fixed it. We wish they hadn't moved the trash cans to the back, and we'd rather people didn't do laundry after 10 p.m. So our top three are: fix the elevator, move the trash cans back, and quiet hours after ten."

My wishes & preferences (open)

Check each ticked structure: tense after wish matches the time; would rather + base (same subject) or + past (different subject); it's (high) time + subject + past or to + base. Mark the grammar only. Don't comment on how serious or personal the content is, and never ask for more.

Self-check
Q1
You didn't apply for the scholarship, and now you regret it.
A) I wish I had applied for the scholarship.
B) I wish I applied for the scholarship.
C) I wish I would apply for the scholarship.
Why: a regret about a finished past event = wish + past perfect. B is a present wish; C is wish + would about yourself.
Q2
"It's high time you ___ the dishes."
A) washed
B) wash
C) had washed
Why: it's (high) time + subject + past simple, with a present / future meaning. The past perfect isn't used after it's time.
Q3 · new
Your neighbor plays loud music every night.
A) I'd rather you didn't play your music so loud at night.
B) I'd rather you don't play your music so loud at night.
C) I'd rather you not to play your music so loud at night.
Why: would rather + a different subject + past simple (a present / future meaning). B is very common in casual speech but is not standard; C mixes in to.
Errors you will hear most (1.3)

"I wish I would exercise more." · "I wish you will pass." (for hope) · "I wish I had spoken French." (meaning now) · "I'd rather you don't…" · "It's time we go." · "I'd rather to stay." · "I rather walk." (missing 'd) · "I wish I would have known." Correct the wish / hope mix-up and the now / past mix-up every time (they change the meaning); treat "I'd rather you don't" gently in speech, but correct it in writing.

Dictations (Set I)

Answers are the scripts themselves

1.1-B: 1. By the time we got to the station, the last train had already left. · 2. She was reading quietly when the lights suddenly went out. · 3. They'd been driving for hours before they found a place to stop. · 4. I recognized him at once because we'd met before. 1.2-B: 1. If I'd known, I would've called you. · 2. She wouldn't have missed the bus if she'd left earlier. · 3. If we hadn't gotten lost, we wouldn't be here now. · 4. You could have told me! 1.3-B: 1. I wish I hadn't eaten so much cake. · 2. I'd rather you didn't call after ten. · 3. It's high time they fixed the elevator. · 4. I wish it would stop raining.

Mark contracted and full forms as equal (I'd known = I had known). The most common dictation errors: missing 'd (1.1-B 3, 1.2-B 1), "would of" (1.2-B 1), and "recognised" (fine).
