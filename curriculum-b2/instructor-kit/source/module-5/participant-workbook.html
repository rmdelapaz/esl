Small choices that make your English exact. Place an action precisely on a future timeline: still happening, or already done? Say precisely who did the work: you, or someone you paid? And say precisely how much of a group you mean, with a verb that agrees.

5.1

Still Happening or Already Done? — Future Continuous & Future Perfect

Class C14
📖 🗣️ New words — plans, deadlines and milestones
  • This time tomorrow, I'll be flying.
  • 🗓️ a deadline · a milestone
  • 🚀 the launch · opening day
  • ⏱️ ahead of / on / behind schedule

🗣️ Useful frames:

This time next year…By then, we'll have…By the time you get here…In a few years' time…At 10 a.m. tomorrow, I'll be…Will you be joining us?They'll have left by now.
Grammar box

Choose a reference point in the future first. Then ask: is the action still happening then, or already finished?

Future continuous: in progressFuture perfect: already done
Formwill be + -ingwill have + past participle
At 8 p.m.We'll be eating dinner. (still at the table)We'll have eaten dinner. (plates cleared)
This time next yearI'll be studying in Berlin.I'll have graduated.
Negative / questionWe won't be using the room. · Will you be staying long?He won't have arrived by six. · Will you have eaten?
Typical markersthis time tomorrow / next week · at + clock timeby · by then · by the time · in + period
Polite inquiriesWill you use the car tonight? → Will you be using the car tonight? (softer: it asks about your plans, not for a decision)
by the timeBy the time you arrive (present!), we'll have left.  ❌ By the time you will arrive…
by vs untilI'll have finished by six. (= no later than six) · I'll be working until six. (= all the time up to six)  ❌ I'll have finished until six.

5.2

Who Actually Did It? — The Causative

Class C15
📖 🗣️ New words — everyday services
  • get the car serviced / the oil changed
  • have the sink fixed / unclogged
  • have a window replaced
  • get a spare key made / copied
  • have your pants shortened / hemmed
  • get a jacket dry-cleaned
  • have your shoes repaired
  • get the screen replaced
  • have your passport photo taken
  • 💇 get your hair cut · get a haircut
  • 📄 get flyers / copies printed

Places: the auto shop · the hair salon / barber shop · the print & copy shop · the dry cleaner's · the alterations shop · the phone repair kiosk · the key counter at the hardware store · the building manager

Grammar box

I cut my hair. = I did it myself.  ·  I had my hair cut. = someone did it for me (I arranged it).

Recipehave / get + object + past participle
PresentI have my car serviced every year.
Present continuousShe's having her hair cut right now.
PastWe had the house painted last month.
Present perfectI've just had my eyes tested.
FutureThey'll have the roof repaired soon. · I'm going to have the kitchen redone.
ModalsYou should get your teeth checked. · We should have had it fixed sooner.

Only have / get changes. The order is always have → thing → done. ❌ I had repaired my car. (= past perfect: I did it myself, earlier.)

have or get?Same meaning. have = a little more neutral / formal · get = informal, very common in speech; can mean "finally managed": I finally got the heater fixed.
MisfortuneSame form, but something bad happened to you: She had her bag stolen. They had their flight canceled.
get + person + topersuade: I got my landlord to fix the window.
have + person + verbarrange (no to): I'll have the porter bring your bags up.
have + person + -ingcause something ongoing: She soon had everyone laughing.

How Much of the Group? — Determiners & Quantifiers

Class C16
📖 🗣️ New words — talking about groups and data
100%all of us · the whole class · every one of us · each of us
↓almost all of us · the (vast) majority of us · most of us
about 50%about half of us · just over / just under half of us
↓some of us · a few of us · only a handful of us · a minority of us
0%none of us · not one of us · not a single person
2 people / thingsboth (2 of 2) · either (1 of 2, any one) · neither (0 of 2)

Grammar box
all / wholeall (+ the) + plural or uncountable · the / my + whole + singular · all day (no the)all the books · all the information · the whole book · It rained all day / the whole day. ❌ all the day
every / each+ singular noun + singular verb · every = the group as a whole · each = one by one (often two)Every room has a view. · Each student gets a report. · a bag in each hand · almost every day ❌ every people
bothtwo together · plural verb · both … and …Both restaurants are excellent.
neither / either0 of 2 / 1 of 2 · singular verb · neither … nor … · either … or …Neither option is cheap. · Either day works for me. · Neither of them is perfect.
+ ofbefore the / my / this / them / usmost of the people here · all of my friends · none of us · both of them
no ofgeneral statements, bare nounsMost people love music. · All children need sleep. ❌ most of people
none of+ uncountable → singular · + plural → singular (formal) or plural (everyday)None of the water was clean. · None of the students was / were late.

No the? Then no of. · people is already plural, so never every people. · both / neither / either = only two.
