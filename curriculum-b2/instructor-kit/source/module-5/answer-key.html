Module 5 — Precision Grammar: Answers

Answers to every Module 5 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (Project Timeline, Errand Week, Services & Errands, the class survey, the Precision Path board game), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 5.1–5.3Classes C14–C169 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At B2, mark meaning first, then form, then range. An answer is correct if the meaning is right (in progress vs finished · who did the work · how much of the group) and the structure is right: will be + -ing / will have + p.p., present after by the time, by not until for a deadline; have / get + object + p.p. in that order, get … to / have … (no to); singular agreement after every, each, neither, either, and of only before the / my / them. Be generous about optional things: contractions (I'll have, we'll be, won't have) or full forms; have or get in the causative unless the item asks for one; going to where will is not required; none of + plural with a singular or a plural verb; neither of them are in speech (not in the formal items). American English is the model; British forms (flat, trousers, tyres, neighbour, film, timetable, whilst) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

5.1 · Future Perfect & Future Continuous (C14)

Progress Test 1 return — "Let's fix it!" (board errors)

If I had known, I would have called you. (no would in the if-clause) · I wish I hadn't said that yesterday. (past regret → past perfect) · She suggested (that) I apply / applying for the job. (suggest + that-clause or -ing; no "suggest me to") · I'm used to getting up at five. (be used to + -ing) · Extras: She must have forgotten her keys. · Despite the rain / In spite of the rain, we went. · Never have I seen such a mess.

Exercise 2 — In progress or already done?
  1. will be interviewing (in progress between two and four)
  2. will have set up (finished before the guests arrive)
  3. will be running (in progress this time next month)
  4. will have tested (finished within three weeks, before the launch)
  5. will be unloading (in progress at 7:30)
  6. will have planted (finished by 2030)

Also correct: contractions; 3 "the new bus line will be running" / "will have started running" (both make sense: accept either with the right form). Wrong: "will interview" in 1 (loses "don't call, I'm busy then") · "will planted" · "will be plant."

Exercise 3 — by, until or by the time?
  1. by Friday (a deadline: no later than Friday)
  2. until the end of April (all the time up to then)
  3. By the time the guests arrive, … (present in the time clause)
  4. by lunchtime
  5. until 10 p.m.
  6. By the time you get this message, …

Also correct: 1 before / no later than Friday · 4 before lunchtime. Wrong: 1, 4 "until" · 3, 6 "will arrive / will get."

Exercise 4 — Make it polite
  1. Will you be using the projector this afternoon?
  2. How many people will be coming? (or: will be joining us?)
  3. Will you be checking any bags?
  4. Will you be taking (any) vacation in August?
  5. How long will you be staying?

Accept small changes that keep the form (Will you be needing the projector…?). Wrong: "Will you be use…" · "Are you be using…"

Exercise 5 — Find the mistake
  1. By the time you get here, we'll have eaten.
  2. I'll have finished the report by six.
  3. By 2030, she will have finished her degree.
  4. Don't call at 8 — we'll be watching the game. (in progress at 8)
  5. This time next week, I'll be lying on a beach.

Also correct: 2 "I'll be working on the report until six" (a different, correct sentence; accept with a note). Spelling watch in 5: lie → lying, not "lieing."

Dialogue — Joe and Nina

Future continuous: 'll probably be working · 'll be settling · 'll be looking back. Future perfect: 'll have finished · will you have moved · 'll have signed · 'll have earned. Reference points: this time next year · by then · by the time you start · by September · by winter.

Speaking — Project Timeline (classroom materials, Set B)

Open. Check: each point on the frame has one will be + -ing and one will have + p.p. idea that matches the meaning; by the time + present; council questions with Will you be…? / Will you have… by…?. Model (Riverside Community Center card): "Next month the builders will be working on the kitchen, and they'll have finished the roof. By the time the summer program starts, we'll have painted all the classrooms. This time next year we'll be running evening English classes, and we'll have trained twenty volunteers. In five years' time we'll have welcomed over ten thousand visitors." Council: "Will you be hiring local people?" · "Will you have fixed the parking lot by opening day?"

Self-check
Q1
"Don't call at 8 — we ___ dinner then." Which form fits an action in progress at 8?
A) will be having
B) will have had
C) will have
Why: the action is in progress at the reference point, so it's the future continuous. B means dinner is already finished before 8; C loses the "in progress" meaning.
Q2
Which sentence is correct?
A) By the time you arrive, I'll have cooked dinner.
B) By the time you will arrive, I'll have cooked dinner.
C) I'll have cooked dinner until you arrive.
Why: after by the time the verb is present; the future perfect goes in the main clause. B puts will in the time clause; C uses until (duration) instead of a deadline.
Q3 · new
A hotel receptionist wants to ask about your plans in a polite, neutral way.
A) Will you pay by card?
B) Will you be paying by card?
C) Will you have paid by card?
Why: the future continuous asks about plans, not for a decision, so it sounds softer. A is correct English but more direct; C means "finished paying before a future point," which doesn't fit.
Errors you will hear most (5.1)

"By the time you will arrive…" · "I'll have finished until six." · "By 2030 I will finished / will has finished…" · "This time tomorrow I will flying." · "At 8 we'll have eaten" when the speaker means in progress · "In two years I'll have finish." (lost -ed) · writing "will of" for will've. Correct the time-clause will and until every time (they are the classic B2 errors); treat a simple will where the continuous would be nicer as a range "upgrade," not an error.

5.2 · The Causative — have / get something done (C15)

Exercise 1 — Marco's Saturday (Script 5.2-A)

1 serviced · 2 tires · 3 printed · 4 made · 5 cut · 6 to fix · 7 look · 8 Marco did (himself): "I washed the dog myself."

Also accept 4 copied / cut (all are used for keys). 8: any answer that shows Marco did it himself ("He washed it himself").

Exercise 2 — Rewrite with the causative
  1. I have the oil in my car changed every six months.
  2. I'm having my phone screen replaced right now.
  3. We got our wedding invitations printed.
  4. We've just had the leak under our sink fixed.
  5. I'll have my new pants shortened.
  6. We're going to have our living room painted.
  7. She had her car scratched in the parking lot.

Also correct: get instead of have in 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 (the item doesn't require have for meaning; mark get as fine but note the instruction) · 1 "I have my oil changed" · 7 "got her car scratched." Wrong: "I have changed the oil…" (present perfect: I did it) · "I'm having replaced my phone screen" (order) · 3 "We got printed our invitations."

Exercise 3 — Myself or a professional?
  1. cut (I do it myself: plain verb)
  2. had it repaired / had the dishwasher repaired
  3. changes (himself: plain verb)
  4. get your eyes checked
  5. didn't paint … had it painted
  6. fixed (herself: plain verb)

The traps are 1, 3 and 6: no one else does the work, so there is no causative. Accept 4 have your eyes checked / tested.

Exercise 4 — What does it mean?
123456
bcabac

The order decides: object before the participle = causative (a or b; context decides which); participle before the object = past perfect (c). In 5, "had her nails done" = at a nail salon.

Exercise 5 — get someone to / have someone do

1 to replace · 2 bring · 3 to call · 4 reschedule · 5 laughing · 6 send

get + person + to (1, 3) · have + person + base verb (2, 4, 6) · have + person + -ing for an ongoing effect (5). Wrong: "have a porter to bring" · "got my landlord replace" · 5 "laugh" is possible ("had everyone laugh" = made them laugh once) but the lesson's target is laughing; accept with a note.

Dialogue

Causatives: had it detailed · got the dent fixed · get mine serviced · have it checked · having the whole engine rebuilt · have the tires replaced. "Finally managed": I finally got the dent fixed. Note: detailed = professionally cleaned inside and out (British: valeted).

Speaking — Services & Errands role-play (classroom materials, Set D)

Open. Check the customer's three moves: (1) two causatives ("I'd like to have the oil changed and get the brakes checked") · (2) a "when" question with a future form ("Will you have finished it by five?" / "When will it be ready?") · (3) one neutral inquiry ("Will you be calling me when it's ready?"). Service providers should explain the card's problem with a future form: "We'll have to order the part, so we won't have finished until Thursday." Model report: "I'm getting my jacket dry-cleaned. They'll have done it by Saturday, and they're having a new button sewn on too."

Self-check
Q1
A hairdresser cut your hair yesterday. Which sentence is correct?
A) I had my hair cut yesterday.
B) I had cut my hair yesterday.
C) I had cut yesterday my hair.
Why: have + object + past participle: someone else did it. B is the past perfect (I cut it myself, earlier); C is not a possible word order.
Q2
"She ___ on the train." Someone stole her bag; she didn't arrange it.
A) had her bag stolen
B) had stolen her bag
C) stole her bag
Why: the causative form also expresses misfortune. B and C make her the thief.
Q3 · new
Ana persuaded her brother to help her move.
A) Ana had her brother helped.
B) Ana got her brother to help.
C) Ana got her brother help.
Why: get + person + to + verb = persuade. A uses a past participle for a person (it would mean someone helped her brother); C drops to (only have + person takes the base verb).
Errors you will hear most (5.2)

"I had repaired my car" (for a garage repair) · "I got fixed my phone" · "I made the mechanic repair my car" (sounds like force) · "The hotel will have the porter to bring…" · "I got my landlord fix it" · "I had it fix" (lost ending) · "I cut my hair" for a salon visit (fine in casual speech; upgrade to I got my hair cut / got a haircut). Correct word order every time (it changes the meaning); treat "I cut my hair at the salon" as an upgrade, not an error.

Exercise 1 — Circle the correct word

1 the whole · 2 Every · 3 each (two ears) · 4 All (all + the + plural) · 5 Most (no the → no of) · 6 neither (two) · 7 none (five) · 8 every (almost every)

Note on 1: "all morning" (without the) is also correct, but it isn't an option. Note on 5: "Most of the people in my building" would also be correct.

Exercise 2 — Fix the mistake
  1. Every person in the office has a key card. (or: All the people in the office have key cards.)
  2. Each of the apartments has a balcony.
  3. Neither of the buses goes downtown.
  4. Most students finished early. / Most of the students finished early.
  5. Both of the options are fine with me. (or: Either option is fine with me.)
  6. It snowed all week. / It snowed the whole week.

In 3, "Neither of the buses go" is common in speech; the item asks for formal English, so the answer is goes. In 5, Either option is fine is the most natural way to say "I don't mind which": accept it.

Exercise 3 — Two apartments
  1. Both apartments cost $1,400 a month.
  2. Neither apartment has parking.
  3. Neither of them allows pets.
  4. Both apartments are near a bus stop, so either one would work for my commute.
  5. They are both available on June 1.
  6. Neither the Oak Street apartment nor the Elm Avenue one allows pets.

Stretch talk: "Which would you choose?" "The Oak Street one, because it has two bedrooms and costs the same." Accept 3 "Neither of them allow" in speech only.

Exercise 4 — of or no of?

1 ✗ (general: most people) · 2 of (+ the) · 3 of (+ pronoun) · 4 ✗ (general: all children) · 5 of (+ my) · 6 of (+ pronoun)

Note on 4: "All of children" is always wrong; "all of the children in the school" is correct (specific). With all + the, of is optional: "all (of) the books."

Accept 3 Every (the meaning is the same here). Follow-up questions: "How many people answered?" (40, all the staff) · "How many want the schedule in March?" (31, most of them) · "What happens with parking?" (Nothing changes: neither option was popular.)

Dialogue

Determiners: both places · neither option · either choice · neither of them · both of us · each one. Singular verbs: neither option is · either choice means · neither of them is, because neither and either look at one option at a time. both … have / agree are plural.

Speaking — Class Survey & Report (classroom materials, Set E)

Open. Check: totals add up to the number of people in the room · at least one all / most / none of us, one every / each, one both / neither / either · singular verbs after every, each, neither, either · of only before us / the · no names. Model (card 4, coffee or tea, 14 people): "We asked every person in the class whether they drink coffee, tea, both, or neither. Most of us (eight people) drink coffee, and four of us drink both. Only one of us drinks neither, and one person passed. Each coffee drinker has at least one cup before class. Neither of us (in our pair) drinks tea." Upgrades to praise: the majority of us, just over half of us, only a handful of us, not a single person.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) Every student has a locker.
B) Every people have a locker.
C) Every student have a locker.
Why: every takes a singular noun and a singular verb. B uses a plural noun (people); C has a plural verb.
Q2
In careful, formal English, which is correct?
A) Neither of the plans is ready.
B) Neither the plans are ready.
C) Both of the plans is ready.
Why: neither of + plural noun + singular verb in formal English. B is missing of; C gives both a singular verb.
Q3 · new
Which sentence is correct?
A) It rained all the day.
B) Most of people here walk to work.
C) Each of the rooms has a window.
Why: each of + the + plural noun + singular verb. A should be "all day / the whole day"; B should be "most people" or "most of the people."
Errors you will hear most (5.3)

"Every people…" · "Each students…" · "Every student have…" · "Most of people / most of students" · "The most of us…" · "all the day" · "the whole cakes" · "both of my three brothers" · "All us / most them" (no of before a pronoun) · "None came" for people (→ nobody came / none of them came). Correct every / each + plural noun and "most of" + bare noun every time; in speech, don't correct neither of them are or none of us were, which are normal English.
