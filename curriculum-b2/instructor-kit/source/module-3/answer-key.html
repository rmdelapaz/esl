Module 3 — Advanced Structures: Answers

Answers to every Module 3 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, model versions for the open tasks (the news bulletin, the Editor's Desk, the meeting minutes), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 3.1–3.3Classes C7–C99 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At B2, mark accuracy and range. A sentence is correct when the structure is right (be + past participle in every passive, the right infinitive for the timing, -ing vs past participle in a reduced clause, the same subject in a participle clause, the right pattern after a reporting verb) and the meaning matches the original. Be generous about optional things: that can be used or dropped; have and get are both fine in the causative; will be and is going to be are both fine; in suggest / insist that, accept the base verb (that we go, American), should + base, and the backshifted form (that we went, common in British English). Contractions are fine. American English is the model; British spellings (analysed, apologised, cancelled) are acceptable when a learner uses them. When a learner writes a correct sentence that uses a different structure from the target, accept it and point to the target: "Correct! Can you also say it with Pattern B?"

3.1 · The Passive — Advanced & Impersonal (C7)

Exercise 1 — The radio bulletin (Script 3.1-A)
1234567
is thought to bewas foundis believed to have been lefthas been askedwas returnedis said to have been borrowedwill be held

Accept contractions from the audio only where the meaning is the same ('s thought to be). Item 3 is the hardest (Pattern B + earlier action + passive infinitive): if learners write is believed to have left, ask "Did the piano leave, or did someone leave it?"

Exercise 2 — Up the ladder
  1. are analyzed (also: are analysed)
  2. will be built
  3. have been sold (have already been sold if the learner moves "already")
  4. must be completed
  5. should have been finished
  6. was being repaired
  7. are being cleaned
  8. might have been delivered

Wrong: 1 "is analyzed" (subject is plural) · 5 "should have finished" (missing been: active meaning) · 6 "was repairing" · 7 "are cleaning" · 8 "might be delivered" (not past) / "might have delivered".

Exercise 3 — Both ways
  1. A: It is said that the castle is haunted. · B: The castle is said to be haunted.
  2. A: It is believed that the economy is recovering. · B: The economy is believed to be recovering.
  3. A: It was reported that the CEO had left the country. · B: The CEO was reported to have left the country.
  4. A: It is known that she was a brilliant scientist. · B: She is known to have been a brilliant scientist.
  5. A: It is thought that the tortoise is about 120. · B: The tortoise is thought to be about 120.
  6. A: It is said that the singer is living in Canada now. · B: The singer is said to be living in Canada now.

3 and 4 need to have + pp because the reported action happened earlier than the reporting. In 3 the reporting verb is past (reported), so Pattern A keeps the past perfect and Pattern B uses was reported. Also accept in 3: It has been reported that… · 6 B: to live (a state; "now" makes to be living better). Wrong: "It is said the castle to be haunted" (mixing A and B) · 4 B "She is known to be a brilliant scientist" (only correct if she still is: the original says was).

Exercise 4 — Now or earlier?

1 to have been built (earlier, and the castle received the action) · 2 to be announced (future/same-time expectation) · 3 to be living (in progress, "at the moment") · 4 to have escaped (earlier) · 5 to be (he is a great cook now) · 6 to have been sold (earlier; "last week").

In 3, "to have lived" would mean she lived there in the past; with "at the moment," it's wrong. In 2, expected naturally points to the future, so to be announced is right even though "tomorrow" is later than now.

Exercise 5 — The causative
  1. I had / got my car serviced yesterday.
  2. She is having / getting her hair cut (right now).
  3. We are going to have / will have / will get our portraits taken next week.
  4. He had / got his bike stolen last night.
  5. I had / got a new key cut this morning.
  6. We are going to have / get our windows cleaned on Friday.

Wrong: "I had serviced my car" (= I did it myself, earlier) · "She is cutting her hair" (she does it herself) · "I got repaired my car" (word order: object before the participle) · "I had my car service" (no participle).

Dialogue — in the newsroom

Passives: is thought to weigh · It is thought that… · was grown · is said to have fed · 'll (will) be weighed · is expected. Causative: to have it made into pies. Rumors: it weighs about a thousand pounds and she fed it milk every day (reported with is thought / is said). Facts: was grown by Mrs. Olsen · will be weighed on Saturday. A new state record is expected is a prediction, reported as a fact about expectations.

Speaking — News Report Rewrite

Warm-up lines: 1 The new bakery is said to use a 100-year-old recipe. / It is said that the new bakery uses… · 2 The owner is thought to have learned to bake in Italy. / It is thought that the owner learned… · 3 It is going to be opened / It will be opened next Monday. · 4 Long lines are expected on the first day. / It is expected that there will be long lines on the first day.

Model bulletin (Newsroom story 1, the giant sandwich): "Record Sandwich Stretches Across Riverside Park. A sandwich measuring more than 300 feet was made in Riverside Park last night by 40 volunteers. It is said to be longer than a football field and is believed to have taken all night to build. It will be measured by an official judge on Friday, and a new record is expected. It will then be cut into 1,000 pieces and shared with visitors. Tickets must be bought in advance." Model bulletins for all six stories are printed small at the bottom of each Newsroom B card (classroom materials, set C). Check the tick-boxes: Pattern A, Pattern B, a modal passive, another tense, to have + pp.

Self-check
Q1
"People think the manager left the company last year." Which impersonal passive is correct?
A) The manager is thought to leave the company last year.
B) The manager is thought to have left the company last year.
C) It is thought the manager to have left the company last year.
Why: the leaving happened before the thinking, so Pattern B needs to have + past participle. A puts the action at the same time. C mixes Pattern A (It…) with Pattern B (…to have left).
Q2
A mechanic fixed your brakes yesterday. Which sentence correctly uses "have something done"?
A) I had fixed my brakes yesterday.
B) I had fix my brakes yesterday.
C) I had my brakes fixed yesterday.
Why: have + object + past participle. A is the past perfect: it means you fixed them yourself. B has no participle.
Q3 · new
"The form ___ yesterday. Now it's too late."
A) should have sent
B) should have been sent
C) should be sent
Why: the form doesn't send anything: it receives the action (passive), and it's a criticism of the past (modal perfect). Modal + have been + pp. A is active (the form sent something); C is present/future.
Errors you will hear most (3.1)

"It is said him to be rich." · "He is said that he is rich." · "Is said that…" (no It) · "He is thought to steal the money." (for an earlier action) · "The piano is believed to have left by a musician." (active infinitive for a passive meaning) · "It should have done yesterday." · "I cut my hair yesterday" (for a haircut at a salon) · "I had repaired my car" (for the causative) · "The road is repairing." Treat to be / to have been as the key B2 accuracy point, and praise range (widely believed, reportedly, is expected to be completed).

3.2 · Relative & Participle Clauses (C8)

Exercise 1 — The Old Town audio tour (Script 3.2-A)

1 Built · 2 standing · 3 Located · 4 painted · 5 Having climbed · 6 selling · 7 Surrounded.

Follow-up question for each: "Does the noun do it or receive it?" (1 receives · 2 does · 3 receives · 4 receives · 5 the visitors did it, earlier · 6 does · 7 receives.)

Exercise 2 — Reduce the relative clause
  1. The people waiting outside look cold.
  2. The email sent yesterday never arrived.
  3. Any student failing the test can retake it.
  4. The paintings stolen in 2004 were finally found.
  5. The road connecting the two towns is closed.
  6. The man repairing our roof is from Ohio.
  7. Houses built before 1950 often need new windows.

In 3, failing works because it's a general condition (any student, any time). The online lesson uses British resit; accept it. Wrong: 2 "The email sending yesterday" · 4 "The paintings stealing / stole" · 7 "Houses building before 1950" · keeping part of the pronoun ("The email which sent yesterday").

Exercise 3 — Combine
  1. Not feeling well, she stayed at home.
  2. Driving to work, he heard the news on the radio. (also: While driving to work, …)
  3. Having finished dinner, they went for a walk. (also: After finishing dinner, …)
  4. Not knowing his number, I couldn't call him.
  5. Located in the old town hall, the museum is open every day. (also: The museum, located in the old town hall, is open every day.)
  6. Having read the report, she called the team.

Wrong: 1 "Feeling not well" (not goes first) · 3 "Finishing dinner, they went for a walk" (possible, but the sequence is less clear: accept with a note) · 4 "Not knowing his number, he couldn't…" (changes the subject) · 5 "Locating in the old town hall" (the museum doesn't locate anything).

Exercise 4 — Dangler doctor (models: any version that fixes the subject is correct)
  1. Looking out of the window, I thought the mountains were beautiful. / When I looked out of the window, the mountains were beautiful.
  2. Having eaten too much, I felt sleepy. / The meal made me sleepy because I had eaten too much.
  3. Running for the bus, I felt my bag fall open. / As I was running for the bus, my bag fell open.
  4. Opening the fridge, I noticed that the milk smelled terrible. / When I opened the fridge, the milk smelled terrible.
  5. Tired after work, I found the sofa very inviting. / Because I was tired after work, the sofa looked very inviting.

Item 5 is a stretch item: Tired after work is an adjective phrase, not a participle clause, but it dangles in exactly the same way. Two ways to fix any dangler: change the main-clause subject, or expand the participle into a full clause with its own subject.

Exercise 5 — Reduce it, or keep it?
  1. ✔ The people working in this office are very friendly.
  2. ✘ One finished action in the past: "the man fixing my sink yesterday" sounds wrong. Keep who fixed.
  3. ✔ The cake made by my neighbor won first prize.
  4. ✘ Non-defining (extra information, commas): keep it full in careful writing. (My brother, living in Houston, … is possible in literary style, but not the target.)
  5. ✘ for a participle: that is the object (we watched it), so there's no participle to keep. You can only drop the pronoun: The movie we watched last night was boring. (Accept this as a correct "shorter" answer.)
  6. ✔ Passengers traveling with children may board first. (British: travelling)
Dialogue — editing the newsletter

Reduced clauses: The yard sale organized by the tenants' committee · Anyone wanting to sell things. Participle clause: Having finished the draft, I sent it to Maria. (The joke "Having finished the draft, Maria got an email" is a dangler: it says Maria finished it.) Ben keeps Mrs. Cruz, who lives in 3B, in full because it's a non-defining clause: extra information inside commas.

Self-check
Q1
Reduce: "The report which was prepared by the committee is on your desk."
A) The report preparing by the committee is on your desk.
B) The report was prepared by the committee is on your desk.
C) The report prepared by the committee is on your desk.
Why: the report receives the action (it was prepared), so it reduces to the past participle. A uses -ing (active); B keeps was and makes two verbs.
Q2
Which sentence has a dangling participle?
A) Feeling nervous, she took a deep breath.
B) Sleeping deeply, the alarm didn't wake me.
C) Having finished, we left the room quietly.
Why: grammatically, "the alarm" is sleeping. Fix: "Sleeping deeply, I didn't hear the alarm." In A and C the subjects match.
Q3 · new
Which sentence is correct?
A) The books selling here are second-hand.
B) The books sold here are second-hand.
C) The books were sold here are second-hand.
Why: the books don't sell anything: they are sold (passive → past participle). C has two main verbs.
Errors you will hear most (3.2)

"The books selling here…" · "the bridge building in 1890" · "Walking home, the rain started." · "Having finished the report, the printer broke." · "the man calling me yesterday" (one finished action) · "Feeling not well…" · irregular participles ("the paintings stealed," "the letter wrote by John") · reducing inside commas in formal writing. In speech, don't push participle clauses: "the guy who's standing there" is perfectly natural. Praise learners who choose not to reduce and can say why.

3.3 · Reporting Verbs & Patterns (C9)

Exercise 1 — The residents' meeting (Script 3.3-A)
1Ms. Park suggested making a schedule so each floor has its own laundry day.
2Mr. Diaz denied taking down the "Out of order" sign.
3Tom admitted leaving his clothes in the dryer all weekend.
4Mr. Diaz refused to buy a new washing machine until the owner approved it.
5Ms. Okafor agreed to make the schedule and put it on the door.
6Tom insisted on cleaning the laundry room on Saturday.
7Ms. Park reminded everyone to take their clothes out on time.

The pattern after each gap tells you the verb: -ing (1, 2, 3, 6), to (4, 5), person + to (7). In 1, has may be backshifted to had; both are fine because the schedule plan is still true.

Exercise 2 — Choose the best verb

1 offered · 2 refused · 3 admitted · 4 warned · 5 accused · 6 apologized (British: apologised).

Exercise 3 — The correct pattern
  1. promised to call (she promised to call me back)
  2. denied taking (also: denied stealing; denied that he had taken)
  3. advised me to walk (any person: advised him / her to walk)
  4. insisted on paying
  5. suggested taking (also: suggested getting; suggested that we take)
  6. congratulated her / him / them on passing
  7. refused to pay
  8. agreed to work

Wrong: 2 "denied to take" · 3 "advised to walk" (no person) · 4 "insisted to pay" / "insisted in paying" · 5 "suggested to take" / "suggested us to take" · 6 "congratulated her for passing" (accept with a note: for is heard, but on is the standard) · 7 "refused not to pay" (double negative).

Exercise 4 — Fix the pattern error
  1. She suggested taking the earlier train. / She suggested (that) I take the earlier train.
  2. He recommended visiting the old town. / He recommended (that) I visit the old town.
  3. They apologized for being late.
  4. He accused me of stealing his idea.
  5. She admitted breaking the vase. / She admitted (that) she had broken the vase. / She admitted to breaking the vase.
  6. I agree with the new schedule.
  7. He refused to work on Saturday.

5: admit to + -ing is correct and very common in American English; the error is only the infinitive (to break). 7: the original is a refusal about a future action, so deny is the wrong verb, not only the wrong pattern ("He denied working on Saturday" would mean he says he didn't work).

Exercise 5 — The meeting six
  1. Lena suggested starting the meeting at nine instead of eight. / Lena suggested (that) we start the meeting at nine…
  2. Omar refused to change his day off.
  3. Kate denied losing the keys. (She said she had given them to Paul.) / Kate denied that she had lost the keys.
  4. Paul admitted forgetting to give them back. / Paul admitted (that) he had forgotten to give them back.
  5. Mia agreed to order the new chairs.
  6. Sam insisted on staying late and locking up.

Accept having lost / having forgotten (perfect -ing, a stretch form). In 6, both -ing forms are needed after on: "staying late and locking up" (not "and lock up").

Dialogue — Dana and Marco, reported

Model: Dana apologized for forgetting to send the report. Marco reminded her that she had promised to have it ready. Dana admitted (that) she was at fault and promised to finish it that night. Marco suggested setting a reminder on her phone. Dana agreed and insisted on handling the client herself. Finally, Marco warned her not to miss the Friday deadline. Also accept: Marco complained that it was the second time · Dana offered to email the client.

Speaking — Meeting Minutes

Model minutes (Meeting 2, Brightside Café): "Staff meeting, Monday. Present: Alex (manager), Sam, Jo, Lee. Alex suggested a rotating weekend schedule. Sam refused to work on Sunday mornings because of his class, but offered to work Saturday evenings instead. Jo denied breaking the coffee machine and insisted on calling a repair company. Lee admitted forgetting to clean the machine on Friday and apologized for the mess in the kitchen. Lee agreed to cover Sam's Sunday shift. Alex reminded everyone to clean the machine every night. It was agreed that the new schedule would start next week." Check: 6+ different verbs, no said / told, correct patterns (tick each one), backshift in any that-clauses.

Self-check
Q1
"Don't walk on the ice." Which correctly reports this warning?
A) She warned walking on the ice.
B) She warned us not to walk on the ice.
C) She warned us to don't walk on the ice.
Why: warn + person + (not) to-infinitive; the negative goes before to. A has no person and the wrong pattern; C puts don't after to. (Also correct: She warned us against walking on the ice.)
Q2
"Let's eat out tonight." Which report is correct?
A) He suggested us to eat out that night.
B) He suggested to eat out that night.
C) He suggested eating out that night.
Why: suggest takes -ing or a that-clause (suggested that we eat out), never an infinitive and never person + to.
Q3 · new
"I didn't break the printer!" Which report is correct?
A) He denied breaking the printer.
B) He denied to break the printer.
C) He refused breaking the printer.
Why: deny reports saying something is not true, + -ing (or that he had broken). B has the wrong pattern; C has the wrong verb (refuse = say no to a future action, + to).
Errors you will hear most (3.3)

"She suggested me to…" · "He recommended me to…" · "He denied to pay." (for a refusal) · "I am agree." · "He explained me the problem." · "They apologized to be late." · "She insisted to pay." / "insisted in paying" · "He accused me for lying." · "She reminded to call." (no person) · "He refused not to go." · no backshift in that-clauses ("He admitted that he forgets"). The suggest error is the priority: correct it every time, in speech too. For range, praise insisted on + -ing, admitted having + pp, and precise attitude verbs (claimed, conceded, urged).
