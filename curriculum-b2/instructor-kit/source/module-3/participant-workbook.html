Advanced Structures

Three ways to say more with less. Shift the focus with the passive, and report what people believe in the calm voice of the news. Cut long sentences down to tight, elegant ones. And report other people's words with the one verb that says exactly what they were doing: she refused, he admitted, they suggested.

3.1

It Is Said That… — The Passive, Advanced & Impersonal

Class C7
📖 🗣️ New words — the language of the news
a bulletina headlinea rumora claimto confirmaccording to…reportedlywidely (believed)a recordto be expected to…

🗣️ Useful phrases for formal reporting:

It is widely believed that…It is generally accepted that…It has been reported that…… is thought to be…… is said to have…… is expected to…

📖 Services: somebody else does the job for you.

  • have my car repaired
  • have a key cut
  • get my shoes repaired
  • have my passport renewed
Grammar box

The passive in any tense: change only be. The past participle never changes.

Present simple / continuousIt is done. · It is being done.
Past simple / continuousIt was done. · It was being done.
Present / past perfectIt has been done. · It had been done.
FutureIt will be done. · It is going to be done.
ModalIt must / can / should be done.
Modal perfectIt should / might / must have been done.

Impersonal reporting passives: report what people believe without saying who. Verbs: say, think, believe, know, report, expect, consider, understand.

ActivePeople say (that) he is very rich.
Pattern AIt is said that he is very rich.
Pattern BHe is said to be very rich.

In Pattern B, the infinitive shows the timing:

Same timeto be / to doHe is thought to be rich.
In progress nowto be + -ingShe is thought to be living in Florida.
Earlierto have + past participleHe is thought to have left the country.

The causative: have / get + object + past participle = someone else does it for you.

✅ I had my car repaired. (a mechanic did it) · ❌ I had repaired my car. (= I repaired it myself, earlier)

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — in the newsroom

Editor: OK, what have we got for the four o'clock news?

Reporter: A giant pumpkin at the county fair. Everyone's saying it weighs about a thousand pounds.

Editor: Nice. How would you say that on air?

Reporter: "The pumpkin is thought to weigh about a thousand pounds." Or "It is thought that…"

Editor: Good. Do we know who grew it?

Reporter: Yes, it was grown by Mrs. Olsen, a retired teacher. People say she fed it milk every day.

Editor: That's a rumor, so be careful. Say: "She is said to have fed it milk every day."

Reporter: "To have fed," because the feeding happened before. Got it. It'll be weighed on Saturday, and a new state record is expected.

Editor: Perfect. Anything else?

Reporter: Only that I'd love to have it made into pies.

Editor: Ha! Let's keep that out of the bulletin.

✏️ Underline every passive. Circle the one causative. Which sentence is a rumor, and which are facts?

3.2

Fewer Words, Same Meaning — Relative & Participle Clauses

Class C8
📖 🗣️ New words — tight, descriptive writing
located near…built in…surrounded by…made of…written in…having finished…not knowing…feeling (tired)…a drafta word limitconciseto cut / to trim

concise = saying everything that matters in as few words as possible · a dangling participle = a participle clause with the wrong subject (a common writing mistake)

Grammar box
DefiningNon-defining
Jobsays which oneadds extra information
Commas?noyes
that?OKnever
ExampleThe woman who lives next door is a doctor.My sister, who lives next door, is a doctor.

Reduce a defining clause. Ask: does the noun do the action, or receive it?

Does it → -ingThe man who is standing there · The train that leaves leaving at noon
Receives it → past participleThe letter which was written by John · Products that are made locally

Join two sentences with a participle clause. The subject must be the same in both parts.

TimeWalking home, I saw an accident.
ReasonFeeling tired, he went to bed. · Made of glass, it breaks easily.
NegativeNot knowing the way, we got lost.
Earlier actionHaving finished work, she went home.

❌ Walking home, the rain started. (The rain can't walk!) · ✅ Walking home, I got caught in the rain.

Don't reduce one finished past action (the man who called me yesterday), and keep non-defining clauses full in careful writing.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — editing the newsletter

Nora: Can you look at my notice for the building newsletter? The limit is fifty words and I've got eighty.

Ben: Sure. "The yard sale that is being organized by the tenants' committee will be held on Saturday." Cut it: "The yard sale organized by the tenants' committee…"

Nora: Nice. What about "People who want to sell things should bring their own table"?

Ben: "Anyone wanting to sell things should bring a table." Shorter, and it sounds more like a notice.

Nora: And here: "I finished the draft and then I sent it to Maria."

Ben: "Having finished the draft, I sent it to Maria." Just don't write "Having finished the draft, Maria got an email."

Nora: Ha! Then Maria finished it. OK, last one: "Mrs. Cruz, who lives in 3B, is bringing lemonade."

Ben: Keep that. It's extra information with commas, and it reads better in full.

Nora: Forty-eight words. Done!

✏️ Find two reduced clauses and one participle clause. Which clause did Ben decide not to reduce, and why?

3.3

She Refused, He Admitted — Reporting Verbs & Patterns

Class C9
📖 🗣️ New words — meetings and reporting
a meetingthe chairthe minutesthe agendaa decisionto proposeto object toin the endit was agreed that…

the minutes = the written record of a meeting: who was there, what each person said or did, and what was decided

Grammar box

A precise verb reports what the speaker was doing: offering, refusing, admitting… Learn each verb with its pattern.

① verb + toagree, offer, promise, refuse, threatenShe refused to sign.
② verb + person + toadvise, ask, warn, remind, encourage, persuade, inviteHe reminded us to lock the door.
③ verb + (that)admit, explain, claim, deny, insist, complainShe admitted (that) she had forgotten.
④ verb + -ingadmit, deny, suggest, recommend, mentionHe denied taking the key.
⑤ verb + preposition + -ingapologize for, accuse sb of, insist on, blame sb for, congratulate sb on, warn sb againstHe insisted on paying.

The meeting six

suggestShe suggested meeting on Friday. · She suggested (that) we meet on Friday.
insistHe insisted on paying. · She insisted (that) she had paid.
denyHe denied taking it. · He denied (that) he had taken it.
admitShe admitted forgetting. · She admitted (that) she had forgotten.
agreeHe agreed to help. · They agreed (that) it was a good idea.
refuseShe refused to sign. (no not: refuse is already negative)

❌ She suggested me to go. · ❌ He apologized to be late. · ❌ She reminded to call. · ❌ I am agree.

✅ She suggested going. · ✅ He apologized for being late. · ✅ She reminded him to call. · ✅ I agree.

deny = say something is not true (the past) · refuse = say no to doing something (the future)
