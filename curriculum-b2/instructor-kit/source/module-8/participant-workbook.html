8.1

Understanding Natural Speech — Connected Speech & Weak Forms

Class C23
📖 🗣️ Key terms — how fast English really sounds
  • weak form: "a cup of tea" → "a cuppa tea"
  • weak form: "fish and chips" → "fish 'n' chips"
  • linking: "grab a coffee" → "gra-ba-coffee"
  • elision: "sandwich" → "san(d)wich"
stress-timedcontent wordgrammar wordthe schwa /ə/weak formlinkingelisionassimilationreduction

Stress-timed = the important words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives) get the beat and stay clear; the small grammar words between them get squeezed. The information is all still there. It's just wearing a disguise.

Grammar box — the four disguises

1 · Weak forms. Small grammar words usually shrink to the schwa /ə/ ("uh").

to/tə/I want tə go.
of/əv/ or /ə/a cup ə tea · kind ə cold
and/ən/ or /n/fish 'n' chips
for/fər/It's fər you.
was · are/wəz/ · /ər/She wəz late. · They ər ready.
them/ðəm/ or 'emTell 'em.
can ≠ can't/kən/ short · /kænt/ long, stressedI kən go. ≠ I CAN'T go.

2 · Linking. A final consonant jumps onto the next vowel: turn‿it‿off → "tur-ni-toff." Between two vowels you hear a tiny /w/ or /y/: go (w) on · I (y) agree.

3 · Elision & assimilation. Sounds disappear (next day · sandwich · most people) or blend (don't you → "doncha" · did you → "didja").

4 · Reductions.

gonnagoing tod'youdo you
wannawant towhatchawhat are you / what do you
gotta(have) got tolemme · gimmelet me · give me
kinda · sortakind of · sort ofdunnodon't know

⚠️ These spellings only show the sound. When you write, always use the full form: going to, want to, kind of.

8.2

Vague Language & Being Approximate

Class C24
📖 🗣️ New words — three jobs for vague language
  • "I picked up a few things …
  • … milk, bread …
  • … that kind of thing."

≈ 1 · Approximate a number, amount or time:

aboutaroundroughly… or so-ishgive or takethirty-odda couple ofmore or less

🧺 2 · General nouns & placeholders stand in for a list or a word you can't find:

stuffthingsand that kind of thingand stuff like thatand so onor somethingand everythingthe thingy

🪶 3 · Hedges soften a statement or an opinion:

sort ofkind ofa bita littlein a wayI guessI supposeto be honest
Grammar box — where the words go
Before the numberabout · around · roughly · a couple of · a fewThere were about twenty people.
After the numberor so · give or take · -odd (after 20, 30, 100…)Give me ten minutes or so. · thirty-odd guests
Attached-ish (times, ages, colors, some adjectives)Let's meet at seven-ish.
End of a listand that kind of thing · and so on · or somethingpizza, snacks, that kind of thing
Before an adjectivesort of · kind of · a bit · a littleIt's a bit expensive.
Whole sentencein a way · I guess · I supposeIn a way, you're right.

stuff is uncountable: a lot of stuff ✅ · many stuffs ❌. more or less = almost ("It's more or less finished"), not "so-so."

😊 Be vaguechatting with friends · casual estimates · small talk · friendly messages · softening an opinion
🎯 Be precisemedicine and dosages · safety instructions · deadlines · money, contracts, legal and financial papers · reports to a manager
✏️ Exercise 1 — Insert the vague expression

1. There were fifty guests at the wedding. (approximately, before the number)

2. Let's meet at eight. (a suffix meaning "roughly")

3. We bought fruit, vegetables, and that kind of . (general noun)

4. The movie was boring, honestly. (soften the adjective; two words)

5. I'll only be a couple minutes.

6. We could order pizza or . (a vague alternative)

7. Thirty people turned up, maybe thirty-four. (after a round number)

8. It'll take an hour, give or . (complete the set phrase)

✏️ Exercise 2 — Soften the over-precise sentence

1. Blunt: "This is expensive." → "This is expensive." (two words)

2. Exact: "It lasted 32 minutes." → "It lasted about an hour."

3. Blunt: "I disagree." → "I see it differently." (two words, not kind of)

4. Exact: "There were 48 people." → "There were fifty, give or ."

5. Blunt: "You're wrong." → " that's true, but…" (three words meaning "partly")

6. Exact: "The work is 100% finished." → "The work is finished." (three words meaning "almost")

👥 Speaking — Register Switch and Describe It Naturally

🗣️ Register Switch: take an A/B card. Tell the same facts twice: first to a friend (relaxed and vague), then to the person on the card (a manager, doctor, landlord or teacher: clear and precise). Your partner plays both listeners.

8.3

Review Project — A Structured Debate

Class C25
📖 How a formal debate works

A motion is a statement to debate: "Phones should be switched off during family meals." The Proposition argues for it; the Opposition argues against it. Your side is drawn from a card.

RoleWhat you doTime
🪑 ChairOpens and closes, calls each speaker, takes floor questions, runs the vote. Stays neutral.whole debate
⏱ TimekeeperShows the cards: 1 MINUTE LEFT · 15 SECONDS · TIME. Counts the votes.whole debate
🟦 Proposer · 🟥 OpposerOpening speech: state your side and give 2–3 arguments, each with a reason and an example.2 min each
🔁 Rebuttal speakersAnswer the other team's points: concede what's fair, then counter. Add a new point if there's time.1½ min each
🙋 The floorAsk one short question to a named team.4 min in total
🏁 Summary speakersWeigh the debate. No new arguments. Opposition first, Proposition last.1 min each

The order: Chair opens → vote 1 → Proposer → Opposer → 1-min huddle → Proposition rebuttal → Opposition rebuttal → floor questions → Opposition summary → Proposition summary → vote 2 → Chair closes. The secret vote asks one thing: which team made the stronger case?

Language bank — debating moves
OpeningMadam / Mr. Chair, ladies and gentlemen… · We are here to argue that… · Our case rests on three points. · The strongest argument in favor is… · For these reasons, we urge you to support / oppose the motion.
AddingWhat's more… · Furthermore… · Not only that, but… · A further point worth making is…
Conceding & counteringAdmittedly… / It's true that… · That may be so, but… · While I accept that…, I still believe… · Even if that's the case,… · That said,…
RebuttalMy opponent claimed that… However,… · That argument ignores… · The other side would have you believe… But…
Floor questionMy question is for the proposition / opposition. · You said that… Could you explain…? · What would happen if…?
ConcludingWeighing it all up… · On balance, I'd argue that… · All things considered… · The evidence points to…

Concede before you counter. "No, you're wrong" is weak. "That's a fair point, and it's true that… However…" sounds reasonable and is far more persuasive. Attack ideas, never people.

The B2 toolkit — plant these on purpose
Conditionalspredict a resultIf this were introduced, costs would soar.
Impersonal passivesound objectiveIt is widely accepted that…
The causativedescribe arrangementsCompanies would have the work done abroad.
Advanced comparisonweigh optionsThis is by far the more effective approach.
Cleft & inversionadd emphasisWhat really matters is… · Not only does it cost more, but…
Indirect questionsraise an issue politelyWe might ask whether this is fair.
Hedging & vague languagesound natural, not stiffIt's sort of a trade-off, in a way.
