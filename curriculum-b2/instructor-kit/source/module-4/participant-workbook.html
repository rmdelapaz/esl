4.1

Linking Ideas — Contrast, Cause & Result

Class C10
📖 🗣️ New words — everyday trade-offs
  • working from home: no commute, but it can feel isolating
  • the daily commute: time lost, but a clear line between work and home
  • country life: a lower cost of living, but fewer jobs
  • social media: stay in touch, but easy to get distracted
a trade-offan advantage / a drawbackto weigh up both sidesto pivot (to the other side)a balanced conclusiona linker / a connectora clause (subject + verb)
Grammar box

The key question: what comes after the linker? A clause, a noun / -ing form, or a new sentence?

Contrast + clausealthough · though · even though · whereas · whileAlthough it was raining, we went out. · He likes tea, whereas she prefers coffee.
Contrast + noun / -ingdespite · in spite of (+ the fact that + clause)Despite the rain, we went out. · In spite of being tired, she finished.
Contrast: new sentenceHowever, · Nevertheless, · On the other hand,It was expensive. However, we bought it.
Cause + clausebecause · since · asWe stayed in because it was raining. · Since you're here, let's start.
Cause + nounbecause of · due to · owing to (more formal)The match was canceled due to bad weather.
Resultso + clause · so + adj / adv + that · such + (a / an) + noun + thatIt was so cold that the lake froze. · It was such a good movie that I watched it twice.
Result: new sentenceTherefore, · Consequently, · As a result,Sales fell. Therefore, the shop closed.
Purposeso as to · in order to + base verbShe left early in order to catch the train.

✏️ Punctuation: It was late. However, we stayed. · It was late; however, we stayed. · Although it was late, we stayed.

💬 Register: so, but, that's why in a chat · therefore, consequently, nevertheless, whereas in a talk or in writing.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Ana: You moved to the countryside, right? Do you miss the city?

Jon: A bit. It's much quieter here, whereas the city never sleeps.

Ana: But you're happy?

Jon: Very. Despite the longer drive to work, life feels calmer. And the rent is so cheap that we could afford a garden.

Ana: Nice. I couldn't do it, though. I'd get bored.

Jon: Fair enough. Since you love going out at night, you'd probably hate it. So you should stay where you are!

4.2

Adding Emphasis — Cleft Sentences & Inversion

Class C11
📖 🗣️ New phrases — putting the spotlight on it
It was … who …It was … that …What I really want is …What he did was …The thing that worries me is …The reason I called is …All I want is …Not only … but also …Never have I …No sooner … than …
to emphasizeto spotlightto correct a wrong ideato build up to a pointdramaticformaltheatrical (too much!)
Grammar box
It-cleftIt + be + spotlight + who / that + the restIt was Maria who booked the hotel (not me). · It was on Monday that I called.
What-cleftWhat + clause + be + key idea (last!)What I need is a vacation. · What annoys me is the noise.
An actionWhat + subject + did + was (to) + verbWhat he did was (to) ignore me.
Other framesThe thing (that)… is · The reason (why)… is · The place where… is · All (that)… isThe reason I called is to say thank you. · All I want is a quiet weekend.

Negative inversion: a negative word at the start + question word order (auxiliary + subject). It's formal and dramatic: use it like a spice.

NeverI have never seen such a mess. → Never have I seen such a mess.
Not only… but alsoNot only did she win, but she also broke the record.
No sooner… thanNo sooner had we left than it started to rain.
Hardly / Scarcely… whenHardly had I sat down when the phone rang.
Rarely / SeldomRarely does she complain. (no auxiliary? add do / does / did)
Little · Only thenLittle did they know what was coming. · Only then did I understand.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Kai: So you finally finished the marketing plan? Well done.

Lena: Actually, it was Priya who finished it. I only wrote the introduction.

Kai: Oh! Still, it looks great. What's the next step?

Lena: Honestly? What we really need is more time. Not only did the deadline move, but we also lost two people.

Kai: That's rough.

Lena: Never have I worked on a project with so many changes. But the reason I'm still optimistic is the team. Everyone's pulling together.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Rosa: The trouble with cashless shops is that they shut out people who don't have a bank card.

Sam: That's a fair point. Not everyone has one, I won't pretend otherwise. However, the evidence suggests that most customers prefer to pay by card, so shops save time and money.

Rosa: Granted, but we shouldn't forget older customers. My grandmother has always paid in cash.

Sam: I take your point; that's exactly why it matters how it's done. Surely you'd agree that the answer is to help people switch, not to ban cards altogether?

Rosa: Up to a point. What we can't ignore is that some people will always need cash.
