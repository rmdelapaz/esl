7.1

Register — Formal vs Informal English

Class C20
📖 🗣️ New words — talking about register
  • a text (to a friend): informal / casual
  • an email (to a manager): neutral
  • a formal letter (to a company): formal

register = the level of formality you choose for the reader and the situation · a set phrase = a fixed expression (I look forward to hearing from you) · a contraction = I'm, don't, won't · a sign-off = the closing word before your name (Sincerely,) · an inquiry = a formal question or request for information (UK: enquiry) · a complaint

I am writing to inquire about…I would be grateful if you could…I was wondering whether…Would it be possible to…?Unfortunately,…I look forward to hearing from you.Please do not hesitate to contact me.
Grammar box — the four features that shift
FeatureInformalFormal
1 Word choiceask for · get · find out · put off · need · tell · help · but · sorequest · obtain / receive · discover · postpone · require · inform · assist · however · therefore
2 ContractionsI'm · haven't · won'tI am · have not · will not
3 SentencesThe heater's broken. It's really cold. Can someone come?As the heating is no longer working and the apartment has become extremely cold, I would appreciate it if a technician could visit as soon as possible.
4 Set phrasesHi Sam, · Just wondering… · Can you…? · Let me know. · Thanks!Dear Mr. Clarke: · I am writing to inquire about… · I would be grateful if you could… · I look forward to hearing from you. · Sincerely,

Polite speech uses distance: Could you…? · Would you mind + -ing? · I was wondering whether I could… · Would it be possible to…? (Contractions are fine when you speak formally.)

The formal email① Greeting → ② Reason for writing → ③ Detail → ④ Request / action → ⑤ Closing line + sign-off
US sign-offSincerely, (or Best regards,) after any greeting. Dear Mr. Clarke: or Dear Mr. Clarke,
UK sign-offDear Mr Clarke, → Yours sincerely, · Dear Sir or Madam, → Yours faithfully,

⚠️ One register per message. Don't open with "Dear Sir or Madam" and close with "Cheers!"

7.2

Telling Stories & Anecdotes

Class C21
📖 🗣️ New words — making a story vivid

Extreme adjectives go with absolutely or really, not very:

  • very angry → absolutely furious
  • very excited → absolutely thrilled
  • very cold → absolutely freezing
  • very hot → absolutely boiling
  • very scared → absolutely terrified

an anecdote = a short, true-sounding personal story · a hook = the first line that makes people want to listen · the punchline = the funny or surprising last line · a mix-up = a confusing mistake · a coincidence · soaked = completely wet · mortified = extremely embarrassed

You'll never guess what happened…So the other day…This reminds me of the time…Just as…All of a sudden…Before I knew it…To cut a long story short…And that's why…
Grammar box — the shape of a story
Opena hookYou'll never guess what happened to me on the way to work.
Scenepast continuous (+ when + past simple)I was waiting for the bus when it started to pour.
Backstorypast perfectI'd just bought a new white jacket.
Buildconnectors that control the paceAt first… · Then… · A few minutes later… · Just as I… · All of a sudden… · Before I knew it…
Climaxhistoric present · direct speech · emphasisSo I'm standing there, absolutely soaked, and this man goes, "Lovely weather!"
Landan ending phrase, then stopIn the end… · To cut a long story short… · And that's why… · Lesson learned!

💬 In spoken stories, people often say he goes, "…" or she was like, "…" instead of said. Use them only in casual speech.

👂 Listener reactions: No way! · You're kidding! · Oh no! · Then what? · What did you do? · That's hilarious! · You must have been mortified!

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner. Perform it!

Priya: You'll never guess what happened to me on the way to the interview this morning.

Tom: Go on. What happened?

Priya: So I was running a bit late, and I was wearing my best suit. At first everything was fine. Then, just as I was crossing the street, a bus drove straight through a huge puddle.

Tom: Oh no!

Priya: All of a sudden I'm absolutely soaked, head to toe. And this old man just looks at me and goes, "Lovely weather for it!"

Tom: No way! What did you do?

Priya: Well, before I knew it, I was standing in reception, dripping on the floor and trying to smile. I've never been so embarrassed in my life.

Tom: You're kidding! So how did the interview go?

Priya: To cut a long story short, I got the job. And that's why I always carry an umbrella now. Lesson learned!

7.3

Making a Case — Presentations & Persuasion

Class C22
📖 🗣️ New words — making a case

a pitch = a short talk to persuade people to support an idea · a claim = what you want people to believe · evidence = facts, figures or examples that support a claim · a signpost = a phrase that tells listeners where you are in a talk · an objection = a reason someone disagrees · to concede = to admit the other side has a point · to counter = to answer back with a stronger point · a call to action = what you ask the audience to do · a proposal

Today I'll cover…First, let's look at…Moving on to…Take, for example,…The main advantage is…Research suggests…Admittedly,… That said,…To sum up,…That's a good question.
Grammar box — the moves of a persuasive talk
SignpostToday I'll cover… · To begin with… · Moving on to… / That brings me to… · What's more… · For instance… · To sum up… / In conclusion…
ArgueThe main advantage is… · There are two reasons for this. First,… Second,… · Research suggests / The figures show… · As a result,…
Rhetorical questionWouldn't we all like a safer walk home? · Isn't that worth it?
TriplingIt's cheaper, faster and greener. · It saves time, saves money and saves stress.
ContrastIt's not a cost; it's an investment. · We can carry on as we are, or we can do something better.
AppealThink about your own commute. · Together, we can…
Concede → counterAdmittedly, it costs more at first; however, it pays for itself within a year. · While it's true that there are risks, the benefits clearly outweigh them.
Handle a questionThat's a good question. · If I understand you correctly, you're asking… · Let me come back to that. · I don't have the exact figure, but I'll follow up. · Does that answer your question?

⚠️ One connector is enough: ❌ Although it's expensive, but… ✅ Although it's expensive, it…

✏️ Exercise 1 — Complete the presentation phrases.

1. Today I'll two main points. (introduce the talk)

2. To with, let's look at the problem. (first point)

3. That me to my second point. (move on)

4. , for example, the library in Oakdale. (give an example)

5. The figures that use has doubled. (give evidence)

6. To up, this is the best option we have. (conclude)

📖 ✏️ Exercise 2 — Name the technique.

Write: RQ (rhetorical question) · 3 (tripling) · C (contrast) · A (appeal to the listener) · CC (concede and counter)

1. Who wouldn't want a shorter wait at the clinic?

2. It's not a problem; it's an opportunity.

3. The garden will be greener, safer and friendlier.

4. Of course, no plan is perfect; nevertheless, this is the best option available.

5. Imagine walking to your car at night and feeling completely safe.

6. We can keep complaining about it, or we can fix it.

✏️ Exercise 3 — Concede and counter. Match the halves.

1. Admittedly, it will take some time to set up;   

2. While it's true that some staff prefer driving,   

3. There will probably be a few problems at first;   

4. Of course, some families may not use it.   

5. I understand that the budget is tight;   

a) However, the survey shows that 60% of families would. · b) however, this costs less than one month of repairs. · c) nevertheless, the other centers that tried it solved them within weeks. · d) that said, once it's running, it needs almost no work. · e) at least twelve people already cycle and have nowhere to park.

✏️ Exercise 4 — Handling questions. Circle the best response.

1. "How much will this cost per year, exactly?" (You don't know.)
   A) About a million, probably.   B) That's a good question. I don't have the exact figure, but I'll follow up this week.   C) Next question, please.

2. A long, confusing question.
   A) If I understand you correctly, you're asking who would manage it?   B) I don't understand you.   C) Yes.

3. "Isn't this just a waste of money?"
   A) No, you're wrong.   B) Admittedly, it isn't free. That said, it would save us money on repairs.   C) Maybe. I don't know.

4. You've just answered a question.
   A) OK, bye.   B) So, yeah.   C) Does that answer your question?

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — after the pitch

Leila: To sum up, then, a community garden would turn an empty lot into a place that's greener, safer and friendlier. Wouldn't we all rather look at vegetables than trash? Thank you. I'm happy to take questions.

Panelist: Who's actually going to take care of it in the summer, when people are away?

Leila: That's a good question. Admittedly, summer is the hardest time. That said, eighteen families have already signed up, and we'd set up a watering schedule, one week each.

Panelist: And what would it cost the neighborhood?

Leila: I don't have the final figure yet, but the first estimate is under two thousand dollars. I'll send the details to the board this week. Does that answer your question?

Panelist: It does, thank you. I hadn't thought about the schedule.

✏️ Underline Leila's tripling, her rhetorical question, and her concede–counter move.
