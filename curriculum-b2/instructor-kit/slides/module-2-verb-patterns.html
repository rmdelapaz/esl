2.1 · Two ways to follow a verb

-ing or to?

-ing (gerund)to + verb (infinitive)
enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, consider, keep, miss, can't stand
We're considering moving.		want, decide, hope, promise, agree, offer, refuse, manage, afford
We can't afford to wait.
after any preposition
He left without saying goodbye.		after an adjective
It's difficult to explain.
as the subject
Getting up early is hard.		for purpose (why?)
I went out to buy milk.
FEEL -ing = the activity as a thing · to = an arrow toward the future
2.1 · Traps · check the meaning

The little word to

I look forward to the weekend. (noun ✔)
→ I look forward to seeing you.
I went out to buy milk. · for buying
She encouraged him to apply.
Let me help. · They made him wait. (no to)
Can a noun follow to in "look forward to"? (Yes, so it's a preposition → -ing.)
"I want to the weekend"? (No, so "want to go.")
"This knife is for cutting bread." OK? (Yes: its function, not the purpose of an action.)
WATCH look forward to see ❌ · for buying (purpose) ❌ · made him to wait ❌ · I want that you… ❌
2.1 · Same verb, different meaning

⬅️ -ing looks back · to looks forward ➡️

verb⬅️ + -ing+ to ➡️
rememberI remember locking it. (memory)Remember to lock it. (duty)
forgetI'll never forget meeting her.Don't forget to call.
stopHe stopped smoking. (quit)He stopped to smoke. (paused)
tryTry adding salt. (experiment)I tried to open it. (attempt)
regret · go on · meanregret saying · went on talking · means travelingregret to tell you · went on to talk about… · meant to call
"He stopped to smoke." Does he smoke? (Yes!) · "I remember locking the door." Did I lock it? (Yes, a memory.) · "I tried to open the jar." Easy? (No, an attempt.)

2.2 · From present hunches to past verdicts

modal + have + past participle

About NOWAbout the PAST

She can't be serious.She can't have been serious.
They might know.They might have known.
It could be a mistake.It could have been a mistake.
SAY IT must've /ˈmʌstəv/ · might've · could've · should've  ·  WRITE IT have, never "of"
2.2 · The certainty scale · check the meaning

How sure are you, and which way?

~95% ✔must have + ppThe ground's wet. It must have rained.
~50%might / may / could have + ppShe might have missed the bus.
maybe notmight not / may not have + ppBen may not have gone. He was sick.
~95% ✘can't / couldn't have + ppHe can't have finished already.
"She mustn't have seen us"? (No! "She can't have seen us." mustn't = not allowed.) · "It must have rained." Did I see the rain? (No, I see the evidence.)
2.2 · Judging past choices

should have · could have

should have + ppright thing, didn't happenYou should have called me.
shouldn't have + ppwrong thing, did happenI shouldn't have eaten that.
could have ①a possibilityWhere's Sam? He could have gone home.
could have ②a missed chanceI could have gone, but I stayed home.
"You should have called me." Did you call? (No.) Good idea? (No, a mistake.) · "You could have warned me!" Did you warn me? (No.) Am I annoyed? (A little!)

2.3 · One word, three meanings

Past · now · changing

PAST (gone)I used to take the bus.I don't now.
NOW (a state)I'm used to the hills.They're normal for me.
CHANGING (a process)I'm getting used to the rain.It's getting easier.
"I used to take the bus." Do I take it now? (No.) · "I'm used to the hills." Hard for me now? (No, normal.) · "I'm getting used to the rain." Normal yet? (Not yet, still changing.)
2.3 · The key contrast

Base verb, or noun / -ing?

used to+ base verbpast onlyI used to swim every day.
be used to+ noun / -ingany tenseI'm used to swimming in cold water.
get used to+ noun / -ingany tenseI'm getting used to swimming at dawn.
"IT" TEST I'm used to it ✔ → preposition → -ing · I used to it ✘ → base verb
WATCH I'm used to work ❌ · I didn't used to ❌ · I use to jog (now) ❌ → I usually jog · US: I've gotten used to it
2.3 · Model · a new job

"Are you used to it yet?"

Grace: How's the new job at the warehouse?

Luis: I used to work in an office, so I used to sit all day. Now I'm on my feet for eight hours!

Grace: Are you used to it yet?

Luis: I'm getting used to it. But I'm still not used to getting up at four-thirty.

Grace: You'll get used to it. I used to stay up until one. Now I'm used to being in bed by nine!

SORT past · now · changing  ·  say it: used to = /ˈjuːstə/
