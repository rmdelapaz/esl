5.1 · Future perfect · will have + past participle

Finished by when?

by / by thenBy Friday, we'll have finished the roof.
by the time + presentBy the time you arrive, we'll have left.
in + periodIn two years, she'll have qualified. · In two hours, we'll be landing.
by ≠ untilI'll have finished by six. ✔ · I'll be working until six. ✔ · I'll have finished until six. ✘
WATCH by the time you will arrive ❌ · will finished ❌ · I'll have /ˈaɪləv/, not "I'll of"
5.1 · Model · find the reference points

"Where do you see yourself this time next year?"

Joe: So, Nina, where do you see yourself this time next year?

Nina: Well, I'll have finished my training by then, so I'll probably be working at the new clinic downtown.

Joe: Nice. And by the time you start, will you have moved into the city?

Nina: I hope so. By September we'll have signed the lease, and by winter we'll be settling into the new place.

Will Nina still be training next year? (No, finished.) · In winter, will the move be finished or in progress? (In progress: settling in.)

5.2 · have vs get · misfortune · word order

Same form, different stories

I had the report translated. / I got it translated.same meaning · get = more informal
It took weeks, but I finally got the heater fixed.get = finally managed
She had her bag stolen on the train.misfortune: she didn't arrange it!
He had stolen a wallet.past perfect: he was the thief
"She had her bag stolen." Did she arrange it? (No!) Did she steal it? (No.) · How do we know it's a misfortune? (Common sense: nobody pays a thief.) · Thing before or after "done"? (Before!)
5.2 · People patterns · model dialogue

get him to help · have him help

get + person + toI got my landlord to fix it. (persuaded)
have + person + verbI'll have the porter bring your bags. (arranged)
have + person + -ingShe had everyone laughing.
make + person + verb= force! Not for services.

A: Your car looks brand new! Did you clean it yourself?

B: No way. I had it detailed. And I finally got the dent fixed.

A: I really need to get mine serviced.

B: You should have it checked soon!

5.3 · all vs whole · every vs each

Small words, big precision

allall the books · all the information · all daythe wholethe whole book · the whole day · my whole life
everythe group together: Every room has a view.eachone by one: Each student gets a report.
"all the day"? (No: all day / the whole day.) · "a bag in every hand"? (No, two hands: each.) · "Every people needs water"? (No: every person / all people.) · Verb after every / each? (Singular.)
SAY IT whole = hole /hoʊl/ · each /itʃ/ ≠ itch /ɪtʃ/
5.3 · Only for two · both / neither / either

2 of 2 · 0 of 2 · 1 of 2

bothBoth restaurants are excellent.plural · both … and …
neitherNeither restaurant is cheap. · Neither of them is perfect.singular · neither … nor …
eitherEither day works for me.singular · either … or …

A: The apartment in the city or the house by the coast — which are you leaning toward?

B: Honestly, both places have their charms, but neither option is cheap.
