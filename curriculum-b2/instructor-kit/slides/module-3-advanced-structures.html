be + past participle, in any tense

Report a belief without saying who

ActivePeople say (that) he is very rich.
Pattern AIt is said that he is very rich.
Pattern BHe is said to be very rich.

3.1 · Pattern B · timing · check the meaning

Now, in progress, or earlier?

TimingInfinitive
same timeHe is thought to be rich.
in progressShe is thought to be living in Florida.
earlierHe is thought to have left the country.
earlier + passiveThe piano is believed to have been left by a musician.
"The tortoise is thought to be 120." Who thinks so? (We don't say.) Fact or belief? (Belief.)
"He is said to have been rich." Rich now? (No, before.)
"to have been left": did the piano leave? (No! Someone left it.)
3.1 · The causative · a first look (more in Lesson 5.2)

I didn't do it. I had it done.

had my car repaired
had a key cut
got my shoes repaired
had my passport renewed
have / get + object + past participle · "I had my hair cut." Did I cut it? (No, a hairdresser did.) · "I had cut my hair." (I did it myself: different meaning!)

Does it, or receives it?

Full clauseReducedWhy

3.2 · Participle clauses · time, reason, sequence

Two sentences → one

MeaningTwo sentencesOne sentence
timeI was walking home. I saw an accident.Walking home, I saw an accident.
reasonHe felt tired. He went to bed.Feeling tired, he went to bed.
reason (–)We didn't know the way. We got lost.Not knowing the way, we got lost.
sequenceShe had finished work. She went home.Having finished work, she went home.
SAY IT Feeling tired ↗ | he went to bed ↘ — pause at the comma
3.2 · The subject rule · check the meaning

Who is walking? 🌧️

"Walking home, the rain started."
Who is walking, grammatically? (The rain!)
Can rain walk? (No: a dangling participle.)
Fix it? ("Walking home, I got caught in the rain.")
"Reading the letter, tears filled her eyes."
Who is reading? (The tears!)
Fix 1: change the subject. ("…, she began to cry.")
Fix 2: a full clause. ("While she was reading the letter, tears…")
CHECK the first noun after the comma = the one doing the -ing

3.3 · Model · the conversation, then two reports

What was she doing with her words?

Dana: I'm really sorry. I forgot to send the report yesterday.

Marco: Why don't you set a reminder on your phone?

Dana: Good idea. I'll finish it tonight, I give you my word.

Vague: She said she was sorry. He said she could set a reminder. She said she'd finish it.
Precise: Dana apologized for forgetting the report. Marco suggested setting a reminder. Dana promised to finish it that night.
3.3 · Five patterns · learn the verb + pattern together

Which pattern follows?

① + toagree, offer, promise, refuseShe refused to sign.
② + person + toadvise, warn, remind, persuadeHe reminded us to lock it.
③ + (that)admit, explain, claim, insistShe admitted (that) she had forgotten.
④ + -ingdeny, admit, suggest, recommendHe denied taking the key.
⑤ + prep + -ingapologize for, accuse sb of, insist onHe insisted on paying.
3.3 · The meeting six · check the meaning

suggest · insist · deny · admit · agree · refuse

suggested going / (that) we go
insisted on paying / (that) she had paid
denied taking it · admitted forgetting
agreed to help · refused to sign
"I didn't take the key." Past or future? (Past → denied taking.)
"I won't give you the key." (Future → refused to give.)
"She was agree"? (No! She agreed.)
NEVER suggested me to go ❌ · recommended me to ❌ · apologized to be late ❌
3.3 · Model · a meeting becomes minutes

The laundry room meeting

Park: Why don't we make a schedule, so each floor has its own day?

Diaz: I didn't take down the sign! It fell off.

Tom: Actually, I left my clothes in the dryer all weekend. Sorry.

Diaz: I won't buy a new machine until the owner approves it.

Minutes: Ms. Park suggested making a schedule. Mr. Diaz denied taking down the sign. Tom admitted leaving his clothes in the dryer. Mr. Diaz refused to buy a new machine until the owner approved it.
