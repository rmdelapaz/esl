4.1 · Everyday trade-offs

Every choice has two sides

the daily commute
country life
social media
😐 Remote work is convenient. It can feel lonely. Companies save money. Some people get less done.

4.1 · Contrast · what comes next?

Clause, noun, or new sentence?

+ CLAUSEAlthough it was raining, we went out. · He likes tea, whereas she prefers coffee.
+ NOUN / -INGDespite the rain, we went out. · In spite of being tired, she finished.
THE BRIDGEDespite the fact that it was raining, we went out.
NEW SENTENCEIt was expensive. However, we bought it. · Nevertheless, · On the other hand,
WATCH Despite it was raining ✘ · Although the rain ✘ · However it was late, we stayed ✘
4.1 · Check the meaning

Concept check

"Although it was raining, we went out."
Was it raining? (Yes.) Did we go out? (Yes.) Surprising? (A little.)
"Despite ___, we went out." Can I say "it was raining"? (No: a noun or -ing.)
What can I say? (the rain · being wet · the fact that it was raining)
"It was late. However, we stayed." How many sentences? (Two.) What comes after However? (A comma.)
4.1 · Cause & result

Why? · So what happened?

CAUSE + CLAUSEWe stayed in because it was raining. · Since / As you're here, let's start.
CAUSE + NOUNWe stayed in because of the rain. · The flight was delayed due to fog.
SO + ADJ + THATIt was so cold that the lake froze.
SUCH + NOUN + THATIt was such a cold day that we stayed in.
NEW SENTENCESales fell. Therefore, the store closed. · Consequently, · As a result,
PURPOSEShe left early in order to / so as to catch the train.
QUICK TEST a noun follows? → such · only an adjective or adverb? → so

4.1 · Your turn · groups of 3

The Argument Builder

  • Round 1: A argues FOR, B argues AGAINST (1 min each). Play a linker card when you use it correctly.
  • C listens, checks the grammar, gives a verdict.
  • Round 2: a balanced 2-minute talk: hook → side 1 → pivot → side 2 → conclusion
Your real opinion? (No, your side's cards.)
When do you play a card? (When the grammar after it is right.)
What does C do? (Checks, tallies, gives a verdict.)
Where's the contrast linker? (The pivot.)
4.2 · It-clefts · the spotlight

It + be + SPOTLIGHT + who / that…

PLAINJohn broke the window yesterday.
THE PERSONIt was John who broke the window yesterday. (…not Sam)
THE THINGIt was the window that John broke yesterday. (…not the door)
THE TIMEIt was yesterday that John broke the window. (…not last week)
"So you booked the hotel?"
"Actually, it was MAria who booked it, not me."
4.2 · What-clefts & frames · save the best for last

What I need is… a vacation

WHAT-CLEFTI need a vacation. → What I need is a vacation.
AN ACTIONHe ignored me. → What he did was (to) ignore me.
THE THINGThe thing that worries me is the cost.
THE REASONThe reason I called is to say thank you.
ALLAll I want is a quiet weekend. (nothing more)
SAY IT What I need ↗ | is a vaCAtion ↘
4.2 · Negative inversion · the dramatic one

Never have I seen such a mess.

NEVERNever have I felt so welcome.
NOT ONLYNot only did she win, but she also broke the record.
NO SOONER… THANNo sooner had we left than it started to rain.
HARDLY… WHENHardly had I sat down when the phone rang.
RARELY · LITTLE · ONLY THENRarely does she complain. · Little did they know. · Only then did I understand.
RULE question word order · no auxiliary? add do / does / did · formal: use it like a spice
4.2 · Model dialogue

Listen for the spotlight

Kai: So you finally finished the marketing plan? Well done.
Lena: Actually, it was Priya who finished it. I only wrote the introduction.
Kai: Oh! Still, it looks great. What's the next step?
Lena: Honestly? What we really need is more time. Not only did the deadline move, but we also lost two people.
Kai: That's rough.
Lena: Never have I worked on a project with so many changes. But the reason I'm still optimistic is the team.
4.2 · Your turn · pairs → trios

Make It Emphatic

  • Set the Record Straight: A has the facts wrong. B corrects: "Actually, it was… who…"
  • Plain → Dramatic: draw a card, say it two ways
  • Emphatic opinion (60–90 sec): a frame, a correction, a build-up, one optional inversion to close
Who has the true facts? (B.)
How many versions per card? (Two.)
How many inversions must you use? (Zero or one.)
Listeners hear a cleft: they…? (Raise a hand, silently.)
4.3 · Signposting · give them a map

The talk map

① OPENI'd like to talk about… · I'd like to argue that…
② PREVIEWI'll cover three points: …, … and …
③ POINTSLet me start by… · Firstly, · Moving on to… · Turning now to… · Finally,
EACH POINTclaim + reason + example / evidence (The evidence suggests… · One key advantage is…)
④ CLOSETo sum up, … · So, to bring it all together…
SAY IT Moving on to | the cost. (pause after every signpost)
4.3 · Discussion moves

Concede, then counter

🤝 CONCEDE → COUNTER
That's a fair point, but…
I take your point; however,…
You're right to an extent, though…
Granted, but we shouldn't forget that…
🎯 PERSUADE · 🌫️ HEDGE
The fact is,… · The bottom line is…
What we can't ignore is…
arguably · to some extent · up to a point
It seems to me that…
TONE push the idea, never the person · save "The fact is…" for your best point
4.3 · Task 1 · groups of 4

Present: 2 minutes + questions

  • Notes, not a script. Open → preview → 2–3 points → close
  • Use Modules 1–4: a conditional, a passive, a linker, a cleft…
  • Listeners: one question or challenge each
  • Answer: concede, then counter
How long? (Up to two minutes.)
Read your notes? (Only a quick look.)
A challenge? ("That's a fair point, but…")
Must you present to the group? (No: the teacher only is OK.)

Devil's advocate? (Argues the losing side.)
