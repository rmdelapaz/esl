C · Game — Pattern Path (2.1)

Rules (read them to the class, or give one copy per group)
  1. Groups of 3–4. One board, one coin (heads = move 1, tails = move 2) or a die, one counter each.
  2. Land on a square and do the task. Complete: say the whole sentence. Meaning: explain the difference between the two sentences. Error Hunt: find the mistake and say the correct sentence. Talk: speak for 20 seconds with the pattern.
  3. The referee is the player on your right. They check the referee sheet and say "Correct, stay!" or "Not quite, go back." Then the sheet moves one person to the left.
  4. First to FINISH wins. If you finish early, start again: Talk squares need a new example the second time.

D · "Two Minutes About Me" — Interview Cards (2.1)

One A card and one B card per pair. The speaker talks for two minutes from their workbook notes. The listener ticks the patterns they hear, then asks two questions from their card (in full, with the right pattern). Swap. "Pass" is always OK.

Card A · listener

Ask two of these after your partner talks

  1. What do you enjoy doing on weekends?
  2. Is there anything you've stopped doing recently? Why?
  3. What are you hoping to do this year?
  4. What's something you're really good at?
  5. What do you always forget to do?
  6. What are you looking forward to?

I heard: ☐ 3 × -ing verbs · ☐ 3 × to verbs · ☐ preposition + -ing · ☐ purpose (to…) · ☐ remember / stop / try

Card B · listener

Ask two of these after your partner talks

  1. What can't you stand doing?
  2. What do you avoid doing if you can?
  3. What would you like to learn to do?
  4. What do you keep meaning to do but never do?
  5. Do you remember learning to swim, ride a bike or drive?
  6. What are you saving up to do or buy?

I heard: ☐ 3 × -ing verbs · ☐ 3 × to verbs · ☐ preposition + -ing · ☐ purpose (to…) · ☐ remember / stop / try

E · The Mystery of Room 12 (2.2)

Groups of 4: each learner gets one card and reads it aloud without showing it (groups of 3: one learner takes C and D). Everyone takes notes. Then the group makes at least six deductions and agrees on a verdict. The answer is Script 2.2-B (set H): read it only after every group has given its verdict. All clues are text only, on purpose: learners must listen to each other.

Setting · read aloud to the class

Saturday, 9:15 a.m. — Maple Street Community Center, Room 12

You arrive early for your 9:30 English class. The door of Room 12 is open, but nobody is there. The heater is on, and something strange has happened. You and your classmates have fifteen minutes before class starts. What happened here? Who was here, and where did they go?

Clue card A

What's on the table

A1 The coffee pot is half full, and it's still warm.

A2 There are fifteen paper cups on the table. Only four have been used.

A3 A banner on the wall says "HAPPY 70th BIRTHDAY, ROSA!" Only one end is taped up. The other end is hanging down.

Clue card B

The cake and the weather

B1 In the trash, there's a receipt from Lee's Bakery: "1 large chocolate cake · paid · Saturday 7:50 a.m."

B2 There's a cake knife and a stack of paper plates on the table. They're all clean. There's no cake anywhere in the room.

B3 A wet umbrella is standing by the door. It rained hard this morning, but it stopped at about 8:00.

Clue card C

Things people left behind

C1 The sign-in sheet by the door says: "Room 12 · Saturday Walking Club · 7:30 a.m. · Maria, Joe, Ahmed, Lin." Rosa's name isn't on it.

C2 A phone charger is still plugged into the wall. There's no phone.

C3 A set of car keys with a gym tag is on the floor under the table.

Clue card D

Notes and time

D1 A note on the notice board, from Friday: "Walking Club: I'll come in early tomorrow, around 9:30, to put away the new chairs. Rosa (Club Leader)"

D2 On a napkin, in messy handwriting: "PLAN B!! Riverside Park, picnic tables, 10:30. Tell everyone!"

D3 It's 9:15 now. Riverside Park is a five-minute walk from the center.

Detective notes (1 per learner, if they don't have the workbook with them)

ClueDeduction: must have / can't have / might have / should have + ppHow sure?
the coffee is still warmThey can't have left long ago.95% ✘

Our verdict (most sure → least sure): We're almost sure that… · They can't have… · They might have… · In hindsight, they should have…

Roles: 🕵️ Skeptic: challenge every must have ("Are you sure? It might have been…"). ✍️ Secretary: write the verdict. 🗣️ Speaker: gives the verdict to the class. The fourth person is the timekeeper.

F · "Adjusting to Something New" — Change Cards & Role-plays (2.3)

Put the change cards face up on each table. Learners choose one, or use a change from their own life (real or invented), or their new identity card. Every card is about an everyday change, on purpose: don't add "moving to a new country" and don't ask about it. Each card has the same three prompts plus a piece of advice.

Change · 1

💼 A new job

From an office to a warehouse, a store, a hospital, a kitchen… or the other way around.

Role-play cards (early finishers · 3 minutes, then swap)

Role-play 1 · A

Two weeks on the early shift

You started working at a bakery at 5 a.m. two weeks ago. You used to start at nine. You're tired and not used to going to bed early. Tell your friend how it's going.

Role-play 1 · B

An early-shift expert

You've worked early shifts for years. You're used to it now, but you remember how hard it was. Ask questions and give advice: You'll get used to… · It took me… to get used to…

Role-play 2 · A

The new scheduling app

Your company started using a scheduling app. You used to get your schedule on paper. You're getting used to it, but you keep missing messages. Ask your coworker for help.

Role-play 2 · B

The app is easy (now)

You learned the app last month. At first you couldn't get used to it; now you're used to checking it every morning. Give tips: Try turning on… · Remember to…

Role-play 3 · A

The new coach

You just started volunteering as a soccer coach for kids. You didn't use to talk in front of groups. You're still getting used to all the noise! Tell a friend about it.

Role-play 3 · B

Last year's coach

You coached the team last year. Ask how it's going. Share what you got used to and one thing you never got used to. Give advice.

Role-play 4 · A

Across town

Role-play 4 · B

The friendly neighbor

You've lived here for ten years. You're used to everything! Welcome your new neighbor, ask what they're getting used to, and recommend places: You'll soon get used to…

H · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural speed, with contractions (I've, I'm, must've, could've), because that is what learners hear outside class. Read each script twice: once for the main idea, once to check. For voicemails, hold a phone to your ear. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

2.1-A · Kai's new routine (Workbook 2.1, Ex 1)

Hi, I'm Kai. This year I decided to change my routine. First, I've stopped staying up late watching videos. Now I try to be in bed by eleven. I've started running in the morning, to clear my head before work. At first I hated getting up at six, but honestly, I don't mind it now. I'm thinking about joining a running club, and I'm really looking forward to meeting new people. Running shoes are expensive, and I can't afford to spend much, so I avoid buying expensive gear. My goal? I'd love to run a 10K by the fall. I just need to remember to charge my headphones!

Answers: 1 to change · 2 staying · 3 to be · 4 to clear · 5 getting (or to get) · 6 joining · 7 meeting · 8 buying · 9 to charge. Follow-up: "Find one more pattern." (I've started running · I can't afford to spend · I'd love to run)

2.1-B · Meaning-change dictation (read each sentence 3 times; learners write it and mark ⬅️ looks back or ➡️ looks forward)

1. I remember locking the door. · 2. Remember to lock the door. · 3. He stopped to talk to his neighbor. · 4. He stopped talking to his neighbor. · 5. Try restarting your phone. · 6. I tried to call you three times.

Answers: 1 ⬅️ memory · 2 ➡️ duty · 3 ➡️ paused in order to talk · 4 ⬅️ quit (they don't talk now: why? A good discussion question) · 5 ⬅️ experiment · 6 ➡️ attempt (it didn't work).

2.2-A · Three short puzzles (Workbook 2.2, Ex 5 · pause after each)

Puzzle 1. Last night, Ana came home at eight. The kitchen light was on, and there was a pot of soup on the stove. It was still warm. On the fridge there was a note: "Back soon!" Her roommate's shoes were gone, and so was her big shopping bag. · Puzzle 2. On Monday morning, Ben's desk at work was completely tidy. His computer was off. There was a sign on his chair: "Out of the office until the 20th." And on his plant there was a note: "Please water me on Wednesday. Thanks!" · Puzzle 3. It's 6:05, and your English class starts at six. But the classroom is empty. On the board someone has written: "Room change: Room 8." The door of Room 8 is closed, and you can hear people laughing inside.

Model answers: 1 Her roommate must have made the soup. She can't have left long ago (the soup is warm). She might have gone shopping (the bag). · 2 Ben must have gone on vacation. He can't have left in a hurry (tidy desk, a note for the plant). · 3 The class must have moved to Room 8. The teacher might have written the note. They can't have gone home (the laughing).

2.2-B · Maria's voicemail: the solution to the Mystery of Room 12 (read after every group gives its verdict)

Hi, this is Maria from the Saturday Walking Club. I'm so sorry about the mess in Room 12! We came in at seven-thirty to get ready for a surprise party. It's Rosa's seventieth birthday today! Lin went to pick up the cake at Lee's Bakery, and it was pouring rain, so she borrowed Joe's umbrella. We made coffee, and Ahmed started putting up the banner. Then Joe saw Rosa's note on the board. She was coming in early to put away the chairs! If she'd walked in, the surprise would've been ruined. So we changed to Plan B, grabbed the cake and ran to Riverside Park. We left in such a hurry that Joe forgot his umbrella, and I think I left my phone charger in the wall. Oh, and if anyone finds a set of car keys with a gym tag, they're Ahmed's. He must have dropped them. We should have booked the park in the first place! Anyway, if Rosa comes in, please don't tell her anything. Just say the Walking Club is meeting at the park at ten-thirty. Thanks!

Score: 1 point each for the surprise party · Rosa's note · Plan B at the park · they left in a hurry · the dropped keys. Listen for Maria's own deduction (He must have dropped them), her regret (We should have booked the park) and a third conditional from C2 (If she'd walked in, the surprise would've been ruined).

2.3-A · Dana's new shift (Workbook 2.3, Ex 4)

Hi, I'm Dana. I work in a hospital lab. Three months ago, I changed from the day shift to the night shift. I used to start work at seven in the morning and finish at three. Now I start at eleven at night! The first two weeks were really hard. I wasn't used to sleeping during the day. My street is so noisy in the afternoon! So I bought some earplugs and dark curtains, and slowly I got used to it. Now I'm used to having "breakfast" at six in the evening, which my kids think is hilarious. The thing I still can't get used to is driving home when everyone else is driving to work. It feels so strange! My advice? Give it a month. You'll get used to it. Really.

Answers: 1 start · 2 sleeping · 3 got used to · 4 is ('s) used to · 5 get used to · 6 get used to.

2.3-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times; learners write it and label it past / now / changing)

1. I used to work in an office. · 2. I'm used to getting up early. · 3. She's getting used to the new app. · 4. Did you use to live near here? · 5. It took me a month to get used to it.

Answers: 1 past · 2 now · 3 changing · 4 past (spelling: use, no d) · 5 changing (finished process). Listen for the /s/ in used to /ˈjuːstə/.
