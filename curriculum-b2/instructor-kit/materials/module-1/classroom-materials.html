B · Welcome Question Cards (C1 mingle)

One set per 12 learners (print two sets for a bigger class). Each learner takes one card of each color. They ask one question and one follow-up per partner, answer the partner's question in 3–4 sentences, swap cards, and find a new partner. The small line is the follow-up. STORY cards test narrative tenses, IF cards test conditionals, OPINION cards test discussion skills. Every card works with a new identity. None asks about the home country, age, family, migration or health, on purpose. On OPINION cards, anyone can play devil's advocate.

STORY
Tell me about a time you took the wrong bus or train, or got a little lost.
+ What were you doing when you realized?
STORY
What's the funniest thing that has happened to you at work or in a class?
+ What had happened just before that?
STORY
Tell me about a meal you remember well.
+ Who had cooked it? What were you celebrating?
STORY
Tell me about a time a phone or computer stopped working at the worst moment.
+ What were you trying to do?
IF
If you had a completely free day tomorrow, what would you do?
+ Who would you spend it with?
IF
What will you do this weekend if the weather is bad?
+ And if it's sunny?
IF
If you could learn any skill instantly, what would it be?
+ How would it change your week?
IF
If you could change one thing about your street or your workplace, what would you change?
+ Who would it help?
OPINION
Is it better to cook at home or to eat out? Why?
+ Do you agree with your partner? Say why.
OPINION
Are phones making us better or worse at talking to each other?
+ Can you give an example?
OPINION
Should everyone learn to drive?
+ What's the best argument on the other side?
OPINION
Are morning people or night people better at their jobs?
+ Which are you, and why?

B · B1 Diagnostic Grid (teacher only · C1)

Listen during the welcome mingle and the report. Mark ✓ easy and accurate · ~ understands, some errors · ✗ not yet. Write one real example in Notes. Keep this page private; compare it with Progress Test 1 in C13.

NameNARRATIVE TENSES
was doing · had done · irregular past		CONDITIONALS
If it rains, I'll… · If I had…, I'd… · I wish I…		DISCUSSION
reason + example · agrees / disagrees politely · asks back		RANGE & FLUENCY
long turns · varied words · self-corrects		Notes (an example I heard)

Reading the grid: mostly ✓ / ~ = ready for B2 (many ~ under NARRATIVE is normal: C1 teaches it). ✗ in the first two columns + short turns = check placement privately (toolkit oral interview). Many ✓ + strong range = give the Stretch tasks; a C1 candidate later.

C · New Identity Cards (1.1 · 1.2 · 1.3)

Any learner may use a card instead of their own life, in any task: no reason needed. In 1.1 they tell the small story (and can add layers). In 1.2 they talk about the small turning point. In 1.3 they use the wishes & preferences. All the stories are everyday and cheerful on purpose.

New identity · 1

Rosa — hospital cleaner (night shift)

A small story: Last week I'd been working for ten hours when the lights went out in the whole building. A doctor and I finished the floor using our phone lights!

A small turning point: If my friend hadn't shown me the job ad, I wouldn't work at the hospital now.

Wishes & preferences: I wish the bus came more often at night. I'd rather work days than nights. It's high time they fixed the break-room fridge.

New identity · 2

Daniel — bus driver

A small story: I'd been driving for an hour when a passenger's dog jumped onto the front seat. It had been sitting there quietly the whole time!

A small turning point: If I hadn't failed my first driving test, I would never have practiced so much, and I might not be a driver now.

Wishes & preferences: I wish drivers wouldn't park at the bus stop. I wish I'd brought a jacket today. I'd rather passengers said good morning.

New identity · 3

Amina — student and mother

A small story: I was baking cookies when my daughter came home with fifteen friends. She'd invited the whole soccer team!

A small turning point: If I hadn't joined the library book club, I might never have started studying again.

Wishes & preferences: I wish the library opened earlier. It's about time I learned to drive. I'd rather my kids didn't play video games at dinner.

New identity · 4

Chen — cook in a restaurant

A small story: By the time the customers arrived, I'd been cooking for six hours, and then the oven stopped working.

A small turning point: If my boss hadn't been sick that day, I would never have cooked for a famous chef.

Wishes & preferences: I wish I hadn't bought such expensive knives. I'd rather work in a small kitchen than a big one. It's high time the restaurant got a new oven.

New identity · 5

Lucía — grandmother

A small story: I'd been gardening all morning when I found an old ring in the tomatoes. My husband had lost it twenty years before!

A small turning point: If my grandson hadn't shown me video calls, I wouldn't talk to my friends every week now.

Wishes & preferences: I wish my phone had bigger buttons. I'd rather you called than texted. It's time I tried that salsa class.

New identity · 6

Yusuf — forklift driver

A small story: While I was eating lunch, the warehouse cat stole my sandwich. It had been watching me for days!

A small turning point: If I hadn't played soccer with my coworkers that first Friday, I wouldn't be the team captain now.

Wishes & preferences: I wish the warehouse had a better coffee machine. I wish I'd stretched before the game. I'd rather we played on Saturdays.

E · Game — Chain of Consequences (1.2)

Rules (read them to the class, or give one copy per group)
  1. Groups of 4. Put one START card face up and one ENDING card face down. Any ending works with any start.
  2. Player 1 turns the start into a third conditional: "If Ana's alarm had gone off, she wouldn't have missed the bus." Write it on a slip and put it down.
  3. Player 2 starts with the result of the last slip: "If she hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't have walked through the park." Keep going clockwise. No links out of turn.
  4. After 4 links, turn over the ENDING card. Reach it in 3–4 more links.
  5. The last link is a mixed conditional about now: "…she wouldn't be running a food truck today!"
  6. Points: 1 per correct link · +1 for a link with could have or might have · +2 for a correct mixed-conditional ending. Everyone checks each slip.

G · Wish List Meeting — Scenario Cards (1.3)

One scenario per group of 4. All four are about shared places and decisions, never about anyone's private life. The group agrees on the top 3 changes; the note-taker reports.

Scenario A · 🏢

Maple Street apartments

  • The elevator breaks down almost every week.
  • There are 2 washing machines for 40 apartments.
  • Some people play loud music after midnight.
  • Last year the owner moved the trash cans to the back of the building, far from the door.
  • The front-door lock is old and sometimes doesn't close.
Model: We wish the elevator worked. It's high time the owner fixed it. We wish they hadn't moved the trash cans. We'd rather people didn't play music after midnight.
Scenario B · 💼

Brightside office

  • The printer jams every single day.
  • The kitchen is always dirty.
  • Meetings start late and go on too long.
  • Last month the company stopped the free coffee.
  • The air conditioning is either freezing or off.
Model: I wish the printer would work for one whole day! We wish they hadn't stopped the free coffee. It's about time meetings started on time.
Scenario C · 🌳

Riverside Park & the bus stop

  • Two streetlights on the path are broken.
  • The bus stop has one bench and no roof.
  • Last spring the schedule changed: now the last bus leaves at 9 p.m.
  • Dogs run around without a leash.
  • The park bathrooms have been closed for months.
Model: We wish the last bus left later. We wish they hadn't changed the schedule. It's high time the city put a roof on the bus stop. We'd rather dog owners used a leash.
Scenario D · 📚

Our English classroom

  • The room is too hot or too cold.
  • The whiteboard markers never work.
  • There's no coffee or tea at the break.
  • Homework: too much? too little? just right?
  • Last term, the class moved to this room on the third floor.
Model: I wish the markers worked! It's time we had a coffee break. I'd rather the teacher gave us homework on Monday than on Thursday. (Complain about the room, not about people.)

Role cards (1 set per group of 4)

Role · Chair 🪑

You run the meeting

Open: "OK, let's start. Our aim is to agree on three changes." Make sure everyone speaks: "What do you think, ___?" Close: "So we agree on…"

Your language: I wish… · It's high time… · Shall we vote?

Role · Note-taker 📝

You write and report

Write down every idea in short notes. At the end, write the top 3 and report them to the class: "We wish… It's high time… We'd rather they…"

Your language: So, to sum up,… · Our top three are…

Role · Devil's advocate 😈

You argue the other side

Argue against at least two ideas, politely. It's a role: it doesn't have to be your real opinion.

Your language: I see your point, but… · I'd rather they didn't spend money on that. · Is it really that urgent?

Role · Timekeeper ⏱️

You watch the clock

The meeting is 9 minutes. Say "Five minutes left!" and "Two minutes: time to decide!" Give your ideas too.

Your language: It's time we decided. · I'd rather we moved on.

Talking tokens (3 per learner · cut out, or use coins)

Put one token in the middle each time you speak. No tokens left? Listen until everyone has used theirs, then everyone takes them back.

H · Find Someone Who — "Everyday Wishes" (1.3 · game)

One copy per learner. Mingle: turn each line into a question ("Do you wish you could cook better?"). When someone says yes, write their name and ask the extra question. You can only write each person's name twice. Everyone can answer as their new identity card, or say "Pass." Short version (C3): 6 minutes, first to 6 names. I · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at natural B2 speed the first time, with contractions (I'd, she'd, would've); slow down a little the second time, but keep the contractions. The online lessons have 🔊 audio for their own model sentences; these scripts are for class.

1.1-A · The wrong train (Workbook 1.1, Ex 1 · read twice)

Last Saturday I was going to my friend Leo's birthday lunch on the other side of the city. I'd been looking forward to it all week. I was sitting on the train, reading my book, when I suddenly noticed that the train was going the wrong way. I'd gotten on the express train by mistake! By the time I got off, I'd traveled twenty minutes in the wrong direction. I'd been reading so carefully that I hadn't heard any of the announcements. When I called Leo, they'd already started eating. But when I finally arrived, an hour late, they'd saved me the biggest piece of cake.

Gist: the speaker took the express train by mistake (reading, didn't hear the announcements). Answers: 1 was going · 2 had been looking · 3 was sitting · 4 had gotten · 5 had traveled · 6 had been reading · 7 hadn't heard · 8 had already started. Follow-up: "What had they saved for her / him?" (The biggest piece of cake.)

1.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. By the time we got to the station, the last train had already left. · 2. She was reading quietly when the lights suddenly went out. · 3. They'd been driving for hours before they found a place to stop. · 4. I recognized him at once because we'd met before.

1.2-A · The picnic that wasn't (Workbook 1.2, Ex 1 · read twice)

Last Saturday my friend Rita and I planned a picnic by the lake. We didn't check the weather forecast, and it rained all afternoon. We didn't bring an umbrella either, so we got completely soaked. We ran into a little café to dry off, and there, at the next table, was Leo, an old friend from my first English class. We hadn't seen him for five years! Now the three of us have lunch together every month. So, honestly? I'm glad it rained.

Answers: 1 had checked … wouldn't have planned · 2 had brought … wouldn't have gotten · 3 hadn't gone … wouldn't have met · 4 hadn't met … wouldn't be (mixed: the result is now). Follow-up: "What's the silver lining?" (They met Leo again.)

1.2-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times, with natural contractions)

1. If I'd known, I would've called you. · 2. She wouldn't have missed the bus if she'd left earlier. · 3. If we hadn't gotten lost, we wouldn't be here now. · 4. You could have told me!

After sentence 1, ask: "How did you spell would've?" Anyone who wrote "would of" gets the explanation once, kindly.

1.3-A · The office kitchen (Workbook 1.3, Ex 1 · two voices, or ask a confident learner to read Joe)

Ana: Ugh, there's nothing to eat. I wish I'd brought my own lunch today. · Joe: And look at the microwave. I wish people would clean the microwave after they use it. · Ana: And the fridge is full again. I wish we had a bigger fridge. · Joe: Is there any coffee? … No. Oh, that was me. If only I hadn't finished the coffee yesterday! · Ana: Well, this machine is ten years old. It's high time the company got a new coffee machine. · Joe: Honestly, I'd rather go to the café across the street than drink this. · Ana: Me too. But I'd rather we asked the manager together. She listens when two people ask. · Joe: Good idea. It's time we went back to our desks, though. The meeting starts in five minutes.

Answers: 1 had brought (P) · 2 would clean (N) · 3 had (N) · 4 hadn't finished (P) · 5 got (N) · 6 'd rather (N) · 7 we asked (N). Extra: "What does Joe say about the meeting?" (It's time we went back to our desks.)

1.3-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. I wish I hadn't eaten so much cake. · 2. I'd rather you didn't call after ten. · 3. It's high time they fixed the elevator. · 4. I wish it would stop raining.

Ask after each: "Now or past?" (1 past regret · 2 now / future · 3 now, overdue · 4 now, annoyed.)
