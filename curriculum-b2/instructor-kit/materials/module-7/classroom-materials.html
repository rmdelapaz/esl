C · Register Switch Workshop — Situation Cards (7.1)

One card per group of 3. A writes the text to a friend, B the email to a manager, C the formal letter or email to an organization. Every card gives the facts, so nobody invents personal details. The small gray line is a model B (the middle of the dial) for you; fold it back or cut it off before class.

Situation · 1 · work

The Saturday shift

You can't work your shift on Saturday, May 10 (a family commitment). Your coworker Maria has agreed to cover it.

A your friend Leo at work · B your manager, Mr. Patel · C the staffing agency that placed you (Dear Staffing Team)

B: Hi Mr. Patel, I'm afraid I won't be able to work on Saturday, May 10, due to a family commitment. Maria has kindly agreed to cover my shift. Could you please update the schedule? Thank you, Ana
Situation · 2 · work

The broken printer

The office printer has been broken for a week. Staff are printing at the library. The printer is under a service contract (no. 7731).

A a coworker friend · B the office manager, Ms. Klein · C the printer company's service department

B: Hi Ms. Klein, I wanted to let you know that the printer on the second floor has been out of order since last Monday. Would it be possible to contact the service company? It's covered by our contract (no. 7731). Many thanks, Daniel
Situation · 3 · work

The first-aid course

Your employer mentioned a first-aid course. It's two evenings, next month, and costs $90. You'd like to take it and hope the company will pay.

A a friend who took the course · B your supervisor, Mr. Reyes · C City Training Center (an inquiry about dates and places)

B: Dear Mr. Reyes, I'm very interested in the first-aid course you mentioned at the team meeting. I was wondering whether the company would be able to cover the $90 fee. I'd be happy to share what I learn with the team. Best regards, Mei
Situation · 4 · home

The dented washing machine

Your new washing machine (order 48213, delivered March 3) arrived with a large dent on the side. You'd like a replacement.

A your friend · B the store's customer service manager, Ms. Ortiz · C a formal complaint to the manufacturer (Dear Customer Relations Team)

B: Dear Ms. Ortiz, I'm writing about order 48213, which was delivered on March 3. Unfortunately, the machine has a large dent on the side. I'd be grateful if you could arrange a replacement. I've attached two photos. Sincerely, Samir Haddad
Situation · 5 · work

E · Anecdote Prompt Cards (7.2)

Spread the cards face-up on a side table. Each has three starter facts; the learner adds the scene, the connectors, one line of direct speech and an ending. A learner can also tell a true story or a new identity story seed. Nobody asks "Is it true?" All the topics are light on purpose; keep it that way.

Prompt · 1

🚌 The wrong bus

You were reading on your phone on the number 12 bus. ▸ You looked up: fields, no buildings. ▸ The driver announced: "Last stop: the airport!"

Prompt · 2

🍳 A cooking disaster

You were cooking for important guests. ▸ The smoke alarm went off. ▸ You ordered pizza and pretended you'd made it.

Prompt · 3

🌍 The small world coincidence

You were on vacation in another city. ▸ You sat down in a café. ▸ The person at the next table was your neighbor from home.

Prompt · 4

🔑 Lost and found

You lost your keys (or wallet) on a busy day. ▸ You searched everywhere. ▸ They were somewhere very strange.

Prompt · 5

Prompt · 6

🤝 A kind stranger

Something went wrong: a flat tire, a missing ticket, heavy rain. ▸ A stranger helped. ▸ You met them again later.

Prompt · 7

A pet (yours, a friend's or a neighbor's) did something unexpected. ▸ At the worst possible moment. ▸ Everyone laughed except you.

Prompt · 8

Prompt · 9

F · Pitch Topic Cards (7.3)

One card per team of 3. Teams argue for their card, not their personal views. Each card has the problem, the solution, the cost and two facts for evidence. Teams may add one invented example ("Take, for example, our coworker…"). The panel is a community board (community cards) or a management team (workplace cards).

Pitch · 1 · workplace

🚲 Bike parking at work

Problem: 12 staff cycle to work; nowhere safe to lock a bike; 2 bikes stolen last month. Solution: a covered rack for 20 bikes by the side entrance. Cost: about $1,500. Facts: the company spends about $800 a month on parking permits · research suggests regular cyclists take fewer sick days.

Pitch · 2 · workplace

🗣️ A staff language-buddy program

Problem: new staff speak 9 different languages; some feel isolated and make avoidable mistakes. Solution: pair each new worker with a volunteer "buddy" for 6 weeks; 30 minutes a week, paid time. Cost: about 3 staff hours a week. Facts: 1 in 4 new staff leave in the first 3 months · a similar program at a sister site cut that in half.

Pitch · 3 · community

🔧 A community tool library

Problem: most people use a drill for about 15 minutes in its whole life, but every family buys one. Solution: a tool-lending shelf at the community center; borrow with a library card. Cost: $600 for shelves; tools donated. Facts: 40 neighbors offered tools in a survey · the center is open 6 days a week.

Pitch · 4 · workplace

💡 Better lighting in the parking lot

Problem: half the lights in the staff parking lot are broken; evening staff feel unsafe; one person tripped last winter. Solution: 10 new solar LED lights. Cost: $4,000, no electricity bills. Facts: 60% of staff work evening shifts · the insurance company offers a discount for better lighting.

Pitch · 5 · workplace

🔄 A shift-swap board

Problem: swapping shifts means texting everyone and asking the manager again and again; mistakes happen. Solution: a shared online board (a free app) where staff post and accept swaps; the manager approves with one click. Cost: free; 1 hour of training. Facts: the manager spends about 4 hours a week on swaps · 3 no-shows last month were swap mix-ups.

Pitch · 6 · community

📚 An after-school homework club

Problem: many children are home alone after school; parents work until 5 or 6. Solution: a free homework club at the public library, Monday to Thursday, 3:30–5:30, run by volunteers. Cost: $1,200 a year for snacks and supplies. Facts: 25 volunteers (retired teachers and college students) have signed up · the library's study room is empty in the afternoon.

Pitch · 7 · community

🥕 A community garden

Problem: the empty lot on Elm Street is full of trash and weeds. Solution: 20 raised garden beds for neighbors, with a watering schedule. Cost: under $2,000. Facts: 18 families have already signed up · a garden in the next neighborhood reduced litter complaints by half.

Pitch · 8 · community

💧 Water refill stations

Problem: the community center sells about 300 plastic water bottles a week; the old fountain doesn't work. Solution: 2 water refill stations with filters. Cost: $2,500 to install. Facts: the bottles cost users about $450 a week in total · the stations show a counter of "bottles saved."

H · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

7.1-A · Two voicemails (hold a phone to your ear; read the first fast and warm, the second slower and careful)

Voicemail 1 (to Sam, a coworker friend): Hey Sam, it's me! Listen, I can't make it Saturday: family stuff, ugh. Any chance you can cover my morning? I'll take your Sunday, promise. You're the best! Lemme know. Bye!
Voicemail 2 (to Mr. Patel, the manager): Hello, Mr. Patel, this is Ana Silva from the evening team. I'm calling about this Saturday, May tenth. I'm afraid I won't be able to work my shift because of a family commitment. I was wondering whether it would be possible to swap with Sam, who has kindly offered to cover it. I'll also send you an email with the details. Thank you for your understanding. Please let me know if that works for you. Goodbye.

Ask: "Same message? (Yes.) Three differences?" Answers: greeting (Hey Sam, it's me / Hello, Mr. Patel, this is Ana Silva from the evening team) · I can't make it / I'm afraid I won't be able to · Any chance you can cover? / I was wondering whether it would be possible to swap · You're the best! / Thank you for your understanding · Lemme know / Please let me know if that works for you · speed, reductions and slang vs full forms and a careful voice. Note: she still uses contractions in the formal voicemail (I'm, I'll): normal in speech.

7.1-B · Dictation: formal phrases (read each sentence 3 times)

1. I am writing to inquire about the availability of your evening courses. · 2. I would be grateful if you could send me further details. · 3. Please do not hesitate to contact me if you require any further information. · 4. I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience.

Check spelling: inquire (UK enquire OK), availability, grateful (not "greatful"), further, hesitate, require, convenience. Follow-up: "Turn sentence 2 down the dial for a coworker." (Could you send me more details?)

7.2-A · The same story, flat and shaped (read the first in a flat monotone; the second with energy, pauses and voices)

Flat: Last Saturday I went to a bakery. It was far. I walked there. It was closed. And then I went home. And then I ate a sandwich.
Shaped: You'll never guess what happened to me on Saturday. So I'd been dreaming about this famous bakery for weeks: the one with the cinnamon rolls everyone posts online. I was walking across town, absolutely starving, and at first everything was perfect. Sunny, no crowds. Then, just as I got to the door, the owner looks at me, flips the sign to "Closed" right in my face, and goes, "Sorry, we're closed!" At eleven in the morning! To cut a long story short, I walked all the way home and ate a cheese sandwich. And that's why I always call first now. Lesson learned!

Ask: "Same events? Which one would you listen to? What did the second speaker add?" Answers: a hook · backstory (I'd been dreaming) · scene (I was walking) · connectors (at first, then, just as) · historic present + direct speech (looks, flips, goes, "Sorry, we're closed!") · emphasis (absolutely starving, right in my face) · an ending with a point.

7.2-B · Be the listener (stop at each ⏸ and raise a hand; learners call out a reaction)

Did I ever tell you about my first day at the warehouse? ⏸1 · So I was trying really hard to look professional, and my new boss asked me to bring her the blue folder from the office upstairs. I ran up the stairs, grabbed a blue folder, ran back down… and dropped it. Papers everywhere, all over the loading dock. ⏸2 · And just as I was picking them up, a truck driver jumps out and starts helping me, and he goes, "Don't worry, I dropped the whole truck on my first day." ⏸3 · So I finally give the folder to my boss, and she opens it, and looks at me, and says, "This is the lunch menu." ⏸4 · To cut a long story short, it was the wrong folder, the truck driver is now one of my best friends, and that's why I always read the label twice. ⏸5

Any natural reaction: ⏸1 "No, go on!" / "What happened?" · ⏸2 "Oh no!" · ⏸3 "No way!" / "That's so nice!" · ⏸4 "You're kidding!" / "What did you do?" · ⏸5 "That's hilarious!" / "What a first day!" Then: "Find the hook, the scene, the historic present and the ending."

7.3-A · A model pitch: bike parking at work (about 90 seconds; pause at every signpost; learners tick Persuasion Bingo)

Good morning, everyone. Today I'd like to make the case for one small change: safe bike parking at work. I'll cover two things: the problem, and our solution. First, the problem. Twelve of us already cycle to work, and there is nowhere safe to lock a bike. Last month, two bikes were stolen from the fence. Moving on to our solution: a covered rack for twenty bikes next to the side entrance. The main advantage is health. Research suggests that people who cycle regularly take fewer sick days. For instance, since our coworker Leo started cycling, he hasn't missed a single day. And wouldn't it be great to arrive at work already awake? It's healthier, cheaper and greener. Admittedly, the rack isn't free: it costs about fifteen hundred dollars. That said, the company spends about eight hundred dollars a month on parking permits, so if even a few people switch, it pays for itself. Think about your own commute: the traffic, the parking, the stress. A safe place to park isn't a luxury; it's basic. So, to sum up: safer bikes, healthier staff, lower costs. We're asking you to approve the rack this month. Thank you.

Signposts: Today I'd like to… · I'll cover… · First,… · Moving on to… · For instance,… · To sum up… · Argue: The main advantage is… · Research suggests… · Techniques: rhetorical question (wouldn't it be great…?) · tripling (healthier, cheaper and greener; also safer bikes, healthier staff, lower costs) · contrast (isn't a luxury; it's basic) · appeal (Think about your own commute) · concede–counter (Admittedly… That said…) · call to action (We're asking you to…). Every version A square is heard; version B: all except Take, for example and That's a good question (heard in 7.3-B).

7.3-B · The Q&A (two voices, or ask a confident learner to read the Panelist)

Panelist: Thank you. But honestly, isn't this just a nice extra for a few people who like cycling? · Presenter: That's a good question. If I understand you correctly, you're asking whether enough people would benefit. Admittedly, twelve people isn't the whole staff. That said, when the office in Riverside built a rack, the number of cyclists doubled in six months. Take, for example, their warehouse team: eight of them cycle now. · Panelist: And who's going to maintain it? · Presenter: I'm not sure yet. Let me come back to that; I'll check with facilities and follow up by Friday. Does that answer your question? · Panelist: It does. Thank you.

Ask learners to list the handling phrases: That's a good question · If I understand you correctly, you're asking… · Let me come back to that · I'll follow up · Does that answer your question? Point out: the presenter admits not knowing and still sounds in control. That's the goal.
