D · Prefix/Suffix Race (6.1)

Rules (read them to the class, or give one copy per team)
  1. Teams of 3–4. Spread your base cards and affix cards face up on the desk.
  2. Round 1 · Flip it (3 min): the teacher says a base. Hold up the right negative prefix and say the word. First team = 1 point.
  3. Round 2 · Build it (4 min): build as many real words as you can: base + up to two affix cards (un + help + ful). Spelling changes are OK (rely + able = reliable). 1 point per real word. Then challenge other teams: an invented word = −1 point. The referee's dictionary decides.
  4. Round 3 · Fit it (4 min): the teacher reads a sentence with a gap and a base in CAPITALS. Write the form that fits. 2 points for the right form; 1 for the right job with a spelling slip.

E · Game — Comeback Story Scenarios & Bingo (6.1)

One set of scenario cards per group. A learner can tell a scenario, a light real story, or their identity card's comeback story. The teller uses six or more forms from the word families; listeners mark each form they hear, used correctly, on their bingo grid. Three in a row = "Bingo!" (quietly, after the story).

Comeback · 1

The night-market stall

Two friends open a noodle stall. First month: almost no customers. What went wrong? (menu, prices, place?) What did they decide? How did it end?

Try: failure · disappointed · decision · decisively · improvement · successful

Comeback · 2

The community garden

Neighbors plant a garden. Heavy spring rain floods everything. What did they do differently the next year? What do they grow now?

Try: disappointing · creative · creatively · strengthen · success · careful

Comeback · 3

The losing soccer team

A work team loses every game for a season. A new coach arrives. What changed? How did the next season go?

Try: unsuccessful · competition · competitive · strength · improve · succeed

Comeback · 4

The bake sale

A school bake sale: the oven breaks and the cakes burn. The parents have two hours. What did they do instead? How much money did they raise?

Try: disaster · decide · creativity · helpful · successfully · unbelievable

Comeback · 5

The neighborhood app

A small team builds an app to share tools with neighbors. Nobody downloads it. Why? What did they rebuild or rewrite? Who uses it now?

Try: rewrite · useless · useful · reliable · unreliable · improvement

Comeback · 6

H · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural speed, with contractions and weak forms (as /əz/, than /ðən/, Could you /ˈkʊdʒə/), because that is what learners hear outside class. Read each script twice: once for the main idea, once to check. For phone calls, hold a phone to your ear. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

1. PHOtograph · phoTOgraphy · photoGRAPHic · phoTOgrapher. · 2. eCOnomy · ecoNOmic · eCOnomist. · 3. creATE · creAtion · creAtive · creaTIvity. · 4. EDucate · eduCAtion. · 5. comPETE · compeTItion · comPEtitive. · 6. deCIDE · deCIsion · deCIsive.

6.2-B · The highway or the coast road? (Workbook 6.2 dialogue · two voices, or ask a confident learner to read B)

A: So, which route do you recommend: the highway or the coast road? · B: The highway is far quicker, but honestly, it's nowhere near as pretty. The coast road is twice as long, though. · A: Hmm. The earlier we leave, the less traffic we'll hit, right? · B: Exactly. And the coast road gets more and more beautiful the farther south you go. It's slightly slower, but I think it's worth it. · A: And gas? Doesn't the long way cost a lot more? · B: Only a little more. It's not quite as cheap, but it's the same car, the same trip, and a much better view. · A: OK, you've convinced me. Let's take the coast road. The earlier we set off, the better!

First listening: they choose the coast road because it's far prettier and only slightly slower. Notice the rise–fall on "The earlier we leave ↗ | the less traffic we'll hit ↘."

6.3-B · Rise or fall? (learners show a hand going up ↗ or down ↘, then say what the speaker means)

1. Wasn't that a great movie? ↘ · 2. Didn't you get my message? ↗ · 3. Isn't the library closed on Mondays? ↗ · 4. Don't you think we should leave earlier? ↘ · 5. Isn't it a beautiful view? ↘ · 6. Haven't you finished yet? ↗

Answers: 1 ↘ agree with me · 2 ↗ surprise · 3 ↗ checking a belief · 4 ↘ persuading / suggesting · 5 ↘ admiring · 6 ↗ surprise. Then answer each by the facts: "Didn't you get my message?" (I did.) → "Yes, I did."

6.3-C · The office mystery (Workbook 6.3, Ex 5 · read twice; pairs then ask as many questions as they can)

On Friday it was Ms. Tran's birthday. Her team bought a big chocolate cake and put it in the office fridge. At nine o'clock, Leo opened the fridge and saw the cake. At ten, someone left the fridge door open. At eleven, Ms. Tran's manager, Mr. Obi, called a meeting, and everyone went to the meeting room, except Leo. Then, at twelve, something strange happened. Ms. Tran opened the fridge, and half the cake was gone! Next to the fridge she found a fork and a note: "Sorry! It was too good." Ms. Tran called her manager. Nobody knows who wrote the note… but Leo had chocolate on his shirt.

Model questions: Who bought the cake? (subject) · What did the team buy? (object) · Who opened the fridge at nine? (subject) · Who left the door open? (subject: nobody knows!) · Who called a meeting? (subject) · What happened at twelve? (subject) · What did Ms. Tran find? (object) · Who did Ms. Tran call? (object) · Who wrote the note? (subject) · Who had chocolate on his shirt? (subject).
