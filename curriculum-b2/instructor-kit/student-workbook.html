🗣️ Sequencing words to make the order clear:

🗣️ Storytelling phrases:

You'll never guess what happened…All of a sudden,…To make things worse,…Luckily,…It turned out that…In the end,…
Grammar box

Four past tenses, four jobs in a story:

Past simplethe main events, in orderShe opened the door.
Past continuousbackground in progress · was / were + -ingThe rain was falling outside.
Past perfectan earlier event · had + past participleSomeone had broken the window.
Past perfect continuoushow long an activity had been going on · had been + -ingThey'd been arguing for hours.
had donea finished action, or a numberBy noon she'd written three emails.
had been doingthe activity and its duration, or the cause of a resultHer eyes were tired. She'd been reading all morning.
State verbsknow, be, have (own), want, like, believe stay simpleI'd known him for years. ❌ I'd been knowing him

while / as + a long background action (past continuous) · when + the short action that interrupts it (past simple): While I was cooking, the phone rang.

Use the past perfect when the order matters. With before and after, the past simple is often enough: After I finished, I left. ✅

The Third Conditional — What Could Have Happened

Class C2
📖 🗣️ New words — imagining a different past

a turning point = a moment after which things change · a chain reaction = one event causes the next, and the next… · by chance = without a plan · in hindsight = looking back, with what I know now · a silver lining = something good in a bad situation · It's a good thing (that)… = I'm glad that…

🗣️ Phrase bank:

If I had known,…I would have…You could have…!We might have…If only I'd…If I hadn't…, I wouldn't be… nowThings would have been different if…I never would have…
Grammar box

The third conditional imagines the opposite of what really happened. It can't change now.

RealityI didn't leave early. I missed the train.
Third conditionalIf I had left early, I would have caught the train.
FormIf + had + past participle, … would have + past participle
Other orderI would have caught the train if I'd left early. (no comma)
would havecertainIf we'd left earlier, we would have caught it.
could haveit was possibleIf we'd left earlier, we could have caught it.
might havemaybe, not sureIf we'd left earlier, we might have caught it.

Can it still change? Yes → second conditional: If I had money, I'd travel. · No → third: If I'd had money, I'd have traveled.

Mixed: past → nowIf + had + pp, … would + base verbIf I'd taken that job, I would be rich now.
Mixed: always → pastIf + past simple / were, … would have + ppIf I were more careful, I wouldn't have lost my keys.

We say would've /ˈwʊdəv/, and it sounds like "would of." But we always write would have or would've. ❌ would of · ❌ If I would have known

Wishes, Regrets & Preferences — Wish, If Only, Would Rather, It's Time

Class C3
📖 🗣️ New words — wishes and complaints

overdue = it should have happened already · to put something off = to delay it · to get around to (doing) something = to finally find time for it · to be fed up with = to be tired of and annoyed by something · a habit = something you do often, without thinking · a complaint / a suggestion

🗣️ Phrase bank:

I wish I had…I wish I'd…If only…!I wish you wouldn't…I'd rather… than…I'd rather not.I'd rather you didn't…Would you rather…?It's time we…It's high time…It's about time…It's time to…
Grammar box

One trick: a past form can mean unreal or not yet.

Present wishwish / if only + past simple (or could, were)I wish I had more time. (I don't.)
Past regretwish / if only + past perfectI wish I had set an alarm. (I didn't.)
Annoyancewish + someone / something else + wouldI wish the printer would work!

❌ I wish I would exercise more. → ✅ I wish I exercised more. / I wish I could find the time.

I'd rather + base verbmy preferenceI'd rather walk than wait for the bus.
I'd rather + someone + past simplewhat I want someone else to do (now / later)I'd rather you didn't call after ten.
It's time + subject + past simpleit should happen nowIt's time we left.
It's high / about time + past simpleit's overdue (a little annoyed)It's high time they fixed the elevator.
It's time to + base verbno person namedIt's time to go.

hope = possible: I hope you pass! · wish = unreal: I wish I'd studied more.

Gerunds & Infinitives — Choosing the Right Form

Class C4
📖 🗣️ New words — habits, goals and feelings about activities
  • I enjoy cooking.
  • We decided to go hiking.
  • I'm learning to play the guitar.
  • I've stopped swimming.

consider = think about doing · can't afford = don't have enough money or time · manage = succeed in doing something difficult · refuse = say no to doing · can't stand = hate · look forward to = feel happy about something in the future · be committed to = be seriously dedicated to

🗣️ Useful phrases for talking about yourself:

I really enjoy…ingI've stopped…ingI keep…ingI can't stand…ingI'm hoping to…I'm saving up to…I'm good at…ingI'm looking forward to…ing
Grammar box
verb + -ingenjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, imagine, consider, keep, practice, miss, can't standI don't mind waiting.
verb + towant, decide, hope, promise, agree, offer, refuse, manage, learn, seem, affordWe can't afford to wait.
preposition + -inggood at, interested in, tired of, instead of, before, after, without, look forward toHe left without saying goodbye.
-ing as subjectthe activity is the subjectLearning a language takes time.
adjective + tohappy, difficult, easy, ready, surprisedIt's difficult to explain.
purposeto + verb (not for + -ing)I went out to buy milk.
verb + person + towant, ask, tell, advise, allow, encourageShe encouraged him to apply.
no "to"after modals and let / make + personLet me help. · They made him wait.

Is to a preposition? Try a noun after it. "I'm looking forward to the weekend" works, so use -ing: "looking forward to seeing you."

Same verb, different meaning: -ing looks back (a memory, the activity itself). to looks forward (a duty, a purpose, an attempt).

+ -ing+ to
rememberI remember locking the door. (a memory)Remember to lock the door. (a duty)
forgetI'll never forget meeting her. (a memory)Don't forget to call me. (a duty)
stopHe stopped smoking. (he quit)He stopped to smoke. (he paused in order to)
tryTry adding salt. (an experiment)I tried to open it. (an attempt, it was hard)
regretI regret saying that. (sorry about the past)We regret to tell you… (bad news now)
go onShe went on talking. (continued)She went on to talk about money. (next topic)
meanThis job means traveling. (involves)I meant to call you. (intended)

Little or no change: like, love, hate, prefer, begin, start, continue. "I like swimming" ≈ "I like to swim."

The passive in any tense: change only be. The past participle never changes.

Present / past perfectIt has been done. · It had been done.
FutureIt will be done. · It is going to be done.

Same timeto be / to doHe is thought to be rich.

Earlierto have + past participleHe is thought to have left the country.

📖 How a formal debate works

A motion is a statement to debate: "Phones should be switched off during family meals." The Proposition argues for it; the Opposition argues against it. Your side is drawn from a card.

RoleWhat you doTime
🪑 ChairOpens and closes, calls each speaker, takes floor questions, runs the vote. Stays neutral.whole debate
⏱ TimekeeperShows the cards: 1 MINUTE LEFT · 15 SECONDS · TIME. Counts the votes.whole debate
🟦 Proposer · 🟥 OpposerOpening speech: state your side and give 2–3 arguments, each with a reason and an example.2 min each
🔁 Rebuttal speakersAnswer the other team's points: concede what's fair, then counter. Add a new point if there's time.1½ min each

The order: Chair opens → vote 1 → Proposer → Opposer → 1-min huddle → Proposition rebuttal → Opposition rebuttal → floor questions → Opposition summary → Proposition summary → vote 2 → Chair closes. The secret vote asks one thing: which team made the stronger case?

✏️ Exercise 3 — Correct the mistake

1. It should have done earlier. → It should have earlier.

2. He is thought to steal the money last year. → He is thought to the money last year.

3. I look forward to hear from you. → I look forward to from you.

4. If I would have known, I'd have told you. → If I known, I'd have told you.

5. The meeting lasted a bit exactly two hours. → The meeting lasted two hours.

6. This is more cheaper than the other. → This is than the other.

Motion: "Remote work should be the default for office jobs." Match each concession (1–5) with the best counter (a–e).

1. Admittedly, working from home saves time on the commute.

2. It's true that some people feel lonely at home.

3. While I accept that offices are expensive to run,

4. That may be so for experienced staff,

5. Even if meetings are easier in person,

a) …but new employees learn far more when they sit next to their team.
b) …I still believe a shared space builds trust that no video call can replace.
c) …most of them can be replaced by a short call or a clear email.
d) That said, many people use that time to work longer hours, not to rest.
e) However, a regular team day each week would solve that without forcing everyone back.
