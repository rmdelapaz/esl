1.1 · Lead-in · same request, three impressions

"Send me the report."

A Could you send that over when you get a sec?
B I would be grateful if you could forward the document at your earliest convenience.
C Could you send me the document, please?
Most formal → least? (B → C → A)
To a client? (B) · your manager? (C) · a close colleague? (A or C)
How formal = register. What attitude = tone.

1.1 · Tone · polite and clear

Soften it once, not five times

Too bluntCourteous
Send the figures now.Could you send the figures when you get a moment?
You're wrong.I see it a little differently. Could we look at it again?
I want a meeting.Would it be possible to arrange a quick meeting?
No.I'm afraid that won't be possible, but here's what I can do…
  • 1 modal question: Could you…?
  • 2 hedge: just · a little · when you get a chance
  • 3 positive framing: I can have it ready by Monday.
"Could you possibly maybe send it sometime if that's OK?" Polite? (Too polite.) How does it sound? (Unsure.)
1.1 · Greetings & introductions · the first thirty seconds

Names, a warm line, a question back

Rafael Good morning. Rafael Mendes, from Northwind Logistics. It's a pleasure to meet you.
Karen Likewise. Please, call me Karen.
Rafael Thanks, Karen. And please call me Rafael. Thanks for making the time.
Karen Can I introduce you to my colleague? This is Dev, who leads our warehouse team.
"Nice to meet you." → Nice to meet you too. / Likewise.
"How do you do?" → How do you do? (not "I'm fine")
First names? Start with Ms. Grant; switch when invited.
"Mr. Rafael"? (No: Mr. Mendes, or Rafael.)

  • 1 subject line
  • 2 greeting

  • 4 body: one ask + deadline

  • 6 sign-off (matches 2)

Three unrelated asks in one email? (The reader answers one. Split it, or number them.) Where does the ask go? (Near the top, with a deadline.)
1.2 · The frame · greeting and sign-off match

Dear Sir … Cheers? Never.

RegisterGreetingSign-off
FormalDear Mr. / Ms. + family name, · Dear Sir or Madam,Sincerely, (US) · Yours sincerely, (UK, named) · Yours faithfully, (UK, Sir or Madam)
NeutralDear Sara, · Hi Sara,Kind regards, · Best regards,
Friendly-proHi Sara, · Hello Sara,Best, · Thanks,
"Dear Sir or Madam": know the name? (No.) UK sign-off? (Yours faithfully.) US? (Sincerely.) · "Dear Sara," too stiff? (No: neutral and warm.) · "Dear Mr. Daniel"? (No: Mr. Okoro.)

"I'll put you through." Who speaks next? (A new person.) "Can I take a message?" Who writes? (The receiver.) "I am Ana" on the phone? (No: This is Ana / Ana speaking.)
