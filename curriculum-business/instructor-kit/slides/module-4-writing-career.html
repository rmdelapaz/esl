4.1 · Model · written last, read first, stands alone

The executive summary

P This report examines the causes of late deliveries in the Northern Region in the first quarter and recommends a response.
F Late deliveries rose from 6% to 14%. It was found that routes are still planned by hand and one van was out of service for five weeks. Notably, 70% of late orders were on Monday and Tuesday routes.
R We recommend three actions: (1) route-planning software by the end of May; (2) a leased van for early-week routes; (3) a review of driver schedules in Q3. Together, these steps are expected to bring late deliveries below 6% by July.
Written first? (No: last.) · "This report will discuss…"? (No: state the results.) · Can the director decide from this alone? (She must be able to.)
4.1 · Register · objective, evidence-based, clear

Email style → document style

4.1 · Recommendations that get approved · S-A-P-C-T

Could someone act on it on Monday?

  • Specific
  • Actionable: who, what, by when
  • Prioritized
  • Cost/benefit-aware
  • Tied to the findings
Vague ✘Approvable ✔
We should improve customer support.We recommend hiring two agents by the end of Q3 to bring waits below five minutes.
Marketing needs more budget.We propose moving 20% of the social budget to email, which returned $6 per $1 (Appendix B).
SAY IT We reCOMmend | HIRing two Agents | by the END of Q3 · fourTEEN ≠ FORty · perCENT, never "percents"
4.1 · Role-play · pairs · brief cards A/B

The decision-maker

  • Write (10 min): your executive summary, 80–120 words
  • Read (60 sec only): your partner's summary
  • Ask two questions from your card
  • Decide: approve · approve with changes · not yet + one reason
  • Swap
I'm happy to approve this on one condition… · Could you clarify…? · I'd need to see… before I can approve it. · Let's start with phase one.

ICQs: How many words? (80–120.) What comes first? (The purpose.) How long do you read? (60 seconds.) Show your budget? (No.)

who + strengths + what you offer · no "hard-working team player"

4.2 · Behavioral questions · "Tell me about a time…"

STAR

S · SituationLast spring, a client brought their launch forward by two weeks.
T · TaskAs the coordinator, I had to deliver forty displays in half the time.
A · Action (I!)So I split the job into daily targets, asked the printer to run two shifts, and checked progress every morning.
R · ResultAs a result, the displays arrived a day early, and the client has since signed a two-year contract.
Longest part? (Action.) I or we? (I.) No Result: finished? (No.) · Tell me about yourself = present → past → future, under a minute ↘

Private details? (No: one sentence is enough.) Apologize? (No.) Hide the dates? (Never.) · Real history, a role card, or a mix: always your choice.

Mock interview

  • Observer: time + checklist · 1 strength + 1 next step, quoted

4.3 · Cross-cultural · tendencies, not stereotypes

What varies between workplaces

Directnesssay it plainly ↔ imply it politely
Formalityfirst names early ↔ titles and seniority
Relationship vs taskagenda first ↔ trust first
Timefixed ↔ flexible
Disagreementopen, in the room ↔ quiet signals
Decisionsone leader, fast ↔ consensus, slower
Contextlow: say it and write it ↔ high: tone and relationship
Everyone in a culture the same? (No: people and companies vary.) Different = wrong? (No.) Not sure? (Observe, mirror, ask.)

4.3 · Scenario trios · then your capstone
