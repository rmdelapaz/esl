3.1 · The shape of a presentation

Tell them · tell them · tell them

OpeningTell them what you'll tell them: greet · introduce · topic + purpose · outline · questions
BodyTell them: 2–3 points, a signpost between each one
CloseTell them what you told them: summary · call to action · thanks · questions
WATCH OUT no structure = the audience switches off · structure is a courtesy
3.1 · Listen (Script 3.1-A) · tick each job

The five jobs of an opening

  • ① Greet: Good morning, everyone, and thank you for coming.
  • ② Introduce: For those who don't know me, I'm…
  • ③ Topic + purpose: Today I'm going to talk about… By the end, you'll…
  • ④ Outline: I've divided my talk into three parts.
  • ⑤ Housekeeping: Please feel free to ask questions as we go.
+ a hook (one or two sentences)
a question · a striking fact · a short story

"By the end, you'll…": for the speaker or the audience? (The audience: the benefit.)
3.1 · Signposting · one stress, then pause

Tell the audience where you are

Moving onThis brings me to my NEXT point. · Let's move ON to…
SequencingLet's START with… · Last but not LEAST…
Back / forwardAs I mentioned EARlier… · We'll come to that SHORTly.
ExampleFor INstance… · A good example of this is…
EmphasisThe KEY point here is… · What I want to STRESS is…
SummarySo, to sum UP… · Let me recap the main points.
"In conclusion…" in the middle of a talk? (No: only at the end.) · "As I mentioned earlier": forward or back? (Back.)
3.1 · Describing visuals · Workbook Ex 4

Guide, don't just label

  • Introduce: This graph shows…
  • Direct: If you look at Q3, …
  • Describe: rose steadily · rose sharply · peaked at · leveled off
  • Interpret: What this tells us is…
81 → 84: sharply or steadily? (Steadily.) · 93%: the highest or lowest? (Highest: it peaked.) · Which step does the audience care about most? (The meaning.)

C10 · 3.2 Negotiations · two people, one meeting room

Position or interest?

Position = what they say: "We need 15% off."
Interest = why: "My budget resets in January."

Ask: What's driving that? · What's most important to you here?

3.2 · Propose · accept · reject · counter

Say no without closing the door

FlatDiplomatic: reason + a way forward
"No. Impossible."I'm aFRAID that doesn't quite work for us, because… What we could offer instead is…
"We can't do that date."We can't do the 1st, but we could do the 8th.
"I want you give me discount."Is there any flexibility on the price?
"That would be difficult." Usually impossible? (No: ask "What would make it easier?") · Propose: How about we…? · Accept: We can live with that.
3.2 · Conditional bargaining · the golden rule

Never give something for nothing

Real offerIf you pay within 14 days, we'll waive the fee.
Testing the waterIf you were to double the order, would you expect a discount?
Only ifWe'll include training provided that / as long as you order today.
"If you sign today, we'll take 5% off." Can the buyer say "Deal!" now? (Yes: a real offer.)
"If you signed today, would you…?" A promise? (No: testing.) · ✘ If you will sign → ✔ If you sign
3.2 · Concessions & deadlock

Spend concessions carefully

  • Small and slow
  • Conditional: always an if
  • Labeled: "That's a big step for us."
  • Reciprocal: ask for something back
  • Reserved: keep one for the close
Stuck?
Let me take that back to my team.
If price is fixed, could we look at delivery or terms?
We're closer than it feels.

Close: So, to confirm what we've agreed… I'll send the details by email.
3.2 · The Deal Room · pairs · A/B cards

Find the win–win

  • Four issues. Some matter a lot to you, some a little. Your partner's are different.
  • Prep 5 · negotiate 13 · summarize + record 2 · add your points 2
  • Below 40 points? Walk away.
Show your card? (Never.) · Know your partner's priorities? (No: ask.) · The goal: beat your partner? (No: a good deal they can also say yes to.) · At the end? (Summarize aloud, record, sign.)

3.3 · Handling objections · LAARC

Acknowledge before you answer

LListen: don't interrupt
AAcknowledge: That's a FAIR concern.
AAsk: Is it the ↗price, or the ↘timing?
RRespond: value · difference · a trial
CConfirm: Does that address your concern?
  • "Too expensive" → reframe to value
  • "We already have one" → what's different
  • "Not the right time" → the cost of waiting
  • "I need to think" → find the real concern
3.3 · Pitch & Push-back · then the elevator sprint

Find the real concern

  • A pitches 60–90 seconds: problem first, one clear ask
  • B objects twice. The real concern is hidden on the card.
  • B says yes only when the real concern is answered. Swap.
Answer the objection immediately? (No: acknowledge and ask first.) · Buyers: reveal the hidden concern at the start? (No: only if asked well.) · The last sentence of a pitch? (One clear call to action, falling tone.)
ELEVATOR 30 seconds · who · what · value · ask · new partner × 3
