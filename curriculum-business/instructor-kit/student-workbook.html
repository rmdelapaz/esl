Class C2

  • a softener
  • to hedge
  • positive framing
  • a first impression
  • rapport
  • an elevator pitch

register = how formal your language is · tone = the attitude your words show (warm, firm, unsure…) · courteous = polite and respectful · blunt = too direct, so it sounds rude · to hedge = to make something softer or less certain (just, perhaps, a little) · rapport = a good, friendly working relationship · an elevator pitch = a short introduction to you and your work · to mirror = to match the formality of the people around you

Language box — The register spectrum
RegisterWhen to use itMaking a request
Formalfirst contact, senior or external people, official documents, bad newsI would be grateful if you could send me the report by Friday.
Neutral (the safe default)most everyday work; colleagues you know a littleCould you send me the report by Friday, please?
Friendly-professionalclose colleagues; a warm, established relationshipCould you send the report over by Friday? Thanks!
Too casual (avoid at work)—send me the report by fri
More formalI would like… · do not, cannot · request, assistance, regarding · Dear Ms. Alvarez, … Sincerely,
Less formalI'd like… · don't, can't · ask, help, about · Hi Sara, … Best,

Three softeners (one is usually enough): 1. A modal question: Could you…? · Would you be able to…? · Would it be possible to…? 2. A hedge: just · a little · perhaps · when you get a chance 3. Positive framing (say what you can do): I can have it ready by Monday. (not only I can't do it today.)

Careful: "Could you possibly maybe send it sometime if that's OK?" has too many softeners. It sounds unsure. Be polite and clear.

the ask = what you want the reader to do · to follow up / to chase = to write again because you haven't had an answer · CC = people who need to know but don't need to act · BCC = hidden copy: other readers can't see these addresses · a thread = a chain of messages on one topic · FYI = for your information (no action needed)

Language box — The email and its frame

Seven parts, in this order: 1 subject line (clear and specific) → 2 greeting → 3 opening line (your reason) → 4 body (one purpose + a clear ask with a deadline) → 5 closing line (the next step) → 6 sign-off (matches the greeting) → 7 name and signature.

RegisterGreetingSign-off
FormalDear Mr. / Ms. + family name, · Dear Sir or Madam,Sincerely, (US) · Yours sincerely, (UK, named person) · Yours faithfully, (UK, after "Dear Sir or Madam")
NeutralDear Sara, · Hi Sara,Kind regards, · Best regards,
Friendly-professionalHi Sara, · Hello Sara,Best, · Thanks,

Note: In modern email, "Dear Mr. Chen, … Kind regards," or "Best regards," is also very common and polite. Never mix a formal greeting with a very casual sign-off ("Dear Sir … Cheers!").

OpeningI'm writing to confirm / ask about / let you know… · Thank you for your email of March 3. · Further to our conversation, …
RequestCould you please send me…? · I would be grateful if you could… · Would it be possible to…? · I'd appreciate it if you could confirm by Friday.
AttachmentPlease find attached the report. · I've attached the figures for your review.
Following upI'm just following up on my email below. · As a gentle reminder, the deadline is Friday.
Bad newsUnfortunately, we won't be able to… · I'm afraid there's been a delay. · Please accept my apologies for…
ClosingPlease let me know if you have any questions. · I look forward to hearing from you. · Thanks in advance for your help.

How this module works

Each exercise heading tells you what to do: Listen, Read, Write, Say or With a partner. In every lesson you practice the phrases first, then use them in a role-play: a meeting, a networking event or a difficult conversation. You never need to talk about your real employer, your job situation or your salary. You can use your real job, a past job, or a role card with a fictional job and company. It is your choice.
