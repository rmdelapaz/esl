Midpoint Performance Check — Modules 1–2

A realistic check of the first half of the course, done the way work is done. Part A is a 35-minute timed writing paper: an email reply that covers three content points, and three chat messages rewritten into the right register. Part B is a set of pair speaking simulations at your table: two short phone calls and a short meeting, marked on the course rubric while the rest of the class works quietly on a reading and ranking task. Results go back privately in the Class 9 warmer.

Part A 35 min · Writing /20 Part B ~7 min per pair · Speaking /20 Covers Lessons 1.1–2.3 Rubric & models: assessment-key.html
How to frame it (say this at the start)

"Today is a check-up, not a final exam. You'll do the same kinds of tasks we've practiced: an email, some chat messages, two phone calls and a short meeting. I'll use the same four criteria we've used all course, so nothing will be a surprise. You'll get two scores, one for writing and one for speaking, plus one or two targets for the second half." Put the four criteria on the board (Task & Purpose · Register & Tone · Language Range & Accuracy · Organization & Delivery) and leave them there all class.

Total: 4 + 35 + 4 + 45 + 2 = 90 minutes.

You need
  • Per learner: the Part A paper and the Reading & Ranking task, printed at 100%
  • One set of speaking cards per pair (Call 1, Call 2, meeting), cut out; one speaking score sheet per learner
  • A table at one side of the room, two chairs back to back for the calls (so they can't lip-read), a phone timer
  • The rubric, model answers and speaking notes: assessment/assessment-key.html
What each part checks
  • Task 1 email: covering every point, a clear ask, apologizing and giving bad news politely (Lessons 1.1, 1.2)
  • Task 2 rewrites: moving up and down the register spectrum for different readers and channels (Lessons 1.1, 1.2)
  • Calls: call stages, taking and leaving messages, spelling, numbers and read-back (Lesson 1.3)
  • Meeting: opinions, polite disagreement, interrupting, clarifying, chairing and action points (Lessons 2.1, 2.3); small talk opens the meeting (Lesson 2.2)
Keep it fair and low-pressure

Every name, company and number on the cards is fictional; no one plays themselves. Seat the calls back to back, as on a real phone, and don't let partners show each other their cards. If a learner freezes, give one neutral prompt ("What would you say to the receptionist now?") and note it; don't rescue with the answer. Pair learners of similar confidence rather than a very strong with a very hesitant speaker, so nobody's score depends on a partner who dominates. A learner who needs adapted conditions (extra writing time, a typed paper) keeps the same tasks; note the change on the paper.

Returning results in the Class 9 warmer

Mark Part A before Class 9 (about 6 minutes per script once you know the models). In the Class 9 warmer (10 minutes), hand back each learner's score sheet folded: Writing /20, Speaking /20, one strength with evidence, and one or two targets for Modules 3–4. Learners copy the scores and targets into their Performance Tracker. While they read, the class does a quiet pair task: "Rewrite one sentence from your email that you'd now write differently." Don't discuss individual scores in front of the class; offer two-minute conversations at the break. The Module 2 Teacher's Guide has the full warmer plan.

3. You are her contact from now on (direct line 555-0162). Ask her to confirm that someone can receive the delivery on Thursday morning.

Write your reply to Ms. Varga. Write about 120–170 words (the subject line and your name do not count). Use a suitable greeting, a clear structure, and a matching sign-off.

Task 2 · Fix the register

about 10 min

Each message below is written in the wrong register for its reader. Rewrite it so it is right for the reader and the channel. Keep the same meaning, and add any small detail that makes it clear and polite (a reason, a thank-you, a time).

a) Team chat → your manager, Rosa · should be: neutral-professional, one clear message

"rosa the report for fri. i cant finish. need till mon"

b) Email → Mr. Julian Ferris, a new client you have never met · should be: formal or neutral email

"hey!! got ur files, will look l8r & get back 2 u 👍"

c) Team chat → Tom, a colleague you work with every day · should be: friendly-professional chat

"Dear Tom, I am writing to inform you that I would be grateful if you could kindly do the needful regarding the printer on the second floor at your earliest convenience. Yours faithfully"

Stop here. Check your spelling, your greeting and sign-off, and that you included all three points in Task 1.

Part B · Speaking Run-Sheet

One pair, about 7 minutes
  1. Seat the pair back to back. Give Call 1 cards (A: caller, B: receptionist). 30 seconds to read. "A, say 'Ring ring' when you're ready." (~2 min)
  2. Call 2: swap roles with the Call 2 cards (B: caller, A: receptionist). 30 seconds to read. (~2 min)
  3. Meeting: turn the chairs to face each other. Give the meeting cards. 45 seconds to read. "A, you're the chair. You have three minutes." Say "One minute left" at 2:00. (~3 min)
  4. Thank them, collect the cards and message pads, call the next pair, and write your scores now.
Pairs and roles
  • Card A chairs the meeting. Give the chair to the learner who needs stretch on chairing, or who hasn't chaired in class yet.
  • Trio (odd number): in Call 1, C takes Card B (receptionist) while B waits; Call 2 runs as normal (B calls, A answers). Everyone then joins the meeting with Card C added. Allow about 9 minutes, with 4 for the meeting.
  • Support: allow the phrase card from the Module 1 materials on the table for a B1 learner; note "phrase card used" on the sheet.
  • Stretch: tell a C1 learner before Call 1: "Offer the caller an alternative contact, and deal with one request you can't meet."

Observer prompt · for you

Listen for these moves

Chair: opening + purpose · "First on the agenda…" · brings others in · summarizes · "Can I confirm…?" · action points with names and days

Participants: "In my view…" · "I see your point, but…" · "Sorry to interrupt, but…" · "Could you clarify…?" · a reason with every opinion · a compromise ("What if we…?")
