The Rubric, Model Answers & Next Steps

Everything you need to score the three checkpoints consistently: the one analytic rubric used for every task in the course, a rough (approximate) CEFR guide, model answers for every writing task with checked word counts, notes on the role cards and what to listen for in each simulation, a class scoring sheet for each checkpoint, and a method for turning scores into two or three individual targets.

Scoring principles

One rubric, everywhere. Every task in this course (diagnostic, performance check, final, and any role-play you choose to mark in class) is scored on the same four criteria: Task & Purpose, Register & Tone, Language Range & Accuracy and Organization & Delivery. Each criterion gets a whole number from 1 to 5; the total is out of 20. At each checkpoint a learner receives two scores, a Writing /20 and a Speaking /20, never one combined number; they record both in the Performance Tracker. When a checkpoint has several writing or speaking tasks, give each criterion one score that reflects all the evidence for that skill.

Score what the task asks for. This is workplace communication, so a message that achieves its purpose with a few small errors outscores a grammatically perfect one that misses the point. Accents are never scored; only whether the listener can understand without effort. American and British spelling and conventions are both accepted (organize / organise, Mr. / Mr).

The Course Rubric · four criteria, 1–5 each

Use the descriptors for 1, 3 and 5. A 2 or a 4 means the performance sits between two described levels (some features of each). Read or listen to the whole performance before scoring. In short: 1 = not yet · 2 = emerging · 3 = workable · 4 = strong · 5 = confident and professional.

Criterion1 · Not yet23 · Workable45 · Confident & professional
Task & PurposeDid it achieve the goal: all points covered, clear action? The goal is not achieved. Several required points are missing or misunderstood, or the text or talk is mostly off-task. The reader or listener can't tell what should happen next. Writing well under half the target length; a call with no usable message; a pitch with no request. between 1 and 3 The goal is mostly achieved. Most points are covered, but one is missing, unclear or only mentioned. There is a next step, but it's vague ("let me know"). Noticeably short or long, or a talk that runs well under or over time. A STAR answer without a clear Result. between 3 and 5 The goal is fully achieved. Every required point is covered accurately and developed where it needs to be. The next step is unmistakable (a dated ask, a decision, action points, a call to action). Length and timing fit the task. In speaking, details are checked and confirmed.
Register & ToneAppropriate formality, politeness, diplomacy The register is mostly wrong for the reader or the situation (text-speak to a client; a stiff formal letter to a close colleague). Requests and refusals sound like orders ("I want you send…", "No.") or are so hedged that the point is lost. Greeting and sign-off missing or mismatched. Flat intonation that sounds rude throughout. between 1 and 3 Generally appropriate, with noticeable lapses: an over-formal formula ("I am writing to inform you that…", "Kindly do the needful") or a casual slip; one or two blunt lines; softening sometimes missing or piled up. The reader would not be offended but might notice. between 3 and 5 The formality is right for the reader, channel and relationship throughout, and shifts smoothly when needed. Requests, bad news, disagreement and refusals are diplomatic and clear (one softener, a reason, a way forward). Tone is confident, warm and courteous. Greeting and sign-off match. Intonation sounds interested and polite.
Language Range & AccuracyPhrases, grammar, vocabulary A very limited range: the task needs phrases and words the learner doesn't have. Frequent errors in basic structures (tense, word order, missing verbs) often make the meaning unclear. between 1 and 3 Enough phrases and vocabulary for the task, with some repetition or reliance on a few set phrases. Simple structures are mostly accurate. Errors are noticeable (articles, prepositions, "discuss about", "I am agree", tense slips) and sometimes make the reader pause, but the meaning is clear. between 3 and 5 A wide range of business phrases from the course (requests, softeners, conditionals for bargaining, document phrases, signposts) used naturally and precisely. Complex structures are mostly accurate. Errors are rare, minor, and never cause confusion. Names, figures and dates are accurate.
Organization & DeliveryStructure, signposting, fluency, pronunciation, interaction Writing: little visible structure (one block, or disordered fragments); the main point is hard to find. Speaking: frequent long pauses or breakdowns; hard to follow; pronunciation often makes understanding difficult; little or no interaction. between 1 and 3 Writing: an overall structure is visible (opening, body, close), but paragraphing or order is uneven, linking is mechanical, or the ask is buried. Speaking: some hesitation and restarts, but easy enough to follow; basic signposting; generally understandable, though the listener sometimes needs effort; interaction mostly reactive. between 3 and 5 Writing: logical and easy to scan: a specific subject line, the purpose up front, one idea per paragraph, headings or bullets where they help, a clear close. Speaking: fluent with natural pauses; clearly staged and signposted; easy to understand, with stress on key words and numbers; manages interaction well (turn-taking, checking, reading back, bringing others in, handling questions).

Total /20 = Task & Purpose + Register & Tone + Language Range & Accuracy + Organization & Delivery.

Rules that keep scoring consistent
  • Caps. Under half the target length, or more than one required point missing: Task & Purpose 2 at most. A memorized text that doesn't answer the task: Task & Purpose 2 at most.
  • Count an error once, under the criterion it affects most. "I want you send me" is Register & Tone if the problem is directness; the missing "to" alone is Language Range & Accuracy.
  • Intonation that makes a polite sentence sound rude belongs to Register & Tone; unclear sounds, stress and pace belong to Organization & Delivery.
  • Pairs and groups: score each learner on what they said. A partner who dominates doesn't lower a quiet learner's score if that learner's turns were effective; note the problem and adjust pairings next time.
  • Unsure between two scores? Re-read the 3 and 5 descriptors, then choose. On the diagnostic only, choose the lower score and write "or ___".

This mapping is approximate. The rubric measures performance on specific workplace tasks, not general proficiency, and a single task can't give a reliable CEFR level. Use it to plan support and stretch and to talk about progress. Don't report it as an official level or print it on a certificate.

Class 1 · Diagnostic Reply Email · model answer

Word counts in this key cover everything from the greeting to the sign-off, counted as a word processor does (numbers and hyphenated words count as one word each). Subject lines, headings, names and signature blocks are not counted.

Subject: Re: Customer Service Skills Day — please reply by Thursday

Hi Mateo,

Thank you for your email and for including me in the Skills Day.

I would prefer the morning session, from 9:00 to 12:30, if there is still space.

One topic I would find really useful is handling complaints on the phone, especially how to stay calm and polite when a customer is upset about a late delivery.

On the day, I will need a parking permit, because I will be driving from the Eastfield office. I have my own laptop, so I don't need one from you.

I also have a quick question: is there anything I should read or prepare before the session?

Thanks again, and I look forward to it.

Best regards,
[Name]
Customer Service, Northwind Logistics

116 words (target 100–150)

Why this is a 5 on every criterion
  • Task & Purpose: answers all three questions (session, topic, needs) in order, asks one relevant question, and makes Mateo's job easy.
  • Register & Tone: neutral-professional to a colleague not yet met: "Hi Mateo" + "Best regards" match; "I would prefer", "if there is still space".
  • Language: useful email phrases ("Thank you for your email", "I look forward to it"); accurate.
  • Organization: one point per paragraph, in Mateo's order; the question is signposted ("I also have a quick question").
Common at this stage
  • Answers only one or two of the questions, or forgets to ask one (Task & Purpose 3 at most).
  • "Dear Mr. Brandt, … Yours faithfully" to a colleague who wrote "Hi": over-formal (Register & Tone 3).
  • "I am writing to inform you that I want the morning." Over-formal opening plus a blunt "I want."
  • No greeting, or "Hello Mateo" with "Cheers" and emoji.
  • All one paragraph; "I'm agree"; "discuss about"; missing articles ("I need parking permit").

Class 8 · Task 1 · Reply to a client · model answer

Subject: Order BF-2291 — new delivery on Thursday before 11 a.m.

Dear Ms. Varga,

Thank you for your email, and please accept my apologies for the missing delivery and for the lack of contact from our side. On Monday, our sorting center had a computer system failure, and several deliveries, including your order BF-2291, were delayed.

I am pleased to confirm that your 40 cartons will now be delivered on Thursday before 11 a.m., in good time for your promotion on Saturday. We will not charge you for delivery on this order.

From now on, I will be your contact for this order. If anything else comes up, please call me directly on 555-0162.

Could you please confirm that someone will be available to receive the delivery on Thursday morning?

Once again, I am sorry for the inconvenience.

Kind regards,
[Name]
Customer Account Coordinator, Northwind Logistics

129 words (target 120–170)

Content pointMust includeAcceptable alternatives
1An apology and the reason (system failure at the sorting center on Monday)"I'm very sorry that…", "We apologize for…", "Unfortunately, a technical problem at our sorting center…"
2Thursday before 11 a.m. and no delivery charge"free of charge", "we will waive the delivery fee", "at no cost to you"
3You as the contact with the number and the request to confirm Thursday-morning receipt"I will personally look after…", "Could you let me know whether someone can…"; "call me at" (US) or "on" (UK) both fine
Scoring notes
  • All three points, each with both parts: Task & Purpose can reach 5. One part missing: 4. One whole point missing: 3. Two points missing: 2 at most.
  • A specific subject line and an ask near the end are Organization & Delivery evidence; a missing subject line alone doesn't drop the score below 4.
  • Blaming a colleague or the client ("your warehouse gave the wrong address") is a Register & Tone problem.
  • "Dear Ms. Varga … Kind regards" and "Dear Ms. Varga … Yours sincerely" are both fine.
Top errors to expect
  • Over-apologizing in every sentence, which sounds unsure; or no apology at all.
  • Burying the new date in the middle of a long paragraph.
  • "We will deliver you the goods" / "deliver to you on Thursday at before 11."
  • "Please confirm me…" (confirm that…); "the informations"; "I am sorry for the inconvenient."
  • Forgetting the clear action: no request to confirm Thursday receipt.

Task 2 is the strongest single piece of evidence for Register & Tone in writing: three correct shifts (down to chat, up to a client, down from over-formal) with softening supports a 5 even if Task 1 is a 4. The expression "do the needful" is standard in some varieties of English; the point is that it sounds out of place in a quick chat to a close colleague in most English-speaking workplaces, not that it is wrong.

Top speaking errors at the midpoint

Direct translations that sound like orders ("I want speak with Sam"; "Give me your number"). Numbers said as one fast block. Receivers who write the message but never read it back. In meetings: silence until asked, then a very long turn; disagreeing with "No, is not good"; a chair who gives their own opinions at length and forgets action points; flat intonation on "Sorry to interrupt," which makes it sound annoyed.

Final · Writing Task 1 · Proposal sections · model answer

Executive Summary

This proposal sets out how to reduce packing errors at the Riverside warehouse. Error returns rose from 2.1% of orders in Q1 to 4.8% in Q2, or about 580 orders a month, at a cost of approximately $22,000 per month. Wrong labels and wrong items caused 83% of these returns.

We recommend two actions: first, installing barcode scanners at all eight packing stations ($16,000, plus $150 a month for support); second, a half-day refresher training course for all packers ($3,500). Together, these measures are projected to save approximately $12,000 per month, and the scanners should pay for themselves within two months. Hiring two additional quality checkers is not recommended at this stage.

112 words (target 90–130), heading not counted

Recommendations

1. Install barcode scanners at all eight packing stations (priority). This addresses the two largest causes of returns, wrong labels (52%) and wrong items (31%). In the Eastfield pilot, scanners reduced these errors by about 60%. At Riverside, this would mean around 290 fewer returns and savings of approximately $11,000 per month. It is recommended that installation be completed by the end of next month.

2. Provide refresher training for all 24 packers. The training ($3,500) targets damage during packing, which caused 17% of returns. If damage errors fall by a third, as expected, the saving would be about $1,250 per month.

3. Review results after three months. Hiring two quality checkers ($84,000 per year) would cover only 30% of orders. We therefore suggest that this option be reconsidered only if error rates remain above 2.5% after the review.

139 words (target 130–180), heading not counted, list numbers counted (136 without them)

The figures, checked
  • Error cost: 580 × $38 ≈ $22,000 per month.
  • Labels + wrong items: 52% + 31% = 83% of 580 ≈ 480 returns; 60% fewer ≈ 290; 290 × $38 ≈ $11,000 per month.
  • Damage: 17% of 580 ≈ 99; a third ≈ 33; 33 × $38 ≈ $1,250 per month.
  • Scanners pay back in about 1.5 months ($16,000 ÷ roughly $10,800 net monthly saving).
  • Learners may round differently; accept any figure that is reasonable and clearly derived from the data. Don't penalize arithmetic slips under Task & Purpose unless they change the recommendation.
What earns the top scores
  • Task & Purpose 5: the summary stands alone (purpose + findings + recommendations); at least two recommendations in priority order, each with a cost and a benefit; option B rejected with a reason.
  • Register & Tone 5: objective and impersonal ("The data indicates…", "It is recommended that…"); hedged where honest ("projected", "approximately").
  • Language 5: document phrases, accurate numbers, passive and subjunctive used correctly.
  • Organization 5: headings, the answer first, numbered recommendations.
Top errors to expect

An executive summary that "teases" ("This proposal will discuss…") instead of stating the recommendation · retelling all the data with no recommendation · recommending all three options with no priority · "I think we should buy scanners because they are good" (chatty, no evidence) · percentages confused with orders ("4.8 orders") · "It is recommended to install…" (acceptable) versus "It is recommended that the scanners are installed" (acceptable in British English; the subjunctive be installed is more formal; don't penalize either).

Final · Writing Task 2A · Cover letter · model answer

Dear Ms. Hale,

I am writing to apply for the position of Operations Coordinator, as advertised on the Kestrel Design careers page. I have followed your studio's work for some time, and I would welcome the chance to help your projects run smoothly.

For the past five years, I have worked as a logistics coordinator at Corran Trading, a mid-size importer. In this role, I introduced a shared delivery calendar that cut late deliveries by 20% in one year. I also prepare weekly status reports for three department heads, which matches your need for someone who can keep clients and colleagues clearly informed.

I am a calm and organized communicator. Last year, I trained four new team members on our scheduling software, and I am confident I could learn your systems quickly. My degree in business administration was earned abroad and has been evaluated as equivalent to a U.S. bachelor's degree.

I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my experience could support your team. I am available for an interview at your convenience, and I have attached my résumé for your review.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Sincerely,
Sam Rivera

191 words (target 180–230)

Look for: the role named in the first line; evidence matched to the ad (scheduling, reports, communication, software); one "why them" sentence; a confident close with availability. Accept "Yours sincerely" or "Sincerely"; "résumé" or "CV". A learner who used their own experience is scored exactly the same way. Common problems: copying the role card as a list; empty adjectives ("hard-working, dynamic team player"); a generic letter that could go to any company; "I am applying for the job" with no role name; over-long sentences that bury the evidence.

Final · Writing Task 2B · Formal email · model answer

Subject: Your contract renewal — pricing and Saturday deliveries

Dear Mr. Ferris,

Thank you for your email and for your continued business. We value our partnership with Tallis Print Co., and I have looked carefully at both of your requests.

I am afraid we are not able to reduce our prices by 12%, as our fuel and staff costs have risen this year. However, if you are able to sign a two-year contract, we would be pleased to offer a 6% reduction on all deliveries.

Regarding Saturday deliveries, these are available at an additional $45 per delivery. Alternatively, we could offer a guaranteed two-hour morning window on weekdays at no extra cost, which may meet the same need.

Would you be available for a short call next Tuesday or Wednesday to discuss which option suits you best? I would be happy to send you a revised quote before our call. Please let me know if you have any questions in the meantime.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely,
[Name]
Account Manager, Northwind Logistics

162 words (target 150–200)

Look for: thanks and relationship first; a refusal with a reason ("I am afraid… as…"); a conditional offer (if + two-year contract → 6%); both Saturday options; a specific next step. Register & Tone 3 or below: a flat "We cannot accept your request"; "You must sign for two years"; an unconditional 6% ("We'll give you 6%"). Common problems: "discuss about the options"; "a two years contract"; forgetting the Saturday request entirely (Task & Purpose 3 at most).

(S) In my last role, one of our biggest customers moved a large order forward by a full week, just before a holiday weekend. (T) I was responsible for arranging the deliveries, so it was my job to make the new date work. Examiner notes for Classes 15–16

Negotiation: score the language and process, not the outcome; a respectful "no deal, here's the next step" can score 5 on Task & Purpose if interests were explored and the close is clear. Pitch: in a pair pitch, score each partner on their own section and their own Q&A answer; both can share the same Organization evidence only if both signposted. Interview: the learner may use a role card; don't probe for real details. If a learner opted in to the "more experience than the role requires" question, listen for a positive, forward-looking answer ("I'm looking for a role where I can…"), never an apology for their past. Speaking /20: one score per criterion from all three tasks (see the score sheet); if the interview is missing, score from the negotiation and pitch and note it.

From Scores to Targets

A score is only useful if it leads to a next step. After each checkpoint, turn each learner's two scores into two or three targets that they copy into section 4 of their Performance Tracker.

  1. Find the lowest criterion in Writing and in Speaking. If several are tied, choose the one that affects the learner's own goals (Class 1, Part 3) most.
  2. Look at the evidence, not the number. Which two or three things caused the low score? (A missing ask? Blunt requests? Numbers said too fast?)
  3. Write the target as a habit you can see: specific, practiced in 2–4 weeks, and checkable. Not "improve my emails" but "put the ask and the deadline in the first two lines of every email."
  4. Attach the practice: one online lesson (https://rays-esl.netlify.app/curriculum-business/lesson_NN.html) and one real-life use.
  5. Add one "keep doing" from the highest criterion, so feedback is never only about problems.
  6. Review at the next checkpoint: "Achieved?" ☐ yes ☐ nearly. A target reached is worth naming out loud, privately.

Returning Results Kindly

Principles
  • Private, never public. No rankings, no reading scores aloud. Hand results back folded or in an envelope.
  • Evidence before numbers. Start with a real strength and a quotation from their work; give the scores after.
  • Two scores, two stories. Writing and speaking often move at different speeds. Say so.
  • Name what is fixable. Most lost points come from habits (a missing ask, no read-back, no softener) that change within weeks.
  • Respect experience. For learners with strong professional backgrounds, make clear the targets are about English, not about their competence.
Things you can say
Your email answered every point, and the client would know exactly what to do next. That's the hardest part. Now let's make the tone a little warmer.
You caught my mistake with the phone number. That's the habit that saves real money at work.
Your speaking score went up three points since Class 1, mostly because you now summarize before you finish. Keep doing that.
You clearly know this field very well. Your target is about showing that in English: shorter sentences and one number in every example.
When a result is hard to hear

Some learners need this course for a job search or a promotion, and a score can feel personal. Give time and privacy, offer a short conversation after class, and keep the discussion on what the next four weeks can change. Don't make promises about job outcomes, and don't give advice about employment law, credential recognition or immigration: point learners to the appropriate service in your organization or community. If a learner mentions serious distress, follow your organization's support and referral procedures.
