B · Meeting Simulation Cards (2.1) — Scenarios 1 and 2

All companies, people and figures are invented. Groups of four: chair, participant A, participant B, observer. Each learner reads only their own card. The positions clash a little, but a compromise is always possible. The chair must finish with at least two action points (who / what / by when). Ten minutes on the timer.

Scenario 1 · Chair

Kestrel Design — the office move

You are the office manager. Kestrel Design is moving to a new office. The new lease starts on 1 June.

Agenda: 1 Moving date: the weekend of June 7–8 or June 21–22? · 2 Packing: who organizes it? · 3 Any other business

Your job: open the meeting, hear both sides, get a decision on the date, and assign: a packing coordinator, and someone to tell the clients the new address.

Useful: The purpose of today's meeting is… · Let's hear from… · So, to summarize… · Can I confirm, you'll… by…?

D · Hard-Conversation A/B Cards (2.3)

Groups of three: A says the difficult thing, B reacts following the card, C observes with the checklist in the workbook planner. Three rounds, rotating roles. All situations are fictional and ordinary. Cards 1–3 are a little easier (good for B1 learners); cards 4–6 are harder (good for C1 learners). In card 6, B speaks first.

A · 1 · Say no

A colleague needs help

Your colleague asks you to present their slides at Friday's client meeting. You are at capacity: you have two deadlines this week.

Your goal: say no, but keep the relationship. Offer an alternative (for example, review the slides on Thursday, or suggest another person).

B · 1

You need help

You ask A to present your slides at Friday's client meeting, because you have a medical appointment.

How to react: push a little at first ("It's only twenty minutes!"). Accept if A offers a useful alternative. Stay friendly.

A · 2 · Push back on a deadline

Wednesday is not realistic

Your manager has given you a new customer report, due on Wednesday. You think a good report needs until Friday.

Your goal: push back politely. Explain why, and offer a solution (a short summary on Wednesday, the full report on Friday).

B · 2

You are the manager

The client asked for the report "early next week." You told them Wednesday.

How to react: at first, say Wednesday is important. Accept a partial delivery on Wednesday if A explains clearly and politely. If A is blunt, be less flexible.

A · 3 · Give feedback (SBI)

The weekly report

You lead a small team. B's weekly report has arrived late three times this month, and last week one figure was wrong. B's analysis is usually very good.

Your goal: give feedback with Situation → Behavior → Impact, one honest positive and a question. Agree a next step.

B · 3

You write the weekly report

You didn't know the late reports were a problem. The sales data often reaches you late on Thursday.

How to react: if A is specific and fair, receive it well ("Thanks for telling me") and explain the data problem. If A attacks you as a person ("you're careless"), become a little defensive.

A · 4 · Handle a complaint

Missing items, again

You are an account manager at Northwind Logistics. B's hotel has received a delivery with missing items for the second time this month.

Your goal: acknowledge the feeling → focus on the problem, not the person → find common ground → offer a clear way forward with a time.

B · 4

You are the customer

You manage a hotel. Today's delivery was missing 20 sets of towels, and guests arrive tonight.

How to react: start frustrated ("This is not good enough."). Calm down only when A acknowledges the problem and offers a specific action and a time. If A blames a colleague, get more annoyed.

A · 5 · Disagree with a plan

Cutting the testing

Your manager wants to cut the testing phase of a project to save a week. You think it's risky: a serious problem after launch would cost more than a week.

Your goal: acknowledge the time pressure, give your view with a reason, and propose a compromise (keep the core testing, cut something else).

B · 5

You are the manager

The project is behind, and the client is not happy. You want to save a week.

How to react: insist at first. Accept A's view only if A gives a reason and a way to save some time. Ask "So what would you suggest?"

A · 6 · Receive feedback

B has feedback for you

B will give you some feedback about yesterday's team call. B speaks first.

Your goal: listen, thank, ask for an example, and don't defend. At the end, say one thing you'll do differently.

Useful: Thanks for telling me. · Could you give me an example? · That's fair. · Let me take that on board.

B · 6 · You speak first

Give A some feedback

In yesterday's team call, A talked over Sara twice while she was explaining the budget, and she didn't finish her point. A's own ideas in the call were useful.

Your goal: use Situation → Behavior → Impact, one honest positive and a question: "How did you feel the call went?"

F · Extra Quiet Task — Rank the Replies (C8 · for early finishers of the Reading & Ranking task)

The situation. Ms. Adeyemi, a new client at Silverleaf Hotels, has emailed Tidewater Packaging. She asks if her order of 400 boxes can arrive on Thursday instead of Friday. The warehouse can send only half the order by Thursday. Here are five possible replies.

A "Hi! Thursday?? Not possible, sorry lol. Friday it is."

B "Dear Ms. Adeyemi, Thank you for your message. I'm afraid we can't deliver the full order by Thursday, but we could send 200 boxes on Thursday and the other 200 on Friday morning. Would that work for you? Best regards, Rosa Delgado"

C "Dear Madam, Kindly note that your request is not possible. Please do the needful and accept Friday delivery. Regards"

D "Hi Ms. Adeyemi, Thanks for your message. Let me check with our warehouse and get back to you by 3 p.m. today. Best regards, Rosa Delgado"

E "No. The delivery date is Friday. We already told you this."

1. Rank the replies from most appropriate (1) to least appropriate (5): 1 ____ 2 ____ 3 ____ 4 ____ 5 ____

2. Why is your number 1 the best? ______________________________________________

3. What is the biggest problem with your number 5? _______________________________

4. (If you finish early) Rewrite your number 5 so it is polite, clear and useful.

Teacher note (not for learners): suggested ranking B, D, C, A, E. B is best: polite, clear, honest about the limit, and offers a partial yes with a question. D is also good (it buys time with a clear deadline) but doesn't solve the problem yet. C is over-formal and uses outdated formulas ("Kindly note," "do the needful") that sound cold; A is far too informal for a new client ("lol," double question marks) and gives no alternative. Accept C and A in either order if the reason is sensible. E is aggressive: no greeting, no softener, blaming ("We already told you"). This task is for practice only and is not scored with the rubric. It uses a different scenario from the performance-check Reading & Ranking task, so learners who finish early are not repeating it.

G · Listening Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

All scripts were written for this course; every person, company and figure is invented. Rehearse each one once aloud. Read at a natural, clear speed. For scripts with more than one speaker, use a second reader, change your voice and position for each speaker, or record them in advance. Read the polite phrases with the intonation you want learners to copy: these scripts are models of tone as well as language.

I plan delivery routes at Northwind Logistics, so I'm probably one of the people causing your delays! · Joanna: (laughs) Maybe! How did you get into route planning? · Hamid: By accident, actually. I trained as a civil engineer, and when I changed careers, I started in a warehouse. Someone noticed I liked maps and spreadsheets. · Joanna: Same here, actually. I started in a completely different field too. I was a chemistry teacher for ten years. · Hamid: That must have been a big change. What do you miss most? · Joanna: The students, sometimes. But not the grading! · Hamid: (laughs) Listen, we're testing a new tracking tool for food customers next month. Shall I send you some information? · Joanna: I'd love that. What's the best way to reach you? · Hamid: Here's my card. My email is on the back. · Joanna: Great, and here's mine. Well, I should go and say hello to a few people before the next session, but it was really good to meet you. · Hamid: You too. I'll send you that information this week.

Answers: version 1 stalls because every question is closed, the answers are one word, nobody gives free information, and the topics jump. Version 2: opener "This line for coffee is quite something, isn't it?" · follow-up questions "What did you think of it?", "How's that going?", "How did you get into route planning?", "What do you miss most?" · Joanna's free information: changing the tracking system; a food company, everything must arrive fresh; a chemistry teacher for ten years · Hamid's: plans routes, "probably one of the people causing your delays"; trained as a civil engineer, started in a warehouse · common ground: both changed careers (and both care about delivery tracking) · contacts: they swap business cards · exit (Joanna): "I should go and say hello to a few people before the next session, but it was really good to meet you." · next step: Hamid will send information about the tracking tool this week.

2.3-A · Feedback on the monthly report (Workbook 2.3, Ex 1 · two voices: Rosa, a team leader, and Daniel · weak version, then strong version)

Weak version. Rosa: Daniel, your reports are always late. You need to be more organized. Anyway, good job on the chart.
Strong version. Rosa: Daniel, is now a good time for a bit of feedback on the monthly report? · Daniel: Sure. · Rosa: First, one thing that worked really well was the new chart on delivery times. The client really liked it. Now, one thing I'd like to look at together. In the last two months, the report has reached me on the morning it's due, not the day before. That means I haven't had time to check the figures before it goes to the client, and last month one number in the summary was wrong. · Daniel: Oh. I didn't realize it was causing a problem. · Rosa: How do you feel the process is going from your side? · Daniel: Honestly, the warehouse data arrives late, so I'm always rushing at the end. · Rosa: That's useful to know. What if we asked the warehouse for the data two days earlier? Then could you send me the draft the afternoon before? · Daniel: That would work. Thanks for telling me. I'll talk to the warehouse today.

Answers: honest positive: the new chart on delivery times (the client liked it) · S: the monthly report, in the last two months · B: it reached Rosa on the morning it was due, not the day before · I: no time to check the figures; one number in last month's summary was wrong · question: "How do you feel the process is going from your side?" · proposal: ask the warehouse for the data two days earlier; send the draft the afternoon before · Daniel's receiving phrase: "Thanks for telling me." (also "I didn't realize it was causing a problem"). The weak version uses "always," judges the person ("be more organized"), gives nothing specific, and ends with a hollow compliment.

2.3-B · The late-delivery complaint (Workbook 2.3, Ex 5 · two voices: Ms. Kovacs, a hotel housekeeping manager, and Sam, customer service at Northwind Logistics)

Ms. Kovacs: Hello, this is Katalin Kovacs from Silverleaf Hotels. I'm calling about our linen delivery. It was late again this morning. That's the second time this month. Honestly, it's not good enough. We had guests waiting for their rooms. · Sam: Ms. Kovacs, thank you for calling, and you're right to raise it. I can see why you'd feel frustrated. A late delivery when guests are waiting makes your whole morning harder, and I'm sorry about that. · Ms. Kovacs: So what are you going to do about it? Last time someone just told me the driver was new. · Sam: Let's focus on fixing the problem rather than who's at fault. Let me make sure I've understood you correctly: the delivery is due at seven, and today it arrived at about nine thirty? · Ms. Kovacs: Nine forty, actually. · Sam: Nine forty. Thank you. We both want your deliveries to arrive on time, every day. So here's what I'd suggest as a way forward. I'll find out exactly where the delay happened today, and I'll call you back by four o'clock this afternoon with a plan. And from next week, we can send you a text message when the truck leaves our warehouse, so you're never caught out again. Would that work for you? · Ms. Kovacs: Yes. That would help. Thank you. · Sam: Thank you for your patience, Ms. Kovacs. I'll speak to you by four.

Answers: the problem: the linen delivery was late again (the second time this month): due at 7:00, arrived at 9:40 · order: 1 acknowledge the feeling ("you're right to raise it · I can see why you'd feel frustrated") · 2 the problem, not the person ("Let's focus on fixing the problem rather than who's at fault") · 3 common ground ("We both want your deliveries to arrive on time, every day") · 4 a way forward ("Here's what I'd suggest as a way forward…") · Sam will find out where the delay happened and call back by 4 p.m. today with a plan; from next week, a text message when the truck leaves the warehouse. Sam also checks understanding ("Let me make sure I've understood you correctly") and does not blame the driver.
