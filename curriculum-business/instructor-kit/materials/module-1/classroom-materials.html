C · Speaking-Sample Interviewer Card (C1)

Interviewer card · 3 minutes

How to run the diagnostic speaking sample

  1. Say: "This is a short practice conversation, about three minutes. There are no right or wrong answers. I'd just like to hear you speak. You can talk about your real job, a role card, or a job you invent."
  2. Part 1, self-introduction (about 1 minute): use the prompt printed in assessment/needs-analysis.html. Neutral follow-ups only: "Can you tell me a little more about that?" · "What do you enjoy about it?"
  3. Part 2, phone-message role-play (about 2 minutes): follow the role-play on the paper. You play the receptionist ("Good afternoon, ___ speaking. How can I help?"); the person the learner wants is unavailable, so offer to take a message. Don't read back for them: note whether they spell their name, give the number clearly and check the details.
  4. If the learner freezes: wait five seconds, repeat the prompt once more slowly, or offer a role card. Then move on: "That's fine. Let's try the next part."
  5. At three minutes: "Thank you. That's all." Smile. The learner goes back to the writing sample and gets the missed minutes.
  6. After the learner leaves (never in front of them), note one or two words per criterion: Task & Purpose · Register & Tone · Language Range & Accuracy · Organization & Delivery. Score 1–5 each with the rubric in assessment/assessment-key.html.

At-home option: the learner records a one-minute self-introduction and a phone message (following the paper's role-play card) on their phone and brings it to C2.

D · Welcome Reception — Situation Cards (C2)

One card per group of 3. Learners use their own details, a role card (set B) or invented ones. Rotate roles every 5 minutes: Host → Visitor → Colleague; in round 3 the third person observes with the checklist (set F). Cards 5 and 6 carry a stretch constraint: give them to groups with C1-level learners.

Reception · 1

A supplier's first visit

Host: you work at Brightline Foods. Welcome a new account manager from your delivery company. Introduce your colleague.

Visitor: it's your first visit. Give your pitch; ask one question about their work.

Colleague: you lead a team that works with this supplier every day. Suggest a next step (a tour, a call next week).

Reception · 2

A new team member's first day

Host: you lead a team at Kestrel Design. A new designer starts today. Welcome them and introduce the office manager.

Visitor: it's your first day. Introduce yourself and ask one practical question (lunch, meetings, the team).

Colleague: you're the office manager. Offer help with one thing (a laptop, a desk, a building pass).

Reception · 3

A conference coffee break

Host: you gave a talk this morning at a hospitality conference. Someone comes to speak to you. Introduce your colleague from sales.

Visitor: you enjoyed the talk. Introduce yourself and say one thing you found useful.

Colleague: you work in sales. Find one way you could work together and suggest exchanging contact details.

Reception · 4

Before a client meeting

Host: you're a sales manager at Harbor Point Supplies. A purchasing manager from a new client arrives 10 minutes early. Offer a drink and introduce your delivery coordinator.

Visitor: ask one question about delivery times.

Colleague: answer the question positively and briefly.

Reception · 5 · stretch

A very senior visitor

Host: the regional director of a major client visits your office. Stay formal (title + family name) until you are invited to use first names.

Visitor: you are senior and a little formal. Invite first names only after two or three exchanges.

Colleague: you're new. Introduce yourself briefly and confidently.

Reception · 6 · stretch

The visitor is late

Host: your visitor arrives 20 minutes late and looks stressed. Put them at ease and still introduce your colleague.

Visitor: your train was delayed. Apologize briefly (once!), then give your pitch.

Colleague: the meeting now has less time. Suggest politely how to use it (positive framing).

E · Supplier Reply Cards (C3)

One card per learner in round 3 of the email exchange. The learner is Mr. Daniel Okoro, Harbor Point Supplies, and writes a short, formal reply under their partner's request. Give card 1 to B1 learners who need a shorter task and cards 3–4 to stronger learners. Model replies are in the Module 1 answer key.

Supplier Reply · 1

Yes, on time

You can send the quote now. The KD-20 costs $389 per desk, including delivery. Total for 40 desks: $15,560. The quote is valid for 30 days. Say that it is attached.

Supplier Reply · 2

Yes, but one detail is missing

You can quote, but first you need the delivery address and the color (black or white). Ask for both politely. Promise the final quote within one working day.

Supplier Reply · 3

A delay, with an alternative

Bad news: the KD-20 is out of stock until next month. Offer two options: the KD-20 delivered in five weeks, or the KD-24 (very similar) at $405 per desk including delivery, available now. Ask which they prefer; you'll send the quote by Friday.

Supplier Reply · 4

A question before quoting

Ask whether they want the desks assembled on site. Assembly costs $25 extra per desk. Promise the full quote within 24 hours of their answer.

F · Video-Meeting Cards (C4)

One set per group of 4: one chair card and three problem cards (choose from five). Participants act their problem; they don't read it out. Four minutes, then a new chair and new problem cards.

Chair

Weekly project update

Agenda: 1 delivery schedule · 2 budget · 3 next steps.

  • greet everyone; check the audio
  • handle each problem with a natural phrase
  • share your screen or the agenda
  • set one ground rule (for example, questions in the chat)
  • hand over item 1 to someone by name
Problem · 1

You're on mute

When the chair checks the audio, answer, but move your lips without sound. When someone says "You're on mute," say: "Sorry about that. Can you hear me now?"

Problem · 2

Your connection is bad

When you speak, say only every second or third word, with gaps. If someone asks you to repeat, speak normally: "Is that better?"

Problem · 3

You're joining late

Wait one minute before you "join." Apologize briefly: "Sorry I'm late. Please go ahead." Don't give a long explanation.

Problem · 4

You freeze

In the middle of a sentence, stop moving and stay silent for about ten seconds. Then: "Sorry, I think I froze. Where were we?"

Problem · 5

You want to speak, but…

You have a question about the budget, but you don't want to interrupt. Lean forward and look as if you want to speak, but wait until the chair invites you by name.

G · Game: Register Dominoes (C3 warmer)

One set of 12 per group of 3–4; shuffle and deal them out. The left half of each domino (teal) is a neutral phrase; the right half (blue) is a formal phrase. A player puts down any domino. The next player must place a domino whose teal half is the neutral partner of the blue half showing, and say the pair aloud ("regarding… about"). Can't go? Pass. The first player with no dominoes wins, but the group must finish the full circle: all twelve make a loop. Early finishers: make one email sentence with each pair, first formal, then neutral.

Teacher key (formal → neutral): I would be grateful if you could… → Could you…? · regarding → about · assistance → help · Please find attached… → I've attached… · at your earliest convenience → as soon as you can · I regret to inform you that… → I'm afraid… · Please do not hesitate to contact me. → Just let me know if you have any questions. · to purchase → to buy · Yours sincerely, → Kind regards, · Dear Ms. Alvarez, → Hi Sara, · to receive → to get · I apologize for the delay. → Sorry for the slow reply. Point out: the formal phrases aren't wrong; "Please do not hesitate…" and "I regret to inform you…" are just very formal and can sound cold in everyday email.

H · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher only)

Read at a natural speed. Where there are two speakers, change your voice a little or read with a confident learner. For message-taking scripts, read the first time at natural speed without stopping: that's the real skill. All scripts are original.

Meeting 1. Karen: Good morning. You must be Mr. Mendes? · Rafael: Yeah. Rafael. · Karen: I'm Karen Grant, operations manager here at Brightline Foods. Welcome. · Rafael: OK. So, I do logistics, deliveries, that kind of stuff. Where's the meeting room? I need a coffee first. · Karen: Oh… of course. It's this way. This is Dev. · Rafael: Hi. So, how long will this take?

Meeting 2. Karen: Good morning. You must be Mr. Mendes. · Rafael: Good morning. Yes, Rafael Mendes, from Northwind Logistics. And you must be Ms. Grant. It's a pleasure to meet you. · Karen: Likewise. Please, call me Karen. · Rafael: Thanks, Karen. And please call me Rafael. Thanks for making the time today. · Karen: Of course. Can I introduce you to my colleague? This is Dev Sharma, who leads our warehouse team. · Dev: Nice to meet you, Rafael. · Rafael: Nice to meet you too, Dev. I've heard great things about your team: your orders are always ready on time. I'm the account manager for Brightline, so I look after all your deliveries from our side. I'm really looking forward to working together. · Dev: Same here. · Karen: Great. Shall we go through to the meeting room? Can I get you a coffee on the way? · Rafael: That would be great, thank you.

Answers: Meeting 2 · Rafael's job: account manager for Brightline (he looks after their deliveries) · "Can I introduce you to my colleague?" · Dev leads the warehouse team · "Thanks, Karen. And please call me Rafael." Phrases heard: pleasure, Likewise, Thanks for making the time, I've heard great things (not "How do you do?" or "Have you two met?"). Ask: "In Meeting 2, when does Rafael start using 'Karen'?" (Only after she invites him.)

1.1-B · Polite or flat? (C2 tone stage · learners hold up P or F)

Read each line once, either with a warm rise-fall (P = polite) or in a low, flat voice (F = flat). Suggested order: 1. Could you send me the figures? (P) · 2. Would it be possible to meet on Friday? (F) · 3. I'm afraid that won't be possible. (P) · 4. Thanks for your help. (F) · 5. Nice to meet you. (F) · 6. Could you check this for me? (P)

Then read the F lines again politely and drill them. Ask: "Same words. Which one would you like to receive from a colleague?" The point: the words are correct in every line; the voice changed the message.

1.3-A · The call that went wrong (C4 lead-in · read once)

Receptionist: Hello? · Caller: Oh, hello. Is this Northwind Logistics? · Receptionist: Yeah. · Caller: This is Kim Tran from Brightline Foods. Could I speak to Tomasz Nowak, please? · Receptionist: [silence: count five seconds] … He's not here. · Caller: Oh. When will he be back? · Receptionist: Dunno. · Caller: Could you ask him to call me back? My number is three-one-two, five-five-five, oh-one-six-four. · Receptionist: Three-one-two, five-five-five, oh-one-six… OK, got it. Bye.

Problems to elicit: no company or name when answering · a silent hold with no signpost · "He's not here" and "Dunno": no information, no positive framing, no offer to take a message · no name, company or reason written down · a guessed, incomplete number with no read-back · an abrupt ending. Then: "Let's make every one of these right."
