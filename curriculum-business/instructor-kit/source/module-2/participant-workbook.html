Business English Workbook · Module 2

Meetings & Interpersonal Skills

Three lessons on speaking with people at work: taking part in meetings and chairing them, starting conversations and building professional relationships, and saying difficult things clearly and kindly. Then show what you can do in the Midpoint Performance Check.

Lessons 2.1–2.3 Classes C5–C8 Level B1+ Name: ____________________
How this module works

Each exercise heading tells you what to do: Listen, Read, Write, Say or With a partner. In every lesson you practice the phrases first, then use them in a role-play: a meeting, a networking event or a difficult conversation. You never need to talk about your real employer, your job situation or your salary. You can use your real job, a past job, or a role card with a fictional job and company. It is your choice.

One idea connects the whole module: acknowledge first, then act. Show people you have heard them before you say your own point.

2.1

Meetings — Participating & Chairing

Class C5
Read and say — Key words
  • chair — the person who runs the meeting (also a verb: to chair a meeting)
  • participant — a person who takes part (also attendee)
  • agenda — the list of topics, sent before the meeting
  • item — one topic on the agenda
  • minutes — the written record of a meeting
  • action point — who does what by when
  • AOB — "any other business": the last agenda item
  • on track — going as planned, on time
  • park (a topic) — stop discussing it now, come back later
  • take it offline — discuss it after the meeting, with fewer people
  • circulate — send a document to everyone
  • sign-off — final approval
Say — Key phrases

Give your opinion

In my view, …It seems to me that…I'd suggest (that) we…From my perspective, …

Agree

Absolutely.That's a good point.I'm with you on that.I completely agree.

Disagree politely (acknowledge, then differ)

I see your point, but…I'm not sure I agree.Have we considered…?I take your point, though I'd add…Could I offer a different view?

Ask for clarification · check understanding

Could you clarify what you mean by…?Sorry, could you expand on that?Just so I'm clear, are you saying…?So the plan is to…, is that right?

Turn-taking: interrupt, hold the floor, bring others in

Sorry to interrupt, but…Can I just come in here?Just to build on that…If I could just finish…What do you think, Sara?

Chair the meeting

Shall we get started?The purpose of today's meeting is…First on the agenda is…Let's move on to…Let's park that and take it offline.So, to summarize, …Are we all agreed?To recap the action points, …

2.2

Small Talk, Networking & Building Rapport

Class C6
Read and say — Key words
  • small talk — friendly, light conversation
  • rapport — a good, comfortable connection with someone
  • networking — meeting people to build professional contacts
  • opener — the first line of a conversation
  • open question — a question that needs more than "yes" or "no"
  • free information — extra detail you give, so the other person has something to ask about
  • common ground — something you share (a city, a role, a challenge, a hobby)
  • mingle — move around and talk to different people at an event
  • exit line — a polite way to end a conversation
  • follow up — contact someone again after meeting them (noun: a follow-up)
  • stay in touch — keep contact
  • turnout — the number of people who come to an event
Say — Key phrases

Open the conversation (shared context)

Great turnout, isn't it?How are you finding the conference so far?Have you been to one of these before?What did you think of the keynote?How do you know the host?

Keep it going (the rapport engine)

Oh really?That sounds interesting — tell me more.How did you get into that?What was that like?That must be challenging.Same here, actually!

Introduce yourself · join a group · exchange contacts

I don't think we've met — I'm…Mind if I join you?Do you have a card?What's the best way to reach you?Let me give you my details.

Exit gracefully

It's been great talking — I should go and say hello to a few others.I won't keep you.Let's stay in touch.I'm going to grab another coffee, but it was really good to meet you.
Language box — questions that keep a conversation going
Closed → openAre you enjoying it? (→ "Yes.") → What are you enjoying most so far? · Start with How, What, Which, Why.
How are you finding…?= What's your opinion of it so far? · How are you finding the new job / the city / the conference?
Tags for agreementGreat turnout, isn't it? · It's busy today, isn't it? · Your voice goes down at the end: you expect "Yes, it is!"
Free informationanswer + extra + question back: "I'm an accountant — I mostly work with small businesses, which keeps it interesting. How about you?"
ExperienceHave you been to one of these before? · I've worked in purchasing for six years / since 2019. (not: I am working here since six years)
Same idea, three registersChat: "Let's stay in touch!" · Follow-up email: "I'd love to stay in touch." · Formal letter: "I would welcome the opportunity to remain in contact." (too formal for networking)

Read and say — Key words
  • passive — not saying what you need; you lose
  • aggressive — blunt or rude; the other person loses
  • assertive — honest and respectful; the goal
  • tactful / diplomatic — careful not to upset people
  • soften — make a message gentler, not weaker
  • hedge — use I think, perhaps, might to sound open
  • push back — politely disagree with a plan or request
  • constructive feedback — specific comments that help someone improve
  • at capacity — completely busy; no time for more
  • trade-off — to get one thing, you give up another
  • take (something) on board — accept it and think about it
  • de-escalate — make a tense situation calmer
Language box — the softening toolkit

Stay firm on substance (the fact, the reason, the proposal) and soft on delivery. Use one or two softeners, not all of them.

MoveFormExample
HedgingI think · perhaps · might / couldI think we might be underestimating the timeline.
Qualifiersa slight · a little · not quiteI have a slight concern about the budget.
Question formsWould it be possible to + verb? · Have we considered + -ing / noun?Would it be possible to push it a week?
Distance (past forms)I was wondering if we could… · I was hoping…I was hoping we could revisit the deadline.
Positive framingsay what you can doI can definitely get you the draft by Thursday.
Prefacingacknowledge firstI take your point, but I'd suggest…
FeedbackSituation (when / where) → Behavior (what happened) → Impact (the effect) + one honest positive + a question
Saying no1 an alternative (I can't…, but I could…) · 2 a trade-off (Which task should slip?) · 3 buy time (Let me check and get back to you.)
Conflictacknowledge the feeling → the problem, not the person → common ground → a way forward
Say — Key phrases

Disagree and push back

I see it a little differently…Can I offer another perspective?I hear you, but I have some concerns about…I'd push back gently on that, because…Help me understand the thinking here.

Give feedback · receive feedback

Is now a good time for a bit of feedback?One thing that worked really well was…One thing I'd suggest for next time…How do you feel it went?Thanks for telling me.Could you give me an example?Let me take that on board.

Say no · calm things down

I'd love to help, but I'm at capacity right now.I'm afraid I can't…, but I could…That won't be possible by Friday — would next week work?I understand this is frustrating.Let's focus on the problem, not who's at fault.We both want the same outcome here.

MPC

Midpoint Performance Check — Prepare & Reflect

Classes C8–C9
Read — What happens in C8
  • Part A · Writing (35 minutes, everyone together): Task 1, reply to a client's email and include every point from your manager's notes (about 25 minutes). Task 2, rewrite three messages so each one is in the right register for its reader (about 10 minutes).
  • Part B · Speaking (about 7 minutes, in pairs, at your teacher's table): two short phone calls (you call once and answer once) and a short meeting. You read each card for a few seconds before you start. All names and companies on the cards are fictional.
  • While you wait: a quiet reading and ranking task. It is not scored.
  • Results: two scores, Writing /20 and Speaking /20, given back privately at the start of C9. It's a check-up, not a final exam.
