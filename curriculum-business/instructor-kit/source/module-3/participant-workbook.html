Business English Workbook · Module 3

Presenting, Negotiating & Persuading

Three lessons, three speaking skills. First, inform: give a talk that people can follow easily, because you tell them where you are at every step. Then, trade: find out what the other side really needs and exchange concessions with "If you…, we'll…". Finally, persuade: turn features into benefits, give honest evidence, ask for one clear action and stay calm when someone says "no."

How this module works

Each exercise heading tells you what to do: Read, Write, Say, Listen or With a partner. Every class ends with a short simulation in a small group: a mini-presentation, a negotiation, a pitch. Nobody has to speak in front of the whole class in this module.

You always choose what to share. For any presentation, negotiation or pitch, you can use your real work, or a role profile from your teacher (a fictional person, job and company with ready-made facts and figures). Never share confidential information, salaries or the name of a real employer. A role profile is not "less real" practice: the language is exactly the same.

3.2

Negotiations — Language & Strategy

Class C10
Read and say — Key words
  • a position — what a person says they want ("We need 15% off.")
  • an interest — why they want it (a budget limit, a deadline, a risk)
  • a concession — something you give up to move the deal forward
  • a trade-off — giving one thing to get another
  • your BATNA / walk-away point — your best plan if there is no deal
  • a counter-proposal — a new offer after you reject theirs
  • deadlock — when both sides are stuck and nobody moves
  • win–win — a deal where both sides get enough to say yes
  • terms — the details of a deal (price, dates, payment)
  • to waive a fee — to not charge it
  • to commit to — to promise to do something (a contract, an order)
  • to label a concession — to say clearly that it has value ("That's a big step for us.")
Read and say — Key phrases

Proposing and accepting

Read and say — Model dialogue: twenty desks

Hana Ito is the office manager at Kestrel Design. Rafael Costa sells office furniture for Maple & Stone Furniture.

Rafael: Thanks for making the time, Hana. I'm sure we can find something that works for both of us.

Hana: I hope so. I'll be honest: we need all twenty desks by the 1st, and your quote says the 15th.

Rafael: I see. Can I ask what's driving the 1st?

Hana: Our new team starts on the 1st. Twelve people, and right now they have nowhere to sit.

Rafael: That's really helpful to know. So it's twelve desks that are urgent, not all twenty?

Hana: Exactly. The other eight are for people who join later in the year.

Rafael: Then here's an idea. If you can accept two deliveries, we'll send twelve desks by the 1st and the other eight on the 15th.

Hana: That works. But two deliveries means two delivery charges, doesn't it?

Rafael: Normally, yes. I'm afraid we can't cover both, because our drivers are fully booked that week. What we could offer instead is half price on the second delivery.

Hana: Could you do a little better? Our budget is very tight this quarter.

Rafael: I can move a little, but I'll need something in return. If you pay within 14 days, I'll waive the second charge completely. That's a big step for us, honestly.

Hana: Fourteen days is fine. I think we have a deal.

Rafael: Wonderful. So, to confirm: twelve desks on the 1st, eight on the 15th, no charge for the second delivery, and payment within 14 days. Have I got that right?

Hana: That's everything.

Rafael: Great. I'll send the details by email this afternoon. It's a pleasure working with you.

3.3

Pitching, Persuasion & Handling Objections

Class C11
Read and say — Key words
  • a pitch — a short talk to get someone to say yes to an idea, product or request
  • a hook — an opening that makes the listener want to hear more
  • a pain point — a problem the listener really feels
  • a feature — what something is or has ("It works offline.")
  • a benefit — what it does for the listener ("You never lose your work.")
  • evidence / social proof — results, figures or other users that show it works
  • a call to action — the one clear next step you ask for
  • an objection — a reason the listener gives for not saying yes (yet)
  • a pilot / a trial — a small, short test before a full decision
  • ROI (return on investment) — what you get back compared with what you pay
  • an elevator pitch — a 30–60 second version of your pitch
  • to overpromise — to promise more than you can deliver
