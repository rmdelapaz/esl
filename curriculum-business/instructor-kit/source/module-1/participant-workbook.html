Four classes to give you a clear starting point and the three tools you use every working day. First, find out what you need English for at work and set your goals. Then learn to control register and tone, write clear professional emails, and handle phone and video calls with confidence.

How this module works

Each exercise heading tells you what to do: Read, Write, Listen, Say, With a partner or In groups. In role-plays, you practice real work situations, then get feedback on four things: Task & Purpose, Register & Tone, Language Range & Accuracy, and Organization & Delivery.

You never have to share personal information: your employer, your job, your salary or your status. When a task asks about your work, use your real job, or a role card from the side table, or a job you invent. All three are equally good.

Business Register, Tone & First Impressions

Class C2
Read and say — Key phrases
  • register
  • tone
  • formal
  • neutral
  • friendly-professional
  • courteous
  • blunt
  • a softener
  • to hedge
  • positive framing
  • a first impression
  • rapport
  • an elevator pitch
  • to mirror (the room)

register = how formal your language is · tone = the attitude your words show (warm, firm, unsure…) · courteous = polite and respectful · blunt = too direct, so it sounds rude · to hedge = to make something softer or less certain (just, perhaps, a little) · rapport = a good, friendly working relationship · an elevator pitch = a short introduction to you and your work · to mirror = to match the formality of the people around you

Language box — The register spectrum
RegisterWhen to use itMaking a request
Formalfirst contact, senior or external people, official documents, bad newsI would be grateful if you could send me the report by Friday.
Neutral (the safe default)most everyday work; colleagues you know a littleCould you send me the report by Friday, please?
Friendly-professionalclose colleagues; a warm, established relationshipCould you send the report over by Friday? Thanks!
Too casual (avoid at work)—send me the report by fri
More formalI would like… · do not, cannot · request, assistance, regarding · Dear Ms. Alvarez, … Sincerely,
Less formalI'd like… · don't, can't · ask, help, about · Hi Sara, … Best,

Three softeners (one is usually enough): 1. A modal question: Could you…? · Would you be able to…? · Would it be possible to…? 2. A hedge: just · a little · perhaps · when you get a chance 3. Positive framing (say what you can do): I can have it ready by Monday. (not only I can't do it today.)

Careful: "Could you possibly maybe send it sometime if that's OK?" has too many softeners. It sounds unsure. Be polite and clear.

Professional Emails & Messaging

Class C3
Read and say — Key phrases
  • a subject line
  • a greeting
  • a sign-off
  • the ask
  • a deadline
  • attached
  • to follow up
  • to chase (politely)
  • CC / BCC
  • Reply / Reply All
  • a thread
  • FYI

the ask = what you want the reader to do · to follow up / to chase = to write again because you haven't had an answer · CC = people who need to know but don't need to act · BCC = hidden copy: other readers can't see these addresses · a thread = a chain of messages on one topic · FYI = for your information (no action needed)

Language box — The email and its frame

Seven parts, in this order: 1 subject line (clear and specific) → 2 greeting → 3 opening line (your reason) → 4 body (one purpose + a clear ask with a deadline) → 5 closing line (the next step) → 6 sign-off (matches the greeting) → 7 name and signature.

RegisterGreetingSign-off
FormalDear Mr. / Ms. + family name, · Dear Sir or Madam,Sincerely, (US) · Yours sincerely, (UK, named person) · Yours faithfully, (UK, after "Dear Sir or Madam")
NeutralDear Sara, · Hi Sara,Kind regards, · Best regards,
Friendly-professionalHi Sara, · Hello Sara,Best, · Thanks,

Note: In modern email, "Dear Mr. Chen, … Kind regards," or "Best regards," is also very common and polite. Never mix a formal greeting with a very casual sign-off ("Dear Sir … Cheers!").

OpeningI'm writing to confirm / ask about / let you know… · Thank you for your email of March 3. · Further to our conversation, …
RequestCould you please send me…? · I would be grateful if you could… · Would it be possible to…? · I'd appreciate it if you could confirm by Friday.
AttachmentPlease find attached the report. · I've attached the figures for your review.
Following upI'm just following up on my email below. · As a gentle reminder, the deadline is Friday.
Bad newsUnfortunately, we won't be able to… · I'm afraid there's been a delay. · Please accept my apologies for…
ClosingPlease let me know if you have any questions. · I look forward to hearing from you. · Thanks in advance for your help.
Read — A model email

Subject: Training room booking for March 18 — confirmation needed by March 6

Dear Ms. Grant,

Thank you for sending your conference brochure last week.

We would like to book your Harbor Room for a one-day training session on Wednesday, March 18, for 20 people. I would be grateful if you could confirm availability and the full price, including lunch and coffee breaks, by Friday, March 6.

Please find attached our draft schedule for the day.

Please let me know if you need any further details. I look forward to hearing from you.

Sincerely,
Sofia Brandt
Office Manager, Kestrel Design

Telephoning & Virtual Calls

Class C4
Read and say — Key phrases
  • to put someone through
  • to hold (the line)
  • to take a message
  • to leave a message
  • to call someone back
  • to read back
  • Bear with me.
  • to spell
  • a callback
  • on mute
  • to break up
  • to share your screen

to put someone through = to connect a caller to another person · to hold = to wait on the phone · to read back = to repeat details to check them · Bear with me. = Please wait a moment. · to break up = (sound) to keep cutting out on a bad connection

Language box — The call, stage by stage
StageReceiverCaller
1 Answer / identifyGood morning, Northwind Logistics, Ana speaking. How can I help?Hello, this is Rafael Mendes from Brightline Foods.
2 PurposeMay I ask what it's regarding?I'm calling about… · Could I speak to…, please? · Is this a good time to talk?
3 Connect / holdI'll put you through. · Could you hold the line, please? · Bear with me a moment. · Thanks for holding.Sure, thanks.
4 UnavailableI'm afraid she's in a meeting / not at her desk / away until Monday. Can I take a message?When is a good time to call back?
5 MessageCan I take your name and number? · Let me read that back to you…Could you ask her to call me back at (312) 555-0147? · Could you let him know that…?
6 EndI'll make sure she gets the message. Thanks for calling.Thanks for your help. Have a good day.

Say it clearly. Phone numbers in groups: three-one-two · five-five-five · oh-one-four-seven. Two of the same: double seven. 0 = oh or zero. 15 or 50? Say fifteen, one-five. Dates: the fourteenth, one-four. Email: s dot brandt at kestreldesign dot com.

Spell with words: A Alpha · B Bravo · C Charlie · D Delta · E Echo · F Foxtrot · G Golf · H Hotel · I India · J Juliet · K Kilo · L Lima · M Mike · N November · O Oscar · P Papa · Q Quebec · R Romeo · S Sierra · T Tango · U Uniform · V Victor · W Whiskey · X X-ray · Y Yankee · Z Zulu. Any clear word is fine: "D as in David."
