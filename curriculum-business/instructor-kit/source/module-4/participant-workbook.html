Business Writing & Career

Three lessons and the Final Assessment. Write reports and proposals that a busy reader can act on. Present your experience with a strong CV, a targeted cover letter and clear interview answers. Then bring the whole course together: adapt to different workplace cultures, rehearse a complete client project, and prepare your capstone presentation.

How this module works

Each exercise heading tells you what to do: Write, Listen, Say, Read or With a partner. In business writing and speaking there is often more than one good answer. What matters is that your message is clear, has the right tone and gets the result you want.

When a task asks about your own work history, you decide what to share. Use your real experience, or a role card (a fictional person with a fictional job and company), or a mix. You never need to name a real employer or share your salary or any private information. You're practicing the English, not handing in your life story.

4.1

Reports, Proposals & Business Documents

Class C12
Read and say — Key vocabulary
  • a report
    presents findings: what happened, what the data shows
  • a proposal
    persuades someone to approve a plan
  • an executive summary
    a short summary at the start, for busy readers
  • findings
    what you discovered from the evidence
  • conclusions
    what the findings mean
  • recommendations
    the actions you advise
  • an appendix (plural: appendices)
    extra detail at the end
  • scope
    what the document covers (and doesn't)
  • objective (adj.)
    based on facts, not feelings
  • to hedge
    to make a claim more careful: This suggests…
  • to prioritize
    to put things in order of importance
  • a call to action
    the clear thing you ask the reader to do

Email register → document register. Documents are more formal and objective than most emails. Clear is better than impressive.

Email style (often)Document style
I think we lost customers because…The data indicates that customer loss was driven by…
We found out that…It was found that… / The analysis shows that…
I'm pretty sure this means…This suggests that… / It appears that…
You should hire more people.We recommend hiring two more agents. / It is recommended that the team be expanded.
contractions, I / you, chatty wordsfull forms, impersonal phrases, neutral tone

Making recommendations.

recommend + -ingWe recommend hiring two agents by the end of Q3.
recommend / propose that + base formIt is recommended that the contract be renewed. · We propose that the deadline be moved to June 30.
should = firmWe should prioritize the billing fix.
could = an optionThe team could also consider a weekend shift.
priorityAs a priority, … · In the short term, …; in the longer term, …

An approvable recommendation is: Specific · Actionable (who, what, by when) · Prioritized · Cost/benefit-aware · Tied to the findings.

Read — Model: an executive summary
Northwind Logistics · Operations Team · April 12

Late Deliveries in the Northern Region, January–March

Executive Summary

This report examines the causes of late deliveries in the Northern Region in the first quarter and recommends a response. Late deliveries rose from 6% to 14% of all orders. It was found that two causes account for most of the delay: routes are still planned by hand, and one of the three vans was out of service for five weeks. Notably, 70% of late orders were on Monday and Tuesday routes. We recommend three actions: (1) introduce the route-planning software already used in the Southern Region by the end of May; (2) lease a replacement van for the busy early-week routes; and (3) review driver schedules in the third quarter. Together, these steps are expected to bring late deliveries back below 6% by July.

1. If the director reads only this, what can she approve?

2. Which word introduces the most striking finding?

3. Find one passive structure.

4. Why are the recommendations numbered?
