Welcome to Business English

Welcome, and thank you for joining us. This course is about using the English you already have at work: writing clear emails and messages, handling phone and video calls, taking part in meetings, giving your opinion politely, presenting, negotiating, and applying for jobs. Every class is a practical workshop. You will hear a model, learn the key phrases, practice them out loud, and then use them in a realistic role-play.

Some of you use English at work every day; some of you are looking for a new job; some of you had a successful career in another country and are rebuilding it here. Your experience and your qualifications are valued in this room. Everyone starts from a different place, and everyone is measured against their own starting point, not against the person next to them.

🤝 How a class works

Link to your work→See a model→Notice the key phrases→Say them out loud→Practice on paper→Role-play→Feedback
  • 🕐Please arrive on time. We start with a short warm-up that reviews the last class.
  • 📵Keep your phone on silent, and use it for class tasks only.
  • 🎭Role-plays are practice, not a performance. Everyone plays roles, and everyone makes mistakes.
  • 📝Your teacher notes mistakes during a role-play and discusses them afterwards, without names.
  • 🌍Workplaces differ. We compare what is usual in different places, and no way is "wrong".
  • 📩If you can't come, please let your teacher know, and do the online lesson at home.

🔒 Your private information stays private: the role-card option

You never have to share your salary, whether you are working, your immigration status, or the name of your employer. For any task about your job or career, you can choose a role card: a fictional job at a fictional company (for example, a project coordinator at Northwind Logistics). Use your real situation or a role card, whichever you prefer, and you can change your mind at any time. Your teacher marks your English, not your story. The same is true for your CV and interview practice in Week 7.

✅ How your work is marked

Every email, call, meeting, presentation, and interview in this course is marked with the same four criteria. Each one gets a score from 1 to 5, so the total is out of 20.

CriterionIn plain words, the question is…
Task & PurposeDid you do the job? Did you cover all the points, and is it clear what happens next?
Register & ToneIs it right for the person and the situation: polite, not too formal and not too casual, diplomatic when it needs to be?
Language Range & AccuracyDid you use useful work phrases, correct grammar, and the right words?
Organization & DeliveryIs it easy to follow? A clear structure, signposts, and, when you speak, clear pronunciation and good listening.
1Not yet: the message is hard to understand
2Partly there: some points are missing or unclear
3Gets the job done, with some problems
4Clear and appropriate, with small slips
5Confident and professional

You get scores in Class 1 (your starting point), Class 8 (the midpoint check), and Classes 15–16 (the final). Write them on your performance tracker. Your scores are private: you decide whether to share them.

🏠 At home: free online lessons

📱 Business English: free

Each of the 12 lessons we teach in class has a matching online lesson with the phrase banks, model emails and conversations, and exercises with answers. It's your homework, and it helps you catch up if you miss a class. It works on a phone, a tablet, or a computer.

rays-esl.netlify.app/curriculum-business/

  2. Choose the lesson your teacher gives you (for example, Lesson 1.2).
  3. Press the 🔊 Listen buttons to hear the model sentences. Say each line with the audio, copying the stress and the polite, friendly intonation. (No sound? Turn up the volume, or try the Chrome browser.)

Aim for 45–60 minutes, 2 times a week. Translation help and free practice tools: rays-esl.netlify.app/curriculum/learning-tools.html

The class and the online lessons both use American English (CV and résumé mean the same document). British forms are correct too: choose one style and keep it consistent.

📈 What to expect: realistic progress

  • In eight weeks, most learners notice the biggest change in confidence and in register and tone: knowing which phrase fits which situation. These changes show quickly.
  • Grammar accuracy and fluency improve more slowly, because they depend on habits built over months. That is normal.
  • A gain of 2–4 points out of 20 between Class 1 and the final is a good result. Some learners gain more; nobody can promise a number.
  • No course can guarantee a job or a promotion. This one gives you tools for emails, calls, meetings, and interviews that you keep using.
