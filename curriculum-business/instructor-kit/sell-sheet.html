Teach Business English: Workplace Communication for Working Adults

Business English is a complete 8-week classroom course for working adults at B1 and above: employees who use English on the job, job seekers, and newcomers rebuilding a professional career. Learners practice the communication that decides how they are seen at work: emails and messages, calls, meetings, small talk, diplomatic feedback, presentations, negotiations, reports, CVs, and job interviews. Every class is a workshop planned to the minute: model, key phrases, spoken drill, practice, role-play or simulation, and feedback on register, tone, and clarity. It works for a trained ESL teacher and for a well-prepared volunteer; no business background required.

What You Get

For every module (×4)
  • Teacher's Guide: one 90-minute plan per class with a board plan, a timed stage table, concept-check and instruction-check questions, drill scripts for stress and polite intonation, role-play management, support and stretch tasks, and cross-cultural watch-outs
  • Student Workbook: clear B1/B2 English, phrase banks, formal-neutral-informal language boxes, model emails and dialogues, rewrite-for-tone exercises, role-plays, "I can…" checklists, and a journal
  • Answer Key: every item answered, with model emails and documents, acceptable alternatives, and the errors to expect
  • Slide deck and classroom materials: A/B role-play and information-gap cards, read-aloud listening scripts (voicemails, meetings, pitches), phrase cards, and a game
Across the kit
  • An 8-week, 16-class syllabus in web and print versions
  • Needs analysis & diagnostic (Class 1): a workplace-needs survey, a reply email, and a speaking sample
  • Midpoint performance check (Class 8): timed email and message writing, plus phone-call and meeting simulations
  • Final assessment (Classes 15–16): a proposal or report section, a cover letter or formal email, a negotiation, a capstone presentation or pitch, and a mock job interview
  • An assessment key with the shared rubric, model answers, role cards, and scoring sheets
  • A learner welcome handout, a one-page performance tracker, and the three combined, print-ready books

Workplace & WIOA-Friendly Outcomes

  • Employability skills, built in. A CV and cover letter, STAR interview answers, and a mock interview in the final week support workforce-preparation and job-search goals.
  • Evidence of progress. The same four-criterion rubric (Task & Purpose, Register & Tone, Language Range & Accuracy, Organization & Delivery) is scored at the start, midpoint, and end, giving learners, teachers, and funders a clear pre/mid/post record. It complements, and does not replace, your required standardized assessment.
  • Useful to employers. Incumbent-worker groups practice the exact tasks of the job: status updates, chairing a meeting, handling a delay with a client, saying no politely, and presenting a proposal.
  • Respectful by design. Learners never have to disclose salary, employment or immigration status, or an employer; every personal task offers a fictional role card. Foreign credentials are treated as assets, and cross-cultural notes describe tendencies, never stereotypes. All companies and figures are fictional.
