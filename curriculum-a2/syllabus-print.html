Instructor-Led Course Syllabus

Everyday English (A2 Elementary)

A 13-week, 26-class English course for adults who know the basics. Twice a week for 90 minutes, you learn to tell stories about your past, talk about your experiences and plans, and handle everyday situations: directions and tickets, restaurants and stores, and the doctor’s office. Between classes, the free online lessons (with audio) are your homework.

Class Schedule

Two classes a week, 90 minutes each. Every class has one lesson from the online course, except the two assessment classes. Lessons 4.3 and 8.3 are review projects that bring each half of the course together in a real-world story. ✓ = the can-do goal for that class.

Week 1

Talking About the Past

Module 1: was / were and regular past verbs, for talking about yesterday and last weekend.
C11.1Past Simple — Was & Werewas / were · wasn’t / weren’t · yesterday, last week, ago · there was / there were✓ I can say where I was and how it was.
C21.2Past Simple — Regular Verbs (-ed)-ed spelling rules · three sounds: /t/ /d/ /ɪd/ · a past day✓ I can describe what I did yesterday.
Week 2

Past Stories & Comparing

Modules 1–2: irregular verbs, did / didn’t, and comparing things.
C31.3Past Simple — Irregular Verbs & Questionswent, had, saw, bought… · didn’t + base verb · Did you…? · What did you do?✓ I can tell a short story about a day or a trip.
C42.1Comparatives & Superlativescheaper than · more expensive · the best · (not) as … as✓ I can compare places, prices, and things.
Week 3

How Much & How Often

Module 2: quantities, and saying how often and how well you do things.
C52.2Quantifiers — some, any, much, manycountable / uncountable · a lot of · a few / a little · How much? How many?✓ I can talk about what’s in my kitchen and make a shopping list.
C62.3Adverbs of Frequency & Manneralways → never · once / twice a week · quickly, carefully · good vs well✓ I can describe my habits and how I do things.
Week 4

Plans & Predictions

Module 3: two ways to talk about the future.
C73.1“Going to” — Plans & Intentionsam / is / are going to · plans vs predictions from evidence · next week, tonight✓ I can talk about my plans for the weekend.
C83.2“Will” & “Might” — Predictions & Decisionswill / won’t · instant decisions, offers, promises · might · will vs going to✓ I can make predictions and offer to help.
Week 5

Arrangements & Experiences

Modules 3–4: making plans with other people, and your life experiences.
C93.3Future Arrangements & Making SuggestionsI’m meeting Sara on Friday · Let’s… · How about -ing? · accepting & declining✓ I can make a plan with a friend and agree on a day and time.
C104.1Present Perfect — Life Experienceshave / has + past participle · ever / never · been vs gone · vs past simple✓ I can ask “Have you ever…?” and talk about my experiences.
Week 6

Course Details

Prerequisites

  • CEFR A1. You can introduce yourself, talk about your family and your daily routine, and use the present simple, can, and there is / there are.
  • Finished English Foundations (A1)? You’re ready. New to our program? Take the shared placement test first.
  • Learners who are still building basic reading and writing are welcome. Every lesson has literacy-support options, and the tests have an adapted version.
  • No computer needed in class. A phone helps for homework, but it isn’t required.

What’s Provided

  • A Student Workbook for all 24 lessons, in clear A2 English with icon instructions (the learner keeps it)
  • A welcome handout with class rules, what to bring, and QR codes to the online lessons
  • For the teacher: a Teacher’s Guide (a 90-minute plan for every class), slides, picture flashcards, role-play cards, listening scripts, games, and answer keys
  • Free access to the companion online course: Everyday English (A2), with 🔊 audio
  • A free learning-tools page that shows how to translate lessons into any language

Assessment

  • Every class: an “I can…” checklist and short speaking checks, with no grades
  • Class 13 · Progress Test 1 (Modules 1–4): listening, vocabulary, grammar, reading, short writing tasks, and a 6-minute pair speaking task
  • Class 26 · Final Test (all 8 modules): the same skills plus an individual speaking interview with a clinic, restaurant, or station role-play
  • Rubrics follow the CEFR A2 descriptors. Results decide the next class, never who gets to celebrate
  • Tasks about your life can always use a “new identity” or an imagined story instead of real details

Homework & the Online Course

  • After each class, do the matching online lesson at home: about 20–30 minutes, with 🔊 Listen buttons for model sentences and dialogues
  • Plus one workbook page to review the class
  • Missed a class? The online lesson covers the same language, so you can catch up
  • After the course: Independent English (B1) continues the same free ladder, from A1 all the way to C2

What You’ll Be Able to Do

By the end of the course, learners reach CEFR A2 and can use English for everyday life:

Tell a story about yesterday, a trip, or something that happened
Describe your childhood and how your life used to be different
Talk about your experiences and how long you have lived, worked, or studied somewhere
Compare places, prices, and things, and say how much and how often
Make plans, suggestions, predictions, and promises
Explain rules and give simple advice
Ask for and follow directions, and buy a travel ticket
Book a table, order a meal, and return something to a store
Call a clinic, make an appointment, and explain a health problem
Join your ideas with and, but, so, because, and although
