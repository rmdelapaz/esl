Module 1 — Talking About the Past: Answers

Answers to every Module 1 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (information gap, -ed sound sort, picture stories, memory game), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 1.1–1.3Classes C1–C39 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At A2, mark meaning first, then form. A past sentence with the right verb and a small spelling slip ("stoped") is a success with a note. A missing past form ("Yesterday I go") or "didn't went" is the error to correct, because it is the point of the module. American English is the model; British forms (film, flat, shop, on holiday, travelled) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

1.1 · Was & Were (C1)

Welcome box — This is me (open)

Personal; do not mark. Use it with the A1 diagnostic: look for I'm + job/noun with an article (I'm a cook), I like + noun or -ing, I usually + base verb (no -s with I), I can + base verb (not "can to"). A new-identity answer is completely fine.

Exercise 1 — was or were?

1 was · 2 were · 3 was · 4 were · 5 were (you = always were) · 6 was (my sister = she)

Exercise 2 — Negative, then question
  1. They weren't at the party. / Were they at the party?
  2. It wasn't cold yesterday. / Was it cold yesterday?
  3. You weren't tired. / Were you tired?
  4. The store wasn't open. / Was the store open?

Also correct: full forms were not / was not. Wrong: "Did they were…?", "They didn't be…", "Was they…?", "The store was not open?" (statement order with a question mark: accept in speech with rising intonation, but not in this exercise).

Exercise 3 — Listen: How was your weekend? (Script 1.1-A)

1 T · 2 F (it was very busy) · 3 T · 4 T · 5 F (she was at her sister's house in the mountains) · 6 T (cold and rainy)

Exercise 4 — Circle the right words

1 last Monday · 2 two hours ago · 3 yesterday · 4 There were (a lot of people = plural) · 5 There was (a café = one) · 6 Were there (tables = plural)

Watch for: "the last Monday," "ago two hours," "last yesterday." There was a lot of people is heard in casual speech, but teach There were for plurals.

Exercise 5 — About you: yesterday (open)

Check: was with I/he/she/it, were with we/they/you, a time or place in each sentence, and an adjective for "how." Model: At 8 a.m. I was at the bus stop. It was cold. At noon I was at work. I was busy. In the evening I was at home with my kids. We were tired but happy.

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) They was happy.
B) They were happy.
C) They is happy.
Why: they takes were. C is present (and wrong: they are).
Q2
"___ you at the meeting?" — "No, I ___."
A) Were … wasn't
B) Was … weren't
C) Was … wasn't
Why: the question uses you → were; the answer uses I → wasn't. The person changes, so the verb changes.
Q3 · new
I was at the doctor's ___.
A) ago two days
B) last two days
C) two days ago
Why: ago comes after a length of time. Last goes before one time word (last Monday), not a number of days. A is a common Spanish/Portuguese transfer (hace dos días).
Errors you will hear most (1.1)

"You was / They was…" · "Yesterday I tired." (no be) · "Yesterday I am at home." (present form) · "Did you were…?" · "Yes, I'm." / "Yes, I's." (short answer) · "ago two days" / "the last week" · "It had many people." (for There were) · "I was at home in last weekend." Correct the first four; recast the rest.

Self-check
Q1
What is the past of study?
A) studyed
B) studied
C) studdied
Why: consonant (d) + y → change y to -ied. We don't double the d.
Q2
Which verb has an extra beat (/ɪd/)?
A) worked
B) played
C) wanted
Why: want ends in a t sound, so we add a syllable: want-ed. Worked and played stay one beat.
Q3 · new
Which verb ends with a /t/ sound?
A) played
B) cleaned
C) watched
Why: watch ends in /tʃ/, a sound with no buzz, so the ending is /t/. Play (vowel) and clean (n) are voiced → /d/.
Errors you will hear most (1.2)

"work-ed / play-ed" (extra syllable) · "Yesterday I walk to the park." (no ending) · "She workeds." · "studyed / plaied / stoped" · "wanted" said as one syllable "want" · "I didn't worked" (early use of didn't: note it for C3) · played / prayed (l/r). The first two matter most for being understood.

1.3 · Irregular Verbs, didn't & Did…? (C3)

Exercise 1 — Write the past form

1 went · 2 saw · 3 bought · 4 took · 5 ate · 6 thought · 7 gave · 8 told · 9 made · 10 drank · 11 met · 12 wrote

Watch for: "buyed/boughted," "brought" for bought, "taked," "eated," "thinked," "drinked/drunk," "writed." A regularized form ("goed") shows the learner knows the rule. Praise that, then give the form.

Exercise 2 — Make it "No"
  1. She didn't have breakfast.
  2. They didn't go to the beach.
  3. We didn't watch TV.
  4. He didn't buy a ticket.
  5. I didn't eat lunch at work.

Also correct: did not. Wrong: "didn't had," "didn't went," "didn't watched," "doesn't buy" (present), "wasn't eat."

Exercise 3 — Did-questions

a.

  1. Did they see the show?
  2. Did she make dinner?
  3. Did you meet your friend?
  4. Did he read the email?
  5. Did you take the bus?

b. (Script 1.3-B) Personal answers. Correct form: Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. For question 5 (What did you eat…?) a short answer is fine: "Rice and fish." / "I ate rice and fish." Wrong: "Yes, I ate." · "No, I didn't eat." (acceptable but long) · "Yes, I do."

Exercise 4 — Listen: Market day (Script 1.3-A)

1 T · 2 F (they took the bus) · 3 T · 4 F (Dina didn't buy anything) · 5 F (at a small restaurant) · 6 T

Stretch corrections: They didn't take a taxi. · Dina didn't buy any fruit. · They didn't eat lunch at home.

Exercise 5 — Word order + was/were or did?

1 What did you do last weekend? · 2 Where did they go? · 3 Who did you meet? · 4 Were (be + at home) · 5 Did (action verb go) · 6 Were (be + tired)

Also correct for 1: Last weekend, what did you do? Wrong: "What you did…?", "Where they went?", "Who you met?"

Speaking — Weekend mingle + one-minute story (open)

Mingle notes can be 2–3 words. In the report, check: he/she + past verb ("She went…", not "She goes / She go"), one didn't + base. Story checklist (from the online lesson): 5+ past verbs; regular and irregular; one didn't + base; one did-question answered. Model: Last summer I went to the lake with my cousins. We took an early bus and arrived at nine. The weather was perfect. We swam, and we ate chicken and rice under a tree. I didn't bring a hat, so my face was very red! Did we have a good time? Yes, we did.

Irregular-verb memory game (classroom materials)

Main 16 pairs: go–went · have–had · do–did · see–saw · get–got · make–made · come–came · take–took · eat–ate · drink–drank · buy–bought · give–gave · find–found · meet–met · write–wrote · think–thought. Bonus 4: know–knew · say–said · tell–told · read–read.

Self-check
Q1
What is the past of buy?
A) buyed
B) bought
C) boughted
Why: buy is irregular: bought, with no -ed. It rhymes with thought and caught.
Q2
Choose the correct sentence.
A) I didn't went to work.
B) I didn't go to work.
C) I wasn't go to work.
Why: didn't already shows the past, so the main verb is the base: go. C mixes be with an action verb.
Q3 · new
Choose the correct question.
A) Did you at home last night?
B) Were you at home last night?
C) Did you were at home last night?
Why: "at home" needs the verb be, and be makes its own question: Were you…? A has no main verb; C puts did with be. (Did you stay home? would also be correct.)
Errors you will hear most (1.3)

"I didn't went." · "Did you went / bought…?" · "What you did yesterday?" · "You went to the party?" (no did; OK in casual speech with rising intonation, but teach Did…?) · "Did he was there?" · "Yes, I went." (short answer) · "I goed / buyed / eated." · "Went to the park." (no subject) · bought / brought confusion · read past said /rid/. Correct the first three in controlled work; note the rest for feedback.

Marking principle

At A2, mark meaning and form, and be generous with spelling while it is new. "Biger" in a correct comparative sentence is a spelling note, not a wrong answer. "More cheaper" is a grammar error: mark it. American English is the model; British forms (further for distance, flat, autumn, the cinema, film, pounds) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

1 than · 2 the · 3 than · 4 the · 5 than · 6 the

Exercise 3 — Listen and complete (Script 2.1-A)

1 the cheapest · 2 more expensive (than the pizza place) · 3 much better · 4 the best (food in the area) · 5 the most expensive · 6 farther away · 7 cheaper. They go to the Thai restaurant.

Accept further in 6 (the online lesson uses it). If a learner writes "the most cheap," the listening is right but the form isn't: go back to the syllable test.

Exercise 4 — Fix the mistake
  1. This bag is cheaper than that one. (one form only: no more)
  2. Tokyo is the biggest city in Japan. (short word: -est, double the g)
  3. My new job is better than my old one. (irregular: no more)
  4. A plane is faster than a car. (than, not then)
  5. This is the hottest day of the year. (short word: -est, double the t)

Also accept 2 "Tokyo is the largest city in Japan." Watch for learners who fix one thing and add a new error: "the most biggest."

Exercise 5 — Three cafés

1 cheaper · 2 the oldest · 3 newer · 4 the best · 5 more expensive · 6 the farthest (or the furthest)

Two more sentences (open). Any true sentence from the table is correct. Models: Café Sol is the cheapest café. The Corner is the most expensive. Bean House is the newest. Bean House is the closest / nearest to school. Café Sol is closer than The Corner. Bean House is better than Café Sol. Café Sol is not as good as The Corner. Check that the fact matches the table: "The Corner is the cheapest" is good grammar but wrong information.

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) This car is more faster than mine.
B) This car is faster than mine.
C) This car is faster then mine.
Why: fast is short, so -er + than. A mixes two forms (more + -er). C uses then (time) instead of than (compare).
Q2
"Of all three restaurants, this one is ___."
A) more expensive
B) the most expensive
C) the most expensivest
Why: three or more → superlative: the most + long adjective. A compares only two. C mixes two forms.
Q3 · new
Choose the correct sentence.
A) This is the worse day of my life.
B) This is the most bad day of my life.
C) This is the worst day of my life.
Why: bad is irregular: bad → worse → the worst. A uses the comparative after the. B treats bad like a long word.
Errors you will hear most (2.1)

Correct the first five during controlled practice; in the information gap, note them for the feedback stage.

2.2 · Quantifiers — some, any, much, many (C5)

Note for strong learners: in a café, "two coffees" / "two juices" (= two cups / glasses) is normal. At A2, keep the basic rule: the drink itself is U.

2.3 · Adverbs of Frequency & Manner (C6)

Exercise 1 — Put the word in the right place
  1. I always drink tea in the morning. (before the main verb)
  2. She is never late for class. (after is)
  3. We sometimes go to the movies. (also correct: Sometimes we go to the movies.)
  4. He often checks his phone.
  5. They are usually tired on Mondays. (after are)
  6. My brother hardly ever cooks.

Accept "the cinema" (British). Wrong: "She never is late," "I drink always tea," "My brother cooks hardly ever" (possible in speech, but teach the middle position), "He often check" (lost -s).

Exercise 2 — Make the "how?" word

1 quickly · 2 slowly · 3 carefully (double l: careful + ly) · 4 easily (y → ily) · 5 happily (y → ily) · 6 simply (-le → -ly) · 7 well (irregular) · 8 fast (no change) · 9 hard (no change)

Module 7 is functional: mark whether the task gets done, politely and clearly. A direction with a small grammar slip ("Turn to left") that gets the partner to the right box is a success with a note. A grammatically perfect order that sounds rude ("I want the soup.") is a register point to coach, not a wrong answer. American English is the model; British forms from the online lessons (single, return, straight on, first turning, starter, bill, zip, fitting room, Have you got…?, chemist's) are always acceptable.

Self-check
Q1
You need help to find the bank. What is the most polite start?
A) Where bank?
B) Excuse me, how do I get to the bank?
C) You must tell me the bank.
Why: open with Excuse me and use a full question with do. A is too short and direct; C sounds like an order.
Q2
You want to go to Chicago and come back the same day.
A) A one-way to Chicago.
B) A round-trip to Chicago.
C) A platform to Chicago.
Why: round-trip = there and back (UK return). A one-way ticket doesn't bring you home; a platform is where you wait.
Q3 · new
You are on a bus. Your stop is next. What do you do?
A) I get off the bus.
B) I get out the bus.
C) I get down the bus.
Why: buses, trains and planes take on/off. Get out of is for cars and taxis (and B is missing of). C translates Spanish bajar / Tagalog bumaba.
Errors you will hear most (7.1)

"How I get to the station?" · "Where is bank?" · "Go to straight." · "Turn to left." / "in the left" · "get in the bus" · "a ticket for return" · "opposite of the park" (no of) · "lef" / "reft" (pronunciation). Correct the first four; they block communication or sound abrupt.

Self-check
Q1
The soup has much more salt than you want.
A) There's too many salt.
B) There's too much salt.
C) There's enough salt.
Why: salt is uncountable, so too much. C means the amount is OK, which is the opposite of the problem.
Q2
A jacket is too small for you.
A) It's not enough big.
B) It's not big enough.
C) It's too big enough.
Why: enough goes after an adjective: big enough. C mixes too and enough.
Q3 · new
Your soup is cold. What is the best thing to say to the server?
A) This soup is bad. Take it.
B) Excuse me, I'm afraid there's a problem with my soup. It's cold.
C) I want new soup now.
Why: B softens (Excuse me, I'm afraid…) and explains the problem clearly. A and C are understandable but sound angry, and they don't say what is wrong.
Errors you will hear most (7.2)

"I want a table / Give me…" (register) · "It's enough big." · "too much people" · "This tea is too good." (as praise) · "The account, please." · "Can I try it?" (for clothes: try it on) · "I'm afraid" pronounced as a real fear, with a worried face. Coach the tone as much as the grammar.

Success at A2 = three clear parts, 10+ sentences, understood by the group. Look for at least: one past simple + one used to or past continuous (part 1); one for / since (part 2); one going to or will + one If…, I'll… (part 3); one verb pattern (I'd like to…, I want to…, I enjoy…). Key words (not full sentences) in the frame are the right way to plan. Literacy support target: 6 sentences, 2 per part.

Model (the online capstone, adapted): I grew up in a small village in the mountains. When I was a child, I used to walk to school with my brother, and my parents were working on a farm. Life was simple and happy. I've lived in the city for three years now, since I came here for work. I've learned a lot, and I've just started an evening English class. Next year, I'm going to look for a better job. I think I'll study more, too. If I improve my English, I'll apply for a job in an office. One day, I'd like to visit every country I've read about!

Don't mark the content, the story choice (real / partly fictional / card), or the length of the past part. A learner who starts at "up to now" has completed the task.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence talks about a past habit?
A) I used to play the piano.
B) I used to played the piano.
C) I going to play the piano.
Why: used to + base verb. B adds -ed twice; C is a future form with am missing.
Q2
"If I ___ the exam, I'll celebrate."
A) pass
B) will pass
C) passed
Why: first conditional: If + present simple, will + verb. No will in the if part.
Q3 · new
I've already signed up. Next year I ___ study nursing.
A) am going to
B) used to
C) have
Why: a decided plan (I've already signed up) + next year → going to. B is the past; C ("I have study") is not a tense.
Errors you will hear most (8.3)

"I am living here since 2019." · "When I had 20 years…" (age with have) · "I used to played" / "I use to" · "If I will get the job…" · "I going to open a shop" (no be) · "I moved here for five years" (→ I've lived here for five years) · no tense change at all, with time words doing the work (Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai speakers) · "I grew up in small town" (article). In the report stage, board only 3–4 of these; praise the stories first.
