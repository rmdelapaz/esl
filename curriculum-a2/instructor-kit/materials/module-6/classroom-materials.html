B · Game — The If-chain: Starter Cards (6.1)

One set per group of 4–5, face down. The first player turns a starter card and finishes it with I'll…. The next player turns that result into a new condition: "If it rains tomorrow, I'll stay home." → "If I stay home, I'll…" Keep going around. When the group hears will after if, everyone says "Beep!" and the speaker tries again. Full rules are in the Teacher's Guide (6.1).

🌧️If it rains tomorrow, I'll… 💵If I win $100, I'll… 📱If my phone breaks, I'll… ⏰If I'm late for class, I'll… 👋If my friend visits this weekend, we'll… 🚌If the bus doesn't come, I'll… 😎If I have a day off, I'll… ❄️If it snows, I'll… 👛If I find a wallet on the street, I'll… 🍲If I cook dinner tonight, I'll… 💼If my boss says "Go home early!", I'll… 🚗If I learn to drive, I'll…

B · The If-chain: Twist Cards

Every third turn, the speaker takes a twist card before speaking and follows it. Put the used card at the bottom of the pile.

C · "Make a Deal" Role Cards (6.1)

Give each pair the matching A and B cards. Learners read their own card only, then talk until they agree a plan. Target: 3+ "If…" sentences each. Any learner may swap roles or take a different pair. Fast pairs: swap cards with another pair.

  • You have a car.

Useful: If it's sunny, we'll… · If we leave at 7, we won't… · If you want, I'll…

D · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural but slow speed, with pauses. Read each script twice: once for gist, once to check. Use contractions (I'll, she'll, won't) as written: learners need to hear them. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give extra listening at home.

6.1-A · Lena's weekend (Workbook 6.1, Ex 1: match the if-clauses and results)

Hi, it's Lena! I have a busy weekend, and it all depends on the weather. If it's sunny on Saturday, I'll go hiking with my friend Sam. We love the mountains. But if it rains, we'll stay home and watch a movie. On Friday, if I finish work early, I'll go to the market. It closes at six. If the market is closed, I might go to the supermarket. I'm not sure. When I get home on Sunday night, I'll call my mom. I call her every Sunday. And if I have time, I'll cook a big dinner for my family. Busy, busy!

Answers: 1 c · 2 e · 3 b · 4 f · 5 a · 6 d. Gist question: "Is Lena's weekend busy?" (Yes.) Detail: "Is she sure about the supermarket?" (No: might.)

6.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times; learners write in "My new words")

1. If it rains, we'll stay home. · 2. You'll pass if you study. · 3. If I have time, I might call you. · 4. When I get home, I'll text you.

Check the comma in 1 and 3, no comma in 2, and 'll (not "I call") in 4.

6.1-C · The teacher's If-chain (model before the game; learners repeat each link)

If it rains, I'll stay home. · If I stay home, I'll cook. · If I cook, I'll eat too much. · If I eat too much, I'll feel sleepy. · If I feel sleepy, I'll go to bed early. · If I go to bed early, I'll get up early. · If I get up early, I'll go for a walk… in the rain!

Point at the change each time: "I'll cook" → "If I cook": the 'll disappears in the if-half.

6.2-A · Sam's street (Workbook 6.2, Ex 4: match the names)

6.2-B · Define it! riddles (model before the game; learners call out the word)

1. It's a person who works in a hospital and helps the doctors. · 2. It's a place where you catch a plane. · 3. It's a thing which keeps food cold. · 4. It's a place where you can send a letter or buy stamps. · 5. It's a thing that you use when it rains. · 6. It's a person who cooks food in a restaurant.

Answers: 1 a nurse · 2 an airport · 3 a fridge · 4 a post office · 5 an umbrella · 6 a chef. After each answer, ask: "Person, place or thing? So… who, where or which?"

6.3-A · Carlos's strange day (Workbook 6.3, Ex 5: circle the linker)

Yesterday was a strange day. I woke up late because my alarm didn't ring. I was hungry, but I didn't have time for breakfast. It was raining, so I took the bus. Although the bus was slow, I got to work on time. I worked all morning and had lunch with my friend Mei. In the evening I was tired, so I went to bed early. Good night!

Answers: 1 because · 2 but · 3 so · 4 Although · 5 and · 6 so. Then ask: "Why did Carlos wake up late?" ("Because his alarm didn't ring.")

6.3-B · Dictogloss: my weekend (optional: C19 if you have time, or the C20 warmer)

Last weekend was busy, but it was fun. On Saturday morning I cleaned the apartment, and then I went to the park with my son. It was cold, so we didn't stay long. In the afternoon we visited my sister because it was her birthday. Although I was tired on Sunday, I cooked a big lunch for my family.

How: read it twice at normal speed. Learners write only key words (not full sentences). Pairs rebuild the paragraph together, with all five linkers in the right places. Then show or read the original: learners check the linkers and the commas. The paragraph has but, and, so, because, although.

G · Step 2: Topic Envelopes — Idea Cards

Cut out each idea card and put each topic in its own envelope. Groups choose one envelope, put the ideas in a good order, and join them with the linker cards into 4–5 longer sentences. The person on the envelope is a "new identity": any learner may write their own paragraph about this person instead of their real life. Model answers are in the Answer Key.

G · because ↔ so · but ↔ although — Flip Cards

For the flip stage in C19 (pairs). A reads a card; B says the same idea with the other linker (shown in the corner). Then swap. The teacher's answers are under the cards.

I drank coffee because I was sleepy. (→ so) We took a taxi because it was late. (→ so) She's happy because she got a new job. (→ so) He was cold, so he closed the window. (→ because) The store was closed, so we went to another one. (→ because) I missed the bus, so I called my boss. (→ because) It was late, but we kept talking. (→ although) The food was cheap, but it was delicious. (→ although)
Teacher's answers (keep)

I was sleepy, so I drank coffee. · It was late, so we took a taxi. · She got a new job, so she's happy. · He closed the window because he was cold. · We went to another store because the store was closed. · I called my boss because I missed the bus. · Although it was late, we kept talking. · Although the food was cheap, it was delicious.

Listen for: the reason after because and the result after so. If a learner flips the logic ("I was sleepy because I drank coffee"), ask: "Why did you drink coffee?"
