C · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural but slow speed, with pauses. Read each script twice: once for the main idea, once to check. Use the relaxed "going to" /ɡoʊɪŋ tə/ and the contractions (I'm, it'll, won't), because that is what learners will hear outside class. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

3.1-A · Nadia's weekend (Workbook 3.1, Ex 1: ✓ or ✗)

Hi, I'm Nadia. This weekend is going to be busy, but fun! On Saturday morning, I'm going to visit my aunt. She lives near the park. My brother is going to drive us there. I'm not going to drive. I don't like driving in the city! In the afternoon, my aunt and I are going to cook together: chicken and rice. I'm not going to clean the house this weekend. I cleaned it last week! On Saturday evening, I'm going to see a movie with my friend Lucy. And on Sunday? I'm not going to work, and I'm not going to go shopping. I'm going to relax at home. And I'm going to go to bed early, because on Monday I'm starting a new class!

Answers: ✓ visit her aunt · ✓ see a movie · ✓ cook · ✓ go to bed early · ✗ clean the house · ✗ go shopping · ✗ drive (her brother drives) · ✗ work.

3.1-B · What's going to happen? (evidence predictions: learners call out the prediction)

1. Look at the sky! It's very dark, and I can hear thunder. · 2. Oh no! My phone battery is at one percent. · 3. The bus is full, and it's 8:55. Class starts at 9:00. · 4. Careful! That glass is at the edge of the table. · 5. The baby is rubbing her eyes and yawning. · 6. Ana is running to the bus stop, and the bus is leaving!

Model answers: 1 It's going to rain / storm. · 2 It's going to die / turn off. · 3 We're going to be late. · 4 It's going to fall (and break). · 5 She's going to sleep. · 6 She's going to miss the bus. Accept any logical prediction with the correct be + going to + base verb.

3.2-A · The weekend weather forecast (Workbook 3.2, Ex 1)

Good evening, and here is the weather for the weekend. On Friday, it will be sunny and hot, about eighty-five degrees. A great day for the park! On Saturday, it will be cloudy. It might rain in the afternoon, so take an umbrella. We're not sure. On Sunday, it will rain all day, and it will be very windy. There will be a storm in the evening, so stay at home! And on Monday, it will be cold. It might snow. We'll know more tomorrow. Have a good weekend!

Answers: Friday sunny and hot (will) · Saturday afternoon cloudy, might rain (might) · Sunday rainy, windy, a storm in the evening (will) · Monday cold, might snow (might). Note: 85°F ≈ 29°C. If your learners use Celsius, say "about thirty degrees" instead.

3.2-B · Sure or not sure? (learners hold up the certainty card: will ✔✔ · might ? · won't ✘✘)

1. I'll see you tomorrow. · 2. It might be cold tonight. · 3. She won't come to the party. · 4. We might go to the beach. · 5. Don't worry, I won't forget. · 6. I think they'll win. · 7. He might not call. · 8. It'll be a great weekend! · 9. (trick!) I want to go home.

Answers: 1 will · 2 might · 3 won't · 4 might · 5 won't · 6 will · 7 might (not) · 8 will · 9 none: it's want, not won't! Use 9 to start the won't / want pronunciation stage.

3.3-A · Leo and Grace make a plan (Workbook 3.3, Ex 5: two voices, or ask a confident learner to read Grace)

Leo: Hi, Grace! It's Leo. Are you doing anything this weekend? · Grace: Hi, Leo! Hmm, let me look at my calendar. · Leo: Would you like to go to the movies on Friday evening? · Grace: I'd love to, but I'm working late on Friday. Sorry! · Leo: No problem. How about Saturday? Why don't we go to the market? There's a food festival. · Grace: Good idea! But I'm taking my mom to the doctor at nine on Saturday morning. · Leo: OK. Shall we meet at eleven, then? · Grace: Eleven is perfect. Where? · Leo: Let's meet at the entrance of the market. · Grace: Great. And how about having lunch there? The food stands are really good. · Leo: Sure, that sounds great. So we're meeting at eleven on Saturday, at the market entrance. · Grace: Yes! See you then, Leo.

Answers: 1 going to the movies on Friday (evening) · 2 she's working late · 3 Saturday · 4 at eleven · 5 at the market entrance · 6 at the market (the food stands). Follow-up: "Find 3 suggestions in the script." (How about Saturday? · Why don't we go…? · Shall we meet…? · Let's meet… · How about having lunch…?)

1. I'm meeting my friend at six o'clock. · 2. Are you doing anything on Saturday? · 3. How about going to the park? · 4. I'd love to, but I'm working tonight.

E · Game — Crystal Ball 🔮 / Weather Forecaster 🌦️ (3.2)

Rules (read them to the class, or give one copy per group)
  1. Groups of 3–4. Choose a deck: Crystal Ball (life, the class, the world) or Weather Forecaster (weather). Put the cards face down. You need one die.
  2. Take a card. Roll the die: 1–2 = won't · 3–4 = might · 5–6 = will. Make a prediction with that word and give a reason: "Phones won't have buttons in 20 years, because everything is touch now."
  3. The group reacts: "I agree!" · "Maybe." · "I don't think so. I think…" The best reaction (clear, polite, with a reason) wins the card. The most cards wins the game.
  4. "You" cards are about the person on your left. Make it kind and light: a nice weekend, a good meal, a fun surprise. Nothing about health, money, family or where people will live.
  5. Anyone can say "Pass!" and take a new card. No reason needed.

F · Diary Information Gap — Find a Time to Meet (3.3)

Pairs sit face to face and don't show their diaries. Step 1: find the one time you are both free: "Are you doing anything on Monday afternoon?" — "Yes, I'm going to the dentist. How about Tuesday evening?" Step 2: suggest an activity from the ideas box: "Let's… / Why don't we…? / How about …ing?" Say a polite no to the first idea if you want. Step 3: agree a time and place and confirm: "So we're meeting on … at … See you then!" Answer (teacher only): Thursday evening.

G · Game — Plans Mingle: Find Someone Who… (C7)

Photocopy one per learner. Learners stand up, walk, and ask "Are you going to cook something special this weekend?" If the answer is "Yes, I am," write the name and ask the extra question. One name per box; ask each person only one or two questions, then move on. Get at least two names in each part. At the end, report with he/she: "Ana is going to visit her family this weekend. She's going to take the bus."

Teacher note

Before the mingle, drill the question on the board: "Are you going to + base verb … this weekend?" and the short answers "Yes, I am. / No, I'm not." (never "Yes, I'm."). A "No, I'm not" is fine: say "OK, thanks!" and ask someone else. Learners with a future identity card answer as their card person. For literacy support, read the rows aloud first and let learners mark a ✓ and the first letter of the name.
