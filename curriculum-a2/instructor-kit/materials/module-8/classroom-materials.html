B · for / since Sort Cards (8.1)

Groups of 3. Put the two header cards on the table. Sort the 18 time cards under for (a length of time: can you count it?) or since (a start point: a day, a date, an event). Then each learner picks 2 cards and says a true sentence: "I've lived here since January."

Answers: for three years · a long time · ten minutes · six months · two weeks · an hour · ages · a few days · a year. since 2019 · Monday · I was a child · last summer · 9 o'clock · my birthday · January · I started this class · yesterday. (ages = a very long time, informal.)

D · To-Do List Information Gap — Set 1: The Trip (8.1)

E · Verb-Pattern Sort Cards (8.2)

Groups of 3. Put the three header cards in a row. Sort the 17 verb cards (3 min). Then turn all the cards face down. Take turns: turn one card over and make a sentence: "keep: My son keeps losing his keys."

+ -ing+ tobothenjoy don't mindfinishkeepsuggest wantneedwould likedecide hopeplanpromiselearn trylikelovehate

Answers: + -ing enjoy · don't mind · finish · keep · suggest. + to want · need · would like · decide · hope · plan · promise · learn · try. both like · love · hate. If a group puts like under -ing only, that's fine; ask "Can you say 'I like to swim'?" (Yes.) Watch for would like under "both": it is to only.

F · Game — Verb Pattern Race (8.2)

No board? Dice-free / two-dice variant (also the C25 warmer)

Roll two dice (or two players each call a number 1–6, or use a phone dice app). Die 1 = the first verb. Die 2 = the topic. Make a true sentence. Correct = 1 point. Play for 5 minutes.

⚀ Die 1: first verb⚁ Die 2: topic
1 · enjoy1 · the weekend
2 · don't mind2 · food and cooking
3 · want3 · this year
4 · would like4 · home
5 · have decided5 · work or school
6 · hope6 · one day in the future

Example: 3 + 2 → "I want to learn to cook Thai food." · 2 + 4 → "I don't mind washing the dishes." Stretch: roll a 6 on die 1 → use any verb from the two-column list.

G · Life-Story Cards (8.3 · C26)

For learners who want a fully fictional story. Any learner can take one: no reason needed. They can also mix: a card's past with their own future, or their own past with a card's future. Learners tell the card in the first person ("I grew up…") and can add details. They may use the same card in the C26 speaking check.

Story card 1

🌊 Rosa

⬅️ Past: I grew up in a fishing town by the sea. I used to help my father sell fish at the market. I was working in a bakery when I met my husband.

⏺️ Up to now: I've lived in this city since 2019. I've worked as a nurse's aide for three years. I've just finished a first-aid course.

➡️ Future: I'm going to apply to nursing school. I think I'll be a good nurse. If I pass the English test, I'll start in September.

Literacy support: read the card aloud to the learner twice, then let them retell it from the three emoji headings (⬅️ ⏺️ ➡️) and 2 sentences per part. Stretch: add 3 new sentences to the card, including one with because and one with while.

H · Story-Circle Question Cards (8.3)

Cut out and put face down in the middle of each story circle. After a speaker finishes, each listener takes a card and asks the question (or asks their own). Rows: past · up to now · future. These are also good practice for the C26 interview.

Where did you grow up?What did you use to do when you were a child?What were you doing five years ago? What was your first job or school like?How long have you lived here?How long have you known your best friend? What have you just started?What haven't you done yet?What are you going to do next year? Trauma-informed: a speaker can always say "Pass" or "That's a secret!" and the listener takes a new card. Model this before the circles start, so it feels normal.

I · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural but slow speed, with pauses. Read each script twice: once for gist, once to check. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

8.1-A · Ana's timeline (Workbook 8.1, Ex 2: for or since + the time)

Hi, I'm Ana. I was born in Lima, in Peru. Before I came to this city, I lived with my aunt in Texas for two years. I've lived in this city since 2021. I work in a hotel. I've worked there for three years, and I love my job! I met my husband, David, at the hotel. We've known each other since 2022, and we've been married for one year. I've studied English since last September. Oh, and I have a new car! Well… a new old car. I've had it for two months.

Answers: 1 since 2021 · 2 for three years · 3 since 2022 · 4 for one year · 5 since last September · 6 for two months · bonus: for two years (past simple: she doesn't live in Texas now).

8.1-B · Leo's moving day (Workbook 8.1, Ex 3: ✓ / ✗ + just / already)

8.3-A · Maya's life story (Workbook 8.3, Ex 1 · same as the online lesson: you can play the 🔊 lines instead)

Sam: So, Maya, tell me a little about yourself. Where did you grow up?
Maya: I grew up in a small town by the sea. When I was young, my family used to run a little café.
Sam: That sounds lovely! What was life like there?
Maya: Busy and warm. Every summer, lots of tourists came to our town, and we cooked all day. I used to help my mother in the kitchen. One summer, I was helping her when my university letter arrived!
Sam: And how long have you lived in the city now?
Maya: I've lived here for four years — since I started university. I've studied design, and I've just finished my final project.
Sam: Congratulations! Have you found a job yet?
Maya: Not yet. But I'm going to apply to a design studio next week. I'm quite hopeful.
Sam: I'm sure you'll do well. What are your plans after that?
Maya: If I get the job, I'll stay in the city for a while. And one day I'm going to open my own studio — maybe back by the sea.
Sam: That's a beautiful dream. I think you'll make it happen.
Maya: Thank you! If I keep working hard, I think everything will be fine.

Circled words (Ex 1 part 2): used to run · were cooking · I've lived · I've just finished · I'm going to apply · I'll stay · If I get. Not in the story: was working · I lived · If I will get.

8.3-B · The teacher's model life story (planning stage · better still: tell your own, real or invented, with the same three parts)

Let me tell you my story. I grew up in a small village in the mountains. When I was a child, I used to walk to school with my brother, and my parents were working on a farm. Life was simple and happy. After school, I moved to the city and got a job in a store. I've lived in this city for three years now — since I came here for work. I've learned a lot, and I've just started an evening English class. I haven't found my dream job yet. Next year, I'm going to look for a better job. I think I'll study more, too. If I improve my English, I'll apply for a job in an office. One day, I'd like to visit every country I've read about. That's my story!

After reading, ask: "Which part was the past? Up to now? The future?" Learners raise 1, 2 or 3 fingers as you re-read three sentences. 13 sentences: a good length for C25.

J · Teacher Observation Checklist · C25 → C26

How to use it

Observe during the rehearsal and the story circles. Mark ✓ did it clearly · ~ did it with help, or errors that affect meaning · ○ not yet / not observed. You don't need a mark in every box. After class, use it to (1) plan each learner's C26 interview (start with the part they told best), (2) choose the C26 role-play, (3) choose the two weakest items for the C26 warm-up, and (4) note who needs encouragement. This is not a grade. The C26 interview asks about the same three parts, so these items are the same can-dos the final speaking check looks for.

  • 1 Past simple for finished events (grew up, moved, started), regular and irregular
  • 2 Past background: used to + verb and/or past continuous
  • 3 Present perfect + for / since (time up to now)
  • 4 just / already / yet in the right place
  • 5 Future: going to (plan) and/or will (prediction)
  • 6 First conditional: If + present, will + verb
  • 7 Verb patterns: want / would like / hope to · enjoy + -ing
  • 8 Story is clear (time words, three parts); answers a follow-up question
