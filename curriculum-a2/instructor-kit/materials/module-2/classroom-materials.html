Useful questions (put them on the board)

How many people live in…? · How much is an apartment / a bus ticket in…? · How hot is it in summer? · How old is…? · How many parks are there? · How far is the airport? · How many universities are there? — Then decide: Which city is better for a family? For a student? Why?

B2 · Information Gap — Three Phones (2.1, Set 2)

C · Game — Class Superlatives Quiz (2.1)

Teams of 3–4. Read each question twice. Teams write A, B or C. 1 point for the right answer, +1 bonus for a correct full sentence: "The Pacific is the biggest ocean." Round 2 is about the class, with neutral topics only.

Keep it kind

Never make "class records" about height, weight, age, looks, money, homes or family. The Round 2 questions below are safe. If you add your own, ask yourself: "Could this embarrass anyone?" Learners with a new identity card can answer as their card person.

Round 2 · Class records (guess, then ask)

Teams guess a name for each record first. Then everybody stands up and asks: "How many languages do you know?" Teams check their guesses. 1 point for each right guess.

Who…?Our guessThe answer
1. Who knows the most languages?
2. Who got up the earliest today?
3. Who has the longest first name? (count the letters)
4. Who drank the most cups of tea or coffee yesterday?
5. Who has the oldest thing in their bag today?
6. Whose favorite food is the spiciest? (the class votes)
7. Who can say the longest word in their language?

Report with a superlative: "Amira knows the most languages. She speaks four!" Question 1 is a great moment to celebrate: many adult ESL learners already speak two, three or more languages.

F · How Often? Survey (2.3)

One per learner. Stand up and walk. Ask 4 classmates 2 questions each. Write the name and a short answer ("twice a week," "hardly ever"). Then report 3 sentences with -s: "Ana hardly ever eats fast food." "Jun cooks three times a week." Learners with a habit card answer as their card person.

G · Game — Adverb Mime Cards (2.3)

One set per group of 4–5. Cut the cards and make two piles, face down: actions and adverbs. A learner takes one of each and mimes (no talking; miming at a desk is fine). The group guesses in the present continuous: "You're drinking coffee nervously!" First correct full guess keeps the cards. Nobody has to mime alone: pairs can mime together.

Variation — "In the manner of the word" (whole class, 5 min)

A volunteer goes outside. The class chooses one adverb card. The volunteer comes back and gives commands to the class or to one learner: "Say hello in the manner of the word." "Open your book in the manner of the word." Everyone does it that way. The volunteer has three guesses: "Is it… loudly?" Take angrily out of the pile if anyone looks uncomfortable.

I · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural but slow speed, with pauses. Read each script twice: once for gist, once to check. For dialogues, change your voice for A and B, or ask a confident learner to read one part. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

2.1-A · Where should we eat? (Workbook 2.1, Ex 3: listen and complete)

A: Where should we eat? There are three places near here. · B: Well, the pizza place is the cheapest, but the food isn't great. · A: The Thai restaurant is more expensive than the pizza place, right? · B: Yes, but it's much better. Honestly, it's the best food in the area. · A: And the sushi bar? Is it more expensive than the Thai place? · B: It's the most expensive of the three, and it's farther away too. Let's go Thai: better than sushi, and cheaper!

2.1-B · Comparative dictation (optional, or the C5 warmer; learners write in their notebook)

One: The train is faster than the bus. · Two: My phone is cheaper than your phone. · Three: This is the biggest supermarket in town. · Four: Winter here is colder than fall. · Five: Her English is better than my English. · Six: It's the most expensive hotel in the city.

2.2-A · At the shop (Workbook 2.2, Ex 6)

Shopper: Excuse me, do you have any fresh bread? · Seller: Yes, we have some over here. How many loaves would you like? · Shopper: Just one, please. And I need a few tomatoes and some rice. · Seller: No problem. Here are four tomatoes. How much rice: a small bag or a big one? · Shopper: A small bag is fine. We don't need much. How much is it all? · Seller: That's five dollars. Would you like some coffee too? It's on sale today. · Shopper: No, thanks. We still have a lot of coffee at home!

2.2-B · C or U? dictation (C6 warmer: learners write the word, then C or U)

eggs · rice · money · apples · information · bread · bananas · water · furniture · tomatoes

2.3-A · How often do you cook? (Workbook 2.3, Ex 4)

A: How often do you cook at home? · B: Almost every day. I usually cook quickly after work, but on Sundays I cook slowly and enjoy it. What about you? · A: I hardly ever cook, to be honest. I'm a bad cook! I order food twice a week. · B: Come on, you're not that bad. Do you exercise? · A: Yes! I go running three times a week. I run quite fast, but I don't swim well at all. · B: Same here. I always start well and then get tired. We should train together sometimes!

2.3-B · Listen and stand (word order check: stand up if the sentence is correct, stay sitting if it is wrong)

One: I usually get up at seven. · Two: I drink always coffee. · Three: They are never late. · Four: She usually is late. · Five: He speaks well English. · Six: We sometimes eat out on Fridays. · Seven: I don't never eat meat. · Eight: My sister drives carefully.

Answers: stand for 1, 3, 6, 8. Sit for 2, 4, 5, 7. After each "sit," a volunteer says the correct sentence.
