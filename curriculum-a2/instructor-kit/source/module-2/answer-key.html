Module 2 — Describing & Comparing: Answers

Answers to every Module 2 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (city and phone information gaps, superlatives quiz, countable/uncountable sort, kitchen-cupboard information gap, listen-and-stand), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Marking principle

At A2, mark meaning and form, and be generous with spelling while it is new. "Biger" in a correct comparative sentence is a spelling note, not a wrong answer. "More cheaper" is a grammar error: mark it. American English is the model; British forms (further for distance, flat, autumn, the cinema, film, pounds) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

The superlative column must have the. Without it = half a mark / a note. Watch for: "biger," "nicer" spelled "niceer," "happyer," "more happy" (accepted in speech by some native speakers, but teach happier), "gooder," "baddest."

Exercise 2 — than or the?

1 than · 2 the · 3 than · 4 the · 5 than · 6 the

Tip for learners: -er or more before the gap → than. -est or best after the gap → the. Wrong: "then" in 1, 3, 5 (spelling; mark it, and teach than = compare, then = next).

Exercise 3 — Listen and complete (Script 2.1-A)

1 the cheapest · 2 more expensive (than the pizza place) · 3 much better · 4 the best (food in the area) · 5 the most expensive · 6 farther away · 7 cheaper. They go to the Thai restaurant.

Accept further in 6 (the online lesson uses it). If a learner writes "the most cheap," the listening is right but the form isn't: go back to the syllable test.

Exercise 4 — Fix the mistake
  1. This bag is cheaper than that one. (one form only: no more)
  2. Tokyo is the biggest city in Japan. (short word: -est, double the g)
  3. My new job is better than my old one. (irregular: no more)
  4. A plane is faster than a car. (than, not then)
  5. This is the hottest day of the year. (short word: -est, double the t)

Also accept 2 "Tokyo is the largest city in Japan." Watch for learners who fix one thing and add a new error: "the most biggest."

Exercise 5 — Three cafés

1 cheaper · 2 the oldest · 3 newer · 4 the best · 5 more expensive · 6 the farthest (or the furthest)

Two more sentences (open). Any true sentence from the table is correct. Models: Café Sol is the cheapest café. The Corner is the most expensive. Bean House is the newest. Bean House is the closest / nearest to school. Café Sol is closer than The Corner. Bean House is better than Café Sol. Café Sol is not as good as The Corner. Check that the fact matches the table: "The Corner is the cheapest" is good grammar but wrong information.

Speaking — Compare three things (open)

Success = 4 comparatives with than (one -er and one more) and 2 superlatives with the, no mixed forms. Model (the three city cards): Port Mara is bigger than Greenfield. Greenfield is cheaper than Port Mara. Lake Sora is hotter than Greenfield. Port Mara is more expensive than Lake Sora. Port Mara is the oldest city. Greenfield has the most parks.

Class superlatives quiz (materials Set C)

Round 1: 1 B the Pacific · 2 C Everest · 3 A the cheetah · 4 B the blue whale · 5 B Venus (the trick! Mercury is closer to the sun, but Venus is hotter) · 6 C Russia · 7 B India (since 2023; China is second) · 8 C Antarctica.

Bonus: 9 light ("Light is faster than sound.") · 10 neither: "A kilo of rice is as heavy as a kilo of feathers!"

Bonus point = a correct full sentence: "The Pacific is the biggest ocean." "The cheetah is the fastest land animal." Accept "the most big" as the right fact but no sentence point. Round 2 (class records) has no fixed answers: the team with the most correct guesses wins.

Script 2.1-B — comparative dictation (optional)

1 The train is faster than the bus. · 2 My phone is cheaper than your phone. · 3 This is the biggest supermarket in town. · 4 Winter here is colder than fall. · 5 Her English is better than my English. · 6 It's the most expensive hotel in the city. Mark spelling of the adjective forms and than; ignore small slips elsewhere.

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) This car is more faster than mine.
B) This car is faster than mine.
C) This car is faster then mine.
Why: fast is short, so -er + than. A mixes two forms (more + -er). C uses then (time) instead of than (compare).
Q2
"Of all three restaurants, this one is ___."
A) more expensive
B) the most expensive
C) the most expensivest
Why: three or more → superlative: the most + long adjective. A compares only two. C mixes two forms.
Q3 · new
Choose the correct sentence.
A) This is the worse day of my life.
B) This is the most bad day of my life.
C) This is the worst day of my life.
Why: bad is irregular: bad → worse → the worst. A uses the comparative after the. B treats bad like a long word.
Errors you will hear most (2.1)

"more cheaper" · "more big" / "more good" · "the most biggest" · "bigger that / bigger of" · "the biggest of the world" · "It is most expensive" (no the) · "He is tall than me" (no -er) · "biger / hoter" · "more better" · "then" for than · "the more expensive" for the superlative. Correct the first five during controlled practice; in the information gap, note them for the feedback stage.

2.2 · Quantifiers — some, any, much, many (C5)

New words — C or U? (the boxes)

C: apple, banana, egg, tomato, vegetables. U: rice, bread, milk, water, sugar, cheese, coffee, butter, salt, juice, money.

Note for strong learners: in a café, "two coffees" / "two juices" (= two cups / glasses) is normal. At A2, keep the basic rule: the drink itself is U.

Countable / uncountable sort cards (materials Set D)

C: egg, apple, banana, tomato, sandwich, muffin, cup, chair. U: rice, bread, milk, water, sugar, cheese, coffee, salt, butter, juice, money, information, furniture, homework.

The "surprise U" cards: bread, money, information, furniture, homework. Expect groups to put them under C. That is the point: discuss, then fix.

The most-missed items: 5 money, 8 bread, 10 information, 12 furniture. Use the containers: a loaf of bread, a piece of information, a piece of furniture.

Exercise 2 — some or any?

1 some (+) · 2 any (−) · 3 any (?) · 4 some (+) · 5 some (a request: we expect "yes") · 6 any (−)

Item 5 is the trap. "Can I have any water?" is not wrong English in every context, but it sounds strange for a polite request: mark it and explain.

Exercise 3 — much, many or a lot of?

1 many (eggs = C, question) · 2 much (time = U, negative) · 3 a lot of (positive) · 4 much (water = U, question) · 5 many (bananas = C, negative) · 6 a lot of (positive)

Also correct: 2 "a lot of" and 5 "a lot of" (I don't have a lot of time is natural). Wrong: 3 "many" is possible in formal English but not the natural choice here; 6 "much coffee" in a positive sentence sounds unnatural: correct it to a lot of.

Exercise 4 — a few or a little?

1 a little (sugar = U) · 2 a few (apples = C) · 3 a little (cheese = U) · 4 a few (friends = C) · 5 a little (milk = U)

Exercise 5 — How much or How many?

1 How many (eggs) · 2 How much (rice) · 3 How much (price: always how much) · 4 How many (bottles: we count the bottles) · 5 How much (money)

Item 3 is the A1 price question: "How much are the bananas?" asks about money, not the number of bananas. Item 4: water is U, but bottles is C.

Exercise 6 — At the shop (Script 2.2-A)

a) bread ✓ · tomatoes ✓ · rice ✓ · coffee ✗ · eggs ✗ · milk ✗

b) 1 one (loaf) · 2 a few (accept "four"; the seller counts four) · 3 a small bag · 4 five dollars ($5) · 5 They have a lot of coffee at home.

Eggs and milk are not in the dialogue: learners who tick them are guessing from the topic. Replay the line.

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) We don't have some bread.
B) We don't have any bread.
C) We don't have any breads.
Why: negatives take any. C is wrong because bread is uncountable: no plural.
Q2
"How ___ eggs are in the box?"
A) many
B) much
C) a little
Why: eggs are countable, so How many. Much and a little go with uncountable nouns.
Q3 · new
"I take ___ milk in my coffee."
A) a few
B) a little
C) many
Why: milk is uncountable, and it's a small amount: a little. A few and many are for countable nouns.
Errors you will hear most (2.2)

"a bread" / "some breads" · "informations," "furnitures," "moneys" · "How much eggs?" · "I have much friends" · "There is milk?" (no any) · "We don't have some…" · "a few milk / a little eggs" · "three egg" (no -s) · "I don't have no money." Correct the countable/uncountable errors every time in controlled practice; in the kitchen task, recast and move on.

2.3 · Adverbs of Frequency & Manner (C6)

Exercise 1 — Put the word in the right place
  1. I always drink tea in the morning. (before the main verb)
  2. She is never late for class. (after is)
  3. We sometimes go to the movies. (also correct: Sometimes we go to the movies.)
  4. He often checks his phone.
  5. They are usually tired on Mondays. (after are)
  6. My brother hardly ever cooks.

Accept "the cinema" (British). Wrong: "She never is late," "I drink always tea," "My brother cooks hardly ever" (possible in speech, but teach the middle position), "He often check" (lost -s).

Exercise 2 — Make the "how?" word

1 quickly · 2 slowly · 3 carefully (double l: careful + ly) · 4 easily (y → ily) · 5 happily (y → ily) · 6 simply (-le → -ly) · 7 well (irregular) · 8 fast (no change) · 9 hard (no change)

Watch for: "carefuly," "easyly," "simplely," "goodly," "fastly," and "hardly" for 9. Hardly is a real word but it means "almost not."

Exercise 3 — good or well?

1 good (+ noun teacher) · 2 well (how he plays) · 3 well (how they cook) · 4 good (+ noun idea) · 5 well (how he speaks) · 6 good (after is: it describes the soup)

Item 6 surprises learners: after be (and look, feel, taste) we use the adjective. "This soup is well" is wrong. "I feel well" (= not sick) is also correct English, but not part of this lesson.

Exercise 4 — Listen (Script 2.3-A)

1 every day · 2 quickly · 3 slowly · 4 hardly ever · 5 twice · 6 three times · 7 fast … well

Exercise 5 — How often do you…? (open)

Answers are personal; check only the form. Good answers: short ("Twice a week.") or full ("I exercise twice a week." / "I usually cook dinner." / "I call my family every day." / "I go to the supermarket once a week."). Wrong: "I exercise two times in week," "I usually am cook," "Always I cook."

Exercise 6 — My week (open)

Look for: 2 frequency adverbs in the right place (before the main verb / after be) and 2 manner adverbs after the verb or verb + object, with well not good. Model: I usually get up at six. I am always hungry at lunch. I drive carefully. I speak English slowly, but I understand well.

How often? survey + adverb mime game (materials Sets F, G)

Survey: no fixed answers. Check the report sentences for the he/she -s and the adverb position: "Ana hardly ever eats fast food." "Jun cooks three times a week." Mime game: the target sentence is present continuous + manner adverb: "You're drinking coffee nervously!" Accept "You drink coffee nervously" for the point, then recast. No point for the adjective form ("You're driving careful").

Script 2.3-B — Listen and stand (word order)

1 ✅ stand · 2 ❌ sit (I always drink coffee) · 3 ✅ stand · 4 ❌ sit (She is usually late) · 5 ❌ sit (He speaks English well) · 6 ✅ stand · 7 ❌ sit (I never eat meat) · 8 ✅ stand

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence puts the frequency adverb in the correct place?
A) I drink always coffee.
B) I am always busy in the morning.
C) I always am busy in the morning.
Why: with be (am), the adverb goes after it. A puts it after a main verb (it must go before). C puts it before am.
Q2
"She dances really ___."
A) well
B) good
C) goodly
Why: the word describes the verb dances, so we need the adverb well. Good is an adjective; goodly is not the adverb of good.
Q3 · new
"I ___ go to the gym. Maybe once a year."
A) always
B) usually
C) hardly ever
Why: once a year is almost never: hardly ever (~10%). Always and usually mean most of the time.
Errors you will hear most (2.3)

"I drink always coffee" · "She usually is late" · "She speaks very well English" · "I don't never…" · "one time / two times a week" · "She sings good" · "fastly" · "I work hardly" (= almost not!) · "carefuly / easyly" · "She usually walk" (lost -s) · "Always I…" at the start. Correct word order in controlled practice; let one time a week go in fluency work and recast it.
