Instructor Kit · Teacher's Guide

Module 1 — Talking About the Past

The first three classes of A2. Learners meet a new group and show you what they can already do in English. Then they learn the past simple: first was / were, then regular -ed verbs and their three sounds, and last the common irregular verbs with didn't and Did…?. By C3 they can ask a classmate "What did you do last weekend?" and tell a short true story.

3 lessons Lessons 1.1–1.3 Classes C1–C3 Weeks 1–2 90 min per class CEFR A2

How to Use This Guide

  • One lesson block = one 90-minute class. Every class follows the same shape: warmer → lead-in → Meaning / Form / Pronunciation with concept-checking questions (CCQs) → drill (choral, then individual) → controlled practice (workbook) → freer practice (pairs, groups, mingle) → feedback with delayed error correction → homework.
  • The stage plan is your clock. Each one adds up to 90 minutes. The numbers in the Time column are minutes.
  • Present from slides/module-1-the-past.html. Print materials/module-1/classroom-materials.html before C1. It holds the flashcards, the welcome question cards and the A1 diagnostic grid, the new identity cards, the "Where were they?" information gap, the -ed sound sort cards, the picture-story cards, the irregular-verb memory game, the weekend mingle sheet and all the listening scripts.
  • The online lessons are the learners' homework and review. Each one has 🔊 Listen buttons, so learners can hear every model sentence again at home.
  • American English is the classroom standard. The online lessons sometimes use British forms (on holiday, flat, film, shop, practise, memorise) and British phonetic symbols (/wɜːkt/, /wɒtʃt/). Teach vacation, apartment, movie, store, and accept the British forms when learners use them. Both are correct English.
  • Dashed boxes = lines to say out loud, CCQs and ICQs (instruction-checking questions). Green boxes = scripts you read aloud. Amber = watch-outs.
The through-line

How was it? → What did I do? → What did you do? In C1 learners describe where they were and how it was (was / were: no action verbs yet). In C2 they add actions with regular -ed verbs, positive sentences only, and learn to say the ending three ways. In C3 they add the irregular verbs and the question and negative with did. Now they can have a real conversation about the weekend. Every class ends with the same freer task in a bigger form: tell someone about a real past day (yours, or a "new identity" card).

Trauma-informed: the past is personal

"What did you do last weekend?" is an easy question for most people. But for some adults, the recent past holds hard things: a lost job, a hospital visit, a court date, a family far away. Questions like "Where were you last year?" or "When did you come here?" can be painful for newcomers and refugees. Never ask about migration, the home country or "last year" in a whole-class task. Keep personal questions to yesterday and last weekend. For every personal task, offer three choices out loud: your real life, a new identity card, or an imagined day ("your dream weekend"). Say it every time, so choosing a card is normal.

If you run long

The welcome mingle is the most important part of C1. Do not cut it: the group needs it, and you need the diagnostic. Cut instead: the dictation (Script 1.1-B) and Ex 4 go to homework, and the information gap becomes a 5-minute task with 3 people only. Never skip the personal "How was your weekend?" question at the end.

Running the diagnostic — what to listen for

The mingle is a friendly first conversation, not a test. Learners must never feel watched. Hold the grid on a clipboard, stand near a pair for 30–60 seconds, and join in sometimes ("Oh, can you cook rice? I can't!"). Mark each learner ✓ (easy and accurate) · ~ (understands, some errors) · ✗ (can't produce it yet) in three columns:

  • BE: I'm / she's / they're, Are you…? Listen for a dropped be ("I tired," "She teacher") and "I have 30 years."
  • PRESENT SIMPLE: I work / I don't work, Do you…?, and he/she -s in the report at the end ("Ana likes tea"). The third-person -s is the hardest A1 point; ~ is normal.
  • CAN: I can / I can't + base verb, Can you…? Listen for "I can to swim" and "I can swimming."

What to do with the results: Mostly ✓ and ~ = the learner is ready for A2. Many ~ for BE = go slowly today, because was/were is built on am/is/are. Many ✗ in two or three columns = check the placement privately (the toolkit's oral placement interview takes 5 minutes) and give extra support from Day 1. If half the class drops the he/she -s, spend 3 minutes of the C2 warmer on it. Never share the grid with learners. Keep it; compare it with the Progress Test in C13.

Meaning · Form · Pronunciation

Meaning. Was / were is the past of am / is / are. We use it for where people were, how things were, and what places were like, in a finished time (yesterday, last week).

"I was tired last night." Now or before? (Before.) Am I tired now? (We don't know. Maybe not.) Which word shows the past? (Was.)
"They were at the park." One person or more? (More.) So was or were? (Were.)
"You were right, Ana." How many people is "you" here? (One.) Was or were? (Were: you is always were.)
"Were you at home? — No, I wasn't." Was she at home? (No.) Do we say "Did you were…?" (No! Be never needs did.)

Form. Put the A1 forms next to the new ones so learners see the pattern:

PositiveI / he / she / it + was · you / we / they + wereShe was at home. We were happy.
Negativewasn't (was not) · weren't (were not)He wasn't at school. They weren't ready.
QuestionWas / Were + subject…?Were you late? Was it cold?
Short answerYes, I was. / No, I wasn't.(never "Yes, I's")
Wh-Wh- word + was / were + subjectHow was your weekend? Where were you?
PlaceThere was + one / uncountable · There were + pluralThere was a café. There were many people.

Time words. last + a time word (last Monday, last week, last night); a length of time + ago (two days ago, an hour ago); in + a year (in 2020). No "the": last week, not "the last week."

Pronunciation. In positive sentences was and were are weak and fast: /wəz/, /wər/: "I w'z TIRED." Stress goes on the important word. In short answers they are strong: "Yes, I WAS." /wʌz/. wasn't has two syllables /ˈwʌznt/; weren't has one /wɝnt/ (it sounds like "went" with an r). Questions with How was…? fall at the end; yes/no questions (Were you…?) usually rise.

Drill script
  1. Back-chain: "last night" → "tired last night" → "I was tired last night." Choral ×3, half the room, then 4–5 individuals. Keep was weak.
  2. Subject substitution (point at people in the room): T: "I… at home." S: "I was at home." T: "They…" S: "They were at home." T: "Ana…" S: "Ana was at home." T: "You…" S: "You were at home." Speed up.
  3. Negative transformation: T: "He was at work." (shake head) → S: "He wasn't at work." T: "We were late." → S: "We weren't late."
  4. Question and short answer, open pairs: S1 → S2: "Were you at home last night?" S2: "Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't." S2 → S3: "Was it cold yesterday?"… around the room. Then: "How was your weekend?" → "It was great / OK / terrible."
ICQs before the mingle and the information gap
Welcome mingle: How many cards do you take? (Three, one of each color.) How many questions do you ask each person? (One.) What do you do after the question? (Swap cards and find a new partner.) Can you use a new identity? (Yes.)
Information gap: Can you show your paper? (No.) What do you ask? ("Where was… on Saturday? How was it?") Do you write or draw? (Write.) What do you say if you don't understand? ("Can you say that again, please?")
Differentiation
Literacy support

In the mingle, read the question card to the learner once and let them keep one card only. In the grammar stage, color-code: was in blue with I/he/she/it, were in red with you/we/they, and let them copy the table. In the information gap, they can point to a place picture (flashcards) instead of writing. Dictation 1.1-B: sentences 1–2 only.

Stretch

Ask two follow-up questions in the mingle (the small text on each card). In the freer task, add There was / There were and a reason: "It was great because there weren't many people." Try When I was a child, I was… (only if they choose to). Early finishers write 5 sentences for Ex 5 with no help.

L1 watch-outs
  • "You was / They was": all learners, and common in some spoken English too. Keep pointing at the two columns. You is always were, even for one person.
  • Dropping be: "Yesterday I tired." / "The party very good." (Arabic, Russian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai and others have no linking verb here.) Recast: "You were tired."
  • No past form at all: "Yesterday I am at home." (Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai, Indonesian and Tagalog speakers often show the past with a time word only.) Praise the time word, then add the verb: "Yesterday I was…"
  • "Did you were…?" / "I didn't was…": learners who know a little about did (Spanish, Portuguese and Arabic speakers often). Be never takes did; put was / were first.
  • "Ago" in the wrong place: "ago two days" (Spanish hace dos días, Portuguese há dois dias, Arabic, Turkish and others). "The last week" (Spanish la semana pasada, French la semaine dernière). Drill the chunks: two days ago · last week.
  • "It had many people" / "Had a party" for "There were…" (Portuguese tinha, Spanish había, Tagalog may, Chinese yǒu, Vietnamese có). The same "have = there is" transfer as in A1.

Meaning · Form · Pronunciation

Meaning. The past simple is for a finished action at a finished time: yesterday, last week, two days ago. It is often a story: this, then this, then this.

"I cooked dinner last night." Now or in the past? (Past.) Is it finished? (Yes.) Which part of the verb shows the past? (-ed.)
"She worked yesterday." Do we add -s for she? (No.) So: I worked, she worked, they worked… All the same? (Yes!)
"worked." How many beats? (One.) "wanted." How many beats? (Two.) Why? (After t, we add a syllable.)

Form — the four spelling rules.

Most verbs+ -edwork → worked · play → played · watch → watched
Ends in -e+ -dlike → liked · live → lived · arrive → arrived
Consonant + yy → -iedstudy → studied · try → tried (but vowel + y: play → played, stay → stayed)
1 syllable: vowel + consonantdouble + -edstop → stopped · plan → planned (never double w, x, y: showed, fixed, played)

Pronunciation — the three sounds. Spelling and sound are two different steps.

/ɪd/after a t or d sound: one extra syllablewanted · needed · started · visited · decided
/t/after a voiceless sound (p, k, s, sh, ch, f): no buzzworked · watched · stopped · danced · finished · liked
/d/after a voiced sound (vowels, b, g, l, m, n, r, v, z): buzzplayed · called · cleaned · lived · arrived · studied

The /t/ vs /d/ difference is small, and nobody misunderstands it. What matters for being understood: (1) say an ending at all, and (2) don't add a syllable except after t/d ("work-ed" sounds wrong). In fast speech the -ed before a consonant almost disappears (walked to → "walk-to"), so the time word carries the past. Point this out when learners listen: "Listen for yesterday, too."

Drill script
  1. Base → past (flashcards): T shows cook: "cook" → S: "cooked." Choral, then individuals. Hold up fingers for the sound (1, 2, 3) as you go.
  2. Sound groups: drill each column as a chain: "worked, watched, cooked, walked" (/t/) · "played, called, cleaned, stayed" (/d/) · "wanted, needed, started, visited" (/ɪd/, clap the extra beat).
  3. Minimal contrast: T: "I work every day." (point forward) / "I worked yesterday." (point back). Learners say which one: "Now or past?" Then they produce both.
  4. Chain story: S1: "Yesterday I cleaned the kitchen." S2: "Yesterday I cleaned the kitchen and I called my mother." S3 adds one more… Stop at 5 and start a new chain.
The -ed sound sort — how to run it
  • Groups of 3–4, one set of 24 word cards + 3 header cards (/t/ 🤫 · /d/ 🐝 · /ɪd/ 👏). Cards face down in a pile.
  • Turn by turn: take a card, say it out loud with a hand on the throat, put it under a header. Others agree or say "Say it again!" The group may move a card only after saying it again together.
  • When the pile is empty, each learner reads one column aloud. Then turn over the answer strip and check. Talk about the mistakes: "Why is danced /t/?" (The c sounds like s: no buzz.)
  • Round 2 (stretch): each learner takes 3 cards and makes one true sentence about yesterday with each.
ICQs before the sort and the picture story
Sort: Do you read the word quietly or say it out loud? (Out loud.) How many columns? (Three.) What do you say if you are not sure? ("Say it again!")
Picture story: Can your partner see your card? (No.) Who talks first? (A.) What does B do? (Listens and numbers the pictures.) Do you say "clean" or "cleaned"? (Cleaned: it's the past.)
Differentiation
Literacy support

Pictures do the work: in the sort, the teacher or a partner reads each card and the learner decides the sound with fingers. Focus on 8 verbs only (worked, cooked, walked, played, called, cleaned, wanted, visited). Spelling rules are for recognition; copying the verbs is enough. Freer task: 4 sentences with picture cards is a success.

Stretch

Add the doubling rule for longer words for recognition only (preferred, traveled; British travelled). In the freer task, use 8+ verbs, a time for each, and after that / later. Retell the partner's day in the third person to a new partner: "Ali worked, then he…"

L1 watch-outs
  • "Work-ed", "play-ed" (an extra syllable on every verb): learners who read English "as it is written," e.g. Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Arabic, Swahili speakers. Clap the beats: worked = 1, wanted = 2.
  • No ending at all: "Yesterday I walk to the park." (Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai, Korean, Japanese; Vietnamese speakers also drop final consonants in general.) Make the ending visible: put your hand out and "catch" the -t. Accept progress, not perfection.
  • An extra vowel after the ending: "worked-o", "play-do" (Japanese, Korean, Brazilian Portuguese: words don't end in a consonant cluster). Model the "quick" ending and let it be soft.
  • l / r: played / prayed, collected / corrected (Japanese, Korean, some Chinese speakers). Only correct it when the meaning changes.
  • Spelling transfer: "studyed", "plaied", "stoped", "visitted". Point to the rule on the board, not the learner.
  • "He cookeds" / "She workeds": learners who over-apply the present -s from A1. "Good memory! But in the past: no -s for anyone."

Meaning · Form · Pronunciation

Meaning. Same meaning as 1.2: a finished action in the past. Irregular verbs only change the positive form. In negatives and questions, every verb is the same: did / didn't + base verb.

"I didn't go to the party." Did I go? (No.) Which word shows the past? (Didn't.) So do we need "went"? (No: go.)
"Did you see the game?" Is it a question about now or the past? (The past.) The answer is yes. What do you say? ("Yes, I did." not "Yes, I saw.")
"Were you at home?" / "Did you stay home?" Which one uses the verb be? (Were you…) Can we say "Did you were…"? (No! Be never uses did.)

Form.

Positiveregular: verb + -ed · irregular: learn the formI played. / I went.
Negativesubject + didn't + base verbShe didn't have breakfast. We didn't watch TV.
QuestionDid + subject + base verb…?Did you go out? Did she eat lunch?
Short answerYes, I did. / No, I didn't.(don't repeat the verb)
Wh-Wh- + did + subject + base verb…?What did you do? Where did they go? Who did you meet?

Pronunciation. Did you joins in natural speech: /dɪdʒə/ ("Didja go?"). What did you sounds like "Whadja" /ˈwʌdʒə/. Learners should understand this; they can say it slowly. didn't = /ˈdɪdnt/: the final t is often silent, so the listener hears "didn'". Stress the main verb and the negative: "I DIDN'T GO." Short answers are strong: "Yes, I DID." Irregular vowel sounds to drill: saw /sɔ/, bought, thought /ɔt/ (the gh is silent), said /sɛd/ (not "sayed"), read /rɛd/, ate /eɪt/.

Drill script
  1. Base ↔ past ping-pong: T: "go" → S: "went." T: "bought" → S: "buy." Speed up; mix directions. Then pairs do the same with the memory cards face up.
  2. Negative transformation: T: "I went to the market." (shake head) → S: "I didn't go to the market." T: "She had lunch." → S: "She didn't have lunch." Listen for "didn't went."
  3. Question and short answer, open pairs: S1 → S2: "Did you watch TV last night?" S2: "Yes, I did. / No, I didn't." S2 → S3: "Did you cook yesterday?"… around the room.
  4. Wh- question build: write the answer, learners make the question: "I went to the beach." → "Where did you go?" "I met my cousin." → "Who did you meet?" "I bought shoes." → "What did you buy?"
  5. be or did? T: "at home" → S: "Were you at home?" T: "stay home" → S: "Did you stay home?" T: "tired" → "Were you tired?" T: "sleep well" → "Did you sleep well?"
The irregular-verb memory game — how to run it
  • Groups of 3–4. Shuffle the 32 cards (16 base + 16 past) and place them face down in rows. White cards are base verbs; shaded cards are past forms.
  • Turn by turn: turn over 2 cards and say both words out loud. A match = a base card + its past (buy / bought). To keep the pair, say a sentence with the past verb about last weekend: real, a new identity, or silly ("I bought a cat"). The group checks the sentence. Match → play again. No match → turn them back.
  • Most pairs at the end wins. Then each learner turns their pairs into negatives: "I didn't buy a cat."
  • Easier: play face up (a matching race) with 8 pairs. Harder: add the 4 bonus pairs, and the sentence must include a time word.
ICQs before the memory game and the mingle
Memory game: How many cards do you turn over? (Two.) Do you say the words or only look? (Say them.) What do you do to keep a pair? (Say a sentence in the past.) If it's not a match? (Turn them back.)
Mingle: How many people do you talk to? (Four.) What's the first question? ("What did you do last weekend?") Do you need to talk about your real weekend? (No. Real, a card, or a dream weekend.) Do you write long sentences? (No, notes: 2–3 words.)
Differentiation
Literacy support

Play the memory game in a picture-only version: the teacher or a partner reads each card. Learn 8 verbs first (went, had, ate, drank, saw, bought, made, took). In the mingle, ask only 2 questions and draw notes (🛒 🍲 🌳). Allow "I go… went to the park" self-repair: that is a success.

Stretch

Add the bonus pairs and the families (bring → brought, teach → taught, pay → paid, sell → sold, begin → began). In the mingle, ask two follow-ups ("Who did you go with? What did you eat?"). Tell the one-minute story with one was/were sentence, one negative and one because.

L1 watch-outs
  • "I didn't went" / "Did you went?": the #1 past-tense error, for all L1s. Use the board rule: "1 past word is enough." Correct it every time in controlled practice; let it go during the mingle and note it for feedback.
  • "I goed, buyed, eated": this is a good sign (the learner knows the -ed rule). Praise the rule, give the irregular form.
  • "You went to the park?" with rising intonation and no did (Spanish, Portuguese, French, Russian, Arabic, Chinese). Understandable; recast: "Did you go to the park?"
  • "What you did yesterday?" / "Where you went?" (many L1s keep statement word order in questions). Drill the question frame: What + did + you + base verb.
  • "Went to the park" with no subject (Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Arabic, Japanese, Korean, Chinese can drop the subject). "Who went? I went."
  • "Did he was there?" / "Were you go?": mixing be and did. Use the "be or did?" drill.
  • bought / brought, thought / taught: they sound alike for many learners. Teach bought first; brought is stretch.
