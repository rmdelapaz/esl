Module 4 — Experiences & Advice: Answers

Answers to every Module 4 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (Find Someone Who, rules information gap, travel role-plays), a model travel story, and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 4.1–4.3Classes C10–C129 self-check items16 exercises
Marking principle

At A2, mark meaning and the target form. In 4.1 the target is the tense choice (present perfect with no time, past simple with a time); a spelling slip in a participle ("writen") is a note, not a wrong answer. American English is the model; British forms (practise, holiday, centre, mustn't in speech, have got to) are acceptable when a learner uses them. Informal American "Did you ever…?" is real English, but mark the present perfect as the expected answer in written exercises.

4.1 · Present Perfect — Life Experiences (C10)

Exercise 1 — Past participles
seeeatgo (experience)writetrydrinktakeridemeetbreak
seeneatenbeenwrittentrieddrunktakenriddenmetbroken

For go, accept gone with a note: it is a real participle, but for experience we use been. Common slips: "drank" (past, not participle), "writen" (one t), "rided," "breaked."

Exercise 2 — Listen to Lina and Tom (Script 4.1-A)
Has Lina ever…?✓ / ✗When?
been to another country✓2021 (Canada)
seen snow✓in 2021 / in Canada / "in the winter"
ridden a horse✓when she was twelve
eaten sushi✗—
met a famous person✗—

Listening trap: Tom has eaten sushi (last week), not Lina. Learners who tick sushi heard the right words for the wrong person.

Exercise 3 — Present perfect or past simple?
  1. have visited (no time word: experience)
  2. visited (last summer = a finished time)
  3. have never eaten (life up to now)
  4. saw (on Saturday)
  5. been (participle after have; went is the past)
  6. lost (two weeks ago: the switch to past simple)
  7. broke (in 2020)
  8. have tried ("I don't remember when": no time, so experience)
Exercise 4 — Questions + follow-ups
  1. Have you ever seen a whale? → Where did you see it?
  2. Have you ever met a famous person? → Who did you meet?
  3. Have you ever cooked for ten people? → What did you cook?
  4. Have you ever ridden a horse? → When did you ride it?

The follow-up uses the base verb after did. Wrong: "Where did you saw it?" "Have you ever see…?" "Did you ever seen…?" "Have ever you…?" (word order).

Exercise 5 — been or gone?

1 gone (she is at the store now) · 2 been (went and came back, three times) · 3 gone (he is at home, not here) · 4 been (experience).

Dialogue — underline / circle (open task)

Present perfect: Have you ever been · I've been · Have you ever tried · I haven't · I've never eaten · I've been to Italy · I haven't seen · he's gone. Past simple: did you go · I went · I ate · I saw. The switch: "Wow! When did you go?" The time question moves the talk into the past simple. (He's flying is the present continuous for a future arrangement, from 3.3.)

Speaking — Interview + Exercise 6 (open)

Check: Have you ever + participle; a short answer; the detail in the past simple with a time word; never without not. Model: I've ridden a horse. I've been to Canada. I've cooked for twenty people. I've never broken a bone. I've never seen a whale. Detail: I went to Canada in 2021.

Find Someone Who (classroom materials, Set B)

Answers are personal. Listen for the pattern in every "yes" box: Have you ever…? → Yes, I have. → When / Where did you…? → I + past simple. Report sentences: Amir has ridden a horse. He rode one in Morocco when he was ten. The participles on the sheet: been, eaten, swum, sung, cooked, lost, won, met, seen, climbed, made, danced, drunk (bonus row: "No, never" gets the name).

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct present-perfect sentence.
A) She has eat sushi.
B) She has eaten sushi.
C) She have eaten sushi.
Why: has (she) + past participle (eaten). A uses the base verb; C uses have with she.
Q2
Which is correct? "I ___ Paris ___."
A) have visited … last year
B) visited … last year
C) have visit … last year
Why: last year is a finished time, so the past simple. The present perfect never takes an exact past time word.
Q3 · new
"Have you ever been to Mexico?" "Yes, I have. I ___ there in 2020."
A) have gone
B) went
C) have been
Why: this is the switch. The experience is open ("Yes, I have"); the detail has a time (in 2020), so the past simple.
Errors you will hear most (4.1)

"I have been there last year." · "Yes, I did." / "Yes, I have been." (short answer) · "She has went." / "I have saw." · "I haven't never…" · "Have ever you…?" · "Where did you went?" (after the switch) · "He has gone to Italy" about someone sitting in the room · "I been there" (no 've).

4.2 · Have to / Must / Should — Rules & Advice (C11)

Exercise 1 — Signs

1 c · 2 d · 3 a · 4 b · 5 e · 6 f.

Accept spoken versions with can't for 1 and 5 ("You can't smoke here"): this is the usual American form.

Exercise 2 — Sam's first day (Script 4.2-A)

1 has to (wear a badge) · 2 doesn't have to (wear a suit) · 3 mustn't (use his phone in the kitchen) · 4 has to (sign a form) · 5 doesn't have to (bring lunch) · 6 should (go to bed early).

For 3, accept can't. For 6, has to is wrong: it's advice from his friend, not a rule.

Exercise 3 — Circle

1 have to · 2 don't have to · 3 mustn't · 4 doesn't have to · 5 have to · 6 has to (he).

Exercise 4 — Fix the mistake
  1. You must show your ticket.
  2. She should see a doctor.
  3. He has to work on Sunday.
  4. She has to work late.
  5. Do I have to sign here? (also correct but formal: Must I sign here?)

Rule: must and should + base verb, no to; the -s goes on has only; must takes no do.

Exercise 5 — Advice (examples; accept any sensible advice)
  1. You should go to bed early. / You shouldn't drink coffee at night.
  2. You should practice every day. / You should listen to the online lessons. / You shouldn't be afraid of mistakes.
  3. You should drink some water and rest. / You shouldn't eat so fast next time.
  4. You should set an alarm. / You should leave home earlier. / You shouldn't stay up so late.
  5. Maybe you should talk to them. / Maybe you should write them a friendly note. / Maybe you should talk to the building manager.

Mark the form: should / shouldn't + base verb. Wrong: "You should to sleep," "You should sleeping," "You must sleep" (grammatical, but too strong for friendly advice: a note, not an error).

Exercise 6 + journal (open)

Model: At work I have to wear a badge. On Sunday I don't have to get up early. I think I should call my family more often. Check has to for he/she and don't have to used for a real choice.

Self-check
Q1
"The concert is free. You don't have to buy a ticket." This means…
A) Buying a ticket is not necessary. It's your choice.
B) You are not allowed to buy a ticket.
C) You have to buy a ticket.
Why: don't have to = not necessary. B is the meaning of mustn't; C is the opposite.
Q2
Your friend has a bad cough. The best advice:
A) You should to see a doctor.
B) You should see a doctor.
C) You shoulds to see a doctor.
Why: should + base verb, no to, no -s, for every person.
Q3 · new
The sign says "No food." You ___ eat here.
A) don't have to
B) mustn't
C) should
Why: the sign says it is not allowed, so mustn't (or can't). A would mean "eating is optional."
Errors you will hear most (4.2)

"You must to go." / "You should to rest." · "She have to work." / "She has to works." · "You don't have to smoke here" (meaning mustn't) · "Do you must…?" · "I must to go now" as a goodbye (teach I have to go / I've got to go) · "You should going…" · very strong advice to friends ("You must see a doctor!"): fine in an emergency, bossy otherwise.

4.3 · Review Project — Telling a Travel Story (C12)

Exercise 1a — Which phrase fits?
  1. A ticket to Boston, please.
  2. I have a reservation. (The name is…)
  3. Excuse me, how do I get downtown?
  4. What time is check-out?

Accept polite variations: "Can I have a ticket to Boston, please?" · "Hi, I booked a room online." · "Excuse me, where is downtown?" · "When is check-out?" Also accept the center / the centre for 3.

Exercise 1b — Dictation (Script 4.3-B)
  1. Excuse me, where's the check-in, please?
  2. What time does the train leave?
  3. Is breakfast included?
  4. Can you say that again, please?

Mark one point per sentence for meaning; ignore a missing comma or capital. where is for where's is fine.

Exercise 2 — Choose the right form

1 went (last summer) · 2 was / were (a lot of shops = plural) · 3 warmer · 4 have never tried ('ve never tried) · 5 have ever had ('ve ever had) · 6 going to visit.

Item 4 keeps the experience in the present (I want to try it next year), like the online lesson — the past perfect is not taught at A2. If a learner writes had never tried in a past story, accept it: "Good. Later you will learn had never too." Item 5: the best… I've ever had is a fixed pattern; I ever had (no 've) is common in American speech and acceptable orally.

Exercise 3 — Sofia's trip (Script 4.3-A)

a) Top to bottom: 5 (next year, the mountains) · 1 (first time in a big city, cold and windy) · 4 (never seen a building so tall) · 2 (train, museum, market) · 3 (busier than the park, a lot of tourists).

b) Heard: first, then, after that, but, so, because, in the end. Not heard: later.

Exercise 4 — Join the sentences

1 so · 2 but · 3 because · 4 so.

Check with the question: result → so; surprise / contrast → but; reason → because.

Story frame + "My Trip" journal — a model answer
① Two years ago I went to San Diego with my sister. It was summer and the weather was hot. ② First, we took the bus from Los Angeles. Then we walked on the beach and visited the zoo. After that we ate fish tacos at a small restaurant. ③ The beach was quieter than the city, and there were a lot of families. The zoo was the most interesting place, but it was very expensive. ④ I've never seen a panda before, so it was amazing. It was the best day I've ever had. ⑤ Next summer I'm going to go back. I think I'll stay for a week.

Open task: score the spoken story with the speaking checklist (classroom materials, Set H). For the written journal, check that the four underlined examples are real: a past (went), a comparative (quieter than), a present perfect (I've never seen) and a future (I'm going to go).

Self-check
Q1
At the station, you want to know when your train goes:
A) How much is the platform?
B) What time does it leave?
C) Do you have any rooms?
Why: leave = go away from the station. A asks a price; C is a hotel question.
Q2
Which is correct for a travel story?
A) Last year I have went to Peru, and it was great.
B) We visited Peru last year. I've never seen mountains like that!
C) I have gone to Peru yesterday.
Why: last year → past simple (visited); the life experience with no time → present perfect (I've never seen). A mixes have with the past form; C uses the present perfect with yesterday. (The online lesson's version of B says had never seen, the past perfect. Accept that form too; it is not taught at A2.)
Q3 · new
Next summer I ___ visit Greece. I bought the tickets!
A) went to
B) am going to
C) have visited
Why: a future plan (tickets bought) → going to (Module 3). A is past; C is an experience.
Errors you will hear most (4.3)

Present tense for the whole story ("Last year I go…") · "I have been there last year" · "It was the best trip I ever had" (OK in speech) / "the best trip I have ever" (no participle) · "a luggage" / "luggages" · "arrive to the hotel" · "more better" / "the most biggest" · "It was very funny" meaning fun · a list with no links ("We go. We eat. We sleep.") · "Excuse me" missing, so a question sounds abrupt.
