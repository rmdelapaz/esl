Module 6 — Joining Ideas Together: Answers

Answers to every Module 6 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (If-chain model, apartment-building information gap, sentence-half strips, model paragraphs), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 6.1–6.3Classes C17–C199 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At A2, mark meaning and structure first. A joined sentence with the right linker and a small slip (a missing comma, "recieve") is a success with a note, not a wrong answer. The comma after an if-clause and before but / so is a writing habit: mark it as a note, never as a grammar error. American English is the model; British forms (watch a film, neighbour, football for soccer, shop for store) are acceptable when a learner uses them. Where several linkers make sense, accept any answer with clear logic.

6.1 · First Conditional — Real Possibilities (C17)

Exercise 1 — Lena's weekend (Script 6.1-A)
1 sunny on Saturday2 rains3 finishes early4 market closed5 gets home Sunday6 has time
cebfad

Is Lena sure about the supermarket? No, because she says might. Accept "No" / "Maybe" / "She isn't sure." Item 5 uses when: she is sure she will get home. Point this out after checking.

Exercise 2 — Write the verb
  1. If it rains, we'll stay home.
  2. If you study, you 'll pass / will pass the test.
  3. If she calls, I'll answer.
  4. We won't be late if we leave now.
  5. If I have time, I 'll help / will help you.
  6. If you don't hurry, you 'll miss / will miss the bus.

Also correct: do not hurry; might pass / might help (the meaning changes to "maybe," but the grammar is right). Wrong: "If it will rain," "If she will call," "you'll to pass," "If I has time," "If you won't hurry."

Exercise 3 — Match + warning / promise / plan
1If you touch the stove, b you'll burn your hand.⚠️ warning
2If you help me today, d I'll help you tomorrow.🤝 promise
3If the weather is nice, a we'll have a picnic in the park.📅 plan
4If you don't take an umbrella, e you'll get wet.⚠️ warning
5If I get the job, c I'll buy you dinner!🤝 promise

Item 5 can also be a 📅 plan if a learner explains it well. Accept it.

Exercise 4 — Change the order
  1. We'll go to the beach if it's sunny. (no comma)
  2. If I finish early, I'll call you. (comma after early)
  3. I'll text you if the bus is late. (no comma)

A missing or extra comma is a writing note. The online lesson treats "If it rains we'll stay home" as a punctuation tip, not a grammar error — do the same: "good grammar, add the comma."

Exercise 5 — Circle

1 When (I will get home tonight: sure) · 2 If (a lot of money is only possible) · 3 might ("I'm not sure") · 4 will ("Don't worry! He's very kind" = sure) · 5 won't (the result needs will not, not the present).

Item 2: a learner who says "When I win…" as a joke about a sure win is using when correctly for their meaning. Laugh, then ask: "Are you sure?"

Speaking — the If-chain (teacher's model, Script 6.1-C)

If it rains, I'll stay home. → If I stay home, I'll cook. → If I cook, I'll eat too much. → If I eat too much, I'll feel sleepy. → If I feel sleepy, I'll go to bed early. → If I go to bed early, I'll get up early. → If I get up early, I'll go for a walk… in the rain!

What to listen for: the result's verb moves into the if-half without 'll ("I'll cook" → "If I cook"), and the he/she -s when a learner uses a card person ("If she cooks, she'll…"). Any logical chain is correct; funny chains are better.

My "If… I'll…" plan (open)

Look for: present simple after if; 'll / won't / might + base verb; one might; one when with something sure. Model: If it's sunny on Saturday, I'll go to the park. If I finish work early on Friday, I'll visit my sister. If I'm hungry after class, I'll buy a sandwich. If my friends are free, we'll watch a movie. If I have time, I might go to the library. When I get home tonight, I'll call my mother.

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) If it will rain, we'll stay home.
B) If it rains, we'll stay home.
C) If it rains, we stay home tomorrow.
Why: present simple after if, will in the result. A puts will in the if-clause; C has no will for a future result.
Q2
Which sentence shows the speaker is sure it will happen?
A) When I get home, I'll call you.
B) If I get home, I'll call you.
C) If I get home, I might call you.
Why: when = it will happen for sure; only the time is open. if = maybe.
Q3 · new
"I'm not sure. If I have time, I ___ come to the party."
A) will
B) might
C) am
Why: "I'm not sure" = a possible result, so might. will is too sure; am come is not a verb form.
Errors you will hear most (6.1)

"If it will rain, …" · "When I will get home, …" · "If it rain, we stay home." (no -s, no will) · "I'll to help you." · "I will helps." · "I call you" for "I'll call you" (the /l/ disappears) · "I won't" heard as "I want" (and the reverse) · "If I will have time…" in the If-chain (Beep!).

6.2 · Relative Clauses — who, which, that, where (C18)

Exercise 1 — who, which or where?

1 who · 2 which · 3 where · 4 who · 5 where · 6 which

Also correct: that in 1, 2, 4 and 6 (people and things). In 2 the pronoun can be dropped ("the phone I bought yesterday"), but the exercise asks for a word. Wrong: that in 3 and 5 (places need where).

Exercise 2 — Make one sentence
  1. I have a friend who lives in Texas.
  2. This is the car which broke down.
  3. That's the school where I studied.
  4. He's the man who fixed my roof.
  5. This is the park where we play soccer.

Also correct: that in 1, 2 and 4; "the school (that / which) I studied at" and "the park (that) we play soccer in" (stretch forms); football for soccer. Wrong: "a friend who she lives," "the car which it broke down," "the school where I studied there."

Exercise 3 — Find the mistake
  1. That's the man who (or that) lives next door. (people → who)
  2. She's the woman who works at the bank. (delete she)
  3. This is the town where I grew up. (place → where; or "the town that I grew up in")
  4. Is this the book that (or which, or nothing) you bought? (never what)

Exercise 5 — Define it! (open)

Models: 1 A nurse is a person who works in a hospital and helps doctors / takes care of sick people. 2 An airport is a place where you catch a plane / planes land. 3 A key is a thing which opens a door / you use to open a door. 4 Open. Check the pronoun matches the icon (person → who, place → where, thing → which/that) and that the word itself is not repeated.

Look for: the right pronoun for each icon, no repeated subject, no comma before the clause. Model: My neighbor Ana is a person who always says hello. My brother is the friend that I call every day. Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) That's the man which lives next door.
B) That's the man who lives next door.
C) That's the man who he lives next door.
Why: who is for people. A uses which (things); C repeats the subject (who = he).
Q2
Which word joins "This is the café. We met there."?
A) where
B) who
C) which
Why: the café is a place, and there becomes where: "This is the café where we met."
Q3 · new
Choose the correct sentence.
A) That's the book I bought yesterday.
B) That's the man lives next door.
C) That's the town that I grew up.
Why: in A, a new subject (I) follows, so that can be dropped. In B a verb follows, so who must stay. C needs where (or "…grew up in").
Errors you will hear most (6.2)

"the man which works…" · "the woman who she works…" · "the book what I bought" · "the town that I was born" · "the café where we met there" · "the man, who lives next door" (comma) · "the lives-next-door man" (word order) · "a place who you buy bread" · "It's a person where…" in Define it! (point at the icon).

6.3 · Linking Words — and, but, so, because, although (C19)

Exercise 1 — Choose the linker

1 so (result) · 2 because (reason) · 3 but (contrast) · 4 and (add) · 5 Although (surprise, starts the sentence) · 6 so (result)

Also correct: Though in 5. Wrong: because in 1 or 6 (it gives a reason, not a result: "I was hungry because I made a sandwich" has the wrong logic).

Exercise 2 — because or so?
  1. I took the bus because it was raining. / It was raining, so I took the bus.
  2. I was late for work because I missed the bus. / I missed the bus, so I was late for work.
  3. She stayed home because she was sick. / She was sick, so she stayed home.

Also correct: Because it was raining, I took the bus. (comma after the first half). Wrong: "It was raining because I took the bus" (logic reversed) · "Because it was raining, so I took the bus" (two linkers).

Exercise 3 — but → although
  1. Although the room was small, it was comfortable.
  2. Although he has a lot of money, he isn't happy.
  3. Although it was cold, we went to the beach.

Also correct: Though…; the although half second ("It was comfortable although the room was small."). Wrong: "Although the room was small, but it was comfortable." A missing comma after the first half is a writing note.

Exercise 4 — Make it smooth (several right answers)
  1. It was cold, so I stayed inside and read a book.
  2. The bus was late, so I took a taxi, but it was expensive.
  3. I love my job because I help people, although it's hard. / My job is hard, but I love it because I help people. / Although my job is hard, I love it because I help people.

Accept any version with logical linkers and no double linker. Literacy-support learners: two ideas joined with one linker is a success ("It was cold, so I stayed inside.").

Sentence builder — Step 2 model paragraphs (topic envelopes)

My job (Rosa): I'm a cleaner at a hospital, and I work at night. The work is hard, but I like it because I help the nurses and patients. I finish at six in the morning, so I sleep in the afternoon. Although the pay isn't great, my coworkers are very kind.

My town (Emil): I live in a small town near the mountains. It's quiet, but there's a big park and a good library. The library is open late, so I study there after work. I like my town because the people say hello. Although the winters are long, the summers are beautiful.

My weekend (Amal): On Saturday I went to the market with my daughter, and we bought fruit and vegetables. It was raining, so we took the bus home. On Sunday I wanted to rest, but my friends visited. Although I was tired, we had a great time because we cooked together.

Groups' versions will differ: check that each linker's logic is right and that although has no but.

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) Although it rained, but we went out.
B) Although it rained, we went out.
C) Although it rained, so we went out.
Why: one contrast word is enough. A adds but; C adds so.
Q2
"I stayed home ___ it was raining."
A) because
B) so
C) but
Why: "it was raining" is the reason. so introduces a result; but a contrast.
Q3 · new
"It was cold, ___ I wore a coat."
A) because
B) so
C) although
Why: wearing a coat is the result of the cold. With because the logic reverses ("It was cold because I wore a coat").
Errors you will hear most (6.3)

"Although it rained, but we went out." · "Because I was tired, so I slept." · "I was so tired so I slept." · "I was tired, because I slept." (reason/result reversed) · "Because it was raining." as a lonely written sentence · "and… and… and…" with no other linker · "althought" / "altough" (spelling) · no comma before but / so (a note).
