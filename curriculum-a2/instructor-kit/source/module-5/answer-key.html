Module 5 — Telling Stories: Answers

Answers to every Module 5 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (picture stories, Story Road), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 5.1–5.3Classes C14–C169 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At A2, mark meaning and form together, but be generous with small slips: "I was studing" is the right tense with a spelling note, not a wrong answer. In when/while sentences, accept any version where the long action is continuous and the short action is simple, in either order. American English is the model; British forms (film, having a shower, travelling, flat, football for soccer) are fully acceptable.

My goal after Progress Test 1 (workbook front page)

Personal; no marking. Check only that the goal is concrete and small ("say five irregular verbs every morning," "use the 🔊 buttons three times a week"). Revisit it privately at the end of C16.

5.1 · Past Continuous — What Was Happening (C14)

Exercise 1 — At eight o'clock last night (Script 5.1-A)
1 Mr. Díaz2 Mrs. Okafor3 her son4 the two students5 Mr. Chen6 the woman on the balcony
d (cooking)a (reading)b (playing the guitar)c (dancing)e (sleeping in his armchair)f (drinking tea)

g (swimming) is extra. Listening traps: the son "wasn't doing his homework," the students "weren't studying," and Mr. Chen's TV "was on, but he wasn't watching it." Learners who write "TV" for Mr. Chen have missed the negative. Replay that line.

Exercise 2 — was or were?

1 was · 2 were · 3 was · 4 were · 5 was · 6 were (my sister and I = we)

Exercise 3 — Make it "No"
  1. They weren't listening carefully.
  2. She wasn't working on Sunday.
  3. We weren't playing outside.
  4. You weren't listening to me!

Also correct: were not / was not (full forms). Wrong: "They didn't listening," "She wasn't work," "We not playing."

Exercise 4 — Word order: questions
  1. What were you doing at nine?
  2. Was she working yesterday morning?
  3. Where were they going?
  4. Was it raining at 6 a.m.?

About you: Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't. (Longer answers are fine: "No, I wasn't. I was watching a movie.") Wrong: "Yes, I was sleeping" as a short answer is not wrong English, but in the drill we want "Yes, I was." "Yes, I did" is the common error.

Exercise 5 — while
  1. While the children were playing, we were watching TV.
  2. While she was studying, her brother was singing.
  3. While I was waiting for the bus, it was raining.

Also correct: the other order without a comma (We were watching TV while the children were playing.). No. 3 can also be "It was raining while I was waiting for the bus." My yesterday (open): check was/were + -ing and a time phrase in each sentence. Model: At 8 a.m. I was taking the bus to work. At 1 p.m. I was having lunch with a friend. At 9 p.m. I was watching TV.

Speaking — The Alibi Game (open)

No fixed answers. Success = 5 time slots completed with was/were + -ing, and detectives ask 6+ correct questions ("What were you doing at 8:30?"). In the report, listen for said + past continuous: "Ana said they were watching TV." Don't correct reported speech beyond that; it is B1.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) I cooking dinner at eight.
B) I was cooking dinner at eight.
C) I was cook dinner at eight.
Why: the past continuous needs two parts: was + verb-ing. A has no was; C has no -ing.
Q2
"___ they watching TV?" — "No, they ___."
A) Were … weren't
B) Was … wasn't
C) Were … wasn't
Why: they takes were in the question and in the short answer. C mixes the two.
Q3 · new
I ___ his name, so I said hello.
A) was knowing
B) knew
C) was know
Why: know is a state verb, not an action you can "photograph," so it stays in the past simple. C is also missing -ing.
Errors you will hear most (5.1)

"I cooking dinner." (no was) · "They was watching TV." · "I was cook." · "Were you sleep?" · "Yes, I did." (for "Were you…?") · "What you were doing?" (statement word order in a question) · "I was knowing / wanting…" · "studing," "runing," "writting" (-ing spelling) · "cookin'" (/ɪn/). Correct the first three; let the /ɪŋ/ go during the Alibi Game.

5.2 · Past Continuous vs Past Simple — when & while (C15)

Exercise 1 — Lena's Rainy Saturday (Script 5.2-A)
a coffeeb parkc bus stopd sunnye umbrellaf readg happyh 🏃‍♀️ run
71583264

The same order works for the Set C Story A cards: park → read on a bench → start to rain → run to the bus stop → wait for the bus / a man arrive → old friend Marco → drink coffee, talk → sun shine again.

Exercise 2 — Past simple or past continuous?

1 was reading · 2 rang · 3 began · 4 was driving · 5 were watching · 6 was sleeping

Wrong: "ringed," "begun" (past participle), "was ringing" for no. 2 (the ring is the short event). Contractions are fine.

Exercise 3 — when or while?

1 when · 2 While · 3 while · 4 when · 5 While · 6 when

No. 2: When I was walking… is also possible English (when = at the time that), so accept it with a smile. The lesson's target is while + the long action. No. 3: the long action is "was running," so while.

Exercise 4 — One sentence with when or while
  1. She was crossing the road when a car stopped. / While she was crossing the road, a car stopped.
  2. We were watching the movie when the baby woke up. / While we were watching the movie, the baby woke up.
  3. I was waiting for the bus when I saw an accident. / While I was waiting for the bus, I saw an accident.

Also correct: …the film… (British). Check the comma when the sentence starts with While. Wrong: "When she was crossing the road, a car was stopping." (the stop is short).

Exercise 5 — Write Lena's story (open)

Check: 5 sentences, one when, one while, continuous for the background and simple for the events. Model: Last Saturday Lena was walking in the park. She was reading on a bench when it started to rain. She ran to the bus stop. While she was waiting for the bus, her old friend Marco arrived. They went to a café, and while they were drinking coffee, they talked for two hours.

Speaking — Something happened! (open)

Success = scene (1–2 past-continuous sentences) + event (past simple, with when or suddenly) + an ending; 6+ sentences; the listener reacts and asks "And then what happened?" For story cards (Set D), any logical ending is correct.

Picture-story cards — Story B, "Omar's Kitchen" (Set C, stretch)

Most logical order: cook pasta / sing → phone ring (his mother) → sit on the sofa / talk → smell smoke / alarm → run to the kitchen / pasta black → order pizza / laugh. Model: Omar was cooking pasta and singing when his phone rang. It was his mother. While he was talking to her on the sofa, the pasta was burning. Suddenly the smoke alarm started. He ran to the kitchen. The pasta was black! In the end, he ordered a pizza and laughed. Accept "sit on the sofa" before "phone ring" if the group explains it ("He was sitting on the sofa when the phone rang").

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) I was walking home when it was starting to rain.
B) I was walking home when it started to rain.
C) I walked home when it started to rain.
Why: the walking is the long background (continuous); the rain starting is the sudden event (simple). A makes the dot continuous; C loses the background.
Q2
"___ she was cooking, the phone rang."
A) While
B) When
C) During
Why: while + the long action. During needs a noun (during dinner), not a subject + verb. B is possible in real English, but while is the best answer here.
Q3 · new
When the movie ended, we ___ home.
A) were going
B) went
C) was going
Why: nothing was in progress; one event happened, then another. Both verbs are past simple. C also has the wrong form for we.
Errors you will hear most (5.2)

"…when it was starting to rain." (continuous for the event) · "While I fell…" (simple for the background) · "During I was sleeping…" · "When I was sleeping the phone was ringing." · "the phone ringed," "I falled," "it begun" · no comma after an opening While… (writing only) · "suddenly" at the end of the sentence. Correct the tense of the event first; the rest can wait.

5.3 · used to — Life in the Past (C16)

Exercise 1 — Carmen, then and now (Script 5.3-A)
1 farm2 walk to school3 long hair4 big city5 bus to work6 hate vegetables7 love vegetables8 stories to her son
THENTHENTHENNOWNOWTHENNOWNOW

Extra question: No, they didn't. (Also fine: "No. They didn't use to have a TV.") Follow-up in class: turn the THEN answers into used to sentences: "Carmen used to live on a farm."

Exercise 2 — used to or didn't use to?

1 used to · 2 didn't use to · 3 used to · 4 didn't use to · 5 used to · 6 didn't use to

The clue is the second half: "but now she loves it" → before, she didn't like it. Wrong: "didn't used to" (see Ex 3).

Exercise 3 — use to or used to?

1 use to · 2 used to · 3 use to · 4 use to · 5 used to · 6 use to

Rule: after did / didn't → use to. This is a writing-only rule; the two forms sound the same.

Exercise 4 — Right or wrong?
  1. ✗ → I didn't use to smoke.
  2. ✗ → She used to live in Madrid.
  3. ✓ We used to walk to school.
  4. ✗ → Yesterday I went to the doctor. (one time = past simple)
  5. ✗ → He used to have a car.
Exercise 5 — Then vs now
  1. I used to live in a village, but now I live in a city.
  2. I used to have short hair, but now I have long hair. (or: …but now it's long.)
  3. I used to play soccer, but now I watch soccer. (or: …but now I just watch it.)

Any subject is fine (She used to…). football is also correct. Your turn (open): check used to + base verb and but now + present simple. Character sentences are just as good as personal ones.

Speaking — Then & Now (open)

Success = 4 THEN sentences with used to / didn't use to, 1+ but now, and 2 correct questions from the partner ("Did you use to…?" — "Yes, I did. / No, I didn't."). Character cards (Set E) list the facts, so partners can check each other. Model (Captain Rosa): I used to be a sailor. I used to live on a ship. I used to eat fish every day. I didn't use to have a phone. But now I'm a chef, and I live in a small apartment.

Story Road board game (Set G) — squares with fixed answers

3 They weren't sleeping. · 5 were · 8 I knew the answer. · 11 While · 12 rang · fell · began · broke · 15 …when it started to rain. · 17 Did you use to play soccer? · 18 use to · 21 I didn't use to like coffee. · 23 Yesterday I saw a movie. All other squares are open: the group decides if the sentence is OK.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) I used to lived in Madrid.
B) I used to live in Madrid.
C) I used to living in Madrid.
Why: used to + base verb. The past is already in used, so the verb stays plain; not past tense (A) and not -ing (C).
Q2
"___ you ___ play soccer?"
A) Did … use to
B) Did … used to
C) Do … use to
Why: questions use did, and after did the -d goes: use to. C is present, but used to is only for the past.
Q3 · new
Yesterday I ___ an old friend.
A) used to meet
B) met
C) use to meet
Why: yesterday = one time, so we use the past simple. Used to is for habits and long states.
Errors you will hear most (5.3)

"I didn't used to…" / "Did you used to…?" (writing) · "I used to lived / living…" · "I use to go to the gym" (meaning usually, now: Spanish, Portuguese, Tagalog) · "Yesterday I used to…" · "Before I play soccer" (no used to) · "I was used to play…" · "He uses to…" (adding -s) · /juːzd tuː/ with a /z/ and a full "to." Correct meaning errors (present habit, one-time event) first; the use/used spelling only in writing.
