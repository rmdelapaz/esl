Module 3 — Talking About the Future: Answers

Answers to every Module 3 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (Plans Mingle, quick-decision cards, calendar information gap), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 3.1–3.3Classes C7–C99 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At A2, mark meaning and form together, but be generous about the choice of future. Going to, will and the present continuous overlap a lot in real English. Mark a choice wrong only when it changes the meaning (e.g. might for a fixed plan, or the present simple for an arrangement). A small spelling slip ("studing") is a success with a note. American English is the model; British forms (film, cinema, flat, mum, at the weekend, Shall we…?) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

3.1 · "Going to" — Plans & Predictions (C7)

Exercise 1 — Nadia's weekend (Script 3.1-A)
visit her auntclean the housesee a moviego shoppingcookdriveworkgo to bed early
✓✗✓✗✓✗✗✓

Traps in the script: Nadia says "I'm not going to clean the house. I cleaned it last week!" (a past verb, a good A2 contrast), and her brother is going to drive, not Nadia. Spoken follow-up: "She's going to visit her aunt. She isn't going to work."

Exercise 2 — Make a "going to" sentence
  1. I'm going to cook dinner tonight.
  2. She's going to study next week.
  3. We're going to travel in the summer.
  4. They're going to move soon.
  5. He's going to start a new job tomorrow.
  6. Are you going to call me tonight?

Also correct: full forms (I am going to…); move house (British). Wrong: "She going to study" (no is) · "She's going to studying" · "Do you going to call me?"

Exercise 3 — Negative, then question
  1. They aren't going to help us. → Are they going to help us?
  2. You aren't going to call me tonight. → Are you going to call me tonight?
  3. He isn't going to learn to drive. → Is he going to learn to drive?
  4. I'm not going to cook tonight. → Are you going to cook tonight?

Also correct: They're not / You're not / He's not going to… (both negative forms are natural). Wrong: "I amn't" · "I'm not going to cooking" · "Is you going to…?"

Exercise 4 — Plan or prediction?

1 E (you can see the glass) · 2 P · 3 E (gray, windy sky) · 4 P · 5 E (the bus is full) · 6 P.

If a learner argues that 4 is also "evidence" (the wedding is booked), praise the thinking: the key question is "Did someone decide it?" Yes → plan.

Exercise 5 — next or in? Then "My plans"

1 next month · 2 in ten minutes · 3 next year · 4 in two days · 5 next Tuesday.

Also correct in 5: on Tuesday (not offered). Wrong: "in next month," "the next year" (for the future from now).

My plans (open). Look for: the right be form, going to + base verb, a time word in each sentence, one negative, one evidence prediction. Model: This weekend I'm going to visit my cousin. We're going to cook together on Saturday. I'm not going to work on Sunday. Next month I'm going to start a computer class. Next year I'm going to learn to drive. Look at the sky: it's going to be a nice day!

Speaking — Plans Mingle (Find Someone Who)

Answers are personal. Check the question form: Are you going to + base verb…? and the short answers Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. In the report stage listen for He's / She's going to… (not "He going to" and not "He's go to").

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) She's going to studying tonight.
B) She's going to study tonight.
C) She's going to to study tonight.
Why: be + going to + base verb. A uses -ing; C has two tos.
Q2
"___ you going to come?" — "No, I'm ___."
A) Are … not
B) Is … not
C) Are … aren't
Why: you takes are; the short "no" with I is I'm not (there is no "I amn't" / "I aren't").
Q3 · new
I'm going to visit my family ___ two weeks.
A) next
B) in
C) on
Why: in + an amount of time counts forward from now. next goes with a time name (next week), not a number.
Errors you will hear most (3.1)

"I going to travel." (no be) · "She's going to studying." · "I'm going to to go." · "Tomorrow I visit my aunt." (present simple for a plan) · "the next week" / "in next month" · "Is you going to…?" · "Yes, I'm." Correct the first three and the missing be every time; let "gonna" go in speech (but not in writing).

3.2 · "Will" & "Might" — Predictions & Decisions (C8)

Exercise 1 — The weather forecast (Script 3.2-A)

Fridaysunny and hot (about 85°F)will (sure)
Saturday afternooncloudy; it might rainmight (not sure)
Sundayrainy and windy (a storm in the evening)will (sure)
Mondaycold; it might snowmight (not sure)

Accept pictures (☀️ ☁️ 🌧️ 💨 ❄️) or single words. Accept "cloudy" alone for Saturday if the learner circled might.

Exercise 2 — will or won't

1 won't · 2 will · 3 won't · 4 will ('ll) · 5 won't.

In 5 the word order is "he probably won't be" (probably before won't, but after will: "he'll probably be"). Stretch point only.

Exercise 3 — might
  1. We might travel next summer.
  2. She might be at the meeting.
  3. They might move to a new city.
  4. It might not rain.

Also correct: Maybe we'll travel next summer. (same meaning). Wrong: "We might to travel" · "She might is at the meeting" · "It mightn't rain" (rare; accept as British but don't teach it) · "It might won't rain."

Exercise 4 — Quick decisions: match

1 c (I'll get it!) · 2 e (I'll carry it for you.) · 3 a (I'll close the window.) · 4 f (I'll get you some water.) · 5 b (I'll help you after class.) · 6 d (I won't, I promise.)

Exercise 5 — will or going to?
  1. 'm going to (a plan decided last week)
  2. 'll (a decision now)
  3. 'll (an offer)
  4. 'm going to (a plan decided this morning)
  5. 'll (a decision now: ordering)

Also acceptable: I'm cooking pasta tonight for 4 (an arrangement; Lesson 3.3). For 1 and 4, "I'll…" is understood but sounds like a new decision; mark it with a note, not as wrong.

Speaking — My 6 predictions (open)

Check: will / won't / might + base verb, no to, no -s. Model: Next year I'll speak better English. I think I'll make new friends. I won't forget my first English class. Phones won't have buttons in the future. I might change my job. It might snow in December.

Quick-decision cards & Crystal Ball game (classroom materials)

Quick-decision cards: any I'll… answer that fits is correct. The suggested answers are printed small on each card. The game is open: check the modal + base verb and the die word (1–2 won't, 3–4 might, 5–6 will).

Self-check
Q1
"___ you be at the party?" — "No, I ___."
A) Will … won't
B) Will … will
C) Do … won't
Why: questions put will at the front, and the short "no" answer is won't. Do is never used with will.
Q2
You planned this last week: a trip to the beach on Saturday. Which is best?
A) I'll go to the beach on Saturday.
B) I'm going to go to the beach on Saturday.
C) I might go to the beach on Saturday.
Why: a plan decided before now → going to. A sounds like a new decision; C means you are not sure.
Q3 · new
"The phone's ringing!" You decide to answer it now. What do you say?
A) I'm going to get it.
B) I'll get it!
C) I get it.
Why: a decision at the moment of speaking → will ('ll). A is for a plan made before; C (present simple) is not a future here and can mean "I understand."
Errors you will hear most (3.2)

"She will comes." · "I will to help you." · "He might to come." · "Maybe I go." · "I will a coffee." (German/Dutch will = want) · "won't" said like "want" · "Yes, I'll." · "Do you will…?" Correct the -s and to after modals every time; treat won't/want as a pronunciation focus, not a grammar error.

3.3 · Future Arrangements & Suggestions (C9)

Exercise 1 — Now or future?

1 N (now) · 2 F (tomorrow) · 3 F (on Sunday) · 4 N (at the moment) · 5 F (next month) · 6 N (Shh! = right now).

Exercise 2 — What's in the calendar?
  1. We're leaving at 6 o'clock.
  2. She's starting her new job next week.
  3. I'm not working tomorrow.
  4. Are you doing anything tonight?
  5. They're flying to Rome on Friday.

Also correct: full forms (We are leaving…). Going to answers ("We're going to leave at 6") are correct English, but the exercise practices the present continuous: accept with a note. Wrong: "We leave at 6" (for this task) · "I'm not work tomorrow" · "Do you doing anything?"

Exercise 3 — Suggest it three ways
  1. Let's go to the beach.
  2. Why don't we go to the beach?
  3. How about going to the beach?
  4. Let's have a picnic in the park.
  5. Why don't we have a picnic in the park?
  6. How about having a picnic in the park?

Also correct: How about a picnic in the park? (a noun). Wrong: "Let's to go" · "Let's going" · "Why don't we going?" · "How about go?" · "How about to have?"

Exercise 4 — Match the invitation and the reply

1 b (a reason for tonight) · 2 c (already has a Saturday plan) · 3 d (busy right now) · 4 a (accepts, asks the time).

Other matches can work too (e.g. 1 → a). Accept any match where the reply fits the time in the invitation. The key point: every "no" has a reason or "Maybe another time."

Exercise 5 — Leo and Grace (Script 3.3-A)
  1. Going to the movies on Friday (evening). (Accept: "see a movie," "the movie theater.")
  2. She's working late on Friday.
  3. On Saturday.
  4. At 11 (eleven o'clock in the morning).
  5. At the market (at the entrance / the front of the market).
  6. At the market (at the food stands).

Why not 9 a.m.? Grace is taking her mom to the doctor at nine. Good listeners may add this.

Calendar information gap (classroom materials)
A is freeB is free
AfternoonsThursdayMonday · Wednesday · Friday · Sunday
EveningsTuesday · Thursday · Saturday · SundayThursday

The only free time for both: Thursday evening. The activity, time and place are the pair's choice. Success = they find Thursday evening, make at least one suggestion with Let's / Why don't we / How about + -ing, and confirm: "So we're meeting on Thursday at seven at…"

Speaking — My week (open)

Check: -ing form in the calendar ("meeting Tom," "working"); in speech, am / is / are + -ing + the day. Model: On Monday evening I'm going to English class. On Wednesday afternoon I'm taking my daughter to the park. On Friday I'm working late. On Sunday I'm having lunch with my cousins. (Note: "I'm going to English class" = present continuous of go; praise it.)

Self-check
Q1
Which is correct after "How about…?"
A) How about go to the park?
B) How about going to the park?
C) How about to go to the park?
Why: How about takes -ing or a noun. Let's / Why don't we / Shall we take the base verb.
Q2
Choose the plan in your calendar (the future).
A) I'm cooking dinner right now.
B) I'm meeting Sara tomorrow at 8.
C) It will probably rain later.
Why: the time word tomorrow at 8 makes the present continuous a future arrangement. A is now; C is a prediction.
Q3 · new
"Would you like to come to dinner on Friday?" Say "no" politely.
A) No.
B) I'd love to, but I'm working on Friday.
C) No, I don't like.
Why: a polite English "no" shows you want to say yes and gives a reason. A sounds rude; C sounds like you don't like the person or the food (and needs an object: "I don't like it").
Errors you will hear most (3.3)

"How about go to the park?" · "Let's to meet at six." · "Why don't we to go?" · "Why you don't come?" · "I meet Sara tomorrow." (for an arrangement) · "Yes, I would like." · "in Friday" · a flat "No." Correct the verb form after the four suggestion patterns every time; model the warm "I'd love to, but…" instead of correcting a direct "no" as a mistake.
