Module 3 — Talking About the Future

Three classes that give learners the whole future toolkit of A2. First comes going to for plans and for predictions you can see coming. Then will and might for guesses, quick decisions, offers and promises. Last, the present continuous for arrangements in your calendar, plus Let's… / Why don't we…? / How about…? to make plans with other people.

3 lessons Lessons 3.1–3.3 Classes C7–C9 Weeks 4–5 90 min per class CEFR A2

How to Use This Guide

  • One lesson block = one 90-minute class. Every class follows the same shape: warmer → lead-in → Meaning / Form / Pronunciation with concept-checking questions (CCQs) → drill (choral, then individual) → controlled practice (workbook) → freer practice (pairs, groups, mingle) → feedback with delayed error correction → homework.
  • The stage plan is your clock. Each one adds up to 90 minutes. The numbers in the Time column are minutes.
  • Present from slides/module-3-the-future.html. Print materials/module-3/classroom-materials.html before C7. It holds the flashcards, the plans mingle (Find Someone Who), the quick-decision cards, the Crystal Ball / Weather Forecaster game, the calendar information gap, the "future identity" cards and the listening scripts.
  • The online lessons are the learners' homework and review. Each one has 🔊 Listen buttons, so learners can hear the model sentences and dialogues again at home.
  • This is extension, not a first meeting. A1 Lesson 6.3 already introduced going to, the present continuous for plans and Let's…. Many learners will recognize the forms. Check what they know in the lead-in and move faster where they are strong. The new A2 work is the choice between the three futures, evidence predictions, will / might, and polite suggestions and replies.
  • American English throughout. The online lessons and this kit both use American English (movie, the movies, apartment, mom, move, practice). Lesson 3.3 still teaches Shall we…? as one of four suggestion patterns and notes that Americans more often say Should we…?; teach Should we…? next to it. Accept British forms when learners use them (film, cinema, flat, mum, move house).
  • Dashed boxes = lines to say out loud, CCQs and ICQs (instruction-checking questions). Green boxes = scripts you read aloud. Amber = watch-outs.
The through-line

My plans → my guesses → our plans. In C7 learners talk about their own plans and intentions (I'm going to…) and predict from what they can see (It's going to rain). In C8 they predict what they think or are not sure about (will / won't / might) and make decisions on the spot (I'll get it!). In C9 they make plans with another person: they suggest, accept or decline politely, find a free time in two calendars, and confirm the arrangement (So we're meeting on Thursday at seven.). The end of every class is the same freer task in a new form: share real (or imagined) plans with a partner.

Trauma-informed: the future is not easy for everyone

For some adult learners the future is uncertain or painful: a visa or asylum decision, a job search, family far away, temporary housing. Never ask "Are you going to stay in this country?", "Where will you live next year?" or "When will you see your family?" Keep tasks to small, everyday plans (this weekend, tonight, next week) and to light predictions (the weather, sports, the class, technology). For every personal task offer three choices out loud: your real plans, a "future identity" card (in the classroom materials), or a dream / imagined future. Anyone can say "Pass!" in the prediction game. Say it every time, so choosing a card is normal and not a sign that something is wrong.

Before the Module

  • Print the picture flashcards (one set for the board): weekend activities, time expressions, weather and the certainty cards. Card stock helps. Cut them before C7.
  • Print one Plans Mingle (Find Someone Who) sheet per learner for C7.
  • For C8: one set of quick-decision cards per pair and one Crystal Ball / Weather Forecaster set per group of 3–4. Bring one die per group (or use a free dice app on a phone). Cut the cards.
  • For C9: one calendar information gap page per pair (cut A from B) and one blank calendar per learner for round 2.
  • Print the future identity cards and keep them on a side table where anyone can take one without asking.
  • Bring a real paper calendar or a calendar / planner (or show your phone calendar on screen) for the C9 lead-in. A toy phone or a real phone for the "The phone's ringing!" demo in C8 is fun.

3.1 · C7

"Going to" — Plans & Predictions

90 min
Objectives — learners can…
  • form be going to + base verb for every person, with short forms: I'm / she's / they're going to…
  • make negatives (I'm not / isn't / aren't going to), yes/no questions with short answers (Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.) and Wh- questions (What are you going to do?)
  • tell apart a plan (decided before now) and a prediction from evidence (Look at those clouds! It's going to rain.)
  • use future time expressions: tonight, tomorrow, this weekend, next week / month / year, in the summer, in two days, soon, and choose next or in
  • describe their plans for next weekend or this year in 6+ sentences

Meaning · Form · Pronunciation

Meaning 1: plans and intentions. A decision made before now. The person has the plan in their head.

"I'm going to visit my sister this weekend." Is this now or the future? (The future.) Did I decide now, or before? (Before.) Is it a plan? (Yes.) Am I sure? (Yes, it's my plan.)

Meaning 2: prediction from evidence. We can see something now, so we know what will happen.

(Point at the cloud picture on slide 6.) "Look at those clouds! It's going to rain." Is it raining now? (No.) Can I see something? (Yes, black clouds.) Did I plan the rain? (No! I can see it coming.)
"She isn't going to come." Is she coming? (No.) · "Are you going to cook tonight?" Can you answer "Yes, I'm"? (No. "Yes, I am.")

Form. Color the be verb in one color and going to + base verb in another. Only the be part changes.

Positivesubject + am / is / are + going to + base verbI'm going to cook. · She's going to study. · We're going to travel.
Negativesubject + 'm not / isn't / aren't + going to + base verbHe isn't going to come. · They aren't going to wait.
QuestionAm / Is / Are + subject + going to + base verb…?Are you going to come? — Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
Wh- questionWh- + am / is / are + subject + going to…?What are you going to do? · Where is she going to go?
Timenext + time name · in + amount of timenext Monday, next year · in two days, in an hour

Pronunciation. In natural speech to is weak: /tə/, and the stress falls on the main verb and the time: "I'm going to VISit my SISter this WEEKend." Model the relaxed form. Learners will hear "gonna" /ˈɡʌnə/ in real life; teach it for listening only. In writing, always going to. Yes/no questions rise at the end ("Are you going to COOK? ↗"); Wh- questions fall ("What are you going to DO? ↘"). Short yes answers are full and stressed: "Yes, I AM." (never "Yes, I'm.")

Drill script
  1. Back-chain: "this weekend" → "visit my sister this weekend" → "going to visit my sister this weekend" → "I'm going to visit my sister this weekend." Choral ×3, half the room, then 4–5 individuals.
  2. Person substitution (point at a learner, a pair, yourself): T: "She… cook dinner" → S: "She's going to cook dinner." T: "They… travel" → S: "They're going to travel." T: "I… study" → S: "I'm going to study."
  3. Negative transformation: T: "I'm going to work tomorrow." (shake head) → S: "I'm not going to work tomorrow." T: "He's going to come." → S: "He isn't going to come."
  4. Evidence prompts: hold up a flashcard or act: umbrella + dark sky → "It's going to rain." · a learner yawning → "He's going to sleep!" · a cup at the edge of the desk → "It's going to fall!"
  5. Open pairs → closed pairs: S1 asks S2 across the room: "What are you going to do this weekend?" → S2: "I'm going to…" → S2 asks S3… Then all pairs, 1 minute.
Plans Mingle — how to run it
  • Everyone stands with the sheet and a pen. Model with a strong learner first: "Are you going to cook something special this weekend?" — "Yes, I am." → write the name → extra question: "What are you going to cook?"
  • One name per box. Ask each person only 1–2 questions, then move on. A "No, I'm not." is fine: say "OK, thanks!" and ask someone else.
  • The sheet has two parts: this weekend and this year / next year. Everyone must get at least 2 names in each part.
  • Learners with a future identity card answer as their card person. Nobody has to explain why.
  • Report back with he/she (this recycles the he/she forms of the be verb): "Omar is going to learn to drive next year."
ICQs before the mingle
Do you stay in your seat or walk around? (Walk around.) · What is the first word of your question? ("Are.") · If the answer is "Yes, I am," what do you do? (Write the name and ask the extra question.) · How many names in each part? (At least two.) · Can you use a future identity card? (Yes.)
Differentiation
Literacy support

Work only with I'm going to and I'm not going to + the flashcard phrases. In the mingle, give a sheet where the questions are already fully written ("Are you going to…?") and learners only write a ✓ and the first letter of the name, or draw a small face. Target: 4 spoken sentences about their plans, with pictures from the flashcards as prompts.

Stretch

Add a reason with because ("I'm going to learn to drive because I want a new job."). Use two time expressions in one plan ("next month, on the weekend"). In the report stage, compare: "Ana and I are both going to travel, but she's going to go by bus." Early finishers write 3 evidence predictions about the classroom right now.

L1 watch-outs
  • "I going to travel" (no be): Arabic, Russian, Chinese, Vietnamese and Haitian Creole speakers (no "be" in present sentences). Point at the be color on the board every time: "I'm going to…".
  • "I go to travel" / "I'm going to traveling": Spanish voy a viajar, Portuguese vou viajar, French je vais voyager map word for word. Also "going to to go." Drill: "going to + plain verb."
  • Present simple for a plan: "Tomorrow I visit my aunt." (Spanish, German, Russian, Chinese, Japanese: the present can mean the future). It is understood; recast with going to.
  • "the next week" / "in the next Monday" / "next two days": Spanish la próxima semana, French la semaine prochaine. Teach "next week" with no the, and in + an amount of time.
  • "Is you going to…?" / "You are going to come?" (no inversion; Spanish, Portuguese, French, Russian use intonation only). Accept for meaning, then recast: "Are you going to come?"

3.2 · C8

"Will" & "Might" — Predictions & Decisions

90 min
Objectives — learners can…
  • use will / 'll / won't + base verb (the same for every person) to make predictions and give opinions: I think it'll be sunny.
  • make an instant decision, an offer or a promise with I'll…: The phone's ringing. — I'll get it!
  • use might / might not + base verb when they are not sure: I might go to the party.
  • ask Will you…? and answer Yes, I will. / No, I won't.
  • feel the difference: plan → going to; decision now or opinion → will; not sure → might
Materials
  • Workbook 3.2 (New words, Grammar box, Exercises 1–5, dialogue, "My 6 predictions")
  • Slides 9–13 · Weather flashcards + the five certainty cards (will ✔✔ → won't) · Quick-decision cards (1 set per pair) · Crystal Ball / Weather Forecaster game (1 set per group of 3–4) + 1 die per group · a phone (real or toy)
  • Listening scripts 3.2-A (the weekend weather forecast) and 3.2-B (sure or not sure?)

Left: a certainty line: 100% will → probably will → 50% might → probably won't → 0% won't. Weather icons ☀️ 🌧️ ⛅ above it.

Center: I / you / he / she / we / they + will ('ll) + base verb · won't = will not · might (not) + base verb · Will you be there? → Yes, I will. / No, I won't.

Right: three uses of will: 🔮 prediction ("I think it'll rain.") · ⚡ decision now ("The phone's ringing." "I'll get it!") · 🤝 offer / promise ("I'll carry it." "I won't forget.")

If you run long

This is the busiest class (three uses of will + might). Keep "will vs going to" to the slide and the two CCQs; the workbook Ex 5 does the rest. Cut the second half of the quick-decision cards. Never cut the Crystal Ball game: it is where might becomes real.

Meaning · Form · Pronunciation

Meaning: prediction / opinion. What I think will happen. No plan, and often no evidence in front of me.

"I think our team will win." Do I know? (No, I think.) Is it a plan? (No.) Is it the future? (Yes.) · "It won't rain tomorrow." Rain tomorrow: yes or no? (No.) Am I sure? (Yes, quite sure.)

Meaning: might. Maybe. 50/50. Not sure.

"I might go to the party." Am I going to the party? (Maybe.) Is it a plan? (No, not yet.) Is "I might go" the same as "Maybe I'll go"? (Yes.) · "We might not come." Are they coming? (Maybe not.)

Meaning: instant decision / offer / promise. I decide at the moment of speaking.

(Phone rings.) "I'll get it!" Did I plan this yesterday? (No!) When did I decide? (Now.) · "That bag looks heavy. I'll carry it." Is this an offer? (Yes, I want to help.) · "I'll call you tomorrow, I promise." Is this a promise? (Yes.)
Will or going to: "I'm going to paint my room this weekend." Did I decide before or now? (Before. It's a plan.) · "Oh, you dropped your pen. I'll pick it up." Before or now? (Now.)

Form. Modal verbs never change: no -s, no to, no do. Learners who just worked hard on the he/she -s need to hear this clearly.

Positivesubject + will ('ll) + base verbIt'll be sunny. · She will come.
Negativesubject + won't (will not) + base verbI won't forget. · They won't be late.
Possibilitysubject + might / might not + base verbI might go. · We might not have time.
QuestionWill + subject + base verb…?Will you be there? — Yes, I will. / No, I won't.
Wh- questionWh- + will + subject + base verb…?What will you do? · When will they arrive?
SoftenersI think… · I'm sure… · probably · maybeI think it'll rain. · He'll probably be late.

Pronunciation. In speech we use 'll: I'll /aɪl/, you'll /jʊl/, it'll /ˈɪtəl/ (two syllables), we'll /wil/. If learners drop the 'll completely ("I help you"), the future meaning is lost, so this matters. won't /woʊnt/ rhymes with don't; want /wɑnt/ has a different vowel. Mix-ups can reverse the meaning ("I won't go" / "I want to go"). might /maɪt/: say the final t. probably is often said "PROB-ly" in fast speech; accept both. Short answers: "Yes, I WILL." (never "Yes, I'll.") "No, I WON'T."

Drill script
  1. Certainty drill (point at a card on the board line): T: "rain tomorrow" + 100% → S: "It'll rain tomorrow." + 50% → "It might rain tomorrow." + 0% → "It won't rain tomorrow." Choral, then individuals, then faster.
  2. won't / want listen-and-show: "I won't eat." / "I want to eat." / "They won't come." / "They want to come." Learners show 👎 or 👍.
  3. Quick-decision chain: T: "I'm thirsty." → S1: "I'll get you some water." → S1 to S2: "It's dark in here." → S2: "I'll turn on the lamp." → around the room.
  4. Question and short answer, open pairs: S1: "Will you be here next class?" S2: "Yes, I will." S2 → S3: "Will it be sunny tomorrow?" S3: "I think it will. / It might."
  5. Make it softer: T: "She will be late." → S: "She might be late." T: "We will travel next summer." → S: "We might travel next summer."
Crystal Ball game — how to run it
  • Groups of 3–4, one card deck face down, one die. Put the certainty cards (won't / might / will) in the middle as a reminder.
  • Player 1 takes a card ("the weather this weekend," "this class next month," "phones in 20 years") and rolls: 1–2 = won't, 3–4 = might, 5–6 = will. They make a prediction with that word and add a reason: "Phones won't have buttons in 20 years, because everything is touch now."
  • The group reacts with one phrase: "I agree!" · "Maybe." · "I don't think so. I think…" The player who reacts best (clear, polite, with a reason) keeps the card. Most cards wins.
  • "You" cards are about the person on your left, and must be kind and light ("You'll have a great weekend." "You might find a great recipe."). Any player can say "Pass!" and take a new card.
  • Some learners are not comfortable with fortune-telling for cultural or religious reasons. The Weather Forecaster deck uses the same rules with weather maps and needs no crystal ball. Offer the choice without comment.
ICQs before the quick-decision cards and the game
Quick decisions: Does B think for a long time? (No! Answer fast.) · What are the first words? ("I'll…") · Is it a plan from yesterday? (No, a decision now.)
Crystal Ball: What do you do first? (Take a card and roll the die.) · You roll a 3. Which word? (Might.) · A 6? (Will.) · A "you" card: a sad prediction or a kind one? (A kind one.) · Don't like a card? ("Pass!" and take a new card.)
Differentiation
Literacy support

Focus on two uses: weather predictions and I'll… decisions. Use the picture certainty cards (✔✔ / ? / ✘) instead of the words. In the quick-decision round, the teacher or a partner reads the situation card aloud; the learner answers from the picture hint on the card. Target: 3 weather sentences (will / won't / might) and 3 quick decisions.

Stretch

Use probably in the right place ("It'll probably rain." / "It probably won't rain."). Use I don't think… will (more natural than "I think… won't"). In the game, the reacting player must disagree with a reason. Early finishers write 3 predictions about their city in 20 years.

L1 watch-outs
  • "She will comes" / "I will to help" / "He might to come": everyone, but especially learners who just drilled he/she -s, and learners whose L1 uses a full verb for "will" (French il viendra, Spanish vendrá). Chant: "will + plain verb."
  • German and Dutch "will" = want: Ich will ein Kaffee → "I will a coffee" (meaning I want). Show the contrast: I want a coffee (now, a wish) / I'll have a coffee (a decision, ordering).
  • won't / want confusion in sound: Spanish, Portuguese, Arabic, Chinese and Vietnamese speakers may say both as /wɑn/ or drop the final t. Vietnamese speakers also often drop the final t in might and won't. Use the 👍 / 👎 game.
  • "Maybe I go to the party" instead of might (Arabic, Chinese, Korean, Tagalog baka / siguro). It is understood. Give the two correct options: "Maybe I'll go." / "I might go."
  • Will for a plan: "I will visit my aunt on Sunday" for a fixed plan (Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, where the future tense does this job). Not wrong, just less natural. Gently offer I'm going to….
  • "I think it won't rain" (direct translation for Russian, Spanish, Chinese and many others). Correct English, but "I don't think it'll rain" is more natural. Stretch only.

3.3 · C9

Future Arrangements & Making Suggestions

90 min
Objectives — learners can…
  • use the present continuous with a time word for fixed arrangements: I'm meeting Sara on Friday at six.
  • tell "now" from "future" by the time word: I'm working now. / I'm working tomorrow.
  • suggest an activity with Let's / Why don't we…? / Shall we…? + base verb and How about + -ing / noun
  • invite with Would you like to…?, accept warmly, and decline politely with a reason (I'd love to, but I'm working on Friday.)
  • arrange to meet: find a free time, agree an activity, and confirm the plan (So we're meeting on Thursday at seven.)

Meaning · Form · Pronunciation

Meaning: arrangement. A fixed plan, often with another person, a time and a place. It is "in the calendar." Learners know the form from A1 (present continuous for now); only the meaning is new.

"I'm meeting Sara on Friday at six." Am I meeting her now? (No.) When? (Friday at six.) Does Sara know? (Yes, we agreed.) Is it in my calendar? (Yes.)
"I'm working now." / "I'm working tomorrow." Which one is the future? (Tomorrow.) What tells you? (The time word.)

Meaning: suggestions and invitations.

"Let's go to the park." Is it only for me, or for you and me? (You and me: together.) · "How about Saturday?" Is it a question or an order? (A question, an idea.) · "I'd love to, but I'm working." Is it yes or no? (No.) Is it polite? (Yes, very.) · "Would you like to come to dinner?" Is it an invitation? (Yes.)

Form.

Arrangementam / is / are + verb-ing + future timeWe're leaving at 6. · She's starting her new job next week.
Negative / question'm not / isn't / aren't + -ing · Am / Is / Are + subject + -ing?I'm not working tomorrow. · Are you doing anything tonight?
+ base verbLet's … · Why don't we …? · Shall we …? (US: Should we …?)Let's eat out. · Why don't we take the bus? · Shall we meet at 7?
+ -ing / nounHow about …?How about watching a movie? · How about Saturday?
InvitationWould you like to + base verb? · (friendly) Do you want to…?Would you like to come to dinner? — Yes, I'd love to!
Replies👍 Good idea! · Sure, that sounds great. · Yes, I'd love to. · 🙏 Sorry, I can't. · I'd love to, but… · Maybe another time. · Thanks, but I already have plans.Negative suggestion: Let's not stay late.

Pronunciation. Arrangements: stress the verb and the time: "I'm MEETing Sara on FRIday." Why don't we is fast and linked: /waɪ doʊn wi/ ("why-dohn-we"), with the t almost gone. How about links: /haʊ əˈbaʊt/ ("how-a-BOUT"). Let's /lɛts/: say the s. Suggestions start high and friendly. A polite no has a fall-rise on love: "I'd LOVE to, but…" Flat, low intonation on "No." can sound rude in English even with polite words: model the warm version.

Drill script
  1. Calendar drill (point at your calendar on the board): T: "Tuesday?" → S: "You're meeting Ana." T: "Thursday?" → S: "You're working late." Then learners ask: "What are you doing on Sunday?" → T answers.
  2. Now or future? T says a sentence; learners point at the floor (now) or over their shoulder forward (future): "I'm cooking right now." / "I'm cooking for my family on Sunday." / "She's sleeping at the moment." / "She's flying to Rome next month."
  3. One idea, four ways: T: "go to the park" → S1: "Let's go to the park." → S2: "Why don't we go to the park?" → S3: "Shall we go to the park?" → S4: "How about going to the park?" New idea each round.
  4. Reply drill: T suggests; T shows 👍 or 👎. Learners reply: 👍 "Good idea!" / "Sure, that sounds great." · 👎 "I'd love to, but I'm busy. Maybe another time."
  5. Confirm: T: "Thursday, seven, the café." → S: "So we're meeting on Thursday at seven at the café. See you then!"
Calendar information gap — how to run it
  • Pairs sit face to face; A and B cards are different. Each calendar has an afternoon and an evening for Monday–Sunday. Most slots are full.
  • Step 1: find a free time. Learners ask about a slot: "Are you doing anything on Monday afternoon?" The partner reads the arrangement: "Yes, I'm going to the dentist." Ask for the next slot or suggest: "How about Tuesday evening?"
  • Step 2: when both are free, suggest an activity from the ideas box (two ways: "Let's… / Why don't we…?"). The partner accepts or says a polite no to the first idea and suggests another.
  • Step 3: agree a time and place ("Shall we meet at seven? At the station?") and confirm: "So we're meeting on … at … See you then!"
  • Answer: the only free time for both is Thursday evening. Pairs who finish fast use the blank calendars (round 2).
ICQs before the calendar information gap
Can you show your calendar? (No!) · What are you looking for? (A free time for both of us.) · What question do you ask? ("Are you doing anything on…?") · When you find a free time, what do you do? (Suggest an activity, agree a time, then confirm.) · How do you say a polite no? ("I'd love to, but…")
Differentiation
Literacy support

Give an icon calendar (🦷 dentist, 💼 work, 🎂 party, ⚽ football) so learners can "read" the slot without reading words, and a phrase strip: "Are you doing anything on…?" / "Yes, I'm… / No, I'm free." / "Let's…!" / "OK! See you then." Target: find the free time and make one suggestion with Let's. Skip round 2 or do it with a partner who writes.

Stretch

Use all four suggestion patterns and Would you like to…? in round 2. Decline twice before accepting, with a different reason each time. Add a place and a reason ("Let's meet at the station. It's easy by bus."). After the task, write the text message that confirms the plan.

L1 watch-outs
  • Present simple for an arrangement: "I meet Sara tomorrow" (German, Russian, Spanish, French, Chinese, Japanese). It is understood; point at the calendar: "In the calendar → I'm meeting."
  • "Let's to go" / "How about go?" / "Why don't we to go?": very common (Spanish ¿Qué tal si vamos?, Portuguese Que tal irmos?, French Et si on allait?). Use the "one idea, four ways" drill and a hand signal for the -ing.
  • "Why you don't come?" (no inversion; Spanish ¿Por qué no vienes?, Arabic, Chinese). Drill the chunk "Why don't we…" as one piece.
  • Saying no: cultures differ. Some learners (Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Filipino, many Arabic speakers) avoid a clear "no" and say "maybe" when they mean no. Others (German, Dutch, Russian, Israeli) may sound very direct with "No." Teach the English middle: a short reason + "Maybe another time." Say that in English, "maybe" is sometimes really "maybe."
  • "Yes, I would like." to Would you like to…? (French, Spanish). Teach the chunk "Yes, I'd love to."
  • "in Friday" / "on the weekend" vs "at the weekend": on Friday, at seven, on the weekend (US). Accept British at the weekend.
