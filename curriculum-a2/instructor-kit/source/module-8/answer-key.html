Module 8 — Experiences, Plans & Review: Answers

Answers to every Module 8 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (for/since sort, to-do list information gap, verb sort, Verb Pattern Race model sentences), what to look for in the life-story project, and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Marking principle

At A2, mark meaning first, then form. A sentence with the right tense and a small spelling slip ("swiming") is a success with a note. American English is the model. British forms (at the weekend, flat, films, neighbourhood) are acceptable. American speakers also use the past simple with just / already / yet (I just ate. Did you eat yet?): accept it in speaking, but the workbook exercises test the present perfect.

8.1 · Present Perfect — for, since, just, already, yet (C23)

Exercise 1A — for or since? (sort)
for ⏳ (a length of time)since 📅 (a start point)
three years · a long time · ten minutes · six months · two weeks2019 · Monday · I was a child · last summer · 9 o'clock

Quick test for learners: "Can you count it?" → for. "Is it on a calendar or a clock, or an event?" → since.

Exercise 1B — Write for or since

1 for two years · 2 since 2019 · 3 since we were children · 4 for three hours · 5 since last June

Item 3 is the trap: we were children is an event (a start point), even though it sounds long.

Exercise 2 — Listen to Ana (Script 8.1-A)
  1. since 2021
  2. for three years
  3. since 2022
  4. for one year (also: for a year)
  5. since last September
  6. for two months

Bonus: Ana lived in Texas for two years. "Lived" (past simple) because it's finished: she doesn't live in Texas now. Accept any answer with that idea: "finished," "not now," "she lives here now."

Check he's = he has + past participle and yet at the end.

Exercise 4 — just, already or yet?

A. 1 just (one minute ago) · 2 already (done) · 3 yet (question, at the end) · 4 yet (negative, at the end) · 5 just (a moment ago)

Item 2: just is also possible if a learner explains "a moment ago," but already is the best answer (Don't cook for me = it's done). Item 5: already is also acceptable.

B. 6 I've just paid the bill. · 7 Have you called the doctor yet? · 8 She's already bought the tickets.

Wrong: "I've paid just the bill." · "Have you yet called the doctor?" · "She's bought already the tickets." (She's bought the tickets already is heard in relaxed speech; accept it, but teach the middle position.)

Exercise 5 — Now or finished?

1 have lived (since 2022, up to now) · 2 ate (yesterday, finished) · 3 worked (in 2019, finished) · 4 Have you seen (yet) · 5 have known (for ten years, still friends)

Item 4: "Did you see the new movie yet?" is common American speech; accept it if a learner argues for it, but the time-word clue (yet) points to the present perfect.

Speaking — How long have you…? / My life up to now (open)

Check: How long have you + past participle (not "How long do you live…?"); for + length, since + start point; just / already in the middle; yet at the end. Model: I've lived in this city since 2021. I've had my phone for two years. I've known my best friend since school. I've just finished my homework. I've already paid my phone bill this month. Have you eaten lunch yet?

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) I've worked here since two years.
B) I've worked here for two years.
C) I've worked here for 2019.
Why: two years is a length of time → for. A uses since with a length; C uses for with a start point (2019 needs since).
Q2
Which sentence puts the word in the right place?
A) Have you finished yet?
B) Have you yet finished?
C) Have you finished already yet?
Why: yet goes at the end of questions and negatives. C uses two time words together.
Q3 · new
I ___ in this city since 2021.
A) live
B) have lived
C) lived
Why: since + a start point, and I still live here → present perfect. A is the common L1-transfer error (Spanish vivo aquí desde…, French depuis); C means it's finished, but since doesn't work with a finished past.
Errors you will hear most (8.1)

"I live here since 2020." / "I am living here for five years." (the #1 error) · "since three years" · "for 2019" · "I've lived here for five years ago." · "I've finished yet." · "Have you yet eaten?" · "I've ate / I've went / I've saw" · "How long do you live here?" · "She's worked" read as "she is worked" · "I've eaten at seven o'clock." (finished time → I ate).

8.2 · Verb Patterns — -ing or to-infinitive (C24)

Exercise 1 — Which column?
+ -ing+ to
enjoy · finish · don't mind · keep · suggestwant · decide · hope · need · plan · promise · would like

5 in the -ing column, 7 in the to column. If a learner asks about like: both patterns are OK; would like is to only.

Exercise 2 — -ing or to?

1 reading · 2 to travel · 3 painting · 4 to pass · 5 waiting · 6 going · 7 to speak · 8 to swim

Spelling notes: swim → swimming (item 8 needs no -ing, but learners who write "to swimming" have mixed both patterns). A spelling slip ("waitting") = right pattern, spelling note.

Exercise 3 — Find the mistake
  1. I enjoy reading. (enjoy + -ing)
  2. I want to go home. (want + to)
  3. I'd like to go out tonight. (would like always + to)
  4. She finished eating. (finish + -ing)
  5. We decided to stay one more night. (decide + to)

Also correct for 1: I like to read / I love reading only if the learner changes the first verb on purpose; the task is to fix the pattern, so prefer I enjoy reading.

Exercise 4 — Lena and Omar (Script 8.2-A)

1 L enjoys dancing · 2 O loves hiking · 3 L doesn't mind cooking · 4 O keeps buying books · 5 L wants to learn to swim · 6 O hopes to visit his brother (in Chicago) · 7 O would like to learn to play the guitar · 8 L has decided to join a swimming pool

Exercise 5 — Write about you (open)

Check the second verb only: 1–2 need -ing (I enjoy playing soccer. I don't mind washing the dishes.); 3–6 need a base verb after the printed to (I want to find a new job, not "to finding"). Model: On the weekend, I enjoy sleeping late. I don't mind cleaning the kitchen. This year, I want to get my driver's license. I've decided to save money. One day, I'd like to visit Canada. I hope to open a small business.

Speaking — Hobbies & plans interview (open)

Check that the answer keeps the pattern of the question: "What do you enjoy doing?" → "I enjoy cooking." "What do you want to do?" → "I want to travel." Reporting with he/she: "She enjoys dancing" (the -s goes on the first verb only).

Verb-pattern sort cards (classroom materials, Set E)

+ -ing: enjoy · don't mind · finish · keep · suggest. + to: want · need · would like · decide · hope · plan · promise · learn · try. both: like · love · hate.

Stretch note (don't test at A2): try + -ing also exists with a different meaning ("try adding salt" = experiment); try to = make an effort. stop + -ing / stop + to also differ. Keep to the lesson's list.

Self-check
Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
A) I want travelling this year.
B) I want to travel this year.
C) I want to travelling this year.
Why: want + to + base verb. C mixes both patterns.
Q2
Which sentence is correct?
A) She enjoys dancing.
B) She enjoys to dance.
C) She enjoys dance.
Why: enjoy + -ing. B is the common Romance-language error; C (no change) is common for Chinese and Vietnamese speakers.
Q3 · new
"I'd like ___ a coffee, please."
A) having
B) to have
C) have
Why: would like always takes to, even though like alone can take -ing. C (no to) is common for German speakers (ich möchte haben).
Errors you will hear most (8.2)

"I enjoy to read." / "I suggest to go." / "She finished to eat." (the #1 error) · "I want go." / "I enjoy cook." · "I'd like going out." · "I would like go." · "I want that I go." · "I decided stay." · "He keeps to forget." · "I want to travelling." · spelling: "swiming," "danceing," "makeing" · "sing-g" (hard g). Correct the pattern; let the pronunciation go during the game.

8.3 · Review Project — My Life Story & Future Plans (C25)

Exercise 1 — Listen to Maya's story (Script 8.3-A)
  1. In a small town by the sea. Her family used to run a little café.
  2. For four years — since she started university.
  3. No, not yet. She's going to apply to a design studio next week.
  4. To open her own studio one day, maybe back by the sea. If she gets the job, she'll stay in the city for a while.

Circle (heard): used to run · were cooking · I've lived · I've just finished · I'm going to apply · I'll stay · If I get. Not heard: was working · I lived · If I will get (that one is also wrong grammar!). Accept short answers ("by the sea; a café").

Exercise 2 — Past, up to now, or future?
#AnswerTense
1Ppast simple (grew up)
2Npresent perfect + since
3Fgoing to (a plan)
4Pused to (old habit / state)
5Npresent perfect + just
6Ffirst conditional
7Ppast continuous + past simple (when)
8Fwill (a prediction)
9Npresent perfect + yet
Exercise 3 — Choose the right form

1 used to · 2 have lived · 3 was working (in progress when the phone rang) · 4 are going to · 5 will (first conditional; 'll is fine)

Item 3 is adapted from the online lesson so that the past continuous has a clear "interrupted action" context (Module 5.2).

Exercise 4 — Correct the mistake

1 for three years · 2 since 2015 · 3 want to travel · 4 used to play · 5 yet (negative → yet at the end) · 6 If it rains

Item 5 uses a negative, where already is truly wrong (the online lesson does the same). Note: already in a question ("Have you finished your homework already?") is correct English — it shows surprise.

Story-planning frame and story circles (open)

Success at A2 = three clear parts, 10+ sentences, understood by the group. Look for at least: one past simple + one used to or past continuous (part 1); one for / since (part 2); one going to or will + one If…, I'll… (part 3); one verb pattern (I'd like to…, I want to…, I enjoy…). Key words (not full sentences) in the frame are the right way to plan. Literacy support target: 6 sentences, 2 per part.

Model (the online capstone, adapted): I grew up in a small village in the mountains. When I was a child, I used to walk to school with my brother, and my parents were working on a farm. Life was simple and happy. I've lived in the city for three years now, since I came here for work. I've learned a lot, and I've just started an evening English class. Next year, I'm going to look for a better job. I think I'll study more, too. If I improve my English, I'll apply for a job in an office. One day, I'd like to visit every country I've read about!

Don't mark the content, the story choice (real / partly fictional / card), or the length of the past part. A learner who starts at "up to now" has completed the task.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence talks about a past habit?
A) I used to play the piano.
B) I used to played the piano.
C) I going to play the piano.
Why: used to + base verb. B adds -ed twice; C is a future form with am missing.
Q2
"If I ___ the exam, I'll celebrate."
A) pass
B) will pass
C) passed
Why: first conditional: If + present simple, will + verb. No will in the if part.
Q3 · new
I've already signed up. Next year I ___ study nursing.
A) am going to
B) used to
C) have
Why: a decided plan (I've already signed up) + next year → going to. B is the past; C ("I have study") is not a tense.
Errors you will hear most (8.3)

"I am living here since 2019." · "When I had 20 years…" (age with have) · "I used to played" / "I use to" · "If I will get the job…" · "I going to open a shop" (no be) · "I moved here for five years" (→ I've lived here for five years) · no tense change at all, with time words doing the work (Chinese, Vietnamese, Thai speakers) · "I grew up in small town" (article). In the report stage, board only 3–4 of these; praise the stories first.
