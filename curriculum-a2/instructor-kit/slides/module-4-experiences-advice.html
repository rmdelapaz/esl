4.1 · The key idea · open, then explain

No time → present perfect. A time → past simple.

A: Have you ever been to Mexico? (open)

B: Yes, I have.

A: When did you go? (explain → past simple)

B: I went in 2019. It was great!

4.1 · Check the meaning

"I've been to Mexico."

Did I go? (Yes.)  ·  Do we know when? (No.)  ·  Is the time important? (No, only the experience.)
"I went there in 2019." Do we know when? (Yes.) Present perfect or past simple? (Past simple.)
"I've never broken a bone." In my whole life? (Yes, never.) Do I need "not"? (No!)
"She's gone to the store." Is she here? (No.) "She's been to the store." Is she here? (Yes, she's back.)
WATCH I have been there last year ❌ → I went there last year ✅
4.1 · Model dialogue

Have you ever been to another country?

A: Have you ever been to another country?

B: Yes, I have! I've been to Thailand.

A: Wow! When did you go?

B: I went there in 2018. I ate amazing street food. Have you ever tried Thai food?

A: No, I haven't. I've never eaten it. But I've been to Italy!

B: Where's your brother today? I haven't seen him.

A: Oh, he's gone to the airport. He's flying to Italy tonight!

4.2 · Rules, choices & advice

Three lights + one good idea

🟢 do itI have to wear a badge. · She has to work on Saturday. · You must stop.
🟡 your choiceYou don't have to pay. It's free. · He doesn't have to cook.
🔴 not allowedYou mustn't smoke here. · (US speech: You can't smoke here.)
💡 adviceYou should see a doctor. · You shouldn't worry. · What should I do?
❓Do I have to sign? — Yes, you do. / No, you don't.
+ BASE VERB must go · should go · has to work — no "to" after must / should
4.2 · Check the meaning

don't have to ≠ mustn't

🟡 "You don't have to come."

= It's your choice. Come if you want. 🙂

🔴 "You mustn't come."

= Not allowed. Don't come! 🚫

"You don't have to pay." Can I pay if I want? (Yes.) Do I need to? (No.)
"You mustn't smoke here." Is it my choice? (No. Not allowed.)
"You should see a doctor." A rule? (No. A good idea.)
"She has to work." Does she need to? (Yes. It's her job.)
WATCH You must to go ❌ · She have to work ❌ · Do you must…? ❌
4.2 · Model dialogue · advice

What should I do?

A: I'm not feeling well, and I start my new job tomorrow.

B: Oh no. You should drink some water and go to bed early.

A: Good idea. Do I have to wear a suit?

B: You don't have to wear a suit. But you can't be late on your first day!

A: Got it. Should I bring anything?

B: You should bring your ID. And you have to sign a form at reception.

SOFT Maybe you should… · I think you should…
4.2 · Group task · 15 min

The problem page: "Ask Ana"

  • Groups of 3: 1 reader, 2 advisers
  • Reader: read the card: "Dear Ana, I'm always tired…"
  • Advisers: You should… / You shouldn't… + You have to… / You don't have to…
  • Reader: choose the best advice. Swap roles!
Who reads the card? (The reader.)
Advice word: "should" or "must"? (Should.)
How many ideas from each adviser? (One or two.)
Is it your real problem? (No. It's on the card.)

4.3 · Travel phrases · be polite

Excuse me, … please.

You: Excuse me, a ticket to Denver, please. What time does it leave?

Clerk: At 9:15, from platform 4.

You: And what time does it arrive?

Clerk: At 11:40. Here's your ticket.

You: Hello, I have a reservation. The name is Silva.

Desk: Welcome! Here's your key card.

You: Is breakfast included? What time is check-out?

Desk: Yes, from 7 to 10. Check-out is at 11.

WATCH a luggage ❌ · luggages ❌ → some luggage · two bags ✅
4.3 · Review · the story road

Every tense has a job

① sceneIt was spring. The streets were busy.
② what I didWe arrived at night and took a taxi.
③ compareThe beach was quieter than the city. There were a lot of tourists.
④ experienceI've never seen the ocean before. It was the best trip I've ever had.
⑤ next timeNext year I'm going to go back. I think I'll stay longer.
GLUE first · then · after that · but · so · because · in the end
4.3 · Plan → practice → tell

My travel story

  • Plan: the story frame. Notes, not sentences. Real, a day trip, a trip card, or a dream
  • Practice: tell a partner. Partner asks: Where did you go? What did you do? How was it? Have you ever…? What's next?
  • Tell again to a new partner, faster
  • Story circle: 3–4 minutes each. Listeners ask one question.
Notes or full sentences? (Notes.)
Does it have to be a real trip? (No.)
Which step uses "I've never…"? (Step 4.)
What do listeners do? (Listen, then ask one question.)
