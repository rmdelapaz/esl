2.1 · Comparatives · compare TWO

cheaper than · more expensive than

1 syllablecheap → cheaper thanThe bus is cheaper than the train.
1 syllablefast → faster thanA plane is faster than a car.
2+ syllablesexpensive → more expensive thanThe hotel is more expensive than the hostel.
2+ syllablesdifficult → more difficult thanChinese is more difficult than Spanish for me.
TEST Clap it: cheap = 1 → -er · ex-pen-sive = 3 → more

2.1 · Check the meaning

"The bus is cheaper than the train."

How many things? (Two.)  ·  Which one costs less? (The bus.)
"This is the cheapest phone in the shop." How many phones? (Three or more.)  ·  Is there a cheaper phone? (No.)
"Tom is as tall as his father." Who is taller? (Nobody. They are the same.)
WATCH cheaper ✅ · more cheaper ❌ · the biggest ✅ · the most biggest ❌ · than ✅ then ❌
2.1 · Model dialogue · listen and complete

Where should we eat?

A: Where should we eat? There are three places near here.

B: Well, the pizza place is the cheapest, but the food isn't great.

A: The Thai restaurant is more expensive than the pizza place, right?

B: Yes, but it's much better. Honestly, it's the best food in the area.

A: And the sushi bar?

B: It's the most expensive, and it's farther away too. Let's go Thai: better than sushi and cheaper!

2.2 · some & any · A1 review + one new use

some · any

(+)someThere's some cheese. We bought some eggs.
(−)anyThere isn't any bread. There aren't any eggs.
(?)anyIs there any milk? Do you have any tomatoes?
offer / asksomeWould you like some tea? Can I have some water?
"Would you like some tea?" A question? (Yes.) Why "some"? (It's an offer. We think the answer is yes.)

2.2 · Model dialogue · at the shop

How much rice?

Shopper: Excuse me, do you have any fresh bread?

Seller: Yes, we have some over here. How many loaves would you like?

Shopper: Just one, please. And I need a few tomatoes and some rice.

Seller: How much rice: a small bag or a big one?

Shopper: A small bag is fine. We don't need much.

Seller: Would you like some coffee too?

Shopper: No, thanks. We have a lot of coffee at home!

2.3 · Group game · 13 min

Adverb mime game

  • Take 2 cards: one action + one adverb
  • Mime it. Don't speak! (At your desk is fine.)
  • Group guesses: "You're drinking coffee nervously!"
  • Right action + right adverb = keep the cards
