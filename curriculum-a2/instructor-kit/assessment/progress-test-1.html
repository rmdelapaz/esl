Progress Test 1 — Modules 1–4

A friendly halfway check after Lesson 4.3. It covers the past simple (was/were, regular -ed, irregular verbs, didn't and Did…?), comparatives and superlatives, quantifiers, adverbs of frequency and manner, the three futures (going to, will/might, present continuous for arrangements) with suggestions, the present perfect for experiences, and have to / must / should. It tells you what to recycle in Weeks 7–13. It does not rank anyone.

⏱ ~45–50 min written + 6 min pair speaking Written /55 · Speaking /15 CEFR A2
Why a progress test, and how to frame it

Your learners have already done an A1 course or a placement test, so a test is not new to most of them, but it can still bring back bad memories of job screening or official interviews. Say it plainly: "This is a check, not an exam. It shows me what to teach next, and it shows you how much you know now." Go through the icons on the board, do every example item together, and tell the class they can ask you to repeat an instruction at any time. The listening is read twice, at a natural but unhurried pace.

What each part checks
  • Listening /12: hearing past and future forms, times and prices, key words, a short story
  • Words /10: travel and food words (pictures) + time expressions
  • Grammar /15: one or two items for every lesson in Modules 1–4
  • Reading /8: an email about a trip

  • Speaking /15: talk about the past and make a plan (pairs)
Literacy-adapted version (learners still building reading and writing)

Use the same paper and change how it is done, not what it tests. Read every instruction and every item aloud, one at a time, and enlarge the paper if you can. In Part 2 learners may point to the picture while you say the word; mark it correct if they point correctly or copy the right word from the box. In Part 3 read the choices aloud and let them circle the one that sounds right. Do Part 4 orally: read the email aloud twice and ask the questions. In Part 5 the learner may say the message and the weekend sentences while you scribe; score what they say. Give extra time, or split the paper over two sittings. Write "Adapted" at the top and note what changed. Report these learners' listening and speaking as the main evidence. Low literacy is not low ability. It points to a literacy-support need, and the key says how to act on it.

Personal information is always optional

The writing and speaking tasks ask about last weekend, trips, and plans. Learners can answer with a "new identity" (the cards on the teacher pages) or anything they invent. Say this before the test starts: "Real or imagined, no problem." We are testing the English, not the facts. Never ask follow-up questions about home country, family members who are absent, or immigration status.

Pair Speaking Task · 6 minutes

Two learners sit with you. Give Card A to one and Card B to the other. Give them one minute to read the cards silently, then check the instructions:

Do you only answer, or do you ask questions too? (Both.) Part 2: do you make a plan together? (Yes.) Real weekend or imagined? (Real or imagined, no problem.)

Part 1 (about 3 minutes): they ask and answer the card questions about the past and experiences. Part 2 (about 3 minutes): they make a plan together for next weekend and say it back to confirm. Step in only to restart a stuck pair ("Now ask about next weekend"). Don't correct during the task. Score each learner on the grid, using the rubric in the key. Mixed-level pairs: score each person on what they did, not on the pair's result.

Card A · Student A

👥 Part 1 · Ask your partner

  1. How was your weekend?
  2. What did you do on Saturday?
  3. Did you cook / go out / see friends?
  4. Have you ever been to another city in this country? → When did you go?
  5. Answer your partner's questions. Say 2–3 sentences for each one.

📅 Part 2 · Make a plan

  1. Suggest something for next weekend: How about …ing? / Let's …
  2. Agree on a day and a time.
  3. Say the plan again: So we're meeting at … on …
Card B · Student B

👥 Part 1 · Ask your partner

  1. Where were you last Sunday?
  2. What did you eat? Was it good?
  3. What's the best place to eat in this town?
  4. Have you ever eaten food from another country? → Did you like it?
  5. Answer your partner's questions. Say 2–3 sentences for each one.

📅 Part 2 · Make a plan

  1. Answer your partner's idea: say yes, or say "I'd love to, but …" and suggest another idea: Why don't we …?
  2. Agree on a day and a time: Shall we meet at …?

After the test: close the loop in Week 7

Score the papers before Class 14 with the key. Add up each section across the class, because the weakest section tells you what to recycle. Warmers in Classes 14–19 are a good place for it (for example, a 5-minute irregular-verb chain if many missed items 3–4, or a was/were story circle, which also prepares Lesson 5.1). Give each learner their paper back with one thing they did well and one thing to practice, plus the online lesson that practices it. The key has a class analysis grid and feedback phrases.
