Final Test — A2 Exit Check

The end-of-course check on all eight modules, taken on the last day, which is also a celebration. It shows each learner what they can now do in English (tell a story, make plans, get around town, see a doctor, talk about their life up to now) and gives you the evidence for the next step: Independent English (B1), B1 with a review plan, or another round of A2 with more support. It is never a pass/fail verdict on a person.

⏱ ~60 min written + ~6 min interview each Written /85 · Speaking /15 · Total /100 Covers Modules 1–8
Frame it as "show what you can do"

By Class 26 your learners have met you 25 times. Use that trust: "Thirteen weeks ago you told me about yesterday. Today you can tell me your whole life story, and your plans!" Do every example item together, keep the room warm and unhurried, and plan the celebration so it happens whatever the scores are. Every learner gets a certificate of attendance. The test decides the next class, not who gets to celebrate.

For a large class, start interviews in the second half of Class 25, during the review project.

What each part checks
  • Listening /15: numbers, times, and directions; key words; a phone call to a clinic
  • Words /15: town, travel, restaurant, clothes, and health words (pictures) + odd one out
  • Grammar /20: one item for each main structure in Modules 1–8
  • Reading /15: a life story + a train timetable
  • Writing /20: a message to the teacher + "Then, now, and next"
  • Speaking /15: an individual interview with a role-play
Literacy-adapted version

Same paper, different delivery. Read all instructions and items aloud, enlarge the paper, and give extra time or split it over Classes 25 and 26. Part 2: accept pointing or copying from the word box. Part 3: read the choices aloud. Part 4: read the story and the timetable aloud and ask the questions orally (times and prices can be pointed to). Part 5: the learner says the message and the "then, now, and next" sentences while you scribe, and you score Range and Accuracy from what they say. Mark the paper "Adapted". For these learners, the interview and listening are the main evidence for placement. The key explains how to recommend B1 together with literacy support instead of holding someone back for reading and writing alone.

Real or imagined, always

The interview and Part 5B ask about childhood, where people lived, and how long they have been here. For some learners those are painful or private questions. Say before you start: "Real or imagined, no problem. You can tell me about a new person." New-identity cards from the progress test work here too. Never ask follow-up questions about home country, family members who are absent, or immigration status. If a learner says "I don't want to talk about that," thank them and move to the next stage.

C · A phone call to a clinic (read the whole dialogue twice, then give 1 minute)

Receptionist: Good morning, Green Street Clinic. How can I help you?

Man: Hello. I'd like to make an appointment, please.

Receptionist: Of course. What's the problem?

Man: I've had a bad cough for five days, and I feel very tired.

Receptionist: I'm sorry to hear that. Can you come tomorrow at ten?

Man: Sorry, I'm working tomorrow morning. Is there anything later?

Receptionist: Yes, there's an appointment at a quarter past three.

Man: Perfect. A quarter past three is fine.

Receptionist: And what's your name, please?

Man: David Moreno. That's M – O – R – E – N – O.

Receptionist: Thank you, Mr. Moreno. If your cough gets worse, you should call us again.

Individual Speaking Interview · ~6 minutes

Sit beside the learner, not across a desk, with the grid on your lap. Speak at a natural pace. Ask the core question first. If there's no answer after about 5 seconds, use the support version (a choice question or a model). If the answer is easy, add the stretch question. At A2, listen for answers of two or three sentences joined with and, but, so, because, not single words. Don't correct during the interview. Note one strength and one next step instead.

1

Hello & up to now

~1 min
Hello! How are you today? How was your weekend?
How long have you lived in this town? (real or imagined) How long have you studied English?

Support: "For one year or for five years?" · Stretch: "What have you done this week?" (listen for just / already / yet)

2

The past & a story

~1½ min
When you were a child, what did you use to do after school?
Tell me about a time something happened: a surprise, a small accident, a funny moment. What were you doing when it happened?

Support: "Did you use to play outside? Did you help at home?" · model: "I was walking home when it started to rain." · Stretch: "How was life different then? What has changed?" (used to … but now …)

3

Pictures: describe & compare

~1 min

Show the picture prompts below.

(bus, station, airport) You want to visit a friend in another city. Which is cheaper, the bus or the train? Which is the fastest? Which do you prefer, and why?
(restaurant) Tell me about a restaurant or café you like. What's the best thing on the menu?

Support: "Is the bus cheaper or more expensive?" · Stretch: "Is there a place in this town that people should visit? Why?" (relative clause: a place where…)

4

Plans & possibilities

~1 min
What are you going to do after this course? What are you doing this weekend?
If you have a free day next week, what will you do?

Support: "Are you going to study more English? Are you going to rest?" · Stretch: "What do you think your life will be like in five years?" (will / might)

5

Role-play + your questions

~1½ min

Let the learner choose one role-play card below. You play the other person. Then invite two questions.

(Clinic) Good morning, Green Street Clinic. How can I help? … What's the problem? … How long have you had it? … Can you come on Monday at nine? … You should rest and drink a lot of water.
(Restaurant) Good evening! Do you have a reservation? … Are you ready to order? … What would you like for your main course? … Anything to drink? … Here's the bill.
(Station) Hello. Where would you like to go? … One-way or round trip? … That's … dollars. … The next train leaves at …, from platform … .
Now you ask me two questions. Anything!

After the test

Score with the key and put written + speaking on the placement summary. Tell each learner their next step privately and kindly before they leave, or at a short one-to-one in the following week. Never read scores aloud. The key's "grade to outcome" table shows who is ready for Independent English (B1), who goes on with a review plan, and who should repeat A2 with support.
