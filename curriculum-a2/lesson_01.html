⏳ Lesson 1.1: Past Simple — Was & Were

Welcome to A2! You can already talk about the present. Now we open a door to the past. In this lesson you'll learn the two most important past-tense words in English — was and were — so you can say where you were and how things were.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use was and were to talk about the past
  • Make negatives (wasn't / weren't) and questions (Was…? / Were…?)
  • Use past time expressions like yesterday, last week, and two years ago
  • Say "There was / There were" to describe past places and situations

⏱️ Estimated Time: 45–55 minutes

🎯 Project: Talk (or record yourself) for one minute about your day yesterday and a place you visited recently — where you were and how it was.

From Present to Past

In your A1 course, you learned the verb to be in the present: I am, you are, he is. That little verb is the engine of English — and now we're going to drive it into the past.

Think of it like telling a story. Right now you can say "I am at home." But life is full of yesterdays: "I was at work." "We were on holiday." "The film was great!" The past tense is how you share your experiences — and sharing experiences is how real conversations begin. 🗣️

🧠 Mindset

The past of to be is beautifully small: just two words — was and were. Everything you already know about am/is/are maps straight onto them. You're not starting from zero; you're upgrading something you already own. If it feels new, add the word yet — "I can't tell stories in English… yet." 🌱

Was & Were: The Forms

Here's the whole rule in one table. Notice the pattern: the persons that took am/is now take was, and the persons that took are now take were.

PersonPresent (A1)Past (A2)Example
IamwasI was tired.
he / she / itiswasShe was at home.
youarewereYou were right.
wearewereWe were happy.
theyarewereThey were late.
 
graph LR
    A["I / he / she / it"] --> WAS["was"]
    B["you / we / they"] --> WERE["were"]

✅ Pro Tip

There is no -s and no did here. To be is special: it changes its own shape (was/were) instead of using a helper. That makes it easier — you only need these two words for every past sentence with be.

Read these aloud to feel the rhythm:

I was busy yesterday. My friends were at the party. It was a great night. We were very happy.

Negatives & Questions

To make a negative, add not — and we almost always use the short forms wasn't and weren't.

PersonNegativeExample
I / he / she / itwas not = wasn'tHe wasn't at school.
you / we / theywere not = weren'tThey weren't ready.

To make a question, put was or were at the front of the sentence (just like am/is/are in A1).

QuestionShort answer (yes)Short answer (no)
Were you at home?Yes, I was.No, I wasn't.
Was she happy?Yes, she was.No, she wasn't.
Were they late?Yes, they were.No, they weren't.
⚠️ Important Note: In a short answer, don't use the short form of the positive answer. Say "Yes, I was." — never "Yes, I's." The negative short answer is contracted: "No, I wasn't."

You can also ask for information with Wh- words:

Where were you? How was your weekend? Why were they late? Who was there? When was the party?

Past Time Expressions

Past sentences usually come with a word that tells us when. These are your signposts to the past — when you see or say one, use was/were.

  • yesterday
  • last night
  • last week
  • last year
  • two days ago
  • an hour ago
  • in 2015
  • when I was a child

💡 ago vs last

Use ago after a length of time, counting back from now: "three days ago," "a week ago." Use last before a time word: "last Monday," "last summer." We don't say "last three days" or "ago week."

There Was / There Were

Remember "There is / There are" from A1? Its past is just as useful for describing a place or situation that existed:

UseFormExample
one thingThere wasThere was a café near the hotel.
more than oneThere wereThere were many people at the beach.
negativeThere wasn't / weren'tThere weren't any free tables.
questionWas there…? / Were there…?Was there a pool?

Now let's hear it all in a real conversation:

A: How was your weekend?

B: It was great! I was at the coast with my family.

A: Nice! Was the weather good?

B: Yes, it was sunny. There were a lot of people on the beach, but it wasn't too crowded.

A: Were your kids happy?

B: Very! They weren't tired at all — I was the tired one!

Practice & Project

🏋️ Exercise 1: was or were?

Objective: Choose the correct form.

  1. I at home last night.
  2. They very friendly.
  3. The film boring.
  4. We late for the bus.
  5. You right!
✅ Answer

1. was · 2. were · 3. was · 4. were · 5. were

🏋️ Exercise 2: Make it negative, then a question

Objective: Change the sentence two ways.

Example: She was at work. → She wasn't at work. → Was she at work?

  1. They were at the party.
  2. It was cold yesterday.
✅ Answer

1. They weren't at the party. / Were they at the party?
2. It wasn't cold yesterday. / Was it cold yesterday?

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Match the person to the form: I/he/she/it → was, you/we/they → were.
  • Use short forms wasn't / weren't when you speak — they sound natural.
  • Add a time expression (yesterday, last week, two days ago) to make the past clear.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "You was…" ❌ → "You were…" ✅ (even for one person, you always takes were).
  • "They was…" ❌ → "They were…" ✅.
  • Don't add did: it's "Was he there?" ✅, not "Did he was there?" ❌ — to be never needs a helper.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • I / he / she / it → was; you / we / they → were.
  • Negatives are wasn't and weren't; questions put was/were at the front.
  • To be needs no did — it changes shape by itself.
  • Signal the past with time words: yesterday, last week, two years ago.

🎉 What You've Accomplished

You just stepped out of the present tense for the first time. With was and were, you can now talk about where you were, how you felt, and what places were like — the raw material of every story and every real "how was your day?" conversation. That's a big leap into A2. 🎉

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Why does "you" use "were" even for one person?

English doesn't have a separate singular/plural "you" here — you always takes were, whether you mean one person or many. It's just the rule, and you'll get used to it fast.

When do I use "was" for "we"?

You don't — we always takes were ("We were happy"). Only I, he, she, it take was.

Is "There was" or "There were" for uncountable things like water?

Use There was for uncountable nouns: "There was a lot of water." Use There were only with plural countable nouns: "There were three bottles."
