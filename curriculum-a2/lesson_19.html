🧭 Lesson 7.1: Travel & Directions — Getting Around

You've arrived in a new town. You need the station, the museum, or just a nice café — but which way? This lesson opens Module 7, our functional, real-world module, and it starts the Listening & Speaking strand. Today you'll hear real travel conversations and say them back. By the end, you can stop a stranger, ask the way, understand the answer, and buy a ticket. 🚆

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Ask for directions politely — "Excuse me, how do I get to…?", "Is there a … near here?", "Which way is…?"
  • Give and follow directions — go straight on, turn left/right, take the first turning, it's on your left, opposite/next to/between
  • Use transport and travel vocabulary — station, platform, ticket, single/return, timetable, change at…
  • Buy a ticket and listen to a travel conversation, then repeat the lines aloud

⏱️ Estimated Time: 45–55 minutes

🎯 Project: Role-play asking for and giving directions to a real place near you — spoken with a partner or recorded on your phone.

Finding Your Way

Everyone gets lost sometimes. In your own town, you can ask a friend. In a new place, in English, you need a few simple phrases — and the confidence to use them. The good news is that direction language is short and repeats a lot. Once you learn "Go straight on and turn left," you can understand a hundred conversations.

This lesson is the first in our Listening & Speaking strand. That means the heart of the lesson is a real conversation you can hear. Tap the 🔊 buttons, listen carefully, and say each line back. Speaking out loud — even quietly to yourself — is how the words become yours. 🗣️

🧠 Mindset

You don't need to understand every word to follow directions. You need the key words: left, right, straight, first, second, opposite, next to. If you catch those, you can find the place. And if you miss something, one magic phrase saves you: "Sorry, could you say that again, please?" Asking again is not a mistake — it's smart. 🌱

Asking for Directions

Always start politely. In English we open with "Excuse me" before we ask a stranger anything. Then choose a question:

Excuse me, how do I get to the station? Excuse me, where's the nearest bank? Is there a supermarket near here? Which way is the museum? How far is it? Is it far from here? Can you show me on the map?

Excuse me, how do I get to the town centre? · Is there a pharmacy near here? · Which way is the bus stop?

Notice the little words. "How do I get to…?" is the most useful of all — it works for any place. "Is there a … near here?" asks if a place exists close by. "Which way is…?" asks for the direction. Learn all three and you can ask about anything.

💡 Polite & friendly

Add please and a smile. When someone helps you, always say "Thank you very much" or "Thanks a lot." If you don't understand, say "Sorry, could you repeat that?" — never just walk away confused. People are usually happy to help. 😊

Giving Directions

Now the other side. When you give directions, you use the imperative — the base verb with no subject: Go, Turn, Take, Cross. Here is the toolkit:

  • go straight on /streɪt ɒn/
  • turn left
  • turn right
  • take the first turning
  • take the second right
  • go past the bank
  • cross the road
  • at the corner
  • at the traffic lights
  • at the roundabout

To say where a place is, use these position words:

PhraseMeaningExample
on your left / rightthe side it's onThe bank is on your left.
oppositeacross from, facingIt's opposite the church.
next toright besideThe café is next to the cinema.
betweenin the middle of two thingsIt's between the shop and the park.
in front ofbefore it, on the near sideThere's a bus stop in front of the hotel.
on the cornerwhere two streets meetThe pharmacy is on the corner.
Go straight on. Turn left at the lights. Take the first right. Go past the bank. It's on your left. It's opposite the station. You can't miss it!

✅ A neat ending

English speakers often finish directions with a friendly "You can't miss it!" It means the place is big or clear, so you will find it easily. It's a warm way to say "you'll be fine." 🌟

Transport & Travel Words

To travel around, you need the words for the places and the actions. Here are the essentials:

WordMeaning
stationwhere trains or buses stop
platformwhere you wait for and board a train
ticketthe paper or card you buy to travel
singlea ticket to go one way
returna ticket to go and come back
timetablethe list of times when trains/buses run
change at…get off one train and take another

And some very common travel verbs (phrasal verbs). Notice we say get on / get off a train or bus, but get in / get out of a car or taxi:

  • get on the train
  • get off at the next stop
  • catch the bus
  • miss the train
  • change at the next station
  • take the number 12 bus

💡 Single or return?

At a ticket office, the two big words are single (one way) and return (there and back). In American English these are one-way and round-trip. Both are correct — this course uses the British words single and return, but it's good to recognise both.

Buying a Ticket

Buying a ticket is a short, predictable conversation. If you learn the pattern, you can do it anywhere. Here are the phrases you need:

A single to Oxford, please. Two returns to the city centre, please. What time is the next train? Which platform is it? Do I need to change? How much is it?

The clerk usually answers with the price, the platform, and the time. Listen for numbers and for the words platform and change. We'll hear a full example in the next section.

💬 Quick tip: To ask for the ticket, you can just say the type and the place: "A return to London, please." You don't need a long sentence. Short and polite is perfect.

Listen & Repeat: At the Station

Here is the heart of today's lesson — a real conversation. Priya is a traveller. She's new in town and needs to get to the museum by train. First she asks a local for directions to the station, then she talks to the ticket clerk. Read it, listen to it, and say each line back.

Priya: Excuse me, how do I get to the train station?

Local: The station? Go straight on to the end of this street.

Priya: Straight on. Okay.

Local: Then turn left at the traffic lights and go past the bank.

Priya: Turn left at the lights, past the bank. Is it far?

Local: No, it's about five minutes. The station is on your right, opposite the park. You can't miss it!

Priya: Thank you very much!

Local: You're welcome. Have a good trip!

Priya: (at the ticket office) Hello. A return to the city centre, please.

Clerk: A return, sure. That's eight pounds fifty. Do you want a receipt?

Priya: Yes, please. What time is the next train?

Clerk: The next one leaves at ten fifteen, from platform three.

Priya: Platform three. Do I need to change?

Clerk: No, it's direct. Just stay on until the last stop.

Priya: Perfect. Thank you!

🔊 Listen & Repeat

Now make the conversation yours. Tap the 🔊 speaker names and 🔊 buttons to hear each line, then say it back out loud — copy the rhythm and the friendly tone. Use the 🎧 Audio control (bottom-right corner) to choose a UK or US voice and slow the speed down. Try it three ways:

  • Listen to the whole conversation once, all the way through.
  • Repeat each line right after you hear it — match the sound, not just the words.
  • Role-play: read Priya's lines out loud, then read the Local's and the Clerk's lines. Be both people!

Now check that you understood. Try to answer before you open each one:

1. Where does the local tell Priya to turn left?

At the traffic lights, at the end of the street.

2. Which building is the station opposite?

It's opposite the park, on her right.

3. Did Priya buy a single or a return ticket?

A return — a ticket to go and come back. It cost eight pounds fifty.

4. Which platform does the train leave from, and at what time?

Platform three, at ten fifteen (10:15).

5. Does Priya need to change trains?

No. The clerk says it's direct — she stays on until the last stop.

Practice & Project

🏋️ Exercise 1: The missing travel word

Objective: Fill each gap with the best direction or travel word. Use one word per gap (except number 6).

  1. Excuse me, how do I to the museum?
  2. Go on to the end of the road.
  3. Turn at the traffic lights. (the opposite of right)
  4. The bank is the church, across the road.
  5. Which does the train leave from?
  6. I'd like a to London — I'm going there and coming back today.
✅ Answer

1. get · 2. straight · 3. left · 4. opposite · 5. platform · 6. return

🏋️ Exercise 2: Put the directions in order

Objective: A tourist asks the way to the cinema. The steps below are mixed up. Write the correct number (1 = first, 5 = last) in each gap to put the directions in order.

  1. Go past the supermarket.
  2. Go straight on for two minutes.
  3. The cinema is on your left, next to the café.
  4. Turn right at the corner.
  5. Cross the road at the traffic lights.
✅ Answer

Order: 1 Go straight on for two minutes → 2 Turn right at the corner → 3 Go past the supermarket → 4 Cross the road at the traffic lights → 5 The cinema is on your left, next to the café.

🎯 Your Project: Ask & Give Directions

Choose a real place near where you live or study — a shop, a station, a park, a friend's home. Then role-play the whole journey. Speak out loud with a partner, or record both parts on your phone.

  1. (2 min) Traveller: Start politely and ask the way — "Excuse me, how do I get to…?" Add one more question: "Is it far?" or "Which way is…?"
  2. (3 min) Local: Give clear directions in 4+ steps using go straight on, turn left/right, take the first turning, go past… Finish with where it is: "It's on your left, opposite the…"
  3. (2 min) Swap roles and choose a different place. Now the other person gives directions.
  4. (2 min) Add a ticket! Role-play buying a single or return: ask the time, the platform, and if you need to change.
  5. (1 min) If you recorded yourself, listen back. Did the directions make sense? Could you follow them on a map?

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ I started politely with "Excuse me…"
  • ☐ I asked the way with "How do I get to…?" (or "Which way is…?")
  • ☐ I gave directions in 4+ steps (straight on, turn, take, go past)
  • ☐ I said where the place is (on your left, opposite, next to, between)
  • ☐ I role-played buying a single or return ticket and asked about time/platform
  • ☐ I swapped roles so I practised both asking and giving

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Always open with "Excuse me" before you ask a stranger, and thank them after.
  • Listen for the key words — left, right, straight, first, opposite — not every word.
  • Give directions with the base verb: Go, Turn, Take, Cross (no subject needed).
  • If you don't understand, say "Sorry, could you repeat that, please?" — ask as many times as you need.
  • Repeat back what you heard ("Turn left at the lights — okay!") to check you got it right.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "How I get to the station?" ❌ → "How do I get to the station?" ✅ (keep the do in the question).
  • "Go to straight." ❌ → "Go straight on." ✅ (no to before straight).
  • "Turn to left." ❌ → "Turn left." ✅ (no to before left/right).
  • "get in the train" ❌ → "get on the train" ✅ (on/off for trains, buses, planes; in/out of for cars, taxis).
  • "a ticket for return" ❌ → "a return" or "a return ticket" ✅.

🎓 Key Takeaways

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

What's the difference between "straight on" and "straight"?

In British English, we usually say "go straight on" to mean "keep going forward." Americans often say "go straight ahead." Both are correct. Just don't add to — never "go to straight."

When do I use "single" and "return"?

A single takes you one way only. A return takes you there and back. If you plan to come home, buy a return — it's usually cheaper than two singles. (In the US these are "one-way" and "round-trip.")

What if the directions are too fast and I get lost?

That's completely normal! Say "Sorry, could you say that again, more slowly, please?" You can also repeat each step back: "So, turn left, then…?" This tells the person to continue. Most people are happy to help a second time.
