🗺️ Lesson 1.3: Past Simple — Irregular Verbs & Questions

In the last lesson you made past verbs with -ed. But some English verbs do not follow this rule. We call them the irregular verbs. They are some of the most common words you will ever use (go, have, see, make), so we learn them here. Then comes the good news: negatives and questions in the past are very easy. One little word, did, does all the work.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use common irregular past verbs (went, had, saw, made, took…)
  • Make past simple negatives with didn't + base verb
  • Ask past simple questions with did and give short answers
  • Tell a simple past story mixing regular and irregular verbs

⏱️ Estimated Time: 50–55 minutes

🎯 Project: Tell (or record) a one-minute story about a memorable day or trip, using at least 5 past verbs — a mix of regular and irregular.

The Verbs That Break the Rule

In Lesson 1.2 you learned the -ed rule: walk → walked, play → played. It is simple and easy. But many of the most common English verbs are irregular. They change in their own special way and take no -ed at all.

Look: we don't say "goed" — we say went. We don't say "haved" — we say had. There is no rule for these. You just learn each one, like learning a friend's name. The good news is that there aren't many. You will see them all the time, so you will remember them fast. 🧠

🧠 Mindset

Don't try to learn a big list all at once. Learn a few at a time, and use them in real sentences about your life — "Yesterday I went to work, I had lunch with a friend, I saw a good film." A verb you say about your own day is a verb you'll never forget. You don't know them all yet — and "yet" is the whole journey. 🌱

Common Irregular Verbs

Here are the most common irregular verbs. The past form is the same for every person — I, you, he, she, we, they. No endings to add, no spelling changes. Just learn the second column.

💡 Learn them in little groups

Some irregular verbs come in families that sound alike. Learning them in groups makes them easier to remember:

  • -ought / -aught ending: buy → bought, think → thought, bring → brought, teach → taught.
  • Same vowel change (i → a): drink → drank, sing → sang, swim → swam, begin → began.
  • -ay → -aid / -old: say → said, pay → paid; tell → told, sell → sold.

⚠️ Watch the "read" trap

read looks the same in the present and the past — but the sound changes. Present read rhymes with "need" /riːd/; past read rhymes with "red" /rɛd/. Same letters, different sound: "I read the news every day, and I read it yesterday too."

Say these out loud:

Last night I came home, made dinner, and ate with my family. Then we saw a film. I drank some tea and went to bed early.

Negatives with didn't

Now for the good part. To make any past simple sentence negative — regular or irregular — you use one helper: didn't (= did not). Here is the important point: after didn't, the main verb goes back to its base form (no past, no -ed).

subject + didn't + base verb
I didn't go. · She didn't have time. · They didn't see it.

PositiveNegative
I went to the party.I didn't go to the party.
She had breakfast.She didn't have breakfast.
We watched the game. (regular)We didn't watch the game.
They bought tickets.They didn't buy tickets.

⚠️ The most common past-tense mistake

Never keep the past form after didn't. The word did already shows the past — so the main verb goes back to its base.

  • "I didn't went" ❌ → "I didn't go" ✅
  • "She didn't had" ❌ → "She didn't have" ✅
  • "We didn't watched" ❌ → "We didn't watch" ✅

Here is an easy way to remember it: did and the main verb are never both "past" at the same time. One of them is enough. 🙂

Questions with did

Questions work the same easy way. Put Did at the front, then the subject, then the base verb. Again — no -ed, no irregular past form. Just the plain base.

Did + subject + base verb …?
Did you go? · Did she have fun? · Did they see you?

QuestionShort answer (yes)Short answer (no)
Did you go out?Yes, I did.No, I didn't.
Did she eat lunch?Yes, she did.No, she didn't.
Did they win?Yes, they did.No, they didn't.

For information, start with a Wh- word, then use did + subject + base verb:

Wh- questionPossible answer
What did you do yesterday?I visited my grandmother.
Where did they go?They went to the mountains.
When did the film start?It started at eight.
Who did you meet?I met an old friend.
⚠️ Note: In "What did you do?" there are two forms of the verb do doing different jobs — the helper did (it makes the question) and the main verb do (the action). This is normal: "What did you do?" ✅

Here are some question starters to remember:

What did you do? Where did you go? Did you have a good time? Who did you go with? What did you eat? How did it go?

The Beautiful Simplicity

Here is the best part, and it is really nice. In this lesson you learned irregular past forms like went, had, saw. But look what happens in negatives and questions:

💡 One pattern for everything

In negatives and questions, every verb — regular and irregular — works the same way: did / didn't + base verb. The helper did shows the past, so the main verb goes back to its plain base form.

That means you only need the irregular forms for positive statements. In the negative and the question, went becomes go again, and bought becomes buy again. English makes it easy just where you thought it would be hard. 🎉

VerbPositive (learn this)NegativeQuestion
go (irregular)I went.I didn't go.Did you go?
buy (irregular)I bought.I didn't buy.Did you buy?
play (regular)I played.I didn't play.Did you play?
watch (regular)I watched.I didn't watch.Did you watch?

A Past Story

Let's put it all together. Notice how a real story mixes regular verbs (-ed) and irregular verbs, and how did/didn't appear in the questions and negatives.

Last weekend I went to the mountains with two friends. We took an early train and arrived at nine. The weather was perfect, so we walked for three hours and saw a beautiful lake. At lunch we ate sandwiches and drank hot tea. I didn't bring my camera, but my friend took lots of photos. It was a wonderful day.

And in conversation:

A: What did you do last weekend?

B: I went to the mountains with friends. We took the train.

A: Nice! Did you have a good time?

B: Yes, we did! We walked a lot and saw a lake.

A: Did you take any photos?

B: I didn't, but my friend did. Where did you go?

A: I didn't go anywhere. I stayed home and read a book.

🎯 Your Project: Tell a One-Minute Story

Tell the story of a memorable day or trip. Speak out loud — to a partner, in class, or into your phone's recorder. Use at least 5 past verbs, mixing regular and irregular.

  1. (3 min) Plan quietly. Choose the day. Jot down 5–7 verbs you'll need — mark which are regular (add -ed) and which are irregular (learn the form). For example: went, took, saw, ate, walked, was, bought.
  2. (1 min) Tell the story out loud from start to finish: where you went, who you were with, what you did, what you saw or ate, and how it was.
  3. (2 min) Add one negative (something that didn't happen) and answer one did-question about your day, e.g. "Did you have a good time? — Yes, I did!"
  4. (1 min) If you recorded yourself, listen back. Check: after didn't and did, did you use the base verb? Are your irregular forms correct?

Partner / class option: Tell your story to a partner. Then your partner asks you three past questions ("Where did you go? Who did you meet? Did you enjoy it?") and you answer in full.

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Learn irregular forms in small groups, using them in sentences about your own day.
  • Use the same past form for every person: I went, you went, she went, they went.
  • After did and didn't, always go back to the base verb.
  • Keep the question starters handy: What did you do? Where did you go? Did you have a good time?

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "I didn't went" ❌ → "I didn't go" ✅ (base verb after didn't).
  • "Did you went?" ❌ → "Did you go?" ✅ (base verb after did).
  • "I goed / buyed" ❌ → "I went / bought" ✅ (irregulars take no -ed).
  • Don't mix up to be and action verbs: "Did he was there?" ❌ → "Was he there?" ✅. Use did only with action verbs.

🌍 Register note

In everyday speech and friendly writing, the short form didn't is normal and warm. In very formal writing you may see the full form did not ("The report did not include the figures"), but for conversation, didn't is just right.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Irregular verbs take no -ed — you learn each past form (go → went, have → had, see → saw).
  • The past form is the same for every person.
  • Negatives use didn't + base verb: "I didn't go" (never "didn't went").
  • Questions use did + subject + base verb: "Did you go?" → "Yes, I did / No, I didn't."
  • In negatives and questions, every verb behaves the same — you only need irregular forms for positive statements.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Do I really have to memorise all the irregular verbs?

Not all at once, and not from a boring list. Start with the most common ones (the ones in this lesson), and learn them by using them in real sentences about your day. You use them so often that you'll learn them quickly — you'll be surprised how fast went and had feel easy.

Why is it "didn't go" and not "didn't went"?

The word did already shows the past. Once one word in the sentence is "past," the main verb goes back to its base form. So the past is in did, and go stays plain: "I didn't go." The same is true in questions: "Did you go?"

When do I use "did" and when do I use "was/were"?

Use did / didn't with action verbs (go, have, see, watch). Use was / were with the verb to be (from Lesson 1.1). So: "Did you go?" ✅ but "Were you there?" ✅ — never mix them into "Did you were there?"
