1.1 · Lead-in

The lightning-bug and the lightning

"The difference between the almost right word and the right word is really a large matter: 'tis the difference between the lightning-bug and the lightning." (Mark Twain)

"She's careful with money." Say it so I'll admire her. (She's frugal.) So I'll dislike her. (She's stingy.) Same behavior? (Yes.) Same verdict? (No.)
1.1 · Force · order the word cards under each picture

Every word sits on a cline

small → tiny → minuscule
chilly → cold → freezing → glacial
pricey → expensive → exorbitant
irritated → annoyed → furious → livid
ALSO decent → good → excellent → superb → sublime · tepid → warm → hot → scorching

One fact, three verdicts

NeutralPositiveNegative

"He's resolute." "He's pig-headed." Different facts? (No.) Which goes in a job reference? (Resolute.) The choice of word is the message.
1.1 · The myth of the synonym

surprised is not astonished is not startled

astonishedstronger; slightly formal
startledsudden, physical: a noise
taken abackdisconcerted: his rudeness
flabbergastedvery strong, informal, comic
bemusedpuzzled (NOT amused)
"I was bemused by the instructions." Laughing? (No.) Confused? (Yes.)
"Absolutely flabbergasted" in a report? (No.)
fulsome praise? (Excessive, insincere.)
enormity? (Great wickedness, not just size.)

Mot Juste Editing Workshop

1.2 · The formality cline · move the whole cluster

One message, five levels

1 FrozenNotice is hereby given that the premises will close at 3:00 p.m. on Friday.
2 FormalPlease be advised that the center will close early on Friday.
3 NeutralJust a reminder that we're closing at 3 on Friday.
4 InformalHeads up, we're shutting early Friday.
5 IntimateFriday, 3 o'clock, doors shut. Don't be late!
CLANG Please find attached the aforementioned pics. · The committee will utilize the feedback to commence a review.
1.2 · Tone · the second dial

Same formality, opposite tone

Warm: Thanks so much for flagging this; it's really helpful. I'll get it fixed today and let you know the moment it's done.

Cool: Noted. This will be addressed in due course. You will be informed upon completion.

Which is more formal? (Neither, really.) Which would you rather receive? (The warm one.) Can formal be warm? (Yes.)
WARM · COOL · IRONIC · AUTHORITATIVE · DEFERENTIAL: "The report is ready."
1.2 · Model · re-keying mid-conversation

The architect's call

Nadia: The permit application is, in principle, approvable, subject to a satisfactory structural review.

Joe: Sorry, in plain English: can I open the café in May or not?

Nadia: Ha, fair enough. Yes: you'll get the permit, as long as the engineer says the building is safe. May is realistic.

Joe: Now that I understand.

What changed: field, tenor or mode? (Tenor: a client, not a colleague.) Did the facts change? (No.)

Five Voices

Can the facts change? (No.)
Contractions in the notice? (None.)
How long is the post? (280 characters max.)
The tribute is for…? (A retirement or a closing.)
A clang? (Raise your hand.)

Naturalness Audit + Odds and Ends
