3.1 · Four rooms · the marker announces the register

Which room are you in?

the coffee chat
the work email
the report
the essay
"Right, so the plan didn't work. That said, we learned a lot. Anyway, what next?" Which room? (Coffee chat.) · "The plan did not succeed. Admittedly, however, it yielded valuable lessons. We now turn to what follows." (Report / essay.) Did the facts change? (No: only the joints.)

3.1 · Stance adverbials · one word recolors a clause

"The safety feature caused the accident."

Epistemicadmittedly · arguably · presumably · ostensibly
Attitudefrankly · remarkably · ironically · tellingly
Viewpointtechnically · broadly speaking · reportedly
Focusnotably · if anything · at least
Ironically, the safety feature… Did a fact change? (No.) What changed? (How we read it.)
"Ostensibly, a courtesy call." Was it? (That was the stated reason…)
"Admittedly, my figures were thin." Whose side does it weaken? (Mine: that's why it builds trust.)
FORM Frankly, I disagree. · The result, remarkably, held. · He said nothing, tellingly. · (She spoke frankly = manner)
3.1 · Model · stance and interaction together

After the exhibition

Maya: So, honestly, what did you make of the exhibition?

Theo: Well… mixed. The early rooms were, arguably, the strongest. But the thing is, by the last gallery it felt, I mean, a bit repetitive. Mind you, that might just be me.

Maya: And let's face it, the ticket price was, frankly, absurd.

Theo: Though, to be fair, it is for a good cause.

Which words color a judgment? (honestly, arguably, frankly, to be fair) Which manage the conversation? (the thing is, I mean, mind you, let's face it) Would any belong in a report? (arguably, perhaps; the rest, no.) · Strip them out: is anything left? (The facts, and no personality.)

3.2 · Two virtues · is the wiring missing, or the thought?

Cohesion ≠ coherence

Glued: The committee approved the budget. However, the budget is a document. Moreover, paper comes from trees. Therefore, the committee met on Tuesday.

Bare: The program failed. Nobody had been consulted. Attendance collapsed within a month.

Glued: any grammar errors? (No.) Links? (Everywhere.) Does it make sense? (No: cohesive, not coherent.)
Bare: can you follow it? (Yes.) What's missing? (A little wiring: because, so.) Which is easier to fix? (The bare one.)

The staircase

Theme (given)Rheme (new)

Where does each sentence start? (On the last one's news.) Where does the new information go? (At the end.) Linear or constant theme? (Linear: A→B, B→C.)
3.2 · Lexical chains · bind and develop

Repeat the anchor. Vary the rest.

Repetitionthe drought… the drought
Synonymthe dry spell · the water shortage
Superordinatespaniel → dog → animal
General wordthe crisis · the whole business
"The novel… the book… the work… the volume… the tome." How many books? (One, but the reader has to keep checking.)
Fix? (Repeat "novel" once; let "it" do the rest.)
Is repeating a key word bad style? (No: it's a virtue.)
3.2 · Architecture · signpost the turns, not the steps

The skeleton test

  • Preview: This essay will argue that…
  • Topic sentence: A second objection is more serious.
  • Internal summary: So far, then, …
  • Transition: A very different picture emerges when…
One sentence per paragraph, read as a list. Does it make an argument? (If not, fix the order, not the words.)
Orders: chronological · general → specific · problem → solution · claim → evidence · compare / contrast
CLUTTER In this next part I will now begin by turning to…

3.3 · The three condensing arts

Summarize · paraphrase · synthesize

Summarizesame meaning, far fewer words: delete · generalize · topic sentence · keep the logic
Paraphrasesame meaning, same length, new structure
Synthesizeseveral sources, one account organized by idea
"Some critics worry the reform may raise costs." → "The reform is a costly failure." Shorter? (Yes.) Faithful? (No.) What changed? (some → all · may → is · a worry → a verdict)
3.3 · Rebuild, don't re-skin

Paraphrase vs patchwriting

OriginalThe rapid expansion of social media has fundamentally altered the way young people form their political opinions.
PatchwriteThe quick growth of social media has basically changed the way young individuals develop their political views.
ParaphraseWhere young people once inherited their politics from family and community, many now form those views through the fast-growing world of social media.
Same skeleton in the patchwrite? (Yes: only synonyms.) If I cite it, is it OK? (No: quote it or rebuild it.) Change the words or the structure first? (The structure.)
3.3 · Attribution · the verb carries the stance

notes → argues → points out → concedes → claims

Neutralnotes · observes · describes
Committedargues · contends · maintains
You agreepoints out · shows
Reluctantconcedes · acknowledges
Opposingcounters · disputes
You doubtclaims · alleges
"As Halpern shows, the policy failed." Agree? (Yes.)
"As Halpern claims…" (A raised eyebrow.)
"Ortega concedes that fines raised some money." Happy to say so? (No: it's against her position.)
"According to Hsu, he argues…" (One frame only.)
3.3 · Synthesis · organize by idea, not by source

A list vs an argument

Weak: Source A says… Source B says… Source C says…

Strong: Both X and Y concur that… · Where they diverge is… · Whereas X…, Y counters that… · Z qualifies X's claim… · Taken together, …

First question? (Where do they agree, diverge, qualify each other?)
First sentence? (A governing idea, not a list.)
Must I be neutral? (Fair, not neutral: my view is openly marked.)
