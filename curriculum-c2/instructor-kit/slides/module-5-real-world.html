5.1 · Model · one scene, five Englishes

The delayed train

Priya: The train is delayed only, no? Kindly do not worry. (Indian English)

Callum: Aye, I ken the wee café does a braw coffee. (Scottish)

Maddie: I'm gasping for a cuppa. This arvo's a right shocker. (Australian)

Chidi: No wahala. There is a go-slow on the roads. We shall reach in good time. (Nigerian)

Ray: Elevator's right over there if you've got bags. (American)

Is any of this a mistake? (No: each is standard in its variety.) What do you need to understand all five? (Exposure, context, and the idea: assume variety, not error.)

5.2 · Voice · the narrator is not the author

Who is really speaking?

A: "I was frightened, and I said so."

B: "I was perfectly calm — anyone would have done as I did, and only a coward would say otherwise."

Austen: "It is a truth universally acknowledged, that a single man in possession of a good fortune must be in want of a wife." (1813)

Was B calm? (Probably not: he protests too much.)
Is Austen's "truth" universal? (No.) Who actually wants the marriage? (The neighbors with daughters.) Sincere or ironic? (Ironic.)
5.2 · Form · difficult syntax

Strip → spine → restore → paraphrase

InversionAnd sore must be the storm / That could abash the little bird= The storm would have to be terrible to silence the bird.
PeriodicI went to the woods [because I wished to live deliberately, to front only…] … discover that I had not lived.Spine first; the weight lands last.
VerblessFog everywhere. Fog up the river… fog down the river…No verb on purpose: stillness.

5.2 · Meaning · the engine of interpretation

Technique → effect → theme

allusion · imagery · symbolism · irony
juxtaposition · foreshadowing · motif
the passage evokes… · the image juxtaposes…
the effect is to… · which suggests…
I'd read it slightly differently: …
"There is imagery of fog." Interpretation? (Not yet: a label.)
"The repeated 'Fog' makes it feel inescapable, which suggests a city lost in its own confusion." (Yes: technique, effect, theme.)
"The author wants to say…" (→ suggests / implies.)

5.3 · Fast speech & tone

Whatcha gonna do about it?

What are you going to do? → Whatcha gonna do?
Did you see it? → Didja see it?
Let me know. → Lemme know.
I don't know. → I dunno.
should have → shoulda · next day → "nex day" · ten bikes → "tem bikes"
The car breaks down. "Well, this is going great." Great? (No: the opposite.) How do you know? (The situation + a flat tone.)
Can you look sarcasm up? (No: trust the tone over the text.)
Write "gonna" in a report? (Never.)
