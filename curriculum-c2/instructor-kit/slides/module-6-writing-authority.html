6.1 · The op-ed · one line, argued with conviction

Hook → line → concede → rebut → land

FlatThis article will discuss the topic of urban green spaces and their various benefits for residents.
HookThere is a park bench near my apartment that has, over three years, quietly done more for my mental health than any app I've downloaded.
Concede → turnThe obvious reply is that busy people are simply busy. That is true, and it is exactly the problem.
DELETE In today's fast-paced world… · This article will discuss… · It is important to note that…

"The food was amazing and the vibe was great." What's missing? (A detail: which dish?)
"It's perfect in every way." Credible? (No: a press release.)
"It has good and bad points." Verdict? (None: mush.)

6.1 · Model · voice and the publication

"The voice is all wrong for us"

Editor: Who did you picture reading this?

Writer: Honestly? My old professor. I was still writing an essay.

Editor: Our reader is on a train, half-distracted, deciding by the second paragraph whether to bother.

Writer: So less "one might argue," more "here's what nobody says out loud"?

Editor: Same rigor, human temperature.

Is the writer's English wrong? (No: the register is.) Broadsheet, blog, trade magazine: same voice? (No: name the outlet first.)

6.2 · Architecture · claims, not topics

Read the claims in order

Topic listArgued outline
1. HistoryThe debate is stuck on a false choice.
2. TrafficCar-free centers cut pollution and road deaths.
3. BusinessesThe retailers' fear, granted: lost customers.
4. Other citiesWhere transit came with the ban, foot traffic rose.
5. ConclusionIt's an investment question, not a ban question.
Can you swap 2 and 3 on the left? (Yes: a list.) On the right? (No: the argument breaks.)
"Background" or "Why the old model broke down"? (The second: it states a claim.)
6.2 · Signposting · at every seam

Backward · forward · where we are

BackwardHaving established that output rises, … · As noted in the previous section, …
ForwardThis section argues that … · The following section turns to …
LocationalThe remainder of this report is organized as follows: … · At this point it is worth stepping back.

Student: I stopped signposting. I knew where I was, so I assumed the reader did.

Supervisor: You can hold the whole in your head; your reader can't.

6.2 · Sources · the verb carries your stance

Paraphrase by default · choose the verb

acceptdemonstrates · shows · establishes
positionargues · contends · maintains
distanceclaims · asserts · alleges
admissionconcedes · acknowledges
tentativesuggests · speculates
extendsdraws on · builds on
"Okafor claims the app doubled sales." Do you believe it? (Not fully.)
"Even Okafor concedes the sample was small." (An admission.)
Same structure, swapped synonyms, with a citation: OK? (No: patchwriting = plagiarism.)
6.2 · The literature review · synthesize, don't list

Shopping list → synthesis

ListGarcia (2020) found visits rose. Nwosu (2022) found visits rose. Chen (2023) found loans fell.
SynthesisThere is broad agreement that hubs attract visitors (Garcia 2020; Nwosu 2022). Borrowing, however, tells a different story: Chen (2023) shows loans still falling. What remains unclear is whether new visitors ever borrow books.
MOVES consensus · divergence (Whereas A…, B…) · building on · the gap · positioning (This report therefore asks…)

6.3 · Two commas and a family

The comma that carries meaning

A My brother who lives in Berlin is a doctor.

B My brother, who lives in Berlin, is a doctor.

How many brothers in A? (More than one: the Berlin one.) In B? (One.) Which one is read with a lower voice and pauses? (B.)
The report that I sent is final. / The report, which I sent, is final. (identifies · adds)
OXFORD I'd like to thank my parents, the head chef and the night manager. (Two people or four?) → …the head chef, and the night manager.

"Her goals are: to win." (Clause before the colon isn't complete.)

6.3 · Workshop · 22 min

The Editing Race

  • Teams of 3 · four stations · 4 min each · 8 faults per passage
  • +1 fix · +1 name the point · −1 false alarm (a myth "corrected")
  • +2 one style improvement you can justify (; : —)
  • Roles rotate: editor · namer · judge
Fix only, or fix and name? (Both.)
"To really understand": fault? (No: myth.)
What loses a point? (Changing what isn't wrong.)
Who rules on a myth? (The judge, after listening.)
