4.1 · Lead-in · same content, different force

Which one would you remember tomorrow?

Flat We need to act on this problem now, because it will get worse and cost more if we wait, and future generations will suffer.

Figured We can act now, or we can pay later. We can spend a little today, or a fortune tomorrow. We can spare our children this burden, or bequeath it to them.

Same content? (Yes.) Which one moves? (The second.) What did the speaker do? (Balanced opposites, repetition, three parts, an image of the future.)

4.1 · "Lend It, Don't Buy It" · the architecture

Claim → grounds → warrant → concession → refutation

ClaimThis association should open a tool library.
Grounds35 of 42 drill owners used theirs fewer than three times last year.
WarrantA tool in a garage is not an asset; it is a cost with a handle.
ConcessionI grant that a few tools will come back worse than they left.
RefutationBut borrowers return what they borrow, and a repair fund costs less than 42 drills.
Restated claimLend it, don't buy it.
FIND IT One line is counterfeit. Which one? Why is it dangerous in an honest speech?
4.1 · Fallacies · refuse them, name them, calmly

Spot the counterfeit

Straw man"So you want to ban email altogether?"
Ad hominem"He can't even park straight."
False dilemma"Cancel the party or go over budget."
Slippery slope"One Friday at home, then nobody comes in."
Bandwagon"Everyone has open-plan, so it's best."
Hasty generalization"My cousin hurt her back, so…"
"That's not what I said. I suggested fewer, not none."
"His parking isn't the question; the plan is."
Counter = redirect to the actual claim.
Insult back? (No: that's another ad hominem.)

4.2 · Rhythm · the music of prose

Long, long, long… then short. Hard.

SimpleThe room fell silent.
CompoundShe spoke, and the room fell silent.
ComplexWhen she spoke, the room fell silent.
PeriodicWhen she rose, when she drew breath, when at last she spoke, the room fell silent.
LooseThe room fell silent, all argument spent, every eye turned toward her.
Periodic: when do we get the point? (At the end: suspense.) Loose? (First: relaxed.) Where does a fragment belong? (Story, essay, speech. Not a report.) The test? (Read it aloud.)
4.2 · Economy · omit needless words

Cut the dead, keep the living

42 It is important to note that, due to the fact that the committee had not yet reached a final decision on the matter, we were, at this point in time, quite unable to make any kind of announcement to the general public.

14 Because the committee had not yet decided, we could not yet announce anything publicly.

make a decision decide · give consideration to consider
each and every every · very unique unique
Meaning lost? (None.) "Committee undecided; no announcement." Better? (No: telegram prose.)
4.2 · Voice · the personality in the pattern

One fire drill, three voices

A At 10:14 on Tuesday, a scheduled fire drill interrupted the quarterly budget meeting. The meeting resumed at 10:40.

B Deep in the third hour of the quarterly budget, somewhere in that long gray corridor of spreadsheets, we heard the bell. Then the stairs.

C We reached the parking lot in six minutes, which is faster than we have reached any decision this year.

Fingerprint: sentence length · plain or Latinate · few or many images · distant or intimate · the reader relationship. Voice = consistency. Pastiche: imitate a manner, then diverge.

4.3 · Model · handling the room

The question beneath the question

Priya: So your proposal is a one-line record of every decision. Isn't this just more bureaucracy?

Daniel: If I read your question rightly, the worry is that the log becomes the work. That's a fair challenge. What I'd say is: one line, ninety seconds.

Tom: What happens when someone's name is next to a decision that goes wrong?

Daniel: Let me answer the question beneath the question: will this be used to blame people? It records decisions, not culprits.

What does Priya do before she challenges? (Paraphrases.) Daniel: concede or counter first? (Concede, then counter.)
4.3 · The task · 10 + 22 + 14 min

Mastery in Action

  • Plan: hook · claim · grounds · turn · landing · six layers, one in reserve
  • Speak: 3 min · then 2 min of questions (challengers paraphrase first)
  • Listeners write: the claim in one sentence + the strongest challenge
  • Re-pitch: 150–180 words for your audience card · report the challenge fairly · answer it
How long? (Three minutes.)
Read your notes? (A glance.)
Re-pitch = summary? (No: same argument, new reader.)
Leave out a strong challenge? (Never.)
Speak only to the teacher? (Yes, if you prefer.)
