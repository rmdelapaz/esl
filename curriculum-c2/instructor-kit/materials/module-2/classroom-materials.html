C · Implicature Lab — Source Texts (2.1)

One text per group of 3; give neighboring groups different texts. Fold the model reading under before handing out. All names and organizations are invented. Learners fill the Said / Meant / Mechanism table in their workbook.

C1 · The work email

Hi Sam, thanks for the detailed proposal. It's clear a great deal of work went into it, and it's certainly comprehensive. We've decided to go in a slightly different direction for now, but I'd love to keep some of your ideas in mind for the future. By the way, has the budget section been checked by finance yet? Let's touch base next quarter. Best, Helen

Model: detailed, a great deal of work, comprehensive = too long (quantity: praise of effort, not quality) · a slightly different direction for now = rejected (vagueness) · some of your ideas … the future = most ideas are out · the budget section … yet = the budget is doubtful (presupposition: it should have been checked) · next quarter = not soon. Real message: No, the proposal is too long and the numbers are suspect.
C2 · The reference letter

To whom it may concern: I am pleased to confirm that Mr. Daniel Reyes worked in our accounts department from 2021 to 2024. His attendance record was excellent, and he was a popular presence at team social events. He always dressed smartly. Should you require confirmation of his dates of employment, please do not hesitate to contact me.

Model: nothing about the actual work, accuracy, reliability or skills (quantity: a classic weak reference) · attendance, social events, dressed smartly = the only merits · confirmation of his dates of employment = I will confirm facts, not recommend him. Real message: I can't recommend him, and I won't say why. (Ethics note: this is tactful; inventing a problem would not be.)
C3 · The online review

Three stars. The location is unbeatable, right next to the station, and you'll hear every train. The staff were clearly doing their best. The breakfast room has a lovely view of the parking lot. My room was cozy: I could open the door and touch the window without getting out of bed. I'm sure the renovations will help. The towels were very clean.

Model: unbeatable … hear every train = noisy (juxtaposition) · clearly doing their best = the service was poor (faint praise) · lovely view of the parking lot = irony (quality) · cozy + hyperbole = tiny · I'm sure the renovations will help = it needs them badly (presupposition + irony) · the towels were very clean = the only real positive. Real message: avoid this hotel; three stars is generous.
C4 · The meeting minutes

Item 4: Office move. JT again raised the question of the parking allocation. Following extensive discussion, it was agreed to revisit the matter at a later date. MK noted that the timeline remained "ambitious." The chair thanked all present for a lively exchange of views. Item 5: Any other business. None, owing to time constraints.

Model: again = JT keeps raising it (presupposition) · extensive discussion … revisit at a later date = long argument, no decision (vagueness) · scare quotes on "ambitious" = unrealistic (distancing) · a lively exchange of views = a row (understatement) · owing to time constraints = the meeting overran because of item 4 (juxtaposition). Real message: the office move is in trouble and the team is divided.

C · Implicature Lab — Encode Cards (2.1, Round 2)

One card per group, face down. The group conveys the message by implication only, in a 4–6 line email or exchange, and keeps the card hidden until the peer test. The message is the card's, not the learners' own opinion.

Encode · 1

Your slides have far too much text.

Reader: Tom, a friendly colleague, presenting to a client on Friday. Format: a short email.

Encode · 2

Your music is too loud after 11 p.m.

Reader: a new neighbor you've met once in the elevator. Format: a note under the door, or a four-line conversation.

Encode · 3

I've been doing a colleague's work for months, and I want it recognized.

Reader: your manager, in your monthly check-in. Format: a four-line exchange.

Encode · 4

The proposed deadline is unrealistic.

Reader: the project board, who chose the date. Format: a short email.

Encode · 5

This person is a fine team member but not ready to manage anyone.

Reader: a hiring manager asking for a reference. Format: three or four sentences of a reference letter.

Encode · 6

I'd rather not join your 6 a.m. weekend running club.

Reader: an enthusiastic friend who has asked three times. Format: a text-message exchange.

D · A/B Pair Cards — "Say it without saying it" (2.1)

Pairs. A has a message and must convey it without stating it; B responds naturally in role, then chooses what A really meant from three options. Swap cards and roles. For C4 early finishers or the C5 warmer. Everything is on the card: nobody plays themselves.

2.1 · Pair 1 · A

Dinner guest

The host's soup is far too salty. You'd love some water, and you don't want a second bowl. Don't say "salty" or "no." Use at least one indirect move.

2.1 · Pair 1 · B

Dinner host

You made soup from your grandmother's recipe and you're proud of it. Offer A a second bowl. At the end, choose: A (a) loves the soup and wants more · (b) finds it too salty · (c) is not hungry.

2.1 · Pair 2 · A

Office colleague

B borrows your stapler every day and never returns it. Today it's gone again. You want it back and you want B to stop. Don't say "stop" or "return."

2.1 · Pair 2 · B

Office colleague

You're friendly and a little disorganized. Chat with A. At the end, choose: A (a) wants to borrow your stapler · (b) wants you to stop taking theirs · (c) needs office supplies ordered.

2.1 · Pair 3 · A

Team lead

B's report was due yesterday and you need it today. You don't want to sound angry. Use a presupposition trigger (yet, manage to, still).

2.1 · Pair 3 · B

Team member

You've been busy and you're not sure when the report was due. Chat with A. At the end, choose: A (a) is asking about your weekend · (b) needs the late report today · (c) wants a new report next month.

2.1 · Pair 4 · A

Neighbor

B's dog barks all day while B is at work, and you work from home. You'd like B to find a solution. Don't say "barks" or "noise."

2.1 · Pair 4 · B

Dog owner

You're out 9–5. You think your dog sleeps all day. Chat with A. At the end, choose: A (a) wants to walk your dog · (b) is hinting that your dog is noisy · (c) is thinking of getting a dog.

D · A/B Pair Cards — "What really happened?" (2.2)

Pairs, after the C5 listening. A reads four understated or ironic reports; a neutral voice is fine, because the context does the work. B asks one follow-up question for each ("Only a little damp?") and matches it to a fact on B's card. This is detection practice: nobody performs a joke.

D · A/B Pair Cards — "Name the frame" (2.3)

Pairs, C6 early finishers. Take turns to read a passage aloud (or pass the card silently). The partner names the subject and the source domain, then adds one more sentence in the same frame.

2.3 · A

1. "The negotiation opened with both sides moving pawns. By noon she had sacrificed a small point to take a larger one, and he could see his king was cornered."

2. "The inbox had flooded overnight. By ten I was bailing out messages with both hands, and the important one had sunk without a trace."

Frames: 1 negotiation = chess · 2 inbox = flood / sinking boat
2.3 · B

1. "Our small business is a young tree. This year we put down roots; next year, if the weather is kind, we'll see the first branches."

2. "The rumor caught fire over coffee. By lunch it was spreading from office to office, and nobody could find an extinguisher."

Frames: 1 business = a growing tree · 2 rumor = fire

F · Imagery Passages (2.3, Round 1)

One passage per group of 3. Each has one image from the wrong domain (the intruder) and one cliché. Fold the key under before handing out.

F1 · The morning commute

At seven the city's commuters become a river. They trickle out of side streets into the main avenues, where the current thickens and slows. At the station the whole thing grinds to a halt at the ticket barriers, and the crowd eddies around a man who has stopped to check his phone. Down on the platform the water rises; when the train arrives, it drains the platform in a single gulp and carries its passengers downstream, packed like sardines. By nine the riverbed is almost dry, and only a few late stragglers pool by the coffee cart.

Key: THE COMMUTE IS A RIVER · images: trickle, current, thickens, eddies, the water rises, drains, downstream, riverbed, pool · intruder: grinds to a halt (machinery) → the river narrows to a single channel · cliché: packed like sardines (in-domain, but worn) → pressed together like stones in a riverbed

F2 · The deadline

For three weeks the deadline has been camped outside our walls. At first it was a distant smudge of tents on the horizon, easy to ignore. Now it has dug trenches around the building. Every morning a fresh volley of emails flies over the ramparts, and our supplies of coffee and patience are running low. The designers have retreated to the inner keep; the accountants are rationing their weekends. The ball is now in the client's court. At the end of the day, whether the walls hold on Monday is anyone's guess.

Key: THE DEADLINE IS A SIEGE (an army) · images: camped, tents, trenches, volley, ramparts, supplies, retreated, inner keep, rationing, the walls hold · intruder: the ball is in the client's court (tennis) → the client's messenger is expected at the gate · cliché: at the end of the day → cut, or By Monday night

F3 · The restaurant kitchen

At six the kitchen tunes up. Knives are laid out like bows; pans are set in place like drums. When the first orders arrive, the head chef lifts her hand like a conductor, and every section plays in perfect harmony: the sizzle of the grill, the steady rhythm of the chopping boards, the high whistle of the steamer. By eight the music reaches a crescendo. One burned sauce and the whole symphony could go off the rails. At midnight the last plate goes out, and the exhausted players take their bow to an audience of dirty dishes.

Key: THE KITCHEN IS AN ORCHESTRA · images: tunes up, bows, drums, conductor, section, rhythm, whistle, crescendo, symphony, players, bow, audience · intruder: go off the rails (train) → could fall out of tune · cliché: in perfect harmony (in-domain, but worn) → comes in exactly on the beat

G · Game — Between the Lines (C6 early finishers · C7 warmer)

How to play (groups of 3–4, one die, one counter each): roll and move. Do the task on your square aloud. The group judges with the cheat sheet (below): good = stay; not yet = go back 1. DECODE = say what's really meant · TRIGGER = what's taken for granted? · DISARM = refuse the premise · IMPLY = say it without saying it · DRY IT = make it an understatement · PIVOT = explain the pun · FIGURE = name the figure · MAP = name the conceptual metaphor and add one expression · FIX = fix the mixed metaphor. Nobody has to tell a joke: PIVOT squares are about explaining. Squares go in a snake: 1–6 left to right, 7–12 right to left, and so on. First to FINISH wins.

H · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural speed. Read each script twice: once for the main idea, once to check. In this module tone is content: rehearse the pauses and stress marked in brackets. Change your voice for each speaker, or give one part to a confident learner who volunteers. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

2.1-A · The reference call (Workbook 2.1, Ex 1)

Mr. Okafor pauses before each compliment and says it warmly but slowly. Dana is brisk and polite.

Dana: Hello, Mr. Okafor? This is Dana Price. Thanks for taking my call. I'm calling about Leo Marsh. He's applied for a team-lead role with us, and he gave your name as a reference. You supervised him for two years? · Mr. Okafor: I did, yes. Leo… (pause) Leo was always very punctual. Never missed a day. · Dana: That's good to hear. And how was he with the actual work, the client reports? · Mr. Okafor: Well… (pause) he certainly had a lot of enthusiasm for new projects. And his presentations were always very… colorful. · Dana: I see. Would you say he's ready to lead a team? · Mr. Okafor: He was extremely popular at team social events. · Dana: Would you hire him again? · Mr. Okafor: We've restructured the department since then, so it's hard to say. · Dana: Of course. Did he manage to meet his deadlines in the end? · Mr. Okafor: I think it's fair to say he always got there eventually. · Dana: Right. Thank you, Mr. Okafor. That's very helpful. · Mr. Okafor: Not at all. I'm sure he'll find the right role.

Answers: 1 punctual · 2 enthusiasm · 3 colorful · 4 social events · 5 restructured · 6 eventually. Will Dana hire Leo as a team lead? Almost certainly not. Dana's presupposition: Did he manage to meet his deadlines in the end? Extra: "I'm sure he'll find the right role" implies not this one. Ask: "Did Mr. Okafor say one negative word?" (No.)

2.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. Well, she certainly has a lot of energy. · 2. That question assumes we're behind schedule. · 3. Did you manage to finish the report in the end? · 4. The company reportedly declined to comment.

Ask: "Which sentence is faint praise? Which refuses a premise? Which contains a trigger? Which distances the writer?" (1 · 2 · 3, manage to and in the end · 4, reportedly.) Check the spelling of reportedly, declined, schedule.

2.2-A · Sincere, ironic or understated? (Workbook 2.2, Ex 1)

Read the situation in a neutral voice, pause, then say the line as marked. Don't pull faces: learners should decide from the context and the voice.

1. Your colleague has just fixed the printer. "You're a genius." (warm, lively) · 2. Five minutes later, the printer jams again. "You're a genius." (flat) · 3. It's pouring on the company picnic. "Perfect weather for a picnic." (slow, exaggerated) · 4. It's sunny and 75 degrees. "Perfect weather for a picnic." (bright, sincere) · 5. A friend arrives fifty minutes late for dinner. "Oh, don't rush on my account." (over-polite, drawn out) · 6. Your team has just won a national award. "Well, that's not bad for a Tuesday." (level, dry, a small smile in the voice) · 7. The "quick" meeting has lasted three hours. "Well, that flew by." (flat) · 8. A colleague brings in homemade cookies. "These are amazing. Thank you so much." (warm)

Answers: 1 S · 2 I · 3 I · 4 S · 5 I (sarcasm: a target and an edge) · 6 U (litotes) · 7 I · 8 S. Ask: "What changed between 1 and 2, and between 3 and 4?" (Only the situation and the voice.)

2.2-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. Oh, great. Just what I needed. · 2. It wasn't exactly our finest hour. · 3. I used to be a banker, but I lost interest. · 4. "The weather's a touch damp," said the man, wringing out his coat.

Ask: "Name the device in each." (1 irony · 2 understatement · 3 pun on interest · 4 understatement, deadpan.) Check the punctuation of the quotation in 4.

2.3-A · The library at closing time (Workbook 2.3, Ex 1)

Read slowly, with a pause before each image and a soft fall at the end of each sentence.

At a quarter to nine, the library begins to fall asleep. The overhead lights blink off row by row, like heavy eyelids. The last readers drift toward the doors, and the building exhales, a long, slow breath of warm paper and dust. Somewhere in its belly, the heating rumbles and settles. The returns cart is fed its final meal of books. By nine the great creature is curled around its silence, breathing softly through the vents, dreaming, perhaps, of the thousand stories it has swallowed whole. Outside, the street lamps keep watch until morning.

Answers: 1 blink · 2 eyelids · 3 exhales · 4 belly · 5 fed · 6 curled. Controlling metaphor: THE LIBRARY IS A SLEEPING ANIMAL. Simile: like heavy eyelids. Personification in the frame: the street lamps keep watch. Ask: "Is there any image that doesn't belong?" (No: that's why it works.)

2.3-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. Her argument was a fortress. · 2. We need a few more hands on the night shift. · 3. That idea is still half-baked. · 4. We'll cross that bridge when we come to it.

Ask: "Name the figure or the family." (1 metaphor, ARGUMENT IS WAR · 2 synecdoche · 3 IDEAS ARE FOOD · 4 an idiom from LIFE IS A JOURNEY, correct, not mixed.) Then: "What happens if I say burn instead of cross?" (A mixed metaphor.)
