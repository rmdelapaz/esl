The course is decided by the highest section secured with all easier sections also secured. The total-score column is a quick cross-check only. With four options, a learner who guesses every item scores about 12, which is why the Foundations band runs to 12. Secured Section 6 (C2) confirms Mastery English; it's also a sign the learner may be better served by a specialist course (IELTS, Business) or by C2 modules chosen to fit their goals.

Part A · Answers and explanations

A1

Section 1 · items 1–10

secured = 7/10
#SentenceAnsWhy
1Hello! My name is Ana.bbe with he/she/it/name → is.
2Where are you from?cyou takes are.
3I want an apple, please.ban before a vowel sound; two/these need a plural.
4My sister is 25 years old.cAge uses be. Watch for Spanish/Portuguese/French/Arabic speakers choosing has.
5Do you like coffee?bPresent simple question with you → Do.
6She works in a hospital.bThird person -s.
7There are two chairs in the kitchen.bPlural → there are.
8My mother's brother is my uncle.aFamily vocabulary: uncle.
9Look! It is raining now.bHappening now → present continuous is raining.
10What time is it? — It's half past seven.ahalf past seven = 7:30.

Section 2 · items 11–19

secured = 6/9
#SentenceAnsWhy
11I went to the doctor yesterday.cPast simple irregular: went.
12Did you see the game last night?bPast simple question → Did + base verb.
13My brother is taller than me.bShort adjective comparative → taller.
14We are going to visit my aunt next weekend.bPlan → are going to + verb (needs be).
15Have you ever eaten sushi?cPresent perfect → past participle eaten.
16I was cooking dinner when the phone rang.bShort action interrupting a longer one → past simple rang.
17She has lived here since 2019.bPoint in time → since.
18It's the best restaurant in town.cIrregular superlative → best.
19I need to make an appointment with the dentist.bCollocation: make an appointment.

Section 3 · items 20–28

secured = 6/9
#SentenceAnsWhy
20If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.bFirst conditional → will stay.
21I have been studying English for two years now.cContinuing from past to now → have been studying.
22If I had more money, I would buy a car.bSecond conditional → had.
23This bridge was built in 1920.bPast passive → was built.
24He asked me where I lived.bReported question → statement word order: I lived.
25I used to live in the countryside when I was a child.aused to + base verb → live.
26The woman who lives next door is a nurse.aRelative pronoun for a person → who (that also possible, not offered).
27I'm really looking forward to seeing you.clook forward to + -ing → to seeing.
28Can you look after my cat while I'm on vacation?aPhrasal verb: look after = take care of.
B2

Section 4 · items 29–37

secured = 6/9
#SentenceAnsWhy
29By the time we arrived, the movie had already started.bEarlier past → past perfect had already started.
30If I had known about the traffic, I would have left earlier.cThird conditional → would have left.
31I wish I hadn't eaten so much last night. I feel terrible.bRegret about the past → wish + past perfect hadn't eaten.
32She can't have left already — her coat is still here.aLogical deduction (impossible) → can't have.
33He denied taking the money.bdeny + -ing → taking.
34The report must be finished by Friday.bModal passive → must be finished.
35The new rule had a big impact on small businesses.bNoun collocation have an impact on; affect is a verb.
36Despite the rain, the game went ahead.bdespite + noun → Despite; although needs a clause.
37The meeting has been put off until next Tuesday.aput off = postpone.

secured = 5/7
#SentenceAnsWhy
38Not only was he late, but he also forgot the tickets.bNegative adverbial → inversion was he.
39Had I realized how difficult it was, I wouldn't have attempted it.bInverted third conditional → wouldn't have attempted.
40It's high time the city did something about housing.bIt's high time + past form → did.
41Her argument was so cogent that nobody could find a flaw in it.acogent = clear, logical and convincing.
42The findings, which were published last month, have been widely criticized.bNon-defining relative clause (commas) → which, never that.
43The company is facing mounting criticism over its safety record.aCollocation mounting criticism.

Section 6 · items 45–50

secured = 4/6
#SentenceAnsWhy
45The minister's remarks were widely seen as an attempt to sidestep the issue.asidestep an issue = avoid dealing with it.
46Scarcely had she reached the stage when the lights went out.bScarcely … when → inversion had she reached.
47His apology struck many as perfunctory — polite, but clearly insincere.aperfunctory = done only as a duty, without real feeling.

Using Part B

Text 1 at 4–5/5 supports an A2 written band or higher. Text 2 at 4–5/5 supports B2 or higher. If Part A says B2 but Text 2 is 2/5, treat the written band as B1 and let the interview decide.

Writing descriptors (Part C)

Read all the tasks the learner attempted and choose the best-fit band: the description that matches most of the writing. Don't count errors; ask whether a reader could follow it and how much the learner can say.

BandWhat the writing looks like
Pre-A1Can't yet complete Task 1, or copies/writes only their name. Literacy flag: do the literacy check before placing.
A1Completes the Task 1 form with single words or short phrases. Task 2, if attempted, is 1–3 very simple sentences (I work. I like coffee.) with basic errors in word order and spelling.
A2Task 2: 3–6 short sentences on daily life, linked with and, but, because. Present simple mostly correct; past forms attempted. Errors frequent but meaning clear.
B1Task 2 easy; Task 3 attempted: a connected text with paragraphs or clear sequencing, simple opinions and reasons. Range of tenses; errors noticeable but rarely block meaning. May be shorter than 120 words.
B2Task 3: clear, organized text of the required length that develops ideas with examples and linking (however, as a result). Good control of grammar; errors occasional and minor. Vocabulary varied, with some less common words.
C1Task 3: well-structured, fluent text with a clear line of argument, precise vocabulary and a consistent, appropriate register. Complex sentences used naturally; errors rare.
C2Task 3: polished, persuasive and nuanced. Wide, exact vocabulary and idiom; varied sentence structure used for effect; virtually error-free. Reads like a skilled writer.

Speaking descriptors (Part D)

Rate the highest band the learner handled comfortably: most of the prompts in that band answered with the features below, without breakdown. If you hesitate between two bands, choose the lower and note "borderline" on the profile.

BandRange & accuracyFluencyInteractionPronunciation
Pre-A1Responds with gestures, single words or L1. May recognize a few words (hello, yes, no, numbers) but can't yet build a sentence.Speech limited to isolated words; long silences.Needs the interviewer to point, mime and wait.Hard to understand beyond a few words.
A1Short, memorized phrases and simple sentences about self and surroundings (I'm from…, I like…, It's a bus). Basic words for everyday things, numbers, time.Many pauses to search for words; relies on set phrases.Answers simple questions if they are slow and clear; rarely asks questions back.Understandable with effort; strong L1 accent.
A2Simple sentences joined with and, but, because. Talks about past events and plans using basic forms; describes home, work and routine. Frequent errors, but meaning usually clear.Short turns; noticeable pauses, but keeps going on familiar topics.Handles short exchanges; asks simple follow-up questions.Generally understandable; errors sometimes cause confusion.
B1Tells a connected story; gives opinions and reasons; uses a reasonable range of tenses, including simple conditionals. Can say most things they want to on familiar topics, with some circumlocution.Keeps talking at length with pauses to plan and repair.Starts, maintains and closes a conversation on familiar topics.Clearly understandable; accent evident; occasional mispronunciation.
B2Develops an argument with reasons, examples and a balanced view. Good grammatical control; errors don't cause misunderstanding and are often self-corrected. Varied vocabulary on general topics.Even tempo; few noticeable long pauses.Interacts naturally; takes turns, checks understanding, responds to challenge.Clear, natural intonation; accent doesn't hinder.
C1Explains complex ideas fluently and precisely, using a broad vocabulary, idiomatic expressions and complex structures. Errors rare and hard to spot.Fluent and spontaneous; searches for words only on abstract topics.Uses language flexibly for social and professional purposes; handles disagreement smoothly.Varies intonation and stress to express finer meaning.
C2Conveys fine shades of meaning with precision; uses idiom and register naturally and switches style at will. Grammar consistently accurate.Effortless; any hesitation is for thinking, not for language.Leads and shapes the conversation; persuades, qualifies and concedes with ease.Pronunciation fully natural for a proficient speaker; accent irrelevant to communication.
Accent isn't level

Rate how easy the speech is to understand, not how "native" it sounds. A strong accent with clear, flexible language can still be C1. Be especially careful with learners whose pronunciation is affected by L1 sounds (for example, final consonants dropped by Vietnamese speakers, or p/b for Arabic speakers). Those are teaching points, not placement evidence.
