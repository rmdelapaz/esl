🏠 Lesson 3.3: There is / There are — My Home

Welcome home! In this lesson you'll learn how to say what is in a place — a room, a house, a city street. With just two little phrases, "there is" and "there are," plus a handful of words like in, on, and under, you'll be able to describe the whole world around you.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use there is and there are, including negatives and questions
  • Use prepositions of place — in, on, under, next to, between, behind, in front of
  • Name the rooms of a home and common furniture
  • Describe a room or your home in several sentences

⏱️ Estimated Time: ~50 minutes

🎯 Project: Describe your room or home in 6 or more sentences using there is / there are and prepositions of place.

Saying What's in a Place

Look around the room you are in right now. Maybe there is a window. Maybe there are two chairs. In English, when we want to say that something exists in a place, we don't say "a window is." Instead we start with a special little phrase: there is or there are.

This is one of the most useful patterns in all of English. You'll use it to describe your home, your town, a photo, a hotel room, even a plate of food. Once you can say "there is" and "there are," you can paint a picture of almost anything with words. 🖼️

🧠 Mindset

You already know how to look at a room and see what's inside it — that's not the hard part. The only new thing today is the English words to wrap around what you already see. Don't try to be perfect. Try to be understood. A learner who says "There is one table, two chair" is communicating beautifully, even with a small mistake. Speak first, polish later. 🌱

There is / There are

The rule is simple and it depends on one thing: are you talking about one thing or more than one thing?

  • There is + one thing (singular): There is a sofa in the living room.
  • There are + two or more things (plural): There are two bedrooms.

We also use there is for things we don't count — like water or furniture: There is some water in the glass.

UseFull formExample
One thing (singular)There is a…There is a table in the kitchen.
Many things (plural)There are …There are three chairs.
UncountableThere is some…There is some water on the floor.

💡 The short way: "There's"

In everyday speaking, we almost always shorten there is to there's /ðɛərz/. So "There is a lamp" becomes "There's a lamp." Both are correct — there's just sounds more natural.

We do not usually shorten there are in writing. Say and write the full there are.

⚠️ Important: The choice depends on the word right after is/are. There is a bed and two lamps — we use is because the first thing (a bed) is singular. Look at what comes next!

Negatives & Questions

To say something is not in a place, add not. We usually use the short forms isn't and aren't.

TypeSingular / UncountablePlural
Positive There is a garden. There are two windows.
Negative There isn't a garden. There aren't any windows.
Question Is there a lift? Are there any chairs?
Short answer Yes, there is. / No, there isn't. Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.

Notice that in a question we simply flip the order: There is → Is there…? and There are → Are there…?

✅ Answer with the same words

The easy trick for short answers: use the same little words from the question.

Is there a balcony? → Yes, there is. / No, there isn't.

Are there any shops? → Yes, there are. / No, there aren't.

⚠️ Don't say "Yes, there's." for a short answer — say the full "Yes, there is."

Some & Any

You may have noticed the words some and any above. Here's the light rule for beginners — it's easier than it looks.

📖 The simple rule

Use SOME in positive sentences: There are some plants.

Use ANY in negatives and questions: There isn't any furniture. · Are there any shops?

Sentence typeWordExample
Positive (+)someThere are some books on the shelf.
Negative (−)anyThere aren't any towels in the bathroom.
Question (?)anyAre there any chairs in the hall?

💡 One friendly note

We use some / any with plural things (chairs, plants) and with uncountable things (water, furniture). With one single thing, just use a / an: There is a mirror. Don't worry too much — you'll pick this up naturally with practice.

Prepositions of Place

Now you can say what is in a place. Next, let's say where it is. Small words called prepositions of place tell us the exact position of something. Look at each one with its picture-in-words and example.

PrepositionMeaningExample
ininside somethingThe keys are in the drawer.
onon top of / touching a surfaceThe lamp is on the table.
underbelow somethingThe cat is under the table.
next to / besideat the side ofThe chair is next to the desk.
betweenin the middle of two thingsThe lamp is between the sofa and the wall.
behindat the back ofThe garden is behind the house.
in front ofat the front ofThe car is in front of the garage.
above / overhigher than (not touching)There is a mirror above the sink.

💡 One picture, many prepositions

Imagine a small living room. Watch how the same cat moves 🐈:

  • The cat is on the sofa.
  • The cat is under the table.
  • The cat is next to the lamp.
  • The cat is between the two armchairs.
  • The cat is behind the door.
  • The cat is in front of the TV.

Same room, same cat — only the little word changes. That little word carries all the meaning!

⚠️ Watch out: We say "in front of" (three words together) — not "in front." And the opposite of "in front of" is behind, not "back of."

Rooms & Furniture

To describe a home, you need the words for its rooms and the things inside them. Read each word out loud.

🚪 Rooms of a home

  • kitchen — where you cook
  • bedroom — where you sleep
  • bathroom — where you wash
  • living room — where you relax
  • dining room — where you eat
  • hall — the entrance area
  • garden — outside, with plants
  • garage — for the car
  • balcony — a small outdoor space

🛋️ Furniture & things

  • sofa — long soft seat
  • armchair — soft chair for one
  • bed — for sleeping
  • table
  • chair
  • desk — table for working
  • fridge — keeps food cold
  • cooker / stove — for cooking
  • TV — television
  • lamp — a light
  • shelf — for books
  • wardrobe — for clothes
  • mirror
  • sink — for washing
  • shower
  • window
  • door

💬 Culture & register note

In British English, an apartment is often called a flat, and an elevator is a lift. In American English, they're an apartment and an elevator. A "cooker" is more British; "stove" is more American. Both are correct — use whichever fits where you live. 🌍

Dialogue & Phrases

Here is Sara showing her friend Tom around her new apartment. Read both parts out loud — notice every there is / there are and preposition.

Tom: Wow, your new flat is nice! How many rooms are there?

Sara: Thanks! There are four rooms. There's a living room, a kitchen, a bedroom, and a bathroom.

Tom: Is there a balcony?

Sara: Yes, there is! It's behind the living room. Come and see.

Tom: Nice. Are there any plants on it?

Sara: There are some plants, yes — between the two chairs.

Tom: And in the living room? Is there a TV?

Sara: There's a TV on the wall, and there's a sofa in front of it.

Tom: Is there a garage?

Sara: No, there isn't. But there's a lift, so it's easy.

Tom: It's a lovely home, Sara.

Here are the most useful phrases from the conversation. Keep them ready for your own project.

How many rooms are there? There are four rooms. There's a… Is there a…? Are there any…? Yes, there is. No, there isn't. It's next to the… It's between the… and the… Come and see!

Practice & Project

🏋️ Exercise 1: Choose is or are

Objective: Pick the correct form for singular or plural.

Write is or are in each gap.

  1. There ____ a sofa in the living room.
  2. There ____ two bedrooms.
  3. There ____ a mirror above the sink.
  4. There ____ three windows in the kitchen.
  5. There ____ some water on the floor.
  6. There ____ four chairs at the table.
✅ Answer

1. is (a sofa) · 2. are (two bedrooms) · 3. is (a mirror) · 4. are (three windows) · 5. is (water — uncountable) · 6. are (four chairs)

🏋️ Exercise 2: some or any?

Objective: Choose the right word for positives, negatives, and questions.

Fill each gap with some or any.

  1. There are ____ plants on the balcony.
  2. There isn't ____ furniture in the empty room.
  3. Are there ____ shops near your house?
  4. There are ____ books on the shelf.
  5. There aren't ____ towels in the bathroom.
✅ Answer

1. some (positive) · 2. any (negative) · 3. any (question) · 4. some (positive) · 5. any (negative)

🏋️ Exercise 3: Choose the right preposition

Objective: Match the position to the correct word.

Choose the best preposition for each described picture.

  1. The lamp is sitting on top of the table. → The lamp is (on / under) the table.
  2. The cat is below the bed, hiding. → The cat is (above / under) the bed.
  3. The lamp has a sofa on its left and a wall on its right. → The lamp is (between / behind) the sofa and the wall.
  4. The garden is at the back of the house. → The garden is (in front of / behind) the house.
  5. The keys are inside the drawer. → The keys are (in / on) the drawer.
  6. The car is parked directly before the garage door. → The car is (in front of / next to) the garage.
✅ Answer

1. on · 2. under · 3. between · 4. behind · 5. in · 6. in front of

🎯 Your Project: Describe Your Home

Time to describe your own room or home out loud! Do this to a partner, a teacher, or your phone's voice recorder. Aim for 6 or more sentences using there is / there are and prepositions.

  1. (3 min) Choose one room (or your whole home). Look around it — real or from memory.
  2. (4 min) Write 6+ sentences. Use there is and there are. Say how many things there are.
  3. (3 min) Add where things are with prepositions: The lamp is on the desk. The bed is next to the window.
  4. (2 min) Include at least one negative (There isn't a…) and, if you can, one question for your partner (Is there a…?).
  5. (3 min) Say it all out loud, or record it. Listen back once if you recorded.

Model answer: This is my bedroom. There is a bed next to the window. There is a small desk in front of the wall, and there is a lamp on the desk. There are two shelves above the desk, with some books on them. There is a wardrobe behind the door. There isn't a TV in my room, but there is a big window. It's a quiet, happy room. Is there a window in your room?

  • ☐ I said how many of something there are
  • ☐ I used at least three prepositions of place
  • ☐ I included one negative (There isn't…)
  • ☐ I said 6 or more sentences out loud

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Check the word right after is/are to choose the form: one thing → is, many → are.
  • Use the short form there's when speaking — it sounds natural.
  • Practice prepositions with real objects: put a pen on, under, next to, behind a cup and say each one.
  • Answer questions with the full form: Yes, there is. / No, there aren't.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "There have a table." ❌ — English doesn't use have here. Say There is a table. ✅
  • "There is two chairs." ❌ — Two is plural. Say There are two chairs. ✅
  • "There are any plants." ❌ — Use some in positives. Say There are some plants. ✅
  • "Yes, there's." ❌ — For short answers, use the full Yes, there is. ✅
  • "in front the house" ❌ — Keep all three words: in front of the house. ✅

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Use there is for one thing (or uncountable), and there are for two or more.
  • Negatives: there isn't / there aren't. Questions flip to Is there…? / Are there…?
  • Use some in positives; use any in negatives and questions.
  • Prepositions of place — in, on, under, next to, between, behind, in front of, above — tell us where something is.

🎉 What You've Accomplished

You can now describe the world around you! You can say what is in a room, how many of something there are, and exactly where each thing sits. That's a huge step — you've gone from single words to full, useful descriptions of real places. Look around your home and try it right now. 🎉

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Do I always have to say "there's" instead of "there is"?

No — both are correct. There's is just the natural short form for speaking. In careful writing, or for short answers like "Yes, there is," use the full form.

How do I choose is or are when there's a list?

Look at the first thing after is/are. There is a bed, two lamps, and a chair — the first thing (a bed) is singular, so we use is. Many speakers do this naturally.

When do I use "in" and when do I use "on"?

Use in for inside something (in the box, in the room) and on for on a surface (on the table, on the wall). Picture it: in = inside, on = touching the top.
