🎓 English Foundations

A complete, self-paced A1 Beginner English course. Twelve real lessons take you from your very first letter to your first real conversations — greetings, family, daily routines, the time, food, and getting around.

📚 About This Course

This is real language study, not a list of tips about English. Every lesson teaches actual vocabulary, grammar, and phrases you can use immediately — with dialogues to read aloud, exercises with answers, a hands-on speaking project, and a reflection journal to keep you moving forward.

It follows the CEFR A1 ("Breakthrough") level: the language a complete beginner needs to introduce themselves, ask and answer simple questions, and handle everyday situations in short, simple exchanges.

What You'll Be Able to Do

  • Spell your name and read the alphabet aloud
  • Greet people, introduce yourself, and say where you're from
  • Use numbers for age, prices, time, and phone numbers
  • Describe your family, your home, and everyday objects
  • Talk about your daily routine and what you're doing right now
  • Say what you can and can't do, and what food you like
  • Order in a café, go shopping, and ask for simple directions

Who This Course Is For

  • Self-study learners starting English from zero or refreshing the basics
  • Classroom students — every lesson has speaking tasks that work in pairs or groups
  • Teachers looking for a ready-made, sequenced A1 syllabus

💡 No prior English needed

You can start this course knowing zero English. All you need is about 45 minutes per lesson, a way to speak out loud (a partner, a class, or your phone's voice recorder), and a notebook for your learning journal.

🧭 How to Study This Course

  1. One lesson at a time, in order. Don't rush — it's a marathon, not a sprint.
  2. Speak out loud. Read every dialogue and phrase aloud. Language lives in your mouth, not just your eyes.
  3. Do the project. Each lesson ends with a real speaking task — that's where the learning sticks.
  4. Keep the journal. Five minutes of reflection after each lesson doubles what you remember.
  5. Review often. Come back to earlier lessons. Seeing old words again is how they become permanent.

🧠 A word before you begin

If you've tried to learn English before and stopped, that's okay — most people start more than once. You are not behind. You are exactly on time. Add the word yet to your doubts: "I can't speak English… yet." Let's begin. 🌱
