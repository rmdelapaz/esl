Instructor-Led Course Syllabus

English Foundations (A1 Beginner)

A 12-week, 24-class English course for adults starting from zero. Twice a week for 90 minutes, you learn the English you need every day: saying hello, talking about your family and your routine, shopping, ordering in a café, and talking to a doctor. Between classes, the free online lessons (with audio) are your homework.

Level

Class Schedule

Two classes a week, 90 minutes each. Every class has one lesson from the online course, except the two assessment classes. Lessons 4.3 and 8.1 are review projects that bring each half of the course together in a real-world task. ✓ = the can-do goal for that class.

Course Details

Prerequisites

  • None. This course is for true beginners: adults with no English, or only a few words.
  • New to the Latin alphabet? Welcome. Class 1 starts with the letters, and every lesson has picture cues, tracing, and copying support for learners who are still building reading and writing.
  • Learners who already read and write in English, or who place above A1 on the shared placement test, should start at Everyday English (A2).
  • No computer needed in class. A phone helps for homework, but it isn't required.

What’s Provided

  • A Student Workbook for all 22 lessons, in simple English with picture icons (the learner keeps it)
  • A welcome handout with class rules, what to bring, and QR codes to the online lessons
  • For the teacher: a Teacher’s Guide (a 90-minute plan for every class), slides, picture flashcards, role-play cards, listening scripts, games, and answer keys
  • Free access to the companion online course: English Foundations (A1), with 🔊 audio
  • A free learning-tools page that shows how to translate lessons into any language

Assessment

  • Every class: an “I can…” checklist and short speaking checks, with no grades
  • Class 13 · Progress Test 1 (Modules 1–4): listening, picture vocabulary, grammar, reading, a short form, and a 5-minute pair speaking task
  • Class 24 · Final Test (all 8 modules): the same skills plus an individual speaking interview with a café or doctor role-play
  • Rubrics follow the CEFR A1 descriptors. Results decide the next class, never who gets to celebrate
  • Personal-information tasks can always use a “new identity” instead of real details

Homework & the Online Course

  • After each class, do the matching online lesson at home: about 20 minutes, with 🔊 Listen buttons for every word and dialogue
  • Plus one workbook page to review the class
  • Missed a class? The online lesson covers the same language, so you can catch up
  • After the course: Everyday English (A2) continues the same free ladder, from A1 all the way to C2

What You’ll Be Able to Do

By the end of the course, learners reach CEFR A1 and can use English for simple everyday tasks:

Spell your name and give your phone number, email, and personal details
Greet people, introduce yourself, and introduce your family
Talk about your daily routine, your week, and how often you do things
Tell the time and talk about days, dates, and plans
Describe your home, where things are, and what people look like
Say what you can do, and what you like and don’t like
Shop, ask prices, and ask for and follow simple directions
Order food and drink politely and pay in a café
Talk about clothes and the weather
Tell a doctor what’s wrong and understand simple advice
