☕ Lesson 7.2: At a Café — I'd like…

Let's go to a café! This is a Listening & Speaking lesson. You will learn how to order food and drink politely, ask the price, and pay. Then you will listen and repeat a real café conversation, line by line, until it feels natural in your mouth. By the end, you can walk into any café and order with confidence.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Order politely with "I'd like…" and "Can I have…, please?"
  • Understand and answer "Would you like…?" when someone offers you something
  • Ask the price and pay: "How much is that?", "Here you are," "Keep the change."
  • Listen and repeat a full café conversation and role-play it yourself

⏱️ Estimated Time: ~50 minutes

🎯 Project: Role-play ordering at a café — play both the customer and the server in a short, polite conversation.

Welcome to the Café

A café is a great, low-stress place to practice English. You order one thing, you say please and thank you, you pay, and you sit down. The conversation is short and follows the same pattern almost every time. Once you learn that pattern, you can use it in cafés, coffee shops, and restaurants everywhere.

In the last lesson you learned food and drink words with some and any. Now you will use those words in a real, polite conversation. This lesson is all about speaking and listening — so get ready to say the words out loud, many times.

🧠 Mindset

Many learners feel nervous to speak in a café. That is normal! Here is a secret: servers hear the same few sentences all day. They want to help you. If you say "A coffee, please," that is enough — you will get your coffee! 🌱 Every time you order in English, you get braver. Small steps, real progress. Let's go in. ☕

Café & Menu Words

Here are the words you see and hear in a café. Read each one out loud and picture it.

🥤 Drinks

  • a coffee — hot brown drink
  • a tea — hot drink with leaves
  • a hot chocolate — warm sweet chocolate drink
  • an orange juice — juice from oranges
  • a bottle of water — water in a bottle
  • a glass of milk — milk in a glass

🥪 Food

  • a sandwich — food between two pieces of bread
  • a piece of cake — one slice of sweet cake
  • a biscuit / a cookie — small sweet food
  • a salad — a plate of vegetables
  • a muffin — a small round cake

🪑 In the café

  • a table for two — a table for two people
  • the menu — the list of food and drink
  • the bill / the check — the paper that shows what you pay
  • the change — money the server gives back
  • to eat in — to stay and eat in the café
  • to take away / to go — to carry your order out

💬 British & American words

In British English, the paper you pay is the bill, and you take food away. In American English, it is the check, and you take food to go. Both are correct — use the words for your country. Prices may be in £ (pounds), $ (dollars), or € (euros). 🌍

Ordering Politely

There are two very polite, very common ways to order. Learn both. You will use them every day.

I'd like a coffee, please.

Can I have a sandwich, please?

Polite phraseExample
I'd like…I'd like a tea, please.
I'd like…I'd like a piece of cake, please.
Can I have…?Can I have a coffee, please?
Can I have…?Can I have a bottle of water, please?

💡 I'd like = I would like

I'd like is the short form of I would like. It means I want — but it is much more polite. In a café, always choose I'd like over I want. Say "I'd like a coffee," not "I want a coffee." The little word please makes it even nicer. 🙂

⚠️ Important Note: Don't forget please! In English, ordering without please can sound rude, even if you don't mean it. "A coffee, please" is warm and polite. "A coffee" alone can sound sharp. Please is a tiny word that makes a big difference.

Would You Like…?

The server may offer you something with "Would you like…?" This is the polite way to offer. Notice it uses some (not any) because it is a polite offer — just like we saw in the last lesson!

The server asksYou can say
Would you like anything else?No, thank you. That's all. / Yes, I'd like a cake too.
Would you like some milk with that?Yes, please. / No, thanks.
To eat in or take away?Eat in, please. / Take away, please.
Anything to drink?Yes, I'd like a water, please.

✅ Pro Tip

Two short answers do most of the work in a café: Yes, please. and No, thank you. When someone offers you something, one of these two is almost always the right answer. Easy and polite! 👍

How much is that?  ·  Here you are.  ·  Keep the change.

💡 Reading prices out loud

We say £4.50 as "four pounds fifty" or just "four fifty." We say $3.20 as "three twenty" or "three dollars twenty." You don't need to say the word "point." Short and simple! 💷

The Café Conversation

Here is a full café conversation. Maya is the customer. Leo is the server (the person who works in the café). Read the whole thing first. Then, in the next part, you will listen and repeat it line by line.

Leo: Hi there! Welcome. What can I get you?

Maya: Hello. I'd like a coffee, please.

Leo: Sure. Would you like some milk with that?

Maya: Yes, please. And can I have a cheese sandwich, please?

Leo: Of course. Would you like anything else?

Maya: Yes, a piece of cake, please. Oh — and a bottle of water.

Leo: Great. To eat in or take away?

Maya: Eat in, please. Is there a table for two?

Leo: Yes, there's one by the window. Take a seat, and I'll bring it over.

Maya: Thank you. How much is that?

Leo: That's £8.50, please.

Maya: Here you are. Keep the change.

Leo: Thank you very much! Enjoy your coffee. 😊

What can I get you? I'd like a coffee, please. Can I have a sandwich, please? Would you like anything else? To eat in or take away? Is there a table for two? How much is that? That's £8.50. Here you are. Keep the change.

Listen & Repeat

🔊 Listen & Repeat

Now let's make these words feel natural in your mouth. Go back up to The Café Conversation and do this:

  • Tap the 🔊 buttons and the speaker names (Leo, Maya) to hear each line.
  • After each line, press pause and repeat it out loud. Copy the sound and the music of the sentence.
  • Do it three times: first slowly, then normal speed, then try to sound friendly and relaxed.
  • Finally, read only Maya's lines out loud (you are the customer), then only Leo's lines (you are the server).

Don't rush. Repeating out loud is how your mouth learns English. The more you say it, the easier the real café will feel. 🎧

Now check that you understood the conversation. Try to answer each question before you open it.

1. What does Maya order to drink first?

A coffee, with some milk. She says, "I'd like a coffee, please," and then "Yes, please" when Leo offers milk.

2. What food does Maya order?

A cheese sandwich and a piece of cake. She also asks for a bottle of water.

3. Does Maya eat in or take away?

She eats in. She says, "Eat in, please," and asks if there is a table for two.

4. How much is Maya's order, and what does she say when she pays?

It is £8.50. She says, "Here you are. Keep the change."

5. Which polite phrases did Maya use to order?

She used "I'd like a coffee, please" and "Can I have a cheese sandwich, please?" — both very polite, both with please.

🎯 Your Project: Role-Play at a Café

Now it's your turn to order! You will do a short café role-play. If you have a partner, take turns being the customer and the server. If you are alone, play both parts out loud, using two different voices.

  1. (2 min) Choose your order: one drink and one food from the menu words above.
  2. (3 min) Start as the server: "Hi! What can I get you?"
  3. (4 min) As the customer, order politely: "I'd like… , please." and "Can I have… , please?" Answer "Would you like anything else?"
  4. (3 min) Ask the price and pay: "How much is that?" → "Here you are. Keep the change."
  5. (3 min) Do the whole role-play again, faster and friendlier. Record it if you can, and listen once.

Model role-play:
Server: "Good morning! What can I get you?"
You: "Hi. I'd like a hot chocolate, please. And can I have a muffin, please?"
Server: "Sure. Would you like anything else?"
You: "No, thank you. That's all. How much is that?"
Server: "That's £4.20."
You: "Here you are. Keep the change. Thank you!"

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Use "I'd like…" — it is more polite than "I want…" in a café.
  • Always add please when you order, and thank you when you receive something.
  • Learn two short answers by heart: "Yes, please." and "No, thank you."
  • Listen and repeat the conversation out loud many times, until it feels easy.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "I want a coffee." ⚠️ — Not wrong, but it can sound direct. In a café, say "I'd like a coffee, please." ✅
  • Forgetting "please": Ordering without please can sound rude. Always add it: "A tea, please." ✅
  • "I'd like a water?" with a question voice — I'd like is a statement, not a question. Say it calmly, not rising. Use rising voice for "Can I have…?"
  • "How much it is?" ❌ — The order is "How much is that?" / "How much is it?" ✅
  • Answering an offer with silence: When someone asks "Would you like…?", always reply — "Yes, please" or "No, thank you."

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Order politely with "I'd like… , please" and "Can I have… , please?" — I'd like = I would like, and it is more polite than I want.
  • Answer an offer ("Would you like…?") with "Yes, please" or "No, thank you."
  • Ask the price with "How much is that?"; pay with "Here you are," and "Keep the change" to leave a small tip.
  • Always add please and thank you — small words that make you sound warm and polite.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Is "I'd like" really different from "I want"?

Both mean the same thing, but "I'd like" is much more polite. "I want" can sound like a small child or a bit rude in a shop or café. For ordering, always choose "I'd like…" or "Can I have…?"

Do I always have to tip and say "Keep the change"?

No. Tipping is different in every country. "Keep the change" is just one polite option when you want to leave a small tip. In many cafés you don't need to tip at all. Learn the phrase so you understand it and can use it if you choose.

What if I don't understand the server?

That's OK! Say "Sorry, can you say that again, please?" or "Sorry, I don't understand." Servers are used to helping. Speaking slowly and smiling always helps too. 🙂
