Part A — Shopping & Prices

When you buy something, there are only two things you must do: ask the price and be polite. Let's do both.

Asking the price

English uses two small questions. The choice depends on one thing or many things.

You're asking about…QuestionExample answer
one thing (singular)How much is it?It's five dollars.
many things (plural)How much are they?They're ten euros.
the whole billHow much is that altogether?That's twelve dollars altogether.

💡 Remember is / are from Lesson 2

This is the same to be you already know! One apple → "How much is it?" · Two apples → "How much are they?" The answer uses It's… or They're… and a number. You already know this!

Being polite: the magic words

In English shops and cafés, we make our words softer to sound friendly. The most useful phrase is "I'd like…". It means "I would like." It is the polite way to say "I want."

I'd like a coffee, please. Can I have a bag, please? How much is it? How much are they? How much is that altogether? Here you are. Cash or card? Here's your change. Here's your receipt. Thank you!

✅ "I want" vs. "I'd like"

"I want a coffee" is correct grammar, but it can sound a little rude — like a small child. "I'd like a coffee, please" is warm and polite. Use "I'd like…" and "please" every time you buy something. People will smile at you.

Shopping wordlist

  • shop — a place to buy things
  • supermarket — a big food shop
  • market — an open-air place to buy food/goods
  • price — how much money something costs
  • cheap — not expensive, low price 👍
  • expensive — a high price 💰
  • dollar — money ($) in the US
  • euro — money (€) in much of Europe
  • cash — paper money and coins
  • card — a bank / credit card
  • receipt — the paper showing what you paid /rɪˈsiːt/
  • change — the money you get back
  • bag — you carry your things in it
  • size — how big (small, medium, large)
  • on sale — a lower, special price
⚠️ Careful with "receipt": the p is silent! We say "ri-SEET" /rɪˈsiːt/, not "re-KEIPT."

Listen to a shopping conversation

Read both parts out loud. This is how buying a coffee sounds.

Server: Hi! What would you like?

You: Hello. I'd like a coffee, please.

Server: Of course. Anything else?

You: Yes, a cookie too. How much is that altogether?

Server: That's four dollars fifty.

You: Here you are. Can I pay by card?

Server: Sure. Here's your receipt. Have a nice day!

You: Thank you! You too. 👋

Part B — Asking for Directions

Everybody gets lost sometimes. The good news: you only need a few phrases to ask the way. You need a few more to understand the answer.

Asking the way

Always start with "Excuse me,". It is the polite way to get a stranger's attention.

Excuse me, where's the station? Is there a bank near here? How do I get to the park? Is it far? Thank you very much!

Giving directions (the imperative)

To tell someone where to go, we use the plain verb — no "you," just the action. We call this the imperative. It is short and clear.

PhraseWhat it means
Go straight on / straight ahead.Keep walking forward. ⬆️
Turn left. / Turn right.Change direction. ⬅️ ➡️
Take the first left. / the second right.Count the streets, then turn.
Go past the school.Walk by it and continue.
It's on your left. / on your right.The place is on that side.
It's next to the bank.Right beside it.
It's opposite the café.On the other side of the street.
It's on the corner of Main Street.Where two streets meet.

💡 next to, opposite, on the corner — hello again!

You already met prepositions of place in Lesson 3 (there is / there are). Directions use the same words: next to, opposite, near, on the corner. Review is powerful. The same small words come back and do big jobs.

Listen to a directions conversation

A tourist stops a local person on the street. Read both parts out loud.

Tourist: Excuse me, where's the train station?

Local: The station? Go straight on and take the second left.

Tourist: The second left. Okay.

Local: Then go past the park. The station is on your right, opposite a hotel.

Tourist: Is it far?

Local: No, about five minutes.

Tourist: Thank you very much!

Local: You're welcome. Have a good trip! 👋

Practice & Capstone Project

🏋️ Exercise 1: One thing or many? (is it / are they)

Objective: Choose the right price question.

Fill each gap with is it or are they.

  • This apple — How much ?
  • These shoes — How much ?
  • That coffee — How much ?
  • Those bags — How much ?
✅ Answer

1. How much is it? (one apple) · 2. How much are they? (shoes = plural) · 3. How much is it? (one coffee) · 4. How much are they? (bags = plural)

🏋️ Exercise 2: Follow the directions

Objective: Understand a set of directions.

Read the directions and answer: where is the café?

"Go straight on. Take the first right. Go past the bank. The café is on your left, opposite the school."

  • Do you turn left or right first?
  • What do you go past?
  • What is opposite the café?
✅ Answer

You take the first right. · You go past the bank. · The school is opposite the café.

🏋️ Exercise 3: Make it polite

Objective: Turn plain words into polite shopping English.

Rewrite each sentence to sound friendly and polite.

  • "I want a coffee."
  • "Give me a bag."
  • "Where is the station?"
✅ Answer

"I'd like a coffee, please." · "Can I have a bag, please?" · "Excuse me, where's the station?"

🎯 Your Capstone Project: "A Day Out"

This is your final project — the biggest one in the course, and the most fun. You act out a whole little day. You use skills from many lessons together. Do it alone (talk to your phone, and play both people) or with a partner or class (take turns as the customer and the local).

The scene: You are a visitor in a new town. You meet someone, buy something, and find your way to the station.

  1. (2 min) Greet & introduce. Say hello, your name, and where you are from. "Hi! I'm ___. I'm from ___."
  2. (3 min) Buy something. Order a drink or a snack. Use "I'd like…" Ask the price with "How much is that altogether?" Pay and say thank you.
  3. (3 min) Ask directions. Ask the way to the station (or a bank, park, hotel). "Excuse me, where's the ___?" Then say the directions back to check you understood.
  4. (2 min) Give directions. Now change roles. You give someone directions to a place. Use go straight on / turn left / it's on your right.
  5. (1 min) Say goodbye. End warmly: "Thank you! Have a nice day! Goodbye!"
  6. (2 min) Listen & think. If you recorded it, play it one time. What sounded good? What was hard? Write it for your journal.

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Add "please" and "thank you" every time you shop. People expect it, and it is easy.
  • Say directions back to check: "So, second left, past the park?" It is polite, and it helps you remember.
  • Start every request to a stranger with "Excuse me,"

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "How much" vs "How many": How much asks the price; How many asks the number. For prices, always How much.
  • is it / are they: Match the verb to one thing (is it) or many things (are they).
  • "I want" without "please": It can sound rude. Prefer "I'd like…, please."
  • Silent letters: receipt = "ri-SEET" (no "p" sound).

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

I finished A1 — what's next?

The next step is A2, "Everyday English." It builds on everything here: the past tense (talking about yesterday), plans for the future, and longer, more natural conversations. You will find it on the course roadmap on the home page. You are ready for it.

How do I keep improving from here?

Three easy habits: (1) Review — read a lesson or two again each week. (2) Speak every day — even one minute out loud helps. (3) Use the ESL Hub and other resources to read, listen, and practice a little every day. Small and steady wins.

I still make mistakes. Am I really A1 now?

Yes! Mistakes are not the opposite of learning — they are learning. Even advanced speakers make them. Being A1 does not mean "perfect." It means you can use simple, everyday English. And you can. Keep going.
