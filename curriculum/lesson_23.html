📖 Lesson 8.2: The Past Simple — Regular & Irregular Verbs

Last lesson you learned was and were. Now you get the rest of the past: all the action verbs. You will learn to say worked, played, went, and saw. With the past simple, you can finally tell the story of your day — what you did yesterday, from morning to night.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Make the regular past by adding -ed (work → worked, play → played)
  • Use common irregular verbs (go → went, have → had, see → saw)
  • Make past negatives with didn't + the base verb
  • Ask past questions with Did you…? and give short answers

What Is the Past Simple?

The past simple is the tense for actions that started and finished in the past. When you say "I worked yesterday," you mean the work is over — it happened and it is done. This is how we tell stories about our day, our weekend, our life.

Great news: the past simple has one form for every subject. There is no extra -s for he/she/it, like in the present. I worked, you worked, she worked, they worked — all the same. That part is easy!

💡 Two kinds of verbs

In the past, verbs come in two groups. Regular verbs follow a simple rule: add -ed (work → worked). Irregular verbs have their own special forms you must learn (go → went). Most verbs are regular, so the rule helps you a lot. The irregular ones are common, so you'll learn them fast with practice. 👍

🧠 Mindset

The irregular verbs may look like a big list to memorize. Don't panic! You will not learn them all in one day, and you don't need to. Learn the most common ten first — the ones in this lesson. You already use these actions every day. Say them out loud in little sentences, and they will stick. Progress, not perfection. 🌱

Regular Verbs: Add -ED

Here is the easy rule for most verbs: take the base verb and add -ed. That's the past. It's the same for every subject.

base verb + -ed

Base verbPast simpleExample sentence
workworkedI worked on Monday.
playplayedWe played football.
watchwatchedShe watched a film.
cookcookedHe cooked dinner.
talktalkedThey talked for an hour.
helphelpedYou helped me a lot.

See how it's the same for everyone? I worked, you worked, he worked, we worked, they worked. No changes. Just add -ed and you have the past. 🎉

Spelling & Pronunciation

Most verbs just add -ed. But a few verbs change their spelling a tiny bit. Here are the three small rules.

RuleExamplePast form
Verb ends in -e → just add -dlive, likelived, liked
Verb ends in consonant + y → change y to i, add -edstudy, trystudied, tried
Short verb, one vowel + one consonant → double the last letterstop, planstopped, planned

💡 A light note on sound

The -ed ending has three possible sounds. You don't need to be perfect — just notice them:

  • /t/ — after a "hard" sound: worked, liked, watched (sounds like "workt").
  • /d/ — after a "soft" sound or vowel: played, lived, cleaned (sounds like "playd").
  • /ɪd/ — an extra syllable, only after t or d: wanted, needed, started (sounds like "want-id").

The spelling is always -ed. Only the sound changes. Listen to native speakers and copy them. It will come naturally. 🎧

✅ Pro Tip

Don't stop speaking to worry about the -ed sound. People will understand you either way. Say your sentence, and let your ear improve over time. Communication first, small details later. 💪

Irregular Verbs

Some very common verbs do not add -ed. They have a special past form you learn by heart. The good news: they are the same for every subject too. Here are the ten most useful ones. Read each row out loud with its example.

Base verbPast simpleExample sentence
gowentI went to the shop.
havehadWe had lunch at one.
seesawShe saw her friend.
eatateThey ate pizza.
getgotHe got a new phone.
comecameYou came home late.
makemadeI made a cake.
dodidWe did our homework.
saysaidShe said hello.
taketookHe took the bus.
⚠️ Watch out: Never add -ed to an irregular verb. Say "I went," never "I goed." Say "She saw it," never "She seed it." These special forms replace the whole verb.

💡 Learn them in little sentences

Don't just memorize "go — went." Say a real sentence: "Yesterday I went to work." Your brain remembers stories better than lists. Pick three verbs today and use each in a true sentence about your week. 📝

Negatives with Didn't

Here is a wonderful surprise: making a past negative is very easy, and it's the same for every verb — regular or irregular. Put didn't before the base verb. Didn't is the short form of did not.

subject + didn't + base verb

PositiveNegative
I worked yesterday.I didn't work yesterday.
She played tennis.She didn't play tennis.
We went out.We didn't go out.
They saw the film.They didn't see the film.
⚠️ Very important: After didn't, the verb goes back to its plain (base) form. Say "I didn't go," never "I didn't went." The word didn't already carries the past. So the main verb becomes plain again.

💡 Remember "don't" from Lesson 3.1?

This works exactly like the present! In the present, after don't, the verb is plain: "I don't work." In the past, after didn't, the verb is plain too: "I didn't work." The only change is don't → didn't to show the past. Same idea, new time. 🔁

Questions with Did You…?

Past questions are just as easy. Start with Did, then the subject, then the base verb. This is the same for all verbs.

Did + subject + base verb …?

QuestionYes answerNo answer
Did you go out?Yes, I did.No, I didn't.
Did she call you?Yes, she did.No, she didn't.
Did they eat lunch?Yes, they did.No, they didn't.

Again, the verb after Did is plain: "Did you go?", not "Did you went?" And the short answers are lovely and short: "Yes, I did." / "No, I didn't."

✅ Pro Tip: add a question word

Put a question word before did to ask for details: "What did you do?", "Where did you go?", "When did they come?" These open questions get people talking about the past. 💬

A Short Story & Dialogue

Here is a short story about a weekend. It uses regular and irregular verbs. Read it out loud, and find every past verb.

Last Saturday, Maria got up early. She had breakfast and went to the market. She bought some fruit and talked to her neighbour. In the afternoon, she saw a friend, and they watched a film. In the evening, she cooked dinner and called her mother. She didn't work — it was her day off. She was tired, but she was very happy.

Now two friends talk about the weekend. Read both parts out loud.

Omar: Hi, Sara! How was your weekend? What did you do?

Sara: It was great! On Saturday I went to the beach with my family.

Omar: Nice! Did you swim?

Sara: Yes, I did! We swam and we ate lunch there. Then we played games. What about you?

Omar: I stayed at home. I didn't go out. I watched two films and cooked a big dinner.

Sara: Did you like the films?

Omar: One was good, one wasn't. But I had a relaxing weekend.

Sara: That sounds nice too!

What did you do? I went to the beach. Did you swim? Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. I didn't go out. I watched a film. I had a relaxing weekend.

🏋️ Exercise 1: Write the regular past (-ed)

Objective: Add -ed (with correct spelling) to make the past.

Write the past-simple form of the verb in each gap.

  1. Yesterday I all day. (work)
  2. We football in the park. (play)
  3. She in Madrid in 2020. (live)
  4. He English last night. (study)
  5. The bus here. (stop)
  6. They a good film. (watch)
✅ Answer

1. worked · 2. played · 3. lived (just add -d) · 4. studied (y → i) · 5. stopped (double the p) · 6. watched

🏋️ Exercise 2: Write the irregular past

Objective: Use the special past form of each irregular verb.

Write the past-simple form of the verb in each gap.

  1. I to the shop this morning. (go)
  2. We lunch at one o'clock. (have)
  3. She her friend at the party. (see)
  4. They a big dinner. (eat)
  5. He the bus to work. (take)
  6. You home late. (come)
✅ Answer

1. went · 2. had · 3. saw · 4. ate · 5. took · 6. came

🏋️ Exercise 3: Make a negative or a question

Objective: Use didn't or Did — and remember the verb goes back to plain form.

Follow the instruction in brackets for each sentence.

  • I went to the party. (make it negative)
  • She watched TV. (make it negative)
  • You saw the film. (make it a question)
  • They played football. (make it a question)
✅ Answer

1. I didn't go to the party. · 2. She didn't watch TV. · 3. Did you see the film? · 4. Did they play football?
(Notice: after didn't and Did, the verb is plain — go, not went; see, not saw.)

🎯 Your Project: My Last Weekend

Now tell the story of your last weekend, out loud. Speak to a partner, a teacher, or your phone. Say 6 or more sentences in the past simple.

  1. (3 min) Think about last weekend. What did you do? Where did you go?
  2. (4 min) Write 6+ sentences. Use at least three regular verbs (-ed) and three irregular verbs (went, had, saw…).
  3. (3 min) Add at least one negative with didn't ("I didn't work").
  4. (2 min) Put your sentences in order, from Saturday morning to Sunday night.
  5. (3 min) Say it all out loud. Then ask a partner: "What did you do?" and listen.

Model answer: "Last weekend was busy. On Saturday I got up early and had breakfast. I went to the market and bought some fruit. In the afternoon I saw my friend, and we watched a film. On Sunday I cleaned the house and cooked a big dinner. I didn't work — it was my day off. I called my family in the evening. It was a good weekend."

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Add -ed for regular verbs — the same for every subject (I worked, she worked).
  • Learn the common irregular forms in real sentences (go → went, see → saw).
  • After didn't and Did, use the plain verb (didn't go, Did you go?).
  • Use short answers: "Yes, I did." / "No, I didn't."

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "I goed / I seed." ❌ — These are irregular. Say "I went," "I saw." ✅
  • "I didn't went." ❌ — After didn't, the verb is plain. Say "I didn't go." ✅
  • "Did you went?" ❌ — After Did, use the base verb. Say "Did you go?" ✅
  • "She worked-s / He goes yesterday." ❌ — No extra -s in the past. Say "She worked," "He went." ✅

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Regular past: base verb + -ed (work → worked). Same for every subject.
  • Spelling: live → lived, study → studied, stop → stopped.
  • Irregular verbs have special forms: go → went, have → had, see → saw, eat → ate, get → got, come → came, make → made, do → did, say → said, take → took.
  • Negative: didn't + base verb (I didn't go, not didn't went).
  • Questions: Did + subject + base verb (Did you go?) → "Yes, I did." / "No, I didn't."

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Do I really have to memorize all the irregular verbs?

Not all at once! Start with the common ten in this lesson. You'll meet more slowly, over time. Learning them inside real sentences ("I went home") makes them stick much faster than a long list.

Why is it "I didn't go" and not "I didn't went"?

The word didn't already shows the past. So the main verb doesn't need to show the past too — it goes back to its plain form. It's like don't in the present: "I don't work" → "I didn't work." The helper carries the tense.

How do I know if a verb is regular or irregular?

Most verbs are regular (just add -ed). The irregular ones are common, everyday verbs, and you learn them one by one. When you're not sure, you can check a verb list or dictionary. With practice, you'll just know them.
