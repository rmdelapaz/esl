🎨 Lesson 5.1: Plurals, Colors & Adjectives

Now you can make your English more colorful — really! In this lesson you learn how to talk about more than one thing (plurals), how to name colors, and how to add small describing words called adjectives. Soon you can say "two red cars" and "a nice big blue bag." Let's paint your sentences. 🖌️

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Make regular plurals — add -s or -es, and know the small spelling rules
  • Use common irregular plurals (man → men, child → children, foot → feet)
  • Name the main colors in English
  • Put adjectives before the noun in the right order (a nice big red bag)

⏱️ Estimated Time: ~50 minutes

🎯 Project: Describe 6 or more things around you, using plurals, colors, and adjectives (for example, "two small green plants").

🔤 New Words in This Lesson

Here are some useful words from this lesson. Tap 🔊 to hear a word.

  • plural — the form for two or more things (cars, boxes)
  • singular — the form for one thing (a car, a box)
  • adjective — a word that describes a thing (big, red, nice)
  • noun — a word for a thing, person, or place (bag, man, city)
  • color — red, blue, green, and so on
  • regular — follows the normal rule (cat → cats)
  • irregular — does not follow the rule (man → men)
  • describe — to say what something is like

One and More Than One

In English, a thing can be one or more than one. One thing is singular: a car, a dog, a box. Two or more things are plural: cars, dogs, boxes. Most of the time, we just add a little -s to make the plural. Easy!

Then we can make our sentences richer. We add colors (red, blue, green) and other describing words called adjectives (big, small, nice). With these, you don't just say "a car." You can say "a big red car." Your English starts to sound real. 🚗

🧠 Mindset

Do not try to remember every rule today. Plurals are mostly one thing: add -s. The small spelling rules and the funny words like men and children come with time. If you say "two childs," people still understand you! Speak first, and let the details grow slowly. Every learner makes these small mistakes. That is normal. 🌱

Regular Plurals

Most nouns are regular. That means they follow simple rules. Here are the four rules you need. Rule 1 covers almost everything.

most nouns: just add -s

RuleSingular → PluralExamples
1. Most nouns: add -scar → carsdogs, books, tables, chairs
2. After s, x, ch, sh: add -esbus → busesboxes, watches, dishes
3. Consonant + y: change to -iescity → citiesbabies, countries, parties
4. Some -f / -fe: change to -vesknife → knivesleaves, wives, lives

Rule 2 helps you say the word. After s, x, ch, and sh, we add -es so it is easy to hear the extra sound: bus → buses, box → boxes, watch → watches, dish → dishes.

💡 The "y" rule has a friend

Rule 3 is only for a consonant + y: city → cities, baby → babies. But when there is a vowel + y (a, e, i, o, u before y), you just add -s: day → days, boy → boys, key → keys. So look at the letter before the y. 🔎

⚠️ Important Note: Not every -f word changes to -ves. Common ones do: knife → knives, leaf → leaves, wife → wives. But many just add -s: roof → roofs. Learn the common ones, and don't worry about the rest yet.

Irregular Plurals

Some words are irregular. They do not follow the rules above. They change in a special way, or they do not change at all. There are not many, so you can learn them one by one. Say each one out loud a few times.

SingularPluralExample
manmenTwo men are at the door.
womanwomenThree women work here.
childchildrenThe children are happy.
footfeetMy feet are cold.
toothteethBrush your teeth.
personpeopleMany people are here.
mousemiceThere are two mice.
fishfishI have three fish. (no change!)

✅ Pro Tip

The word people is very common. It is the plural of person. So we say "one person" but "two people" (not "two persons"). And a few words never change: one fish, two fish; one sheep, two sheep. Small, but useful! 🐟

Colors

Colors make your English bright and clear. Here are the main colors. Read each one and think of a thing with that color.

  • red — like an apple 🍎
  • blue — like the sky
  • green — like grass
  • yellow — like the sun
  • black — like night
  • white — like snow
  • orange — like the fruit 🍊
  • brown — like wood
  • pink — like a flower
  • grey / gray — like a cloud

💡 grey or gray?

Both are correct! Grey is more British. Gray is more American. They mean the same color. Use the one for your country. 🌍

To ask about color, you say: "What color is it?" The answer is easy: "It's blue." or "It's black and white."

Adjectives Before the Noun

An adjective is a describing word. It tells us what a thing is like: big, small, new, old, nice, hot, cold. Colors are adjectives too. In English, there are two important rules for adjectives.

adjective + noun (a red car)

📖 Two simple rules

1. The adjective goes BEFORE the noun. We say "a red car," not "a car red."

2. The adjective never changes for plural. We say "two red cars," not "two reds cars." Only the noun gets the -s. The adjective stays the same.

SingularPlural
a big housetwo big houses
a red carthree red cars
a new phonesome new phones
an old bookmany old books
⚠️ Important Note: Never put an -s on the adjective. Say "two red cars" ✅, not "two reds cars" ❌. The adjective is always the same, for one thing or for many.

Word Order: Size & Color

Sometimes you want two or three adjectives together. English likes them in a certain order. Here is a simple, light version for beginners: opinion → size → color.

opinion + size + color + noun

OpinionSizeColorNoun
a nicebigredbag
a lovelysmallbluecar
alittleblackdog

So we say "a nice big red bag," not "a red big nice bag." Don't worry too much about this. Just remember: how you feel about it comes first, then how big it is, then its color.

✅ Pro Tip

Most of the time you only use one adjective: "a red car," "a big house." That is perfect! Use two adjectives only when you want to. And when you do, put the color last, next to the noun. 🎨

Dialogue & Phrases

Here are two friends in a shop. Maya wants to buy a bag. Read both parts out loud. Look for plurals, colors, and adjectives.

Maya: Look at these bags! They're nice.

Leo: Yes! There are red bags, blue bags, and black bags. Which one do you like?

Maya: I like the big red bag. But it's expensive.

Leo: What about this small green one? It's cheap.

Maya: Hmm. I don't like green. What colors are the other ones?

Leo: There are two brown bags and one grey bag over there.

Maya: Oh, the grey one is lovely! A nice small grey bag. Perfect.

Leo: Good choice! It's a beautiful bag.

Here are the best phrases from the conversation. Keep them ready for your project.

Which one do you like? I like the big red bag. What color is it? There are two brown bags. A nice small grey bag. It's expensive. / It's cheap.

Practice & Project

🏋️ Exercise 1: Make the plural

Objective: Change each singular noun into its plural form.

Write the plural of each word in the gap.

  1. one bus → three
  2. one box → two
  3. one city → many
  4. one knife → some
  5. one child → two
  6. one person → many
✅ Answer

1. buses (after -s, add -es) · 2. boxes (after -x, add -es) · 3. cities (consonant + y → -ies) · 4. knives (-fe → -ves) · 5. children (irregular) · 6. people (irregular)

🏋️ Exercise 2: Adjective + noun

Objective: Write the words in the correct order, and use the correct plural.

Put the words in the right order. Remember: adjective before the noun, and only the noun takes -s.

  1. (car / red) — one
  2. (cars / red) — two
  3. (bag / big / a) —
  4. (dog / black / small) — a
  5. (houses / white) — three
✅ Answer

1. red car · 2. red cars (noun takes -s, not the adjective) · 3. a big bag · 4. small black dog (size before color) · 5. white houses

🎯 Your Project: Describe Your Things

Now describe 6 or more things around you. Do it out loud — to a partner, a teacher, or your phone. Use plurals, colors, and adjectives.

  1. (3 min) Look around your room. Find things you can name — books, bags, chairs, cups.
  2. (3 min) Say how many there are. Use plurals: "two books, three cups."
  3. (3 min) Add a color to each thing: "two blue books, three white cups."
  4. (3 min) Add one more adjective to two of them (size or opinion): "a nice big red bag."
  5. (3 min) Say all your sentences out loud. Solo? Record it and listen once. With a partner? Take turns and ask "What color is it?"

Model answer: "In my room there are many things. There are three black books on the desk. There is a big blue bag on the chair. I have two white cups and one small yellow lamp. There is a nice green plant near the window. And there are two brown boxes under the bed. I like my red phone best!"

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ I described 6 or more things
  • ☐ I used at least three plurals
  • ☐ I used at least four colors
  • ☐ I put each adjective before the noun
  • ☐ I used one sentence with two adjectives (size + color)
  • ☐ I said it all out loud (recorded or to a partner)

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • For most plurals, just add -s. It works almost every time.
  • Put the adjective before the noun: a red car, a big house.
  • Keep the adjective the same for one thing or many: one red car, two red cars.
  • Learn the common irregular plurals as whole words: men, women, children, people, feet.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "two reds cars" ❌ — Never add -s to the adjective. Say two red cars. ✅
  • "a car red" ❌ — The adjective comes first. Say a red car. ✅
  • "two childs" / "three persons" ❌ — These are irregular. Say two children, three people. ✅
  • "boxs" / "citys" ❌ — After -x, add -es (boxes); consonant + y → -ies (cities). ✅

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Regular plurals: add -s; add -es after s/x/ch/sh; consonant + y → -ies; some -f/-fe → -ves.
  • Irregular plurals: man → men, woman → women, child → children, foot → feet, tooth → teeth, person → people, mouse → mice, fish → fish.
  • Colors are adjectives: red, blue, green, yellow, black, white, orange, brown, pink, grey/gray.
  • The adjective goes before the noun and never changes: two red cars, not two reds cars.
  • With two adjectives: opinion → size → color (a nice big red bag).

🎉 What You've Accomplished

Look how far you've come! You can now talk about more than one thing. You can name colors. You can add describing words in the right place. Your sentences are no longer just "a car." Now they are "two big red cars." That is real, natural English. Every thing you see, you can now describe. Be proud. 🎉

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Why is it "buses" and not "buss"?

After the letters s, x, ch, and sh, we add -es. This makes an extra sound so the word is easy to say: bus → buses, box → boxes, watch → watches. Try saying "buss" — it's hard! "Buses" is easy.

Do I always put the adjective before the noun?

Yes, when the adjective and noun are together: a red car, a big house. (Later you will learn "the car is red," with the adjective after the verb be. But that is a different pattern. For now: adjective before the noun.)

Is it "grey" or "gray"?

Both are correct! Grey is British, gray is American. Same color. Just pick one and be consistent.
