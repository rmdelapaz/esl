Game 5.1 — Describe & Guess

How to play (groups of 3–4 · 15 min)

1. Put the cards face down in a pile. 2. Player 1 takes a card and doesn't show it. 3. Player 1 gives 3 clues: a color, a size or opinion word, and one more clue. Don't say the word! 4. The others ask: "Is it a/an…?" The first correct guess wins the card. 5. Next player. The winner has the most cards.

Model: "It's yellow. It's long. It's a fruit." — "Is it a banana?" — "Yes, it is!" For plural cards (boots, socks, gloves, jeans) say "They're…" and ask "Are they…?"

Easier: the describer may read the clue line. Harder: cover the clue line with your thumb; use two adjectives ("It's a long yellow fruit.").

Info-gap 5.2 — Spot the Difference

How to run it (pairs · 15 min)

Give A the Room A card and B the Room B card. Pairs sit back to back or hold the cards up so the partner can't see. They ask "Where is the…? / Where are the…?" and compare. There are 6 differences. When they find one, both write it in the table. At the end, pairs put the cards side by side and check. Answers: answer key 5.2.

Info-gap 5.3 — What's in your bag?

How to run it (pairs · 15 min)

Give A the Bag A card and B the Bag B card. Don't show the card! A asks about the 8 things on the list: "Have you got a key?" B answers "Yes, I have. / No, I haven't." A ticks ✓ or crosses ✗. Swap. Then find 2 things you've both got. Finally, join another pair and report: "My partner's got a passport. She hasn't got a phone. We've both got a key."

Privacy: use the cards, not real bags. Learners may choose to show 2–3 things from their own bag afterwards as an extra.

Game 5.3 — Who is it?

How to play (pairs · 8 min)

One board per pair. A chooses one person secretly (writes the name on a small paper). B asks yes/no questions: "Has he/she got glasses?" "Has she got long hair?" "Is it a man?" A answers "Yes, he has. / No, she hasn't." B can guess once: "Is it Grace?" Count the questions. Swap. Fewest questions wins.

Easier: B may point to their own hair/eyes and the teacher models the question. Harder: A describes the person in 3 sentences and B finds them without asking.

Scripts to Read Aloud

5.1 · Listen and draw (C14, stage 6 · 5 min)

Learners need paper and colored pens (or they write the color word). Read each line twice, slowly. Then show your own drawing and check.

1. Draw three small apples. They're red.
2. Draw a big bag. It's blue.
3. Draw two long bananas. They're yellow.
4. Draw a small black dog.
5. Draw two big green trees.

Then a short dictation. Learners write; stretch learners write all five, literacy-support learners write the number + noun only:

two buses · three boxes · four children · five people · a nice big red bag
5.2 · Draw the room (C15, stage 7 · 5 min)

Learners draw simple shapes and labels. Say each line twice. Then draw it on the board and compare.

1. Draw a big table in the middle.
2. Draw a cup on the table.
3. Draw a cat under the table.
4. Draw a lamp next to the table.
5. Draw two books on the table, next to the cup.
6. Draw a clock above the table.
7. Draw a bag in front of the table.

Check questions: Where's the cat? (under the table) · Where are the books? (on the table, next to the cup) · Where's the clock? (above the table).

5.3 · Who is it? (C16, stage 6 · 4 min)

Learners look at the face board and write or point to the name.

1. She's got long curly gray hair. She's got blue eyes and glasses. Who is it?
2. He's got short curly blond hair and a beard. Who is it?
3. She's got short curly dark hair. She's got glasses. Who is it?
4. He's got short straight blond hair. He hasn't got glasses. He hasn't got a beard. Who is it?
5. He's got short gray hair and a moustache. Who is it?

Answers: 1 Grace · 2 Tom · 3 Mei · 4 Sam · 5 Ken.
