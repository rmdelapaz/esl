2.1 · Listening Scripts

A. The dialogue (workbook page). Read it twice at natural speed, with two voices. First time: learners' books are closed. Ask: "Where is Sofia from? Is Leo's friend Italian?" Second time: books open, learners read along.

A: Hi! I'm Leo. — B: Hello, Leo. I'm Sofia. Nice to meet you. — A: Nice to meet you too. Where are you from, Sofia? — B: I'm from Mexico. I'm Mexican. And you? — A: I'm from Italy. I'm Italian. — B: Oh, nice! Is your friend Italian too? — A: No, he isn't. He's from France. He's French.

B. Listen and circle ✅ or ❌. Learners write 1–6 and circle. Read each twice. (Answers: 1 ✅ 2 ❌ 3 ✅ 4 ❌ 5 ✅ 6 ❌)

1. She is from Japan. She is Japanese. · 2. I'm from Spain. I'm Spain. · 3. They are from Brazil. · 4. He are from China. · 5. Are you Korean? — Yes, I am. · 6. Are you Egyptian? — Yes, I'm.

C. Dictation. Read each sentence three times: normal, slow (in chunks), normal. Learners write. Check capitals!

1. Where are you from? · 2. I'm from Mexico. · 3. She is Italian. · 4. They aren't from the USA. · 5. Is he Brazilian?

2.2 · Listening Scripts

A. The guessing game. Two voices, with a real cup, keys, and an orange in your hands. Read it twice, then learners read it in pairs.

A: What's this? — B: It's a cup. — A: And what's that, over there? — B: That's a window. — A: Okay. What are these? — B: They're keys. — A: Is this an apple? — B: No, it isn't! It's an orange. — A: Ha! You're right. Thank you!

B. a or an? Listen and write. Say only the noun. Learners write a or an + the word. (Answers: an egg, a bag, an umbrella, a door, an orange, a watch, an apple, a notebook)

egg · bag · umbrella · door · orange · watch · apple · notebook

C. One or many? Learners hold up one finger (one thing) or open the whole hand (many). This trains the ear for the final -s and for this / these.

1. This is a pen. · 2. These are pens. · 3. Those are boxes. · 4. That is a box. · 5. These are keys. · 6. This is a key. · 7. Those are buses. · 8. That's a bus.

D. Dictation.

1. What's this? · 2. It's an apple. · 3. These are my keys. · 4. Those are windows. · 5. Is that a book?

2.3 · Listening Scripts

A. The family photo. Two voices; show any photo on your phone (it doesn't need to be your family). Ask first: "What's the father's name? How many brothers?"

A: Is that a photo of your family? — B: Yes! Look, here's a picture. — A: Oh, nice! Who's this? — B: This is my sister, Maria. Her son is next to her. — A: And this man? — B: That's my father. His name is Carlos. — A: Do you have any brothers? — B: Yes, I have one brother. His name is Luis. — A: What a lovely family!

B. Listen and draw. Learners draw boxes and write the names as they hear them. Read slowly, twice. (Answer: Mei + Tom → children Lily and Ben; Lily has a daughter, Rose; Grandma Kim lives with them.)

Hi, I'm Mei. This is my family. My husband's name is Tom. We have two children. Our daughter's name is Lily, and our son's name is Ben. Lily has a baby. Her name is Rose. My mother lives with us. Her name is Kim. The children call her Grandma Kim.

C. His or her? Learners hold up a card: HIS or HER. Read the first part, pause, and learners show the card; then say the answer. (Answers: his, her, his, her, their)

1. Tom has a sister. … his sister. · 2. Anna has a brother. … her brother. · 3. Carlos has a daughter. … his daughter. · 4. Maria has a son. … her son. · 5. Rosa and Carlos have a house. … their house.

D. Dictation.

1. This is my mother. · 2. Her name is Rosa. · 3. My brother has two children. · 4. Anna's husband is from Brazil. · 5. These are my parents.

Game · Module 2 Picture Bingo

How to play (10–15 min). Each learner gets a card (four versions: A–D) and 16 small markers (paper squares, beans, coins). You read a sentence from the call list and tick it. Learners cover the matching picture. First to cover a line (across, down, or diagonal) calls "Bingo!" To win, they read back each covered square as a sentence: "These are keys." "This is my aunt." Play again for a full card. Stretch: a fast learner becomes the caller.
