D · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural but slow speed, with pauses. Read each script twice: once for gist, once to check. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

3.1-A · Sam's day (Workbook 3.1, Ex 1: number the pictures)

Hello! My name is Sam. This is my day. I get up at six o'clock. First, I take a shower. Then I get dressed. I have breakfast: bread and coffee. At seven thirty, I go to work. I take the bus. I come home in the afternoon. In the evening, I cook dinner. Then I watch TV. I go to bed at ten. Good night!

3.1-B · Mia's day (Set B picture-sequencing cards)

Hi, I'm Mia. I wake up at five thirty, but I don't get up! I get up at six. I take a shower. After my shower, I brush my teeth and I get dressed. I have breakfast at home. I don't drink coffee. I go to work at seven. I have lunch at work, at twelve. I come home in the afternoon. In the evening, I cook dinner for my family. I don't watch TV. I read a book. I go to bed at ten.

3.2-A · Time dictation (Workbook 3.2, Ex 1b: draw the hands or write the time)

Number one: It's nine o'clock. · Number two: It's half past two. · Number three: It's seven fifteen. · Number four: It's quarter to six. · Number five: It's ten past eleven. · Number six: It's four forty. · Number seven: It's twenty to one. · Number eight: It's noon. Twelve o'clock in the day.

Answers: 9:00 · 2:30 · 7:15 · 5:45 · 11:10 · 4:40 · 12:40 · 12:00. Literacy-support learners may write digits.

3.2-B · The -s sound: listen and show (1 finger = /s/, 2 = /z/, 3 = /ɪz/)

works · plays · watches · gets · reads · finishes · cooks · goes · fixes · sleeps · comes · relaxes · starts · lives · washes

Answers: works 1 · plays 2 · watches 3 · gets 1 · reads 2 · finishes 3 · cooks 1 · goes 2 · fixes 3 · sleeps 1 · comes 2 · relaxes 3 · starts 1 · lives 2 · washes 3. Make /ɪz/ very clear; accept a small /s/ vs /z/ blur.

3.2-C · Omar's day (optional: C8 end or C9 warmer; learners say True or False)

This is my brother, Omar. He is a cook. He works in a restaurant. He doesn't get up early. He gets up at ten o'clock. He has lunch at eleven thirty, and he starts work at noon. He works in the afternoon and in the evening. He finishes work at eleven at night! He doesn't work on Monday. On Monday, he plays soccer and watches TV.

Say: 1 Omar is a cook. (True) · 2 He gets up early. (False: at ten) · 3 He starts work at noon. (True) · 4 He finishes work at eleven at night. (True) · 5 He works on Monday. (False) · 6 On Monday he plays soccer. (True). Stretch: learners correct the false ones with doesn't.

3.3-A · Listen and draw (plain paper; pause after each sentence)

Draw a big square. This is a bedroom. There is a door on the left. There is a window in the top wall. There is a bed under the window. There is a small table next to the bed. There is a lamp on the table. There is a rug in front of the bed. On the right, there are two chairs. There is a desk between the chairs. And… there is a cat under the desk!

3.3-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. There is a sofa in the living room. · 2. There are two chairs in the kitchen. · 3. Is there a garden? · 4. The lamp is next to the bed.

G · Home Cards — Describe and Draw (3.3)

For the "draw your home" task. Most learners draw their own home: any kind of home (one room, a shared room, a shelter room, an apartment, a house, a dream home). A learner who prefers not to can take a Home card instead: they describe the card, and the partner draws it. Put the cards on a side table so anyone can take one without asking.

Home card A

A studio apartment

There is one big room and a small bathroom. There's a bed under the window. There's a table with two chairs. There's a fridge next to the stove. There's a TV in front of the bed. In the bathroom there's a shower and a sink. There's a mirror above the sink. There isn't a sofa.

Home card B

A shared room

This is my room. I share it with my sister. There are two beds. There is a wardrobe between the beds. There's a desk in front of the window, and there's a lamp on the desk. There's a shelf above my bed, with some books. There isn't a TV. The kitchen and the bathroom are down the hall.

Home card C

A dream house

There's a living room, a kitchen, two bedrooms and a bathroom. There's a garden behind the house, and there's a garage next to the house. In the living room there's a big sofa in front of the TV. There are two armchairs. There are some trees and flowers in the garden.

Listener card

You listen and draw

1. Listen. Draw on plain paper.

2. Ask questions:

  • Is there a…?
  • Are there any…?
  • Where is the…?
  • How many… are there?
  • Can you say it again, please?

3. At the end: show your drawing. Is it the same? 😊

4. Swap: now you talk, your partner draws.

H · Game — Find Someone Who… (C9 warmer)

Photocopy one per learner. Learners stand up, walk, and ask "Do you get up before six?" If the answer is "Yes, I do," write the name. One name per box; ask each person only one or two questions, then move on. At the end, report with -s: "Ana gets up before six." Stretch: ask the extra question too.

Find someone who…NameExtra question
1. gets up before sixWhat time…?
2. drinks tea in the morningHow many cups?
3. takes a bus to classWhat number bus?
4. works on SaturdayWhat time do you start?
5. cooks dinner every dayWhat do you cook?
6. watches TV at nightWhat time?
7. doesn't drink coffeeWhat do you drink?
8. reads in the eveningWhat do you read?
9. goes to bed after elevenWhat time?
10. studies English at homeWhen?
Teacher note

Before the mingle, drill the question change on the board: gets up → Do you get up…? · doesn't drink → Do you drink coffee? (a "No, I don't" answer gets the name!). This spirals C7 (Do you…?) and C8 (-s) together. Learners with a new identity card answer as their card person.
