Q1
A friend says, "I have a headache." What is good advice?
B) You should rest.
Why: should + base verb: no to, no -s, for every person.
Q2
Which sentence is about next week (a plan)?
B) Next week I'm going to start a new job.
Why: next week + going to = a future plan. A and C are habits (present simple).
Q3 · new
"My feet ___."
A) hurt
Why: feet is plural (two), so the verb has no -s: My feet hurt. One foot → My foot hurts.
Task 1 — Doctor's office: what success looks like

The patient (1) opens the call politely (I'd like to make an appointment), (2) accepts or changes a day/time using on + day / at + time, (3) spells their card name, (4) gives at least two problem sentences (I have a… / My … hurts / I feel…), and (5) shows they understood the advice (repeats it, or says OK, I should rest. Thank you). The doctor gives two should / shouldn't sentences. It doesn't need to be perfect: a learner who communicates the problem and the appointment with a few grammar slips has done the task.

Task 2 — My Week: model talk & success profile

Model (≈ 10 sentences): "Let me tell you about my week. I usually get up at six thirty. From Monday to Friday I work at a supermarket. In the evening I often cook dinner with my daughter. On Tuesdays and Thursdays I study English. I never work on Sundays. I relax at home! Next week is different. On Monday I'm going to see the doctor. On Wednesday I'm going to meet my friend for coffee. And on Saturday I'm going to buy a warm coat, because it's cold now!"

Success at A1: 8+ sentences (5–6 with literacy support) · at least 2 frequency adverbs · at least 2 correct going to plans · time words that make the time frame clear · answers one listener question. Typical good-but-imperfect talk: occasional missing -s or missing 'm in I'm going to; this is fine if meaning is clear.

Top errors to expect

You should to rest / should rests → should rest · My legs hurts → hurt · I have cold / I am cold (for illness) → I have a cold · It hurts me the head → My head hurts · I have pain in head → I have a headache · I going to visit → I'm going to · Next week I work… (for a plan) → I'm going to work · My son play football → plays · in Tuesday / on 10:30 → on Tuesday at 10:30.
