Marking at A1

Mark for meaning first. If the answer communicates and uses the target form, it's right, even with a spelling slip (shose for shoes). Accept British and American forms everywhere: bill / check, trousers / pants, jumper / sweater, eat in / for here, take away / to go, autumn / fall.

7.1 · Food & Drink — some & any

ExerciseAnswers
Ex 1 · C or U1 rice U · 2 apple C · 3 water U · 4 egg C · 5 bread U · 6 banana C · 7 milk U · 8 tomato C · 9 cheese U · 10 carrot C
Ex 2 · a / an / some1 an egg · 2 some milk · 3 a banana · 4 some rice · 5 an apple · 6 some bread · 7 an orange · 8 some water
Ex 3 · some / any1 some · 2 any · 3 any · 4 some · 5 any · 6 any · 7 some
Ex 4 · much / many1 many (eggs) · 2 much (rice) · 3 many (bananas) · 4 much (coffee) · 5 many (tomatoes) · 6 much (butter)
Ex 5 · word order1 Is there any milk? · 2 There's some bread. · 3 There aren't many apples. · 4 We have a lot of rice.
Fridge taskOpen. The 6 differences are on the A/B cards (see Classroom Materials). A good list uses some + noun: some milk, some eggs, some bread, some bananas, some coffee.

Alternatives: Ex 2, item 7: some oranges is fine if the learner wrote the plural. Ex 5, item 2: There is some bread. is correct. Item 4: We've got a lot of rice. is also correct.

Q1
Which sentence is correct?
B) There's some rice.
Why: rice is uncountable: no plural (rices) and no a. Use some in a positive sentence.
Q2
"How ___ eggs do we need?"
B) many
Why: eggs has a plural -s, so it's countable → how many.
Q3 · new
Which word can you NOT count?
C) rice
Why: you can say one egg, two bananas, but not one rice. Rice is one mass.
Top errors to expect

two breads / some rices → no plural · Are there any milk? → Is there any milk? · There aren't some eggs → any · How much apples? → How many · I want a water → some water / a bottle of water · There's much water → a lot of water in positives.

ExerciseAnswers

Ex 2 · polite words1 like · 2 have · 3 please · 4 thank · 5 much · 6 you
Ex 3 · match1 c · 2 e · 3 d · 4 a · 5 b
Ex 4 · prices (Script 7.2 B)1 $3.50 · 2 $2.75 · 3 $4.15 · 4 $12.20 · 5 $6.40. Say-it: "three fifty" · "four fifteen" · "four fifty" · "ten dollars".
Café role-playOpen. Success = one polite order (I'd like / Can I have … please), a reply to an offer, a price question, and Here you are.

Alternatives: Ex 1: eat in = for here. Ex 4: accept 3.5 for $3.50. Accept any clear way of writing money. Say-it: "three dollars and fifty cents" is also correct.

Q1
Which is the most polite way to order?
C) I'd like a coffee, please.
Why: I'd like (= I would like) + please is polite. I want is direct. Give me can sound rude.
Q2
"Would you like anything else?" You don't want more.
B) No, thank you. That's all.
Why: answer an offer with No, thank you. That's all tells the server you're finished.
Q3 · new
"Would you like some milk?" You want milk.
A) Yes, please.
Why: the short, polite yes to an offer. I'd like alone is incomplete. It needs a noun (I'd like some milk).
Top errors to expect

I like a coffee → I'd like (meaning changes!) · no please · How much it is? → How much is it / is that? · Give me… → Can I have…? · silence after Would you like…? · writing $4.15 for "four fifty" (stress: fifTEEN / FIFty).

7.3 · Clothes, Shopping & Weather

ExerciseAnswers
Ex 1 · this / these1 this (jacket) · 2 these (shoes) · 3 This (dress) · 4 These (jeans) · 5 this (hat) · 6 these (socks)
Ex 2 · weather1 ☀️ sunny · 2 🌧️ raining · 3 🥶 cold · 4 🌬️ windy · 5 🥵 hot · 6 ❄️ snowing
Ex 3 · match1 e (cold → warm coat) · 2 a (raining → umbrella) · 3 b (hot → T-shirt) · 4 c (snowing → boots) · 5 d (sunny → hat)
Ex 4 · order the dialogue1 Hello! Can I help you? · 2 Yes, please. I like this jacket. · 3 What size are you? · 4 I'm a medium. Can I try it on? · 5 Sure. Here you are. · 6 It's perfect! How much is it? · 7 It's $35.
As numbered in the workbook (top to bottom): 4, 1, 7, 2, 6, 3, 5
Ex 5 · about youOpen. Check I'm wearing + clothes (with a for single items: a shirt, none for pairs: jeans) and It's + weather word.
Store role-playOpen. Success = this/these once, a size, Can I try it on?, How much is it?, and a purchase within budget that fits the weather.

Alternatives: Ex 2, item 2: rainy is also correct. Item 6: snowy is also correct. Ex 3, item 5: sunny → T-shirt is logical too; accept it if the learner can say why. Ex 5, item 3: learners from hot countries may write hot or warm. That's a great answer.

Q1
Which sentence is correct?
B) I like these shoes.
Why: shoes are plural (a pair) → these, and these shoes are.
Q2
How do you ask about the weather?
B) What's the weather like?
Why: the fixed question. Here like means "how is it," not "love."
Q3 · new
"Can I try ___ on?"
A) it
Why: with it / them, the small word goes in the middle: try it on, try them on, never try on it.
Top errors to expect

this shoes / this jeans → these · a pant / a jean → (a pair of) pants / jeans · Can I try on it? → try it on · It's rain → It's raining / rainy · Is cold → It's cold · How is the weather like? → What's the weather like? · I wear a coat for right now → I'm wearing a coat.
