4.1 · Can / Can't — Abilities & Food

Exercise 1 — can / can't
  1. Sam can swim.
  2. Sam can't drive. (cannot is also correct)
  3. Sam can cook Italian food.
  4. Sam can't play the guitar.
  5. Sam can speak English and Spanish.
Exercise 2 — Make the question
  1. Can you cook?
  2. Can you ride a bike?
  3. Can you play soccer?
  4. Can you use a computer?

Answers about "you" are personal. Accept only Yes, I can. / No, I can't. (not Yes, I do / Yes, I cook). Yes, I can, a little. is a great stretch answer.

Exercise 3 — Word order
  1. I can swim.
  2. She can't drive.
  3. Can you dance?
  4. I don't like fish.
  5. Do you like tea?

Check capital letters and the full stop / question mark. Low-literacy learners may copy the words in order without punctuation, so praise the order first.

Exercise 4 — Circle
  1. Yes, I can.
  2. No, I don't.
  3. No, he can't.
  4. Yes, I do.
  5. No, thank you. (a plain "No." can sound rude)
Exercise 5 — Count it / don't count it
🔢 Count it💧 Don't count it
apple (an apple) · egg (an egg) · banana (a banana)water (some water) · rice (some rice) · milk (some milk)
Find someone who · Write about you

Personal answers. Check: can + plain verb; like / don't like / love + noun with no article (I like coffee, not I like the coffee). Model: I can cook, but I can't drive. I can sing a little. I love coffee. I don't like fish. When learners report the grid, Ana likes coffee / Omar doesn't like fish recycles the Module 3 he/she -s. Accept Omar don't like fish in the mingle, and correct it later.

Self-check Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) She cans swim.
B) She can to swim.
C) She can swim.
Why: can never takes -s, and the verb after it is plain, with no to.
Self-check Q2
"Do you like tea?" What is the best short answer for "no"?
A) No, I can't.
B) No, I don't.
C) No, I'm not.
Why: the short answer repeats the helper word from the question: Do you…? → No, I don't.
Self-check Q3 · new
"Can you swim?" Best "yes" answer?
A) Yes, I do.
B) Yes, I can.
C) Yes, I swim.
Why: Can you…? → Yes, I can. This is the mirror of Q2 and the most common mix-up in the mingle.
Top errors to expect — 4.1

She cans / he can swims (the Module 3 -s spreading) · I can to swim · Yes, I do after "Can you…?" and Yes, I can after "Do you like…?" · I like the coffee for general likes · a rice / breads · learners can't hear can vs can't, so check with gestures, not just the answer.

4.2 · Present Continuous — Happening Right Now

Exercise 1 — -ing forms
  1. running (double n)
  2. writing (drop e)
  3. playing (just add -ing)
  4. sitting (double t)
  5. cooking
  6. dancing (drop e)
  7. swimming (double m)
  8. sleeping (two vowels, so no doubling)
Exercise 2 — am / is / are
  1. I am cooking dinner. (I'm)
  2. She is watching TV.
  3. The children are doing their homework.
  4. We are waiting for the bus.
  5. Are you working?
  6. It is raining.
Exercise 3 — Sentences
  1. She's reading a book.
  2. They're playing in the park.
  3. I'm drinking water.
  4. He isn't sleeping. / He's not sleeping.
  5. Are you watching TV?

Full forms (She is reading a book.) are grammatically correct. Accept them, but point out that the exercise asked for short forms.

Exercise 4 — Every day or right now?
  1. drinks (every morning = habit)
  2. is sleeping (look! / now)
  3. work (every day = habit)
  4. are studying (at the moment)
  5. walk (usually = habit)
Exercise 5 — Pictures
  1. He's cooking. / He is cooking.
  2. She's singing.
  3. They're dancing.
  4. She's reading (a book).
  5. He's swimming.
  6. I'm drinking (juice / water / a drink). Accept any drink that matches the picture.

If the person in a picture doesn't match the given pronoun, accept the pronoun on the page. The target is be + -ing.

Phone call · Write about now

Personal answers. Every sentence needs am / is / are + -ing. The every-day sentence must be present simple: Every day I take the bus. / Usually I work, but right now I'm studying.

Self-check Q1
The -ing form of "sit"?
A) siting
B) sitting
C) siteing
Why: short word, one vowel + one consonant → double the last letter.
Self-check Q2
Which sentence describes something happening right now?
A) I eat breakfast every day.
B) She usually walks to school.
C) They are watching TV now.
Why: are + -ing and the time word now. A and B are habits (every day, usually).
Self-check Q3 · new
"What are you doing?"
A) I cook.
B) I'm cooking.
C) I cooking.
Why: the question is about now, so use am + -ing. C is missing am, the most common error in this lesson.
Top errors to expect — 4.2

I eating / she reading (no be) · he are working · runing, writeing, siting · present simple for now (What do you do now?) and continuous for habits (I am working here every day) · Yes, she's. as a short answer (must be Yes, she is.) · -ing pronounced -in.

4.3 · Review Project — Shopping & Directions

Exercise 1 — is it / are they
  1. How much is it? (one apple)
  2. How much are they? (shoes)
  3. How much is it? (one coffee)
  4. How much are they? (bags)
  5. How much is it? (one umbrella)
  6. How much are they? (bananas)
Exercise 2 — Prices (dictation, script in the classroom materials)
  1. coffee $2.50
  2. bag $15 (fifteen)
  3. shoes $50 (fifty)
  4. sandwich $6.50
  5. umbrella $13 (thirteen)
  6. altogether $9 (coffee + sandwich)

Accept 2.50, $2.50, or words. If learners write 50 for 15 or 30 for 13, replay with exaggerated stress: fifTEEN / FIFty.

Exercise 3 — Make it polite
  1. I'd like a coffee, please. (also: Can I have a coffee, please?)
  2. Can I have a bag, please? (also: I'd like a bag, please.)
  3. Excuse me, where's the station? (also: Excuse me, where is the station, please?)
  4. I'd like a tea, please. / Can I have a tea, please?

Write-your-own directions (examples): to the café: Go straight ahead. It's on your right. · to the station: Take the first right. It's on your left. · to the hotel: Take the first left. It's on your right. Check on your left/right and the imperative (no you).

Self-check Q1
You want to know the price of two apples. What do you ask?
A) How much is it?
B) How much are they?
C) How many are it?
Why: two apples = plural → are they. Prices always use How much.
Self-check Q2
Someone asks the way to the park. Which is a correct set of directions?
A) Go straight ahead and take the first left. It's on your right.
B) How much is the park, please?
C) Turn the straight and past to left park.
Why: imperatives (go, take) + on your right. B is a price question, and C is not English word order.
Self-check Q3 · new
"It's opposite the bank." It's…
A) next to the bank
B) on the other side of the street from the bank
C) inside the bank
Why: opposite = facing it, across the street. Next to = beside it, on the same side.
Halfway self-check · My Day Out · journal

No right answers. Collect the "Look how much I can do" grids (or photograph them) and compare them with your speaking checklist before C13. A learner who marks 😀 but got ○ on your checklist needs gentle evidence ("Let's try it together"). A learner who marks 😐 but got ✓ needs to hear that they did it.

Top errors to expect — 4.3

How much cost? / How much costs it? · How many is it? · How much is they? · Where station? / Where is station? · Turn to the left / in the right · thirteen ↔ thirty, fifteen ↔ fifty · I want… / Give me… with no please · re-KEIPT for receipt · confusing I'd like (a request) with I like (a feeling).
