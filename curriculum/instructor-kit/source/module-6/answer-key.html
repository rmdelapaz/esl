6.1 · How Often? — Adverbs of Frequency

Exercise 1 — write the sentence with the word
  1. I always get up at six.
  2. She is never late.
  3. We sometimes eat fish. (also accept: Sometimes we eat fish.)
  4. They are usually tired.
  5. He hardly ever drinks coffee.
  6. My bus is often on time.
Exercise 2 — circle the right word

1 always · 2 never · 3 hardly ever · 4 once · 5 twice · 6 usually

Exercise 3 — put the words in order
  1. I often cook at home.
  2. She is usually busy.
  3. How often do you swim?
  4. A nurse usually works at night.
  5. I go to the gym twice a week. (also accept: Twice a week, I go to the gym.)

Exercise 5 — job and place

1 d (hospital) · 2 a (restaurant) · 3 b (garage) · 4 c (school) · 5 e (shop). A doctor is also "in a hospital," and a waiter is "in a restaurant," so accept sensible extra answers.

Speaking & writing — what to accept

Survey report: "Five people always cook." / "Two people never cook." / "Most people sometimes cook." Accept "Nobody never…" only as a noted error: model "Nobody cooks…" or "Zero people…" instead. Write about you: any four true sentences with the adverb in the right place and -s for he/she.

Q1
Which sentence is correct?
B) I always drink coffee.
Why: with a normal verb, the adverb goes before the verb. A puts it after the verb, and C has no verb.
Q2
Which sentence is correct?
B) She is always late.
Why: with be (am/is/are), the adverb goes after it: "be after, verb before."
Q3
"two times a week" — another way to say it
B) twice a week
Why: once = one time, twice = two times. "Two times a week" is understood, but "twice" is more natural.
Top errors to expect (6.1)

"I drink always coffee." · "She always is late." · "I don't never smoke." · "She never eat meat." (missing -s) · "He is nurse." · "How often you cook?" (missing do) · "two times" for twice (not wrong, just less natural).

6.2 · Likes & Dislikes — like/love/hate + -ing

Exercise 1 — -ing form

1 playing (just add -ing; we never double y or w) · 2 writing (drop e) · 3 shopping (double p) · 4 painting · 5 making (drop e) · 6 running (double n) · 7 dancing (drop e) · 8 listening (just add -ing; listen is not a short one-syllable word, so no double)

Exercise 2 — object pronouns

1 it (the song) · 2 them (the shoes) · 3 him (my brother) · 4 her (my mother) · 5 us (we) · 6 me (I)

Exercise 3 — write the sentence
  1. I love dancing.
  2. She hates waiting.
  3. We don't mind cleaning.
  4. He doesn't like swimming. (accept "He don't like swimming" as a noted error; model doesn't)
  5. They enjoy reading. (also accept: They like reading.)
Exercise 4 — listening: Omar's family

1 Omar, cooking ♥♥ · 2 wife, reading ♥♥ · 3 wife, cooking ✗ · 4 son, football ♥ · 5 son, reading ☹️ · 6 daughter, dancing ♥♥ · 7 father, gardening 🙂 (enjoys) · 8 father, shopping 😐 (doesn't mind)

Exercise 5 — short answers

1 Yes, I do. I love it. · 2 No, I don't. I don't like them. · 3 Yes, they do.

Speaking & writing — what to accept

Find someone who: report with the third-person -s: "Ahmed loves cooking." "Mei hates shopping." "Ana doesn't mind cleaning." Write about you: "I like to cook" is also correct. Accept it, and note the -ing form as the target.

Q1
Which sentence is correct?
B) I love cooking.
Why: after love/like/hate we use verb + -ing. A has no -ing, and C keeps the e ("danceing").
Q2
"Do you like these books?" — "Yes, I love ___."
B) them
Why: "these books" = many things → them. One thing → it. Him is for a man.
Q3
Which sentence is correct?
C) She loves dancing.
Why: two endings work together: she → loves, and the activity → dancing.
Top errors to expect (6.2)

"I love cook." · "I like danceing." · "I enjoy swiming." · "I like they." · "She love reading." · "Do you like cooking? — Yes, I like." (needs Yes, I do. or Yes, I like it.) · "I don't mind" misunderstood as negative. Check it with a CCQ.

6.3 · Times & Dates — Making Plans

Exercise 1 — at, in, on

1 at (clock time) · 2 in (month) · 3 on (day) · 4 in (season) · 5 on (date) · 6 at (night) · 7 in (part of the day) · 8 on (American English) or at (British English, used in the online lesson). Both are correct.

Exercise 2 — going to

1 am (or I'm) · 2 are · 3 to · 4 are · 5 going

Exercise 3 — say the date

1 June first · 2 March second · 3 May third · 4 July fourth · 5 December twenty-fifth · 6 October thirty-first. Also accept "June the first" and "the first of June."

Exercise 4 — listening

A. Dates (month / day): 1 6/1 · 2 3/22 · 3 7/4 · 4 9/13 · 5 11/30 · 6 2/21. If a learner writes day/month (1/6, 22/3…), mark it right for listening and show the US order.

B. Phone invitation: day = Friday · plan = see a movie / go to the movies (a new film) · starts at 7:30 (seven thirty) · meet at 7:00 (seven o'clock) at the bus stop.

Exercise 5 — text message

Model: "Hi Ana! Are you free on Saturday? Would you like to go to the park? Let's meet at ten o'clock. I'm going to bring some sandwiches. See you then!" Check: on + day, at + time, base verb after would you like to and going to.

Diary information gap — solution

The week is Monday June 1st – Sunday June 7th. Every other slot is busy for A, for B, or for both, so there are exactly three shared free times:

  • Wednesday, June 3rd — in the evening
  • Saturday, June 6th — in the afternoon
  • Sunday, June 7th — in the afternoon

Plans will vary: "On Wednesday, June 3rd, in the evening, we're going to have dinner at a restaurant." Check on + day/date, in + the evening, going to + base verb.

Q1
Which sentence is correct?
C) I work on Monday.
Why: days and dates take on. In is for months and seasons, and at is for clock times.
Q2
"She ___ going to visit her family."
B) is
Why: she → is. (I am, you/we/they are.)
Q3
"My birthday is ___ July."
A) in
Why: July is a month, a long time → in. But "My birthday is on July 3rd": a date → on.
Top errors to expect (6.3)

"in Monday" · "in night" · "on July" · "I going to visit" · "I'm going to visiting" · "Let's to go" · "June one" for June first · /θ/ dropped in fourth, fifth · day/month order in written dates (3/6 meaning June 3rd).
