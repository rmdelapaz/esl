Module 6 — Habits, Free Time & Plans

Three classes that let learners talk about their real lives: how often they do things, what they love and hate doing, and when they are free to meet. Each class ends in a real exchange: a class survey with a bar chart, a "find someone who" mingle, and a diary information gap where partners find a time to meet.

Lessons 6.1–6.3 Classes C17–C19 Weeks 9–10 3 × 90 min CEFR A1

How to Use This Guide

  • One lesson block = one 90-minute class. The stage plan is the clock. The notes under it are the content: Meaning / Form / Pronunciation with concept-checking questions (CCQs), a drill script, instruction-checking questions (ICQs), differentiation, and L1 watch-outs.
  • Every class follows the course rhythm: lead-in → clarify (M/F/P + CCQs) → drill (choral → individual) → controlled practice (workbook) → freer speaking → delayed error correction.
  • Slides: slides/module-6-habits-plans.html. Print sheets (flashcards, survey, mingle, diary cards, listening scripts): materials/module-6/classroom-materials.html.
  • The online lessons are homework: lesson_16 (6.1), lesson_17 (6.2), lesson_18 (6.3). Learners can press 🔊 Listen at home to hear every word.
  • Dashed boxes = lines to say out loud. Teal = speaking tasks and read-aloud scripts, amber = watch-outs.
The through-line

How often? → Do you like it? → When are you free? Learners first describe their habits (6.1), then their feelings about activities (6.2), then use both to make a plan with someone (6.3). Recycle constantly: in 6.2 follow every "Do you like…?" with "How often do you…?"; in 6.3 invite people to do the hobbies they said they love. By the end of C19 a learner can have a real, short, friendly conversation: "Do you like swimming?" "Yes, I love it. I go twice a week." "Are you free on Saturday? Let's go together!"

Trauma-informed reminder

Habits, hobbies, and plans are personal. Some learners work nights, care for family, or have little free time or money. Never ask "why?" about a habit. Any learner may use a new identity card (in the materials sheet) and answer as that person, and "I don't know" or "Pass" is always a fine answer. In 6.3, birthdays can be real or invented. Some learners do not know their exact birth date, so never make it a test.

Before the Module

  • Print from classroom-materials.html: job + hobby + season flashcards (one set for you), the class survey sheet and bar-chart sheet (one each per learner, C17), the find-someone-who grid (one per learner, C18), and the A/B diary cards (one per pair, C19). Cut the new identity cards.
  • Draw a long arrow on a poster: always 100% → never 0%. It stays on the wall for all three classes.
  • Make a wall calendar for the current month, or print a blank one. It helps with dates in C19.
  • Bring colored pens or crayons for the bar chart (C17) and two sticky notes per learner (C18).

Lesson Plans

6.1 · C17

How Often? — Adverbs of Frequency

90 min
Objectives (can-do)
  • I can say how often I do things with always, usually, often, sometimes, hardly ever, never.
  • I can put the adverb in the right place: before a normal verb, after be.
  • I can ask and answer How often do you…? with once a week, twice a day, every morning.
  • I can say what people in some jobs usually do (a nurse usually works at night).
Materials
  • Workbook 6.1 (words, grammar box, Exercises 1–4, dialogue, speaking, I can…)
  • Flashcards: nurse, driver, teacher, chef, mechanic, waiter, doctor, cleaner, shop assistant
  • Class survey sheet + bar-chart sheet (materials); listening script "Lucy's week"
  • Slides 1–9 (answers on slide 8); the always → never wall poster
always 100% · usually 90% · often 70% · sometimes 40% · hardly ever 10% · never 0%
I always drink coffee.  → adverb before the verb
She is always late.  → adverb after be   "be after, verb before"
How often do you cook? — I cook once / twice / three times a week. · every morning · on Fridays
Stage plan
StageTimeInt.Procedure
Warmer / review C168S–S"Have got" review: in pairs, find three things you have both got (a phone, a brother, a bike…). Two pairs report: "We have both got…". Then elicit five daily-routine verbs from Module 3 (get up, work, cook, watch TV, go to bed) and write them on the board.
Lead-in: jobs & routines7T–SShow job flashcards and drill the names (stress: MEchanic, SHOP assistant). Ask: "Who works at night? Who works in a restaurant?" Model: "A nurse usually works at night." Learners guess one more.
Meaning: the scale10T–STell a true story about your week: "I drink coffee every day, 7 days: I always drink coffee. I go to the gym… 0 days: I never go to the gym." Build the scale on the poster. CCQs (below). Learners stand on an imaginary line across the room: "I sometimes cook" → they walk to the right place.
Form & pronunciation10T–SBoard: subject + adverb + verb, and subject + be + adverb. Chant "be after, verb before." Drill (script below): choral → half-class → individual. Mark the weak stress on "hardly ever" and the linking in often_I.
Controlled practice12S → S–SWorkbook Ex 1 (put the adverb in), Ex 2 (choose the word), Ex 3 (order the words). Learners check in pairs, then confirm on slide 8. Fast finishers: Ex 5 (job and place).
Listening8T–SRead "Lucy's week" (materials) twice. Learners tick how often Lucy does each thing. Check. Then pairs read the Ben & Lucy dialogue aloud (workbook), swap roles once.
How often…? + phrases8T–S, S–SElicit the question from the dialogue. Board: How often + do/does + subject + verb? Teach once, twice, three times a week / a day / a month, every morning, on Fridays. Drill 5 questions. Quick open pairs across the room.
Freer: class survey + bar chart17S–SICQs first (below). Each learner has one survey question (cut strips A–H, e.g. "How often do you cook?"). Mingle 9 min: ask 6–8 classmates, tally answers on the survey sheet. Chart 8 min: draw your bars on the bar-chart sheet (one bar per frequency word) and write two sentences: "Four people always cook. Two people never cook." (See the report frame below.)
Report & feedback5S–T3–4 learners hold up their chart and read one sentence. Delayed error correction: write 3 errors you heard on the board (e.g. "I drink always tea"), and the class fixes them together. Praise good sentences first.
Homework set-up5T–SShow lesson_16 on the screen; point to the 🔊 buttons and the "My Week" project. Workbook: finish "Write about you" + journal. Collect or photograph the bar charts for a wall display.
Total 90 = 8 + 7 + 10 + 10 + 12 + 8 + 8 + 17 + 5 + 5
Meaning — CCQs
"I always drink coffee." Do I drink coffee every day? (Yes.) Is there a day with no coffee? (No.)
"I hardly ever eat fast food." Do I eat fast food every week? (No.) Do I eat it sometimes, maybe one time a month? (Yes, maybe.) Is it zero? (No, almost zero.)
"I never smoke." How many times? (Zero.) Do we say "I don't never smoke"? (No. Never is already "no.")
"once a week." How many times? (One.) "twice a day." How many times? (Two.)
Form & pronunciation — drill script

Point to a picture on the slide, say the model twice, then gesture for the class, then two rows, then single learners. Keep a steady beat and stress the adverb.

I ALways drink coffee. (class) · She USually walks to work. (class) · They're OFten busy. (row 1) · He HARDly ever cooks. (row 2) · I'm NEVer late. (individuals)
Substitution: "I always drink coffee… sometimes!" → I sometimes drink coffee. "…she!" → She sometimes drinks coffee. "…is late!" → She is sometimes late.

Pronunciation: /ˈɔːlweɪz/ always · /ˈjuːʒuəli/ usually (3 syllables are fine: YOO-zhoo-lee) · /ˈɒfən/ often, the t is silent for most American speakers · twice /twaɪs/ ends in /s/, not /z/.

ICQs — before the survey
How many questions do you ask? (One, my question.) How many people do you ask? (Six to eight.) Do you write names? (No, just a tally mark: |.) Where do you draw the bars? (On the bar-chart sheet.) How long? (Nine minutes, then I ring the bell.)

Report frame on the board: "___ people always ___." · "___ people never ___." · "Most people ___." · "Nobody ___." (e.g. "Nobody always eats fast food"). Model one sentence from your own chart first.

Differentiation
  • Literacy support: give the survey question strip with a picture (cook, TV, bus, coffee…) and the six adverbs as columns with the % and dots (●●●●●● → ○○○○○○). Learners only tally and color bars. For Ex 1, provide the adverb on a card to physically place between two word cards.
  • Stretch: add a frequency phrase to every answer ("I usually cook, about five times a week"), write four report sentences, and ask one follow-up question ("What do you cook?").
L1 watch-outs
  • Adverb after the verb ("I drink always coffee"): common for French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, and Vietnamese speakers. Point to the board: verb before.
  • Double negative ("I don't never…"): Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, and Arabic allow or need two negatives. Keep one "no" word.
  • Dropping be ("She always late"): Arabic, Chinese, Russian, and Tagalog speakers. Ask: "Where is the verb?"
  • No article with jobs ("He is nurse"): Chinese, Russian, Arabic, Polish, and Japanese speakers. Also Spanish/Portuguese "is nurse" (no article in L1).
  • -s for he/she: "She never eat meat" is still frequent after Module 3. Recast quietly: "She never eats."
Homework

Online lesson 6.1: https://rays-esl.netlify.app/curriculum/lesson_16.html (listen to every word; do Exercises 1–2 and the Quick Quiz; record "My Week"). Workbook 6.1: "Write about you" and "My new words" + journal.

Check — self-check answers

Q1 correct = B) I always drink coffee. · Q2 = B) She is always late. · Q3 (new) "two times a week" = B) twice a week. Survey: any sentence with the adverb in the right place is a success.

6.2 · C18

Likes & Dislikes — like/love/hate + -ing

90 min
Objectives (can-do)
  • I can say how I feel about activities: love, like, enjoy, don't mind, don't like, hate + verb-ing.
  • I can spell the -ing form (cook → cooking, dance → dancing, swim → swimming).
  • I can use it and them (and me, him, her, us) after a verb.
  • I can ask Do you like…? and answer Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Materials
  • Workbook 6.2; flashcards: cooking, reading, dancing, hiking, swimming, singing, playing the guitar, playing football + word cards painting, gardening, gaming, shopping
  • Find-someone-who grid (one per learner); listening script "Omar's family"
  • Slides 10–15 (answers on slide 14); two sticky notes per learner
♥♥ I love cooking. · ♥ I like reading. · 🙂 I enjoy hiking. · 😐 I don't mind cleaning. · ☹️ I don't like gaming. · ✗ I hate waiting.
cook → cooking · dance → dancing · swim → swimming
Do you like swimming? — Yes, I do. I love it. / No, I don't. · Do you like dogs? — Yes, I like them.
She loves dancing.
Stage plan
StageTimeInt.Procedure
Warmer / review C178S–SPoint to the bar charts on the wall. Pairs ask each other three "How often do you…?" questions from the survey. Recast adverb-position errors. Two learners report a partner: "Ana usually cooks."
Lead-in5T–SShow three hobby pictures. Tell the class: "I love reading (♥♥). I don't like swimming (☹️). I hate waiting at the bus stop (✗)." Learners guess: "Do you like cooking?" You answer.
Vocabulary: hobbies10T–SFlashcards → drill (choral → individual). Mime game: a learner mimes, the class says "dancing!" Keep the -ing forms from the start. Add "hobby" and "free time."
Meaning: the feelings scale8T–SDraw the ♥♥ → ✗ scale beside the frequency poster. CCQs for don't mind and hate (below). Learners hold up a thumb (up / side / down) for "cleaning, cooking, shopping…"
Form: -ing spelling10T–S, SThree columns on the board: +ing / drop e / double. Learners sort 9 verb cards (cook, read, play, dance, make, write, swim, run, shop). Drill whole sentences: "I love cooking." Stress the feeling verb and the first syllable: I LOVE COOKing.
Object pronouns7T–SHold up one book: "Do you like this book?" → "Yes, I like it." Hold up two: "…them." Point to a male/female learner (with permission): "Do you know him / her?" Board the subject → object table. Quick drill: teacher says a noun, class says the pronoun (the film → it, the shoes → them, my sister → her).
Controlled practice12S → S–SWorkbook Ex 1 (-ing forms), Ex 2 (pronouns), Ex 3 (write the feeling with emoji cues). Pair-check, then slide answers.
Dialogue & listening5S–SRead "Omar's family" once; learners circle ♥♥/♥/✗ in Ex 4. Then pairs read Nina & Omar (workbook) and swap roles.
Freer: find someone who… (mingle)15S–SICQs first (below). Learners stand up and ask "Do you like…?" questions to find a person for each box, write the name, and ask one follow-up "How often do you…?" One name per classmate per box, and no more than two boxes per classmate.
Report & feedback5S–TLearners report in the third person: "Ahmed loves cooking. He cooks every day." Board 3 errors for the class to fix (I love cook / She love reading / I like they).
Homework set-up5T–Slesson_17 online (project "My Hobbies"); workbook "Write about you" + journal. Each learner writes one hobby they love on a sticky note and keeps it for C19 (they will invite a classmate to do it).
Total 90 = 8 + 5 + 10 + 8 + 10 + 7 + 12 + 5 + 15 + 5 + 5
Meaning — CCQs
"I don't mind washing the dishes." Do I love it? (No.) Is it a problem for me? (No.) Is it okay? (Yes. It's okay, not good, not bad.)
"I hate waiting." Is it a strong feeling? (Yes.) Is it stronger than "I don't like"? (Yes.)
"I enjoy cooking." Is this a good feeling or a bad feeling? (Good.)
"Do you like these books?" One book or many? (Many.) So: I like it, or I like them? (Them.)
Drill script
I love cooking. (class) · I don't mind cleaning. (class) · She loves dancing. (row 1) · He hates shopping. (row 2) · Do you like swimming? — Yes, I do. I love it. (open pairs)
Substitution: "I like reading… swim!" → I like swimming. "…she!" → She likes swimming. "…hate!" → She hates swimming. "…dance!" → She hates dancing.

Pronunciation: -ing is /ɪŋ/, with no hard /g/ at the end (not "cooking-guh"). don't mind links: don'_mind. loves ends in /z/, likes and hates end in /s/.

ICQs — before the mingle
Do you sit or stand? (Stand.) What do you ask? ("Do you like…?") If the answer is yes, what do you write? (The name.) Then what do you ask? ("How often do you…?") Can you write one name in every box? (No, one person, two boxes maximum.) Can you show your paper? (No, ask with words.)
Differentiation
  • Literacy support: use the picture version of the grid (flashcard images + ♥/✗ icons). Learners ask only "You like…?" with rising intonation, which is acceptable at this stage, and write the first letter of a name or have the partner write it. Reduce to 6 boxes.
  • Stretch: answer with a reason ("I don't like swimming. The water is too cold."), use really enjoy, and report with both: "Ana and I both love hiking."
L1 watch-outs
  • Base verb after like ("I like cook"): common for all L1s, especially Chinese, Vietnamese, and Arabic (no -ing form). Praise the meaning, then add the -ing.
  • "I like to cook": Spanish/Portuguese/French speakers use the infinitive. It is correct. Accept it, but model the -ing form.
  • Subject pronoun after the verb ("I like they / she"): Tagalog and Chinese speakers may also mix him/her (one word for both in L1). Point to the person and repeat.
  • Double consonant spelling (swiming, runing) and danceing: all learners. Keep the three-column sort on the wall.
  • Japanese/Korean speakers may mix l/r in love / rub, read / lead. Gentle minimal-pair clap: "reading or leading?"
Homework

Online lesson 6.2: https://rays-esl.netlify.app/curriculum/lesson_17.html (Exercises 1–2, Quick Quiz, project "My Hobbies"). Workbook 6.2: "Write about you" and the journal.

Check — self-check answers

Q1 = B) I love cooking. · Q2 "these books" = B) them · Q3 (new) = C) She loves dancing.

6.3 · C19

Times & Dates — Making Plans

90 min
This lesson is full, so prioritize

The online lesson has a lot: at/in/on, months, seasons, ordinals, going to, present continuous for arrangements, and inviting. In class, produce at/in/on, dates, going to, and invite/accept. Treat the present continuous for the future ("I'm meeting Sam on Friday") as recognition + chunks: learners use it in the diary task by reading from their card, but don't test it.

Objectives (can-do)
  • I can say the months, seasons, and dates (June 1st = "June first" / "the first of June").
  • I can use at (times, night), in (months, seasons, years, the morning), on (days, dates).
  • I can talk about my plans with going to: I'm going to visit my sister.
  • I can invite someone and accept or say no politely, and agree on a day and time.
Materials
  • Workbook 6.3; flashcards spring, summer, winter + word card fall (autumn); write the 12 months on the board
  • A/B diary cards (one pair set per two learners); listening scripts "Dates dictation" and "A phone invitation"
  • Wall calendar; learners' sticky notes from C18; slides 16–22 (answers on slide 20)
at 7 o'clock · at night · at noon  |  in the morning · in July · in winter · in 2026  |  on Monday · on June 1st · on my birthday
big → in · one day → on · a point → at   (US: on the weekend · UK: at the weekend)
1st first · 2nd second · 3rd third · 4th fourth · 5th fifth · 21st twenty-first   — we write June 1st, we say "June first"
I'm / She's / We're going to + verb: I'm going to visit my sister. · Would you like to…? — Sure, that sounds great! / Sorry, I'm busy.
Stage plan
StageTimeInt.Procedure
Warmer / review C187S–SLearners take out their sticky note ("I love hiking"). Mingle 3 min: "Do you like hiking?" "Yes, I do. I love it!" Find one person who likes your hobby, then sit together. That person is your partner for today.
Months, seasons, ordinal dates11T–S, S–SChant the months (clap the stress: JANuary, sepTEMber). Seasons with flashcards. Ordinals 1st–5th + 21st, 22nd, 23rd, 31st on the board: the endings -st, -nd, -rd, -th. Birthday line-up: learners stand in birthday order, January to December, only by asking "When is your birthday?" "It's on March the third." (New identity or any date is fine.)
at / in / on11T–SDraw three boxes: big (in), day (on), point (at). Learners sort 12 time cards (7 o'clock, Monday, July, the morning, night, my birthday, winter, 2026, June 1st, noon, the evening, the weekend). CCQs. Drill in short chunks: at SEV-en, on MON-day, in JU-ly, with weak at/on/in.
Listening: dates dictation6T–SRead the "Dates dictation" script. Learners write the dates as numbers (6/1, 3/22…). Check on the board. Write the American order: month/day.
Going to + arrangements12T–SShow your diary for next week: "On Saturday I'm going to visit my sister. On Monday at three I'm seeing the doctor." Board the form am/is/are + going to + verb. CCQs. Drill (script below). Learners say two true plans to a partner.
Controlled practice10S → S–SWorkbook Ex 1 (at/in/on), Ex 2 (going to), Ex 3 (write the dates). Pair-check.
Inviting & accepting7T–S, S–SRead "A phone invitation" (materials): learners note day, time, and activity. Elicit the invite/accept/no phrases. Drill with warm intonation (rising on "Would you like to come?"). Pairs read Maya & Leo.
Freer: diary information gap16S–SICQs first. A and B sit face to face and hide their cards. Each has a week (Monday June 1st – Sunday June 7th) with some plans. Ask "Are you free on Wednesday evening?" / "What are you doing on Monday morning?" Mark the partner's busy times with ✗. Find the three times you are both free, then plan three things together: "Let's meet on Saturday, June 6th, in the afternoon. Would you like to go to the park?" Write the plans at the bottom of the card.
Report & feedback5S–TPairs report one plan: "We're going to have dinner on Wednesday, June 3rd, in the evening." Board 3 errors: in Monday · I going to visit · Let's to go. Class corrects.
Homework set-up5T–Slesson_18 online (project "Make a Plan"); workbook "Write about you" + journal. Preview C20 (food & drink: some/any).
Total 90 = 7 + 11 + 11 + 6 + 12 + 10 + 7 + 16 + 5 + 5
Meaning — CCQs
"The party is on June 3rd." Is June 3rd a month, a day, or a time? (A day.) So: in, on, or at? (On.)
"I'm going to visit my sister on Saturday." Is it now or in the future? (Future.) Is it a plan, an idea I have already decided? (Yes.)
"I'm seeing the doctor at three." Is it happening now? (No, it's in the future.) Is the time fixed? (Yes, it's an appointment.)
"Sorry, I'm busy on Friday." Is the answer yes or no? (No.) Is it polite? (Yes.)
Drill script
June first. (class) · the first of June. (class) · on June twenty-first. (row 1) · in the morning · at seven o'clock · at night (fast round)
I'm going to visit my sister. (class) · She's going to start a new job. (row 1) · We're going to travel in summer. (row 2) · Are you going to come? (open pairs)
Would you like to have lunch on Saturday? — Sure, that sounds great! / Sorry, I'm busy. Maybe another day? (open pairs)

Pronunciation: th in ordinals (fourth /fɔːrθ/, fifth): show the tongue between the teeth. third vs thirty. In fast speech "going to" = "gonna": learners should understand it, but say and write going to.

ICQs — before the diary information gap
Can you show your card to your partner? (No.) What do you ask? ("Are you free on…?") When your partner is busy, what do you write? (✗.) How many free times do you need to find? (Three.) What do you do then? (Plan three things together and write them.)
Differentiation
  • Literacy support: diary cards have icons (☕ 🏥 ⚽) next to each plan. Learners may answer "Monday morning: busy / free" without the full sentence. For dates, give a strip with 1st–31st to point at. Workbook Ex 3: copy the dates, not write from memory.
  • Stretch: give the reason for being busy with the present continuous ("I'm meeting my aunt"), suggest a place and a time with at, and write a short text message invitation (workbook Ex 5).
L1 watch-outs
  • "in Monday" (Spanish/Portuguese en, Arabic fi, and others): one word covers in/on/at in many L1s. Use the three-box poster, not rules.
  • Dropping be in "I going to visit": Chinese, Arabic, Russian, and Vietnamese speakers. Point to the gap: "I ___ going to."
  • "going to visiting" or "Let's to go": keep the base verb after going to and Let's.
  • Dates: many learners write day/month (3/6 = 3 June). In the US 6/3 = June 3rd. Always say the month name when it matters (appointments!).
  • /θ/ in ordinals (fourth → "fort", "fours"): Spanish, Portuguese, Arabic (dialects), Vietnamese, Chinese, Japanese speakers. Vietnamese speakers may drop final consonants: "fif" for fifth.
Homework

Online lesson 6.3: https://rays-esl.netlify.app/curriculum/lesson_18.html (Exercises 1–2, Quick Quiz, project "Make a Plan"). Workbook 6.3: "Write about you" and the journal. Optional: say your birthday two ways to someone at home.

Check — self-check answers

Q1 = C) I work on Monday. · Q2 = B) is · Q3 (new) "My birthday is ___ July." = A) in. Diary gap: the shared free times are Wednesday June 3rd (evening), Saturday June 6th (afternoon), Sunday June 7th (afternoon).
