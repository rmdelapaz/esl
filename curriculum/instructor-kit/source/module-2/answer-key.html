Module 2 — Answers & Notes

Every Module 2 workbook exercise and self-check answered, with acceptable alternatives, answers for the classroom information-gap cards, and the learner errors you will hear most.

Marking at A1

Accept full forms or contractions anywhere, unless the exercise asks for one (she is not / she isn't / she's not are all correct). Capital letters on countries and nationalities count: mark them, but gently. For personal answers (write about you, family tree), there is no single answer; check the grammar, not the facts. A learner using a new identity is doing the task correctly.

2.1 · The Verb "To Be": Countries & Nationalities

Exercise 1 · am / is / are
  1. am
  2. is
  3. are
  4. are
  5. is
  6. Are (capital A)
  7. is
  8. are

Items 7–8 are the new ones: a name = he/she → is; "You and Sofia" = you (plural) → are.

Exercise 2 · negatives
  1. She isn't from Japan. / She's not from Japan.
  2. They aren't American. / They're not American.
  3. I'm not from Spain. (only one contraction; never "I amn't")
  4. You aren't a student. / You're not a student.

Full forms (She is not…) are also correct.

Exercise 3 · match
1 Chinac Chinese
2 Spaine Spanish
3 Germanya German
4 the UKb British
5 Italyd Italian
6 Mexicof Mexican
Exercise 4 · word order
  1. Where are you from?
  2. She is from Japan. / She's from Japan.
  3. Is he Brazilian?
  4. I'm not from Italy.

Check the capital letter at the start, the capital on the country or nationality, and the ? on questions.

Exercise 5 · short answers

1 I am · 2 she isn't · 3 they are · 4 he isn't. Items 1 and 3 are the "no contraction in a positive short answer" rule. No, she's not would also be correct in item 2, but it isn't one of the options.

Speaking & writing tasks

World-map mingle grid / Write about you: answers vary. Model: I'm Ana. I'm from Mexico. I'm Mexican. / This is Omar. He's from Egypt. He's Egyptian. Check: am/is/are present; from + country; no from before a nationality; capitals. 2.1 information gap (materials): Kenji, Japan, Japanese · Amira, Egypt, Egyptian · Lucas, Brazil, Brazilian · Priya, India, Indian. A's card holds Kenji's and Lucas's details, B's card holds Amira's and Priya's.

Self-check Q1
Choose the correct sentence.
B) She is from Japan.
Why: she takes is (A is wrong), and after from we need the country, Japan, not the nationality (C is wrong).
Self-check Q2
"Are you Brazilian?" You say yes.
A) Yes, I am.
Why: never contract a positive short answer ("Yes, I'm" sounds unfinished), and I takes am, not are.
Self-check Q3 (new)
Which is correct?
C) I'm from Spain. I'm Spanish.
Why: from + country; I'm + nationality. A mixes them up; B drops the verb to be, the most common A1 error.
Top errors to expect · 2.1

*I from Brazil. (missing am) · *She are… / *They is… · *Yes, I'm. · *I'm from Spanish. / *I'm Spain. · *i'm mexican (lower case) · *I amn't · *Is he from Japan? — Yes, he's. · *Where you are from? (no inversion).

2.2 · This & That, a/an & Everyday Objects

Exercise 1 · a / an
  1. a book
  2. an apple
  3. an umbrella
  4. a chair
  5. an orange
  6. a phone
  7. an egg
  8. a key

The an words all begin with a vowel sound.

Exercise 2 · plurals
  1. pens
  2. boxes
  3. watches
  4. babies
  5. buses
  6. key
  7. city
  8. notebooks

-es after s / x / ch / sh; consonant + y → -ies; keys is only +s (vowel + y). *busses is a spelling slip: mark it, but treat it lightly.

Exercise 3 · this / that / these / those
  1. This (near, one)
  2. Those (far, many)
  3. These (near, many)
  4. That (far, one)
  5. This (near, one)

Capital letter at the start of the sentence.

Exercise 4 · look and write
  1. They're keys. / They are keys.
  2. It's an umbrella.
  3. No, it isn't. It's a cup. (also: No, it's not.)
  4. Yes, they are.

Item 3: "No, it isn't." alone is acceptable; the correction is a bonus. Item 4: *Yes, they're is wrong (positive short answer).

Exercise 5 · circle

1 These (keys = many) · 2 is (one window) · 3 an (orange starts with a vowel sound) · 4 boxes (those = many).

Speaking & writing tasks

"What's on the desk?" information gap (materials): A's card is missing a notebook, keys, an egg; B's card is missing an umbrella, a cup, pencils. Both complete desks show: a cup, keys, a notebook, an umbrella, an egg, pencils, a phone, an apple, a book. Object tour: answers vary. Check that this/that go with is + singular + a/an, and that these/those go with are + plural and no a.

Self-check Q1
Which sentence is correct?
B) It's an apple.
Why: apple starts with a vowel sound → an. C is wrong because a/an means one, so it can't go with a plural.
Self-check Q2
Some keys are next to you.
C) These are keys.
Why: near + many = these, with are. Those is for far away; This is keys mixes one and many.
Self-check Q3 (new)
Three buses are far away, on the street.
A) Those are buses.
Why: far + many = those + are; bus ends in s → buses. B mixes one and many; C uses near and a wrong plural.
Top errors to expect · 2.2

*It's pen. (no article) · *a apple · *a pens · *This are books. / *These is… · *boxs, watchs · *babys / *keies · this and these pronounced the same · "that" for something in the learner's hand · plural -s not pronounced (three pen).

2.3 · My Family & Possessives

Exercise 1 · possessive adjectives
  1. His (Tom is a man)
  2. her (Anna is a woman)
  3. our
  4. Their
  5. Its (belonging → no apostrophe)
  6. your (belonging; you're = you are)
Exercise 2 · 's
  1. my father's car
  2. Tom's phone
  3. Lina's bag
  4. my parents' house (plural owner → apostrophe after the s)

Item 4: my parent's house = one parent. Accept it only if the learner can explain that it means one parent.

Exercise 3 · family words
  1. grandmother (grandma)
  2. uncle
  3. cousin
  4. brother
  5. aunt
Exercise 4 · have / has
  1. have
  2. has
  3. have
  4. has (one name = he)

Accept I've got / She's got if a learner knows it.

Exercise 5 · the Rivera family (his / her)

1 his (Carlos is the owner) · 2 his (Luis) · 3 his (Leo) · 4 her (Rosa). Every item makes the learner look at the owner. Items 1–3 describe a woman with his, which is the point of the exercise.

Rivera family tree information gap (materials)

Full tree: Rosa + Carlos (grandparents) → children Maria and Luis. Maria + David → son Leo. Luis + Sam → daughter Nina. A's card is missing Carlos, David, Nina; B's card is missing Rosa, Luis, Leo. Useful questions: Who is Rosa's husband? (Carlos) · What's Maria's husband's name? (David) · Who is Luis's daughter? (Nina) · Who is Maria's brother? (Luis) · Who is Nina's cousin? (Leo).

Family-tree speaking task

Answers vary, and a real, famous, or invented family is equally correct. Check: at least 4 people; 5 sentences; my, his, and her each used once, matched to the owner; one have/has; This is my… / These are my…. Model: This is my mother. Her name is Rosa. This is my brother. His name is Luis. He has a daughter.

Self-check Q1
Anna has a brother.
B) her brother
Why: the owner (Anna) is a woman, so it's her. The word matches the owner, not the brother.
Self-check Q2
The house of two parents.
B) my parents' house
Why: the owner is plural and already ends in -s, so the apostrophe goes after the s. A = one parent. C mixes in you're (= you are).
Self-check Q3 (new)
Which sentence is correct?
C) She has two sons.
Why: he / she / it take has. B uses the possessive her as a subject; the subject is she.
Top errors to expect · 2.3

*Anna and his brother / *My mother, he is… (he/she mix-up) · *the mother of me · *mother my · *She have a brother. · *It's name is Rex. · *Your kind. (for You're kind) · *brother's Anna (owner second) · *my parent's house for two parents (writing only). Also: daughter pronounced with a hard gh.

Module 2 picture bingo (materials)

No written answers. Winners must say a correct sentence for each covered square, using this/these + a/an or a plural for objects, and This is my… for family pictures.
