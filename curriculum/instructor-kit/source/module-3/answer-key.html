Module 3 — Everyday Life: Answers

Answers to every Module 3 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (picture sequencing, clock information gap, -s sort), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 3.1–3.3Classes C7–C99 self-check items14 exercises
Marking principle

At A1, mark meaning first. A sentence with the right structure and a small spelling slip ("studys") is a success with a note, not a wrong answer. American English is the model; British forms (at the weekend, flat, lift, half seven meaning 7:30) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

Picture-sequencing cards (Script 3.1-B, Mia's day)

wake up → get up → take a shower → brush my teeth → get dressed → have breakfast → go to work → have lunch → come home → cook dinner → read → go to bed. Not used: drink coffee, watch TV, study, go to school. Accept brush my teeth and take a shower in either order if the group argues well. The script says "after my shower," so the script order is shower first.

Exercise 3 — Make it "No"
  1. They don't like tea.
  2. We don't get up early.
  3. You don't watch TV in the morning.
  4. I don't drink coffee at night.

Also correct: do not (full form). Wrong: "don't likes," "not like," "no like."

Exercise 4 — Questions + answers about you
  1. Do you study English? — Yes, I do. (Everyone in the class!)
  2. Do you cook dinner? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  3. Do you go to bed late? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  4. Do you work on Sunday? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't.

The answers are personal; check only the form. Wrong: "Yes, I study." / "No, I not." / "You study English?" (no Do).

Exercise 5 — Circle, then write

1 in the morning · 2 at night · 3 every day · 4 in the evening.

My day (open). Look for: subject + base verb, a time word in each sentence, no -s with "I." Model: I get up at seven every day. I go to work in the morning. I come home in the evening. I go to bed at night.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) They don't works here.
B) They don't work here.
C) They not work here.
Why: after don't the verb is plain. A adds an -s; C has no helper word don't.
Q2
"Do you get up early?" — the "no" answer:
A) No, I get up.
B) No, I do.
C) No, I don't.
Why: short answers repeat the helper, not the verb: Yes, I do / No, I don't.
Q3 · new
I sleep ___ night.
A) in
B) at
C) on
Why: in the morning / afternoon / evening, but at night. Night is the odd one out.
Errors you will hear most (3.1)

"I no work on Sunday." · "You work here?" (no Do) · "I am work in a shop." · "I works" (the -s too early) · "Yes, I work." · "I sleep in the night" (understandable; teach at night) · "I go to home" (teach come home / go home, no to).

3.2 · Telling the Time, Days & He/She (-s) (C8)

New words — missing days

Monday · Tuesday · Wednesday · Thursday · Friday · Saturday · Sunday. A lower-case day ("tuesday") is a spelling note, not a wrong answer at first. Point to the capital.

Exercise 1b — Listen and draw (Script 3.2-A)

1 9:00 · 2 2:30 · 3 7:15 · 4 5:45 · 5 11:10 · 6 4:40 · 7 12:40 · 8 12:00 (noon). Common slip: 4 drawn as 6:45 ("quarter to six" = 5:45) and 7 drawn as 1:20. Go back to the "past goes down, to goes up" board clock.

Exercise 2 — at, on or in?

1 at 9 o'clock (clock time) · 2 on Sunday (day) · 3 in the evening (part of the day) · 4 in July (month) · 5 on the weekend (American; at the weekend is British and also correct) · 6 at night.

Exercise 3 — He / She verb

1 gets up · 2 studies (y → ies) · 3 goes (-es after o) · 4 watches (-es after ch) · 5 plays (vowel + y: just -s) · 6 finishes (-es after sh) · 7 has (irregular) · 8 reads.

Watch for: "studys," "gos," "plaies," "haves," "get ups" (the -s goes on get, not on up).

Exercise 4 — The -s sound
/s//z//ɪz/
works · gets up · cooks · sleepsplays · reads · goes · comeswatches · finishes · fixes · relaxes

Rule of thumb: /s/ after p, t, k, f (no buzz) · /z/ after voiced sounds and vowels · /ɪz/ after s, z, sh, ch, x (an extra syllable). The /ɪz/ column matters most for being understood; don't over-correct /s/ vs /z/.

Exercise 5 — doesn't and Does…?
  1. He doesn't get up early.
  2. She doesn't have lunch at noon. (not "doesn't has")
  3. Does he watch TV? (not "Does he watches")
  4. Does she work on Saturday?

Speaking — My partner's day (open)

Check: he/she + verb-s in all 3 sentences, a time with at, a day with on. Model: Ana gets up at six. She starts work at eight. On Saturday she goes to the market.

Self-check
Q1
3:45 — the classic way is…
A) quarter past three
B) quarter to four
C) quarter to three
Why: 3:45 is 15 minutes before four, so we count "to" the next hour. C is 2:45.
Q2
Which sentence is correct?
A) She doesn't works on Sunday.
B) She don't have a car.
C) She doesn't work on Sunday.
Why: with she, the helper is doesn't (not don't), and the -s lives on doesn't, so the verb is plain.
Q3 · new
He ___ TV in the evening.
A) watchs
B) watches
C) watch
Why: after ch we add -es, and we say it with an extra beat: /ˈwɒtʃɪz/. C has no -s at all.
Errors you will hear most (3.2)

"He work at night." (the #1 error) · "Does he works?" / "He doesn't works." · "She don't…" · "She haves lunch." · "in Monday," "on the morning," "at the morning" · "half seven" for 6:30 (L1 transfer) · "twenty past four" for 3:40 (past/to reversed) · he/she swapped · "Wensday" spelling, lower-case days.

3.3 · There is / There are — My Home (C9)

Exercise 1 — is or are?

1 is (a sofa) · 2 are (two bedrooms) · 3 is (a mirror) · 4 are (three windows) · 5 is (water: uncountable) · 6 are (four chairs)

Exercise 2 — some or any?

1 some (positive) · 2 any (negative) · 3 any (question) · 4 some (positive) · 5 any (negative)

Note for stretch learners: some is also possible in offers and requests ("Would you like some tea?"). That is not tested here.

Exercise 3 — The living-room picture

1 on · 2 under · 3 between · 4 above · 5 in front of · 6 next to

Exercise 4 — Omar's apartment
  1. Yes, there are.
  2. Yes, there is.
  3. No, there isn't.
  4. No, there aren't.

Also correct: "No, there is not." Wrong: "Yes, there's." (don't contract a short answer) · "Yes, it is." · "No, there aren't any." is fine but longer than needed.

Speaking — Draw a home and describe it (open)

Success = the partner's drawing roughly matches. For the 6 written sentences, check: is/are agree with the first noun; at least 3 prepositions; one negative. Model: This is my bedroom. There is a bed next to the window. There is a small desk, and there is a lamp on the desk. There are two shelves above the desk. There is a wardrobe behind the door. There isn't a TV.

Listen and draw (Script 3.3-A) — what the drawing should show

A square room · a door on the left wall · a window in the top (back) wall · a bed under the window · a small table next to the bed with a lamp on it · a rug in front of the bed · two chairs on the right, with a desk between them · a cat under the desk.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) There is two bedrooms.
B) There are two bedrooms.
C) There have two bedrooms.
Why: two = plural = are. English doesn't use have for "there is" (C is common for Portuguese, Chinese, Tagalog and Vietnamese speakers).
Q2
"Are there ___ chairs in the hall?"
A) some
B) any
C) a
Why: questions and negatives take any. C is wrong because chairs is plural.
Q3 · new
"Is there a balcony?" — the "no" answer:
A) No, there isn't.
B) No, there's not have.
C) No, it isn't.
Why: answer with the same words as the question: Is there…? → No, there isn't. C changes there to it.
Errors you will hear most (3.3)

"There is two chairs." · "There have a table." / "Have a table in the kitchen." · "In my room is bed." (no there, no article) · "There are any plants." · "Yes, there's." · "in front the house" · "The lamp is in the table" (in/on) · "dere is" (th). Correct the first three; let the th go during fluency work.
