1.1 · The English Alphabet & First Sounds

Exercise 1 · Write the small letter

B → b · D → d · E → e · G → g · H → h · J → j · M → m · Q → q · R → r

Copy-your-name line: check the capital first letter and left-to-right order. Watch for b/d and q/p reversals from learners new to Latin script; point, don't mark wrong.

Exercise 2 · Listen and write the letter (script 1.1 A)

1 G · 2 J · 3 E · 4 I · 5 H · 6 W · 7 Y · 8 R

Capital or lowercase are both fine. Expect G↔J, E↔I and W↔Y swaps: that's the point of the item set. Replay those pairs.

Exercise 3 · Circle the vowels

book · name · hello · pen · desk · window · teacher

Learners often skip the silent e in name or one letter of oo / ea. All vowel letters count, silent or not. w is a consonant here.

Exercise 4 · Listen and write the word (script 1.1 B)

1 pen · 2 book · 3 desk · 4 door · 5 window · 6 teacher

Top errors: bok (missed "double o"), dor, tiecher (E/I confusion). Literacy route: accept a correct picture-point for any word they can't yet write.

Exercise 5 · Write about you

Open answer (real or new-identity name). Check: capital first letter in first and last name; the spelled line uses letters separated by dashes or spaces (e.g., A – N – N – A, or A – double N – A: both correct).

Speaking · Spell yourself

Open. Success = four names written correctly. The best evidence is that learners used Can you repeat, please? or Slowly, please at least once.

Quick check 1
Which are all vowels?
A) A, B, C
B) A, E, I
C) E, R, T
Why: the five main vowels are A, E, I, O, U. B, C, R and T are consonants.
Quick check 2
How do you spell "Bill"?
A) capital B – i – double l
B) capital B – double i – l
C) capital P – i – l – l
Why: a name starts with a capital; two L's together = "double l." (B has two i's; C starts with P, a common Arabic-speaker slip.)
Quick check 3 · new
The teacher speaks very fast. You say…
A) Slowly, please.
B) Goodbye.
C) Double L.
Why: classroom language for asking for help. "Can you repeat, please?" is also a good answer if a learner writes it.
Top errors to expect in 1.1

E said "ee"/I said "ee" (Spanish, Portuguese, Tagalog) · G and J swapped (French, Spanish) · P said as B (Arabic) · F/L/M/N/S/X losing the final sound (Vietnamese, Chinese) · spelling a double letter twice ("n – n") instead of "double n" (fine, both are understood) · forgetting "capital."

1.2 · Greetings & Introductions

Exercise 1 · Which greeting?
#AnswerAlso accept
1Good morningHello (less ideal at work, but not wrong)
2HiHello · Good afternoon
3Good eveningHello / Hi. Not Good night.
4Good nightGoodbye · Bye · See you tomorrow
5HelloHi
Exercise 2 · Hello or goodbye?

1 Good night = goodbye · 2 Good morning = hello · 3 See you later = goodbye · 4 Good evening = hello · 5 Hi = hello · 6 Bye = goodbye

Exercise 3 · Complete the conversation

1 What's · 2 I'm · 3 name · 4 too · 5 And · 6 later

Also accept "My name is Ana" in gap 2 if written in full (it doesn't fit the word bank, but the meaning is right) and "tomorrow" in gap 6.

Exercise 4 · Write the short form

1 I'm · 2 What's · 3 you're · 4 isn't · 5 What's your name?

Mark the apostrophe: Im, Whats, youre are wrong. your for you're is the classic error.

Exercise 5 · Meet 3 people

Open. Check the sequence: greeting → name → Nice to meet you (too) → How are you? / And you? → goodbye. "Formal or informal" depends on the meeting card: boss / doctor / Mr. or Ms. cards = formal; classmate / neighbor cards = informal.

Quick check 1
You arrive at a party at 8:00 p.m. You say…
A) Good night
B) Good evening
C) Good morning
Why: you are arriving, so it's a greeting. "Good night" is only for leaving or going to bed.
Quick check 2
Short form of "What is your name?"
A) What's your name?
B) Whats your name?
C) You're your name?
Why: the apostrophe replaces the missing i of is. B has no apostrophe; C uses you're (= you are).
Quick check 3 · new
A person says "Nice to meet you." You say…
A) Good night.
B) I'm fine.
C) Nice to meet you, too.
Why: you give the same phrase back and add too. "I'm fine" answers "How are you?".
Top errors to expect in 1.2

"Good night!" on arrival · dropped be: "My name Rosa," "What your name?" · "Nice to meet you" without too in the reply · title + first name ("Ms. Maria") · missing apostrophes (Im, Whats) · your/you're · long, literal answers to "How are you?".

1.3 · Numbers, Age & Personal Information

Exercise 1 · Listen and circle (script 1.3 A)

1 30 · 2 14 · 3 50 · 4 16 · 5 70 · 6 18 · 7 90

If most of the class misses an item, say the pair again with exaggerated stress, then ask the class the confirmation question: "Sixteen, one-six? Or sixty, six-zero?"

Exercise 2 · Write the word or the number

15 = fifteen · 50 = fifty · 13 = thirteen · 40 = forty · 67 = sixty-seven · 84 = eighty-four

nineteen = 19 · ninety = 90 · thirty-two = 32 · seventy-seven = 77

Accept sixty seven without the hyphen at this level, but point it out. Not acceptable: fourty, fivety, threety, eightty.

Exercise 3 · Make the number

twenty-one · forty-eight · ninety-six · sixty-three

Exercise 4 · Listen and write (script 1.3 B)

1 (702) 555-0148 · 2 (702) 555-0163 · 3 (213) 555-0199 · 4 mei.chen@mail.com · 5 carlos27@mail.com

Any grouping or punctuation is fine (7025550148, 702-555-0148) if the digits are right. Top errors: 01 heard as O1 letter (fine: same thing on a phone), 0199 written as 199 (lost "oh"), meichen without the dot, carlos 27 with a space.

Exercise 5 · Find the mistake
#CorrectAlso accept
1I'm 30.I'm 30 years old. · I am 30 (years old).
2She's 25.She is 25 (years old).
340 = forty—
4What's your phone number?What is your phone number?

Not acceptable: I'm 30 years (needs old, or nothing).

Your turn · the form

Open (real or new identity). Check: capitals on names; age written as a numeral or "Sorry, that's private" (both full marks); phone digits complete; email has one @ and a dot. In the pair check, the partner's copy should match exactly.

Quick check 1
You hear "four-TEEN." The number is…
A) 14
B) 40
C) 4
Why: -teen numbers are stressed at the end; -ty numbers (FORty) at the front.
Quick check 2
You are 30. You say…
A) I have 30 years.
B) I'm 30 years old.
C) It's 30 years.
Why: English uses be for age: I'm 30 / I'm 30 years old. "I have 30 years" is a translation from Spanish, Portuguese, French, Italian and others.
Quick check 3 · new
Phone number 707-0155. You can say 0 as…
A) "at"
B) "oh"
C) "dot"
Why: in phone numbers, 0 is very often "oh" ("zero" is also fine). "At" and "dot" are for email: @ and the "." sign.
Top errors to expect in 1.3

"I have 30 years" · "I 30" / "She 25" (no be) · 13/30 … 19/90 confusion, both hearing and saying · "fourty" · "tree" for three · phone numbers said as big numbers ("five hundred fifty-five") · final-consonant drop in eight, five, six (Vietnamese) · "@" read as "a" or "arroba" · "I'm 25 years."
