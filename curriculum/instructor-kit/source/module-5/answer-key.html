Module 5 — Answers

Every Module 5 workbook exercise and quick check answered, plus the answers for the classroom games (Spot the Difference, What's in your bag?, Who is it?), acceptable alternatives, and the learner errors to expect.

Marking at A1

Accept any answer that is correct and clear, even if it isn't the exact form printed here. Contractions and full forms are both right (she's got = she has got). For spelling, accept small slips in writing tasks if the grammar point is correct. Mark the grammar point of the exercise, not everything else.

5.1 · Plurals, Colors & Adjectives

Exercise 1 — Write the plural
1 books2 buses3 boxes4 watches5 cities
6 days (vowel + y → just -s)7 knives8 men9 children10 people

Expect: busses, boxs, citys, dayes/daies, knifes, mans, childs/childrens, persons. "Persons" exists in formal/legal English but is wrong here. Teach people.

Exercise 2 — What color is it?

apple — red · banana — yellow · sofa — blue · armchair — green · boots — brown (They're brown) · skirt — pink · fridge — white (accept white and gray) · remote — black (accept black and red / black and blue etc. for the buttons).

Exercise 3 — Circle the correct words

1 red cars · 2 blue car · 3 children · 4 big red · 5 people · 6 white houses

Exercise 4 — Put the words in order

1 a red car · 2 two white cups · 3 a small black dog · 4 three blue books · 5 a nice big red bag

Item 5: a big nice red bag is understandable but not natural. Show the order opinion → size → color. Don't mark it as a serious error at A1.

Exercise 5 — Write about you

Answers vary. Check: a number or a/an + (adjective) + color + noun, and a correct plural. Model: two black pens · a small green notebook · three white cups · a big blue bag.

Describe & Guess (speaking)

No fixed answers. Listen for: color before the noun, no -s on adjectives, It's (one) / They're (boots, shoes, socks, gloves, jeans). Correct questions to "Is it a/an…?" ("Is it an apple?", not "It is apple?").

Quick check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) I have two reds cars.
B) I have two red cars.
C) I have two cars red.
Why: the adjective goes before the noun and never takes -s. Only the noun is plural.
Q2
What is the plural of "child"?
A) childs
B) childes
C) children
Why: child is irregular. Learn it as a whole word: one child, two children.
Q3 · new
Which is correct?
A) a red big bag
B) a big red bag
C) a bag big red
Why: size comes before color, and both come before the noun (opinion → size → color → noun).

5.2 · Where Things Are — Prepositions of Place

Exercise 1 — Look and write

1 on · 2 between · 3 in · 4 under · 5 behind · 6 above (accept over) · 7 next to (accept near) · 8 in front of

Common confusions: 1 vs 6 (on = touching, above = higher, not touching); 5 vs 8 (behind = at the back, you see only a little of the ball); "in front" without of.

Exercise 2 — Circle the correct word

1 on · 2 in · 3 under · 4 between · 5 behind · 6 next to

Exercise 3 — is or are? It's or They're?

1 is — It's · 2 are — They're · 3 are — They're · 4 is — It's · 5 are — They're

Item 5: glasses is always plural (like jeans, trousers). Expect "Where is my glasses?" Also accept Where's for items 1 and 4.

Exercise 4 — Read and write

1 next to · 2 opposite · 3 under · 4 above · 5 on

Exercise 5 — at or in?

1 at home · 2 at work · 3 at school · 4 in the fridge · 5 in the kitchen

Item 3: in school is also used in American English with the meaning "a student," but at A1 teach at school for place. Personal sentences: check subject + is/are + preposition + the + place.

Quick check
Q1
The cup has a book on its left and a book on its right. Where is the cup?
A) The cup is behind the books.
B) The cup is between the two books.
C) The cup is under the books.
Why: between = in the middle of two things.
Q2
Which sentence is correct?
A) I am in home.
B) I am at home.
C) I am on home.
Why: at home, at work, at school are fixed phrases.
Q3 · new
Where ___ my keys?
A) is
B) are
C) am
Why: keys is plural (two or more), so Where are…? and the answer is They're…

5.3 · Have Got — People & Belongings

Exercise 1 — have got or has got?

1 have got (I've got) · 2 has got · 3 have got · 4 has got · 5 have got · 6 has got

Accept contractions ('ve got / 's got). Also accept plain have / has (American English), but point out the exercise asks for got.

Exercise 2 — haven't got or hasn't got?

1 haven't got · 2 hasn't got · 3 haven't got · 4 hasn't got · 5 haven't got

Exercise 3 — Match

1 → c (Yes, I have.) · 2 → d (Yes, she has.) · 3 → b (No, he hasn't.) · 4 → a (Yes, they have.)

Exercise 4 — Look and write

Omar: He's got short dark hair. He's got a beard and glasses. (either order; accept short straight or dark straight)

Lena: She's got long straight blond hair. She's got green eyes and glasses.

Ken: He's got short gray hair and a moustache. He hasn't got glasses.

Accept grey and mustache (American spelling). Expect "long hairs" and "a glasses" / "glass".

Exercise 5 — Write about a person

Answers vary; real or invented people are both fine. Check: has got with he/she; one hasn't got; hair word order (length → type/color → hair); is for tall/short. Model: This is my brother, Omar. He's tall. He's got short dark hair and brown eyes. He's got a beard and glasses. He's got a big blue backpack. He hasn't got a car.

Quick check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) She have got blue eyes.
B) She has got blue eyes.
C) She got blue eyes.
Why: he / she / it → has got. "She got" is the past of get (or very informal speech).
Q2
"Has he got a beard?" What is the correct short answer for "no"?
A) No, he haven't.
B) No, he hasn't.
C) No, he hasn't got.
Why: match has to he, and don't repeat got in short answers.
Q3 · new
In "He's got a beard," what does 's mean?
A) is
B) has
C) his
Why: when got comes after, 's = has. In "He's tall," 's = is.
