⏪ Lesson 8.1: Yesterday — was & were

Something exciting happens today: you talk about the past for the first time. Until now, you talked about today and tomorrow. Now you can talk about yesterday — where you were, how you felt, what your weekend was like. We start with the most useful verb of all: be in the past — was and were.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use the past of be — was and were — with the right subject
  • Make negatives with wasn't and weren't
  • Ask past questions ("Were you…?") and give short answers
  • Use past time expressions and words for feelings (happy, tired, nervous)

⏱️ Estimated Time: ~50 minutes

🎯 Project: Tell the story of your yesterday — where you were and how you felt — in 6 or more sentences using was and were.

Talking About the Past

Think about your week so far. Today you are here, learning English. But what about yesterday? Where were you? How did you feel? To answer, you need the past. This is a big, happy moment in your English — your first step into the past tense. 🎉

We begin with the verb be. You already know it in the present: I am, you are, he is. In the past, am and is become was, and are becomes were. That is the whole idea. Just two words: was and were.

💡 Why start with "be"?

The verb be is the most common verb in English, and it is the easiest one in the past. Other verbs in the past come in the next lesson. Today, you only need two little words. Learn these two well, and you can already talk about your whole yesterday. One step at a time. 👍

🧠 Mindset

The past can feel like a big mountain. It is not. You already know how to remember yesterday in your own language. The thinking is easy — you do it every day. Today you only add the English words for it. Do not wait to be perfect. Say "I was tired yesterday," and smile. You just talked about the past in English! 🌱

Was & Were

Here is the whole map. Some subjects use was. Other subjects use were. Read the table out loud two times.

subject + was / were

SubjectPast of beExample sentence
IwasI was at home yesterday.
He / She / ItwasShe was happy. It was cold.
YouwereYou were late this morning.
WewereWe were at the party.
TheywereThey were tired last night.

Do you see the simple pattern? I, he, she, it use was. You, we, they use were. That's it. Just remember: the "single people" (I, he, she, it) take was, and the "group" words (you, we, they) take were.

✅ A quick memory trick

Say it like a little song: "I was, he was, she was, it was… you were, we were, they were." Repeat it three times. After a day or two, the right word will just come out of your mouth. That is how the ear learns. 🎵

Feelings & Emotions

The past of be is perfect for talking about how you felt. We say "I was happy" or "She was tired." Here are the most useful feeling words. Read each one and think of a time you felt that way.

  • happy — you feel good 🙂
  • sad — you feel bad 😢
  • tired — you want to sleep 😴
  • excited — happy about something coming 🤩
  • nervous — worried before something 😰
  • bored — nothing to do 😐
  • scared — afraid 😨
  • angry — mad about something 😠
  • worried — thinking about a problem 😟
  • surprised — you did not expect it 😲

Now put a feeling word after was or were:

I was happy yesterday. She was tired last night. We were excited about the trip. They were bored at the meeting. He was nervous before the test. I was scared, but I was okay.

💡 A small but useful phrase: "How was…?"

To ask about the past, English loves the phrase "How was…?" — "How was your day?", "How was your weekend?", "How was the film?" A good answer uses was too: "It was great!" or "It was okay." Keep this phrase close — you will use it every day. 😊

Negatives: wasn't & weren't

To make a negative, add not after was or were. In everyday English, we almost always use the short forms: wasn't (= was not) and weren't (= were not).

PositiveNegative (short)
I was happy.I wasn't happy.
He was at work.He wasn't at work.
You were late.You weren't late.
They were at home.They weren't at home.
⚠️ Important: The negative follows the same rule as the positive. Use wasn't with I, he, she, it, and weren't with you, we, they. Say "They weren't tired," never "They wasn't tired."

💡 wasn't = was not · weren't = were not

Both forms are correct. "I was not there" and "I wasn't there" mean the same thing. When you speak with friends, the short forms wasn't and weren't sound natural and relaxed. 🙂

Questions & Short Answers

To ask a question, just change the order. Put was or were first, before the subject. No extra helper word is needed — be does the work itself.

Was / Were + subject …?

QuestionYes answerNo answer
Were you at home?Yes, I was.No, I wasn't.
Was he tired?Yes, he was.No, he wasn't.
Were they at the party?Yes, they were.No, they weren't.
Was it good?Yes, it was.No, it wasn't.

Look at the short answers. You don't repeat the whole sentence. Someone asks "Were you tired?" You just say "Yes, I was." or "No, I wasn't." Short and natural.

✅ Pro Tip: "Where were you?"

You can put a question word first, then was/were: "Where were you yesterday?", "How was your weekend?", "Why were you sad?" These open, friendly questions start real conversations about the past. 💬

Past Time Expressions

To show that we mean the past, we add a past time expression. These little phrases point back in time. They can go at the start or the end of a sentence.

Time expressionWhen?Example
yesterdaythe day before todayI was busy yesterday.
last nightthe night before todayWe were tired last night.
last weekthe week before this oneShe was in Rome last week.
last weekendthe weekend before this oneThey were at the beach last weekend.
two days agotwo days before todayHe was ill two days ago.
in 2019in that past yearI was a student in 2019.

💡 "ago" means "before now"

We use ago to count back from today: two days ago, a week ago, five years ago. The number goes first, then the time word, then ago. It always points to the past, so it's a perfect friend for was and were. ⏳

Dialogue & Phrases

Here are two friends, Ben and Lucy, talking on Monday morning. Read both parts out loud. Look for every was and were.

Ben: Hi, Lucy! Where were you yesterday? I called you.

Lucy: Oh, sorry! I was at my sister's house all day.

Ben: No problem. How was your weekend?

Lucy: It was great! We were at a birthday party on Saturday. I was so happy.

Ben: Nice! Was it a big party?

Lucy: Yes, it was. There were forty people! But I was very tired after. How about you?

Ben: My weekend wasn't exciting. I was at home. I was a little bored, to be honest.

Lucy: Were you sick?

Ben: No, I wasn't. I was just tired from work. Next weekend will be better!

Keep these phrases ready for your own conversations and your project.

Where were you yesterday? How was your weekend? It was great! I was at home. I was so happy. My weekend wasn't exciting. Was it a big party? Yes, it was. / No, it wasn't.

Practice & Project

🏋️ Exercise 1: was or were?

Objective: Choose the correct past form of be for each subject.

Write was or were in each gap.

  1. I at home yesterday.
  2. They at the party last night.
  3. She very happy on her birthday.
  4. We tired after the trip.
  5. It cold last week.
  6. You late this morning.
✅ Answer

1. was (I) · 2. were (they) · 3. was (she) · 4. were (we) · 5. was (it) · 6. were (you)

🏋️ Exercise 2: Make it negative

Objective: Write the short negative — wasn't or weren't.

Fill each gap with wasn't or weren't to make the sentence negative.

  1. I tired this morning.
  2. They at school yesterday.
  3. He angry with you.
  4. We ready for the test.
  5. The film good.
✅ Answer

1. wasn't · 2. weren't · 3. wasn't · 4. weren't · 5. wasn't
(Remember: wasn't for I/he/she/it, weren't for you/we/they.)

🏋️ Exercise 3: Write the question

Objective: Turn each statement into a was/were question.

Change each sentence into a question. Start with Was or Were.

  • You were at the party.
  • She was happy.
  • They were tired.
  • It was cold yesterday.
✅ Answer

1. Were you at the party? · 2. Was she happy? · 3. Were they tired? · 4. Was it cold yesterday?
Now answer each one about a real day: "Yes, I was." / "No, I wasn't."

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Use was with I, he, she, it. Use were with you, we, they.
  • For negatives, use the short forms wasn't and weren't when you speak.
  • For questions, put was/were first: "Were you happy?"
  • Add a past time expression to be clear: yesterday, last night, two days ago.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • "They was here." ❌ — They takes were. Say "They were here." ✅
  • "I were tired." ❌ — I takes was. Say "I was tired." ✅
  • "Was you at home?" ❌ — You takes were. Say "Were you at home?" ✅
  • "No, I didn't." (to a be question) ❌ — Answer with was/were: "No, I wasn't." ✅

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Why does "you" use "were" and not "was"?

It's just the rule for be. In the past, you always takes were — even for one person. Say "You were late." Think of the group words (you, we, they) as the were family.

Is "wasn't" more correct than "was not"?

Both are correct. Wasn't is the short form we use when speaking and in friendly writing. Was not is the full form, useful for careful writing or when you want to stress the "not."

How do I answer "Was it good?" — do I use "did"?

No. When the question uses was/were, the answer uses was/were too. Say "Yes, it was." or "No, it wasn't." We keep did/didn't for other verbs — you'll meet those in the next lesson!
