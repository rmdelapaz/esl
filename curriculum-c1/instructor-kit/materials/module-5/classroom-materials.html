B · Dangler Hunt — "The Intern's Draft" (5.1, Round 1)

Cut into strips; one set per team. Teams sort the strips into sound and dangling, then repair two danglers aloud (one by changing the main subject, one by giving the participle its own subject or a full clause). Answers: in the answer key.

1Having reviewed the budget, the proposal seemed too expensive.
2Built in 1968, the community center now needs a new roof.
3Walking through the new wing, the smell of fresh paint was overwhelming.
4The funding approved, work began the following week.
5Delayed by the storm, the manager rescheduled the delivery.
6Having said that, the second option is considerably cheaper.
7Being a public building, visitors must sign in at the front desk.
8After finishing the survey, the results were posted online.

B · Condense the Report — Report Cards (5.1, Round 2)

One card per team; neighboring teams get different reports. Every report is correct but over-connected (about 125 words). Target: 90 words or fewer · every fact kept · at least four participle or absolute constructions (one absolute) · one fixed phrase.

Report 1 · Riverside Library Wi-Fi upgrade

The library board met on May 3, and it reviewed the results of the Wi-Fi upgrade. The upgrade was completed in April, and it was paid for by a city grant. Because the old routers had been installed in 2012, they could not handle more than 40 users at a time. The new system, which was designed by a local firm, supports up to 200 users. When the board had examined the figures, it noted that complaints had fallen by 70 percent. Since the budget had not been fully spent, the board decided to extend the service to the garden terrace. If the weather allows, the terrace network will open in June. The board thanked the staff who had worked evenings during the installation.

Words: 124 · Target: 90 or fewer
Report 2 · Route 12 night-bus pilot

The city transit office ran a night-bus pilot on Route 12, and the pilot lasted for three months. The buses ran every 30 minutes, and they connected the hospital, the university and the industrial park. Because many riders work late shifts, the office expected strong demand. After the pilot had ended, the office surveyed 400 riders. The survey was carried out by students from the university, and it found that 82 percent of riders felt safer. Because the ridership figures had exceeded the target, the office has recommended a permanent service. If the council approves the funding, the service will start in September. The office thanked the drivers who had volunteered for the late shifts.

Words: 115 · Target: 90 or fewer · Careful: did the pilot run every 30 minutes, or the buses?
Report 3 · Hillside Clinic text-alert trial

The Hillside Clinic tested a text-alert system in its waiting room, and the test ran for six weeks. Patients checked in at the desk, and then they could leave the building. The system sent them a text when the doctor was ready. Because the waiting room had often been overcrowded, staff had received many complaints. When the trial had finished, the clinic analyzed the data. The analysis was done by an outside consultant, and it showed that the average time in the waiting room fell from 38 to 11 minutes. Since patients had responded positively, the clinic has decided to keep the system. If the software company agrees, the system will be extended to the pharmacy. The clinic thanked the patients who had taken part.

Words: 125 · Target: 90 or fewer

C · Formal Email Correction Challenge (5.2)

One copy per team. 12 preposition errors and 5 correct chunks that look suspicious. +1 for each correct fix · −1 for changing a correct chunk · "?" = 0 points. Line numbers help during the reveal.

From: Dana Whitfield, Facilities Coordinator, Riverside College
To: All evening students
Subject: Resurfacing of the North Parking Lot

Dear Evening Students,

(1) With regard of the resurfacing work announced last month, I am writing to confirm the new schedule. (2) In light with recent heavy rain, the work has been postponed until the week of March 10. We apologize for any inconvenience this may cause.

(3) During the work, all drivers must comply to the temporary signs and refrain to park in the marked areas. (4) Vehicles left in closed sections will be subject for towing at the owner's expense. (5) The work will result in a temporary reduction of available parking of about 120 spaces.

(6) We are aware about the difficulties this may create for evening students, many of whom are reliant on their cars. (7) As an alternative for the north lot, the college has arranged a free shuttle bus from the Oak Street garage every twenty minutes. (8) In the event of further delays, we will contact you by email.

(9) The Facilities Committee discussed about several options before reaching this decision. (10) It agreed to the shuttle proposal by virtue to its low cost, and the decision is consistent to the college's accessibility policy.

(11) If you have any questions, please do not hesitate to contact the Facilities Office. On behalf for the Facilities team, thank you for your patience.

Sincerely,
Dana Whitfield

C · Reply Cards (5.2, Round 2)

80–100 words · at least four dependent prepositions and two fixed formal phrases · formal but human. Then swap with another team for a preposition-only proofread.

Reply card A · The student representative

You are the elected representative of the Evening Students' Council. Reply to Dana Whitfield on behalf of evening students.

  • Thank her for the notice and the shuttle.
  • Ask for a shuttle every 10 minutes between 5:30 and 6:30 p.m. (the busiest time).
  • Ask for better lighting at the Oak Street garage, because some classes end at 9:30 p.m.
  • Ask whether March parking permits will be partly refunded.

Reply card B · The Facilities Office

You work in the Facilities Office. A student, Alex Rivera, complained that the notice arrived only two days before the work began. Reply formally and warmly.

  • Apologize for the short notice.
  • Explain that the change resulted from the heavy rain.
  • Explain that, under college policy, notices are normally sent seven days ahead.
  • Offer a 50% reduction in the March permit fee and remind them of the shuttle.

D · Research Abstracts (5.3)

Abstract 1 is also printed in the workbook. Give Abstract 2 to fast or strong teams. Both studies are invented for class use.

Abstract 1 · Short Walking Breaks and Afternoon Concentration in Office Workers: A Pilot Study

Prolonged sitting is widely associated with reduced alertness, yet evidence regarding brief interventions in the workplace remains limited. This pilot study examined whether three five-minute walking breaks per afternoon affect self-reported concentration and task accuracy among office staff. Sixty-two employees at two municipal offices were randomly assigned to a walking-break group or a control group for four weeks. Concentration was assessed through a daily questionnaire, and accuracy was measured using a standardized data-entry task. Participants in the walking-break group reported approximately 18% higher afternoon concentration than the control group, and their error rate declined modestly; however, the difference in accuracy did not reach statistical significance. The findings suggest that brief walking breaks may constitute a low-cost means of sustaining afternoon concentration, although the small sample and reliance on self-report limit the generalizability of the results. Further research involving larger and more diverse workplaces is required.

Abstract 2 · A Deposit-Based Reusable Cup Scheme in University Cafés: Effects on Disposable Cup Use and Waste

Single-use cups constitute a substantial proportion of campus waste, yet the effectiveness of deposit schemes remains uncertain. This study evaluated a twelve-week scheme in three university cafés in which customers paid a $1 refundable deposit for a reusable cup. Sales records and weekly waste audits were compared with the same period in the previous year. Disposable cup use declined by approximately 41% across the three sites. However, total café waste decreased by only 6%, a difference the authors attribute partly to an increase in food packaging during the same period. Staff interviews indicated that the scheme was straightforward to administer, although cup returns declined toward the end of the semester. The findings suggest that deposit schemes can substantially reduce disposable cup use, but their effect on overall waste appears modest. Longer-term studies are needed to determine whether participation is sustained.

D · "Explain It to a Friend" Cards (5.3, Step 2)

Card A · The explainer

You're having coffee with a friend who doesn't work in research. Explain the study in plain, friendly English: what they tested, what happened, and whether it "worked."

Rule: no academic words! Not substantial, demonstrate, significant, participants, constitute… If you slip, your friend will stop you.

Useful: "So basically, they wanted to see if… · It turns out… · but here's the thing… · they're not totally sure because…"
Card B · The friend

Listen and ask at least two questions, for example:

  • "So does it actually work?"
  • "How many people were in it?"
  • "Is that a lot, or not really?"
  • "Would you try it?"

If your partner uses an academic word, say "Sorry, in normal English?"

Swap roles halfway.

E · Game — The Editor's Path

How to play (groups of 3–4): Roll a die (or flip a coin: heads = 2, tails = 1) and move. Do the task on your square. The player on your left checks the answer (fold the answer strip under the board, or give one learner the answer key). Correct: stay. Wrong: go back to where you were. Bonus: if you land on a square someone else has already answered, you must give a different correct answer or go back 1. First to FINISH wins, or whoever is furthest along after 7 minutes.

F · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher only)

Script 5.1-A — The chair's announcement (Workbook 5.1, Ex 1) · read twice, formal "meeting" voice
Good evening, everyone, and thank you for coming. Having reviewed last year's feedback, the committee has made three changes to this year's volunteer fair. First, the fair will be held in the main hall, the gymnasium being unavailable until June. Second, volunteers wishing to run a stall are asked to register by Friday. Third, all materials printed for the fair will be recycled afterward. The schedule being unusually full, each presentation will be limited to five minutes. Weather permitting, we'll also set up tables on the lawn. All things considered, we expect a busy and productive day. Having said that, we still need people to help with setup, so please see me after the meeting. Thank you.
Script 5.1-B — Dictation · read each sentence twice, pausing at the comma
1. Having finished the report, she left the office without a word. · 2. Written in 1920, the novel still speaks to readers today. · 3. The meeting over, the staff went home. · 4. Items damaged in transit will be replaced free of charge.
Script 5.2-A — Voicemail from Human Resources (Workbook 5.2, Ex 1) · phone to your ear, read twice
Hello, this is Priya Nair from Human Resources. I'm calling on behalf of the training team. With regard to next week's workshop, I'd like to confirm a few details. In light of the feedback we received, we've moved the session to Room 204. All participants must comply with the new sign-in procedure, and we ask you to refrain from using laptops during the first hour. In the event of a fire alarm, please follow the staff to the east exit. The workshop consists of three short modules, and we've seen a sharp increase in demand, so please reply by Thursday to keep your place. Thanks so much. Bye.

1. Prices are subject to change without notice. · 2. The results are consistent with earlier research. · 3. She chairs the committee by virtue of her experience. · 4. There has been a sharp increase in demand for housing.
Script 5.3-A — A casual radio item (Workbook 5.3, Ex 1) · relaxed, chatty voice, read twice
Now, here's one for anyone stuck at a desk all afternoon. A new study looked at about sixty office workers, and they found out that if you get up and walk around for five minutes, three times in the afternoon, you feel way more focused. A lot more, actually: something like eighteen percent. Mistakes went down a bit too, but not enough to really prove anything. So it kind of shows that little breaks might help. The researchers say they want to do a bigger study next. So go on, stretch your legs!
Script 5.3-B — Dictation · watch the stress in each academic word
1. The researchers obtained substantial data and examined it in detail. · 2. The findings indicate a significant improvement across all groups. · 3. The evidence suggests, rather than proves, a link between the two. · 4. It would be advisable to implement these changes more widely.
