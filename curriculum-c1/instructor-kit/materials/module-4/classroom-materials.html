In 4.2 they give the Precise Talk on the place or trend and describe the invented colleague with a cline. In 4.3 the point of view works like a persona card.

C · The Register Swap — A/B Story Cards (4.1)

One story per pair. A gets the informal voice note (tell it from memory, like a coworker at the coffee machine; B notes every phrasal verb). The pair then writes a 70–90-word formal update, keeping at least one phrasal verb on purpose (K). B gets a formal memo from a later stage of the same story and turns it into a 30-second spoken update. The small gray model under each A card is for you: fold it back or cut it off before class.

Story 1 · A · Voice note (informal)

The community center leak

"Hey, it's Dev from facilities. So, we looked into the water in the main hall on Monday, and it turns out a pipe under the kitchen has worn out. We've had to put off the repairs till Thursday, because the plumber can't come any sooner. Can you let people know the hall's shut till then? We've moved Saturday's classes over to the library. Oh, and we'll write the whole thing up next week."

Model formal update: see Answer Key 4.1, "Speaking — the Register Swap."
Story 1 · B · Memo (formal)

The community center leak

"The repairs to the main hall were completed on Thursday. An inspection conducted on Friday revealed no further damage, and the hall will reopen on Monday. Users are requested to report any recurrence of dampness immediately. The committee wishes to acknowledge the assistance of the library staff."

→ Tell it in 30 seconds, as you would to a friend.

Story 2 · A · Voice note (informal)

The library app pilot

"Hi all, Mia here. Quick one on the library app. We tried it out with fifty users last month, and honestly it went down really well: people picked it up in minutes. A few folks ran into problems logging in, so we're sorting that out. We want to roll it out to everyone in May, as long as the budget doesn't fall through."

Model: "A pilot of the library app was conducted with fifty users last month and was very well received; most participants learned to use it within minutes. A small number encountered difficulties logging in, and these are currently being resolved. Subject to budget approval, the app will be made available to all members in May." (K option: carried out for conducted.)
Story 2 · B · Memo (formal)

The library app pilot

"Following the successful pilot, the board has approved the full launch of the library app. Implementation will commence on May 1. Staff are required to attend a one-hour training session prior to the launch. Members who encounter difficulties should contact the help desk."

→ Tell it in 30 seconds, as you would to a friend.

Story 3 · A · Voice note (informal)

The clinic's new booking system

"Hey, it's Tomás at the front desk. So the new booking system: we set it up over the weekend, but on Monday it kept crashing, so we had to switch back to the old one. IT is looking into it. Patients are putting up with it pretty well, but the phone lines are going crazy. Can someone help out on the phones tomorrow morning?"

Model: "The new booking system was installed over the weekend but repeatedly failed on Monday, and the previous system has been temporarily restored. The IT department is investigating the fault. Although patients have been very tolerant, call volumes remain exceptionally high, and additional staff are requested for telephone duty tomorrow morning." (K option: set up for installed.)
Story 3 · B · Memo (formal)

The clinic's new booking system

"The online booking system has been restored following a software update. Patients may now schedule appointments online. Staff are asked to discontinue the paper waiting list with immediate effect and to direct all inquiries to the reception supervisor."

→ Tell it in 30 seconds, as you would to a friend.

Story 4 · A · Voice note (informal)

The school bake sale

"Hi everyone, it's Grace from the PTA. The bake sale's been called off because of the weather, sorry! We're going to put it off to the 18th. The good news is that tons of parents have signed up to help, and the bakery on Main Street offered to give us bread for free. Can someone look after the cash box this time? Last time it nearly went missing."

Model: "Owing to adverse weather, the bake sale has been postponed until the 18th. We are pleased to report that a large number of parents have volunteered to assist, and the Main Street bakery has generously offered to donate bread. A volunteer is requested to take responsibility for the cash box." (K option: signed up.)
Story 4 · B · Memo (formal)

The school bake sale

"The Parent-Teacher Association is pleased to report that the rescheduled bake sale raised $1,240. The proceeds will be allocated to the purchase of library books. The Association wishes to thank the volunteers and local businesses who contributed."

→ Tell it in 30 seconds, as you would to a friend.

Story 5 · A · Voice note (informal)

The delivery schedule

"Hey team, Kwame here. Heads-up: from next week they're bringing in a new route plan, and the early shift's getting moved up to 6 a.m. I know, I know. They've worked out that it'll cut down on traffic delays. If anyone can't make it, talk to Jess, and she'll sort something out. Just don't not turn up!"

Model: "A new route plan will be introduced next week, and the early shift will consequently begin at 6 a.m. Management anticipates that the change will reduce delays caused by traffic. Employees who are unable to attend at the revised time should contact Jess Moreno to discuss alternative arrangements. Attendance without prior notice of absence is expected."
Story 5 · B · Memo (formal)

The delivery schedule

"Following a review of delivery performance, the revised schedule has reduced average delivery times by 14 percent. The company will therefore retain the 6 a.m. start. Employees who wish to request an alternative shift should submit a written application to Human Resources."

→ Tell it in 30 seconds, as you would to a friend.

Story 6 · A · Voice note (informal)

The food bank move

"Hi volunteers, it's Lena. So, we're moving out of the old community hall at the end of the month: the rent went up again, and we just can't keep up with it. We've come across a great space above the hardware store. We need people to help pack up on Saturday and set everything up on Sunday. Pizza's on me!"

Model: "The food bank will vacate the community hall at the end of the month, as the rent has increased beyond what the organization can sustain. Suitable premises have been identified above the hardware store on Grant Street. Volunteers are needed to assist with packing on Saturday and with setting up (K) the new site on Sunday. Refreshments will be provided."
Story 6 · B · Memo (formal)

The food bank move

"The food bank has relocated to 12 Grant Street (second floor). Opening hours remain unchanged. Clients with mobility requirements are advised that an elevator is available at the rear entrance. The committee extends its gratitude to the 40 volunteers who assisted with the relocation."

→ Tell it in 30 seconds, as you would to a friend.

Character card: Describe (a) a designer vase in a gift-shop ad, (b) a young tree after a dry summer, in a neutral garden report, (c) a stray cat found in the rain, in a text to a friend.

Envelope 3 · confidence

Character card: Nina, a new manager, speaks first in every meeting and rarely asks for input. How would her boss describe her in a reference? How might her team describe her after work?

Envelope 4 · fame

renowned · famous · well-known · notorious · infamous

Character card: Port Riley, an invented seaside town, is famous for its oysters and for its summer traffic jams. Write one sentence for a tourist brochure and one for a complaint to the town council.

Brochure: renowned for its oysters · complaint: notorious for its traffic.
Envelope 5 · curiosity

inquisitive · curious · interested · nosy

Character card: Mr. Brandt, an invented neighbor, reads every package label on the doorstep, but he also asks brilliant questions at the library talks. Describe both habits.

Envelope 6 · determination

Character card: Coach Ruiz won't change the training plan, even after three losses. Her supporters speak first, then her critics.

Supporters: determined / firm · critics: stubborn / pig-headed (very informal).

Bonus pairs (stretch or early finishers): childlike (+) / childish (−) · inexpensive · affordable (+) / cheap (±) / tacky (−) · youthful (+) / immature (−) · assertive (+) / pushy (−).

"Use it or lose it" — discussion cards (1 per group of 3 · 4 minutes)

Discussion · 1

Is it true of vocabulary?

  • Which English words did you know well a few years ago but rarely use now?
  • Are some words "safe" because you meet them every day?
  • What would a realistic weekly "use it" plan look like?
Discussion · 2

Are idioms more fragile?

  • Why might an idiom be easier to lose than a collocation?
  • Is it better to own ten idioms perfectly or recognize fifty?
  • Which idiom from today would you actually say this week, and to whom?
Discussion · 3

Skills beyond language

  • Name a skill (a sport, an instrument, a recipe, a software tool) that faded when you stopped. Real or invented.
  • How long did it take to come back?
  • Does the same thing happen with a language?
Discussion · 4

Making chunks stick

  • Phrasebook, flashcards, or real conversations: which works best for you?
  • Is "use it or lose it" too pessimistic? What stays even without practice?
  • Choose three chunks and a real place to use each one.

F · The Advanced Communicator — Topics, Sides, Personas, Challenges, Tokens (4.3)

Put the topic cards on a side table at the end of C11 so learners can choose early. Speakers may argue either side; the side is not their personal opinion unless they want it to be. Each card has two starter reasons for each side so nobody begins from zero.

Topic · 1

Employers should give every employee one paid learning day a month.

For: skills keep pace with technology · staff stay longer. Against: lost working time · hard for small teams.

Topic · 2

Office jobs should move to a four-day week.

For: rested staff are more productive · easier recruiting. Against: customers expect five days · longer days are tiring.

Topic · 3

Phones should be locked away during the school day for students under 16.

For: better focus · less online bullying. Against: safety contact with parents · a missed chance to teach digital habits.

Topic · 4

An apprenticeship is a better start than a university degree.

For: earn while you learn · no debt. Against: fewer options later · some careers require degrees.

Topic · 5

Town centers should be car-free on Sundays.

For: cleaner air · busier cafés and markets. Against: harder for older and disabled visitors · deliveries and shift workers.

Topic · 6

Students should be allowed to use AI tools for homework.

For: it's a workplace skill · faster feedback. Against: less real thinking · unfair assessment.

Topic · 7

Soft drinks with added sugar should cost more than water in every workplace cafeteria.

For: healthier habits · cheaper water. Against: adults should choose · punishes low earners.

Topic · 8

Public libraries should become community hubs, not just book collections.

For: classes, repair cafés, job help · more visitors. Against: less quiet study space · staff aren't trained for everything.

Topic · 9

Remote work should be the default for office jobs.

For: no commute · a wider talent pool. Against: weaker teamwork · harder for new staff to learn.

Topic · 10

Every adult should complete basic first-aid training.

For: saves lives in the first minutes · confidence. Against: cost and time · skills fade (use it or lose it!).

G · C12 Speaking & Writing Checklist — The Advanced Communicator (feeds C13)

Score each learner's spoken performance across the talk and the Q&A (five criteria, 0–3 each, /15) and the written summary after class (four criteria, 0–3 each, /12). 0 = no response · 1 = not yet C1 · 2 = C1 · 3 = strong C1 (moving toward C2). Score what a patient, expert listener understands, not an accent. Tick M1–M4 when you hear or read a structure from that module used correctly. Write one sentence you heard or read in the notes column; it makes the slip easy to write.

How the results feed Progress Test 1 (C13)

Copy each learner's totals and module ticks to the class register. In C13, after the test, send each learner to the catch-up station for any module with no tick, or for their lowest criterion: no M1 → time & modality station · no M2 → conditionals & emphasis station · no M3 → cohesion & hedging station · no M4 → lexis station (Sets C and D of this pack) · S4 or W3 ≤ 1 → register-shift station (Workbook 4.3 Ex 2 and 4 + a "three rungs" round) · S5 ≤ 1 → shadowing station (the 🔊 model sentences in online lessons 4.1–4.3) or the Register Ladder (Set H) · W4 ≤ 1 → editing station (two sentences of the learner's own summary, rewritten with your correction codes). Learners you didn't hear in C12 present to you at a station in C13.

H · Game — The Register Ladder (Modules 1–4 review · C12 warmer · C13 station)

Rules
  1. Groups of 3–4, one board, one die, a counter for each player. Start on square 1. Follow the numbers: 1–6 left to right, 7–12 right to left, and so on.
  2. Roll, move, and answer the square in full sentences. The group checks with the answer strip (fold it under the board). Correct: stay. Wrong: go back 1.
  3. LADDER squares: answer correctly and climb 3 squares. SLIDE squares: answer correctly or slide back 3.
  4. USE IT squares: report one of your "use it or lose it" chunks from the C11 homework: where you used it and how it went. (Didn't use one? Use it now, in a sentence about your week.)
  5. Anyone can say "Pass!" and move back 1. First to FINISH, or nearest to it when the teacher stops the game, wins.

Read at a natural speed. Read each script twice: once for the main idea, once to check. For the voice message, sound relaxed and conversational; for the model talk, slow down at the signposts and pause after each one. 4.1-A · The launch debrief, a voice message (Workbook 4.1, Ex 1)

Hi everyone, it's Priya. Quick update on the app launch before Monday's meeting. So, the launch nearly fell through last week. Two of our suppliers pulled out at the last minute, which meant we had to put it off by a week and set up new contracts from scratch. I know that was stressful, and honestly, you all held up brilliantly. The good news is that since the launch, things have really picked up. Downloads have taken off, and the reviews are great. Jonah's going to look into why the suppliers backed out, and going forward, we'll cut down on single-source deals so we don't get caught out again. Thanks, everyone. See you Monday.

Answers: 1 fell through (①) · 2 pulled out (①) · 3 put it off (②) · 4 set up (②) · 5 held up (①) · 6 picked up (①) · 7 look into (③) · 8 cut down on (④). Extra phrasal verbs to notice: taken off, backed out, caught out. Follow-up: "Which three verbs would change in a formal email?"

4.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. We'll have to put off the launch until the software is ready. · 2. Could you fill it in and hand it back by Friday? · 3. The committee will investigate the complaint and respond in writing. · 4. The breakdown of the talks came as no surprise.

Check: the pronoun position in 2 (fill it in, hand it back), breakdown as one word in 4. Ask: "Which sentence is spoken, and which is written?" (1–2 spoken · 3–4 written.)

4.2-A · The pilot program meeting (Workbook 4.2, Ex 1 · two voices)

Dana: So the pilot program flopped. Where do we stand? · Sam: Honestly? The launch date was the last straw. We cut corners to hit it, and users noticed. · Dana: You've hit the nail on the head. And the complaints we've seen are only the tip of the iceberg. · Sam: Maybe the delay is a blessing in disguise, then. Let's make sure we're all on the same page before round two. · Dana: Agreed. That's food for thought. I think we'll have to bite the bullet and tell the board we need another month. · Sam: I'll draft the email. Formally, of course. No idioms!

Heard: the last straw · cut corners · hit the nail on the head · the tip of the iceberg · a blessing in disguise · on the same page · food for thought · bite the bullet. Not heard: get cold feet · a gray area. Follow-up: "Sam writes the email to the board. Rephrase the tip of the iceberg for it." (only a small fraction of the problem)

4.2-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. The proposal met with fierce resistance from the board. · 2. It's highly unlikely that we'll meet the deadline. · 3. She made a heartfelt apology and promised to do better. · 4. They're not stingy; they're simply thrifty.

Check: the collocations met with fierce resistance, highly unlikely, meet the deadline, made a heartfelt apology. Ask: "In 4, what changes between the two words?" (the judgment, not the behavior).

4.3-A · The model talk and Q&A: a paid learning day (Workbook 4.3, Ex 1 · three voices · about 3 minutes + Q&A)

Amara: Ask yourself this: when did you last learn something new at work, not because you had to, but because someone gave you the time? For most of us, the honest answer is "I can't remember." I'd like to make the case that every employer in our region should offer staff one paid learning day a month. There are three points I want to raise: skills, loyalty and cost. Let me begin by looking at skills. The tools we use are changing faster than ever, and our training simply hasn't kept up. What we need is not another annual course, but a steady, regular habit of learning. A monthly learning day would allow people to take an online course, shadow a colleague in another department, or simply look into a new piece of software properly. This brings me to loyalty. Staff turnover remains a pressing concern for local employers. The evidence would appear to suggest that people stay where they feel they are growing. Not only does a learning day show staff that they are valued, but it also gives them a reason to build their future here rather than elsewhere. Had we offered something like this five years ago, we would not now be losing so many experienced people to larger firms. Turning now to cost, and I know this is where the objections will come from. Yes, one day a month is roughly five percent of working time. But recruiting and training a replacement costs far more. By the end of the second year, I'm confident the scheme will have paid for itself. For that to happen, however, it is essential that the day be protected: no meetings, no emails, no "just this once." In essence, then, the choice is a simple one. We can keep paying to replace people, or we can invest in the people we have. The introduction of a learning day is a small change with a large return. Thank you. Leo: Thanks, Amara. But isn't there a risk that small businesses simply can't spare people for a whole day? · Amara: I take your point, but I'd argue that the day doesn't have to be the same day for everyone. A team of five could stagger it, so only one person is out at a time. · Grace: What would you say to someone who thinks staff will just treat it as a day off? · Amara: That's a fair challenge. What I'd say is that each learning day should end with a short note on what was learned. Trust, but with a little structure.

Signposts in order: Ask yourself this (1) · I'd like to make the case that (2) · There are three points I want to raise (3) · Let me begin by (4) · This brings me to (5) · Turning now to (6) · In essence (7) · I take your point, but I'd argue (8) · That's a fair challenge. What I'd say is (9). Structures: future perfect (will have paid for itself) · subjunctive (that the day be protected) · mixed conditional (Had we offered…, we would not now be losing…) · not only inversion · what-cleft (What we need is…) · nominalization (The introduction of a learning day) · hedging (would appear to suggest) · strong collocation (a pressing concern). Not heard: needn't have, ellipsis with so / not. Follow-up: "Which device opens the talk? Which one closes it?" (a rhetorical question · a contrast: keep paying to replace people, or invest in the people we have).

4.3-B · Clash or consistent? (learners say "Clash!" or "Consistent" · read each sentence twice · C12 clinic or C13 station)

1. The board will convene on Thursday to resolve the outstanding issues. · 2. Hey team, pursuant to our discussion, let's grab a coffee. · 3. We regret to inform you that your application has been, like, turned down. · 4. Any chance you could send me those documents? · 5. The committee will sort out the discrepancies. · 6. Should we commence? I'm starving. · 7. Additional funding will be required before the project can begin.

Answers: consistent 1, 4, 7 · clash 2 (Hey team + pursuant to), 3 (We regret to inform you + like, turned down), 5 (committee, discrepancies + sort out), 6 (commence + I'm starving). Learners fix each clash both ways.
