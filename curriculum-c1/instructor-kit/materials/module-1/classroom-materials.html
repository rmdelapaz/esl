B · Welcome Question Cards (1.1 · C1 mingle)

Print on four colors of paper if you can (or use the colored top edges). Each learner takes one card of each color. Every card says: real life, new identity, or invented; or say "Pass." TAKE A POSITION cards carry an assigned side so no one has to reveal a personal view.

LOOK BACK · 1

Tell your partner about a day when a plan at work or school went completely wrong. What had you been expecting, and what happened instead?

LOOK BACK · 2

Describe a moment when you realized you had been doing something the wrong way for a long time. How did you find out?

LOOK BACK · 3

Talk about a decision you're glad you made. What would have happened if you hadn't made it?

LOOK BACK · 4

Tell your partner about a time you had to learn something very quickly. How did you manage, and what would you do differently now?

WHAT IF · 1

If you could change one thing about the way meetings or classes are run, what would it be, and why?

WHAT IF · 2

Imagine that a new technology could do half of your job (or your studies) tomorrow. What would you do with the time?

WHAT IF · 3

If you had been in charge of a place you know (a workplace, a school, a neighborhood) last year, what would you have done differently?

WHAT IF · 4

If you could master one skill overnight, which would you choose? How would your life be different now if you had learned it years ago?

TAKE A POSITION · 1 · argue FOR (or your own view)

"Working from home is better for everyone." Give two reasons and an example.

TAKE A POSITION · 2 · argue AGAINST (or your own view)

"Everyone should learn to code at school." Give two reasons and an example.

TAKE A POSITION · 3 · argue FOR (or your own view)

"A four-day work week should be the standard." Give two reasons and an example.

TAKE A POSITION · 4 · argue AGAINST (or your own view)

"Handwriting is no longer worth teaching." Give two reasons and an example.

SAY IT TWO WAYS · 1

You need someone to cover your shift on Saturday. Ask a close friend at work. Then ask your manager.

SAY IT TWO WAYS · 2

You disagree with a proposal. Tell a friend over coffee. Then say it in a team meeting.

SAY IT TWO WAYS · 3

You can't meet a deadline. Send a quick message to a teammate. Then tell the client.

SAY IT TWO WAYS · 4

Decline an invitation to a dinner: first from a friend, then from the director of your organization.

B · B2 Diagnostic Grid (teacher only · never share)

Mark each column ✓ confident and accurate · ~ communicates well, some errors or gaps · ✗ avoids it or can't produce it yet. What to listen for: Range = narrative tenses incl. had been + -ing, third / mixed conditionals, passives and reporting verbs, linkers, precise vocabulary · Accuracy = systematic errors vs self-corrected slips · Appropriacy = register shift on the SAY IT TWO WAYS cards, softening, tone · Fluency & interaction = long turns, follow-ups, turn-taking, recovery. E · The Evidence Ladder — Case Files (1.2)

Four invented workplace and community puzzles. No case has a villain, and none involves a crime, an accident or an illness. Give each group the summary card face up and the six clue cards clipped in three rounds (1–2, 3–4, 5–6) to the evidence reader. The solutions are in the answer key (1.2). Two groups can work on the same case.

F · Policy Memo — Issue Briefs (1.3)

All workplaces are invented. Give one brief per group of 3 and assign Position A or B (fold the other one back). Learners argue the brief, not their own view. If two groups share a brief, give them opposite positions and let them compare memos at the end.

Brief 1 · Lakeside Logistics

Meeting overload

  • A survey of 120 staff: an average of 14 hours a week in meetings.
  • 62% say half their meetings have no agenda.
  • The average meeting has nine attendees.
  • Constraint: no budget; changes must start next quarter.

Position A: recommend a written agenda for every meeting, a meeting-free Wednesday and a limit of six invitees.

Position B: recommend shorter default meetings (25 or 50 minutes), optional attendance, and a written summary for people who don't attend.

Brief 2 · Northgate Health Clinic

New staff leaving early

  • Five of the twelve people hired last year left within three months.
  • Exit interviews: "I didn't know who to ask." "I was on my own on day one."
  • Current training: one day.
  • Constraint: no new trainers can be hired this year.

Position A: recommend a mentor for every new hire and two weeks of shadowing before any solo shift.

Position B: recommend a structured onboarding checklist, a welcome handbook, and check-ins with the manager at weeks 1, 4 and 8.

Brief 3 · Oakridge Tower

The shared kitchen

  • Three companies share one kitchen on the 3rd floor.
  • 41 complaints in two months: dirty dishes, food left in the fridge for weeks.
  • The cleaning contract covers the floors only.
  • Constraint: the building manager has $2,000 for this year.

Position A: recommend a cleaning rota shared by the three companies and a fridge clear-out every Friday.

Position B: recommend a professional cleaner twice a week and a new dishwasher, paid for from the $2,000.

Brief 4 · Crestview College admin office

Hybrid desk booking

  • 40 staff, 25 desks. Staff are expected in the office Tuesday to Thursday.
  • On Tuesdays, 38 people come in; on Thursdays, 14.
  • A booking system exists, but only 30% of staff use it.
  • Constraint: no money for new furniture this year.

Position A: recommend that booking be mandatory and that each team be assigned fixed office days.

Position B: recommend converting the unused meeting room into five extra desks and allowing staff to swap Tuesday for Thursday.

Brief 5 · Riverside Community Center

No-shows on the volunteer rota

  • 22% of volunteer shifts last quarter had a no-show.
  • The rota is posted on paper only, at the front desk.
  • Volunteers sign up three months in advance.
  • Constraint: the center has no paid volunteer coordinator.

Position A: recommend text-message reminders 48 hours before each shift and a monthly (not quarterly) sign-up.

Position B: recommend a team leader for every shift, a standby list and a shift-swap board.

G · Game — The Precision Board (1.1–1.3 · C4 warmer)

How to play (groups of 3–4, one die, one counter each): roll and move. Do the task on your square aloud. The group checks with the answer list (one learner is the checker; rotate each round). Correct: stay. Wrong: go back 1. DIAL squares: say the situation at three points on the certainty dial. MEMO squares: turn the casual line into a formal mandative sentence. FIX IT squares: correct the sentence. First to FINISH wins. Squares go in a snake: 1–6 left to right, 7–12 right to left, and so on.

1.1-A · The Monday stand-up: Harbor Point Community Center (Workbook 1.1, Ex 1)

Morning, everyone. A quick update on the Harbor Point Community Center renovation. As you know, we were going to reopen the main hall in April, but the new roof took longer than planned, so we're now aiming for the end of May. Here's where things stand. This time next week, the electricians will be working on the second floor, so please keep that corridor clear. By the end of the month, the painters will have finished all the classrooms, and by the time we reopen, the after-school program will have been running in the temporary space at the library for almost six months, so a big thank-you to the library team. On the money side, we're about to sign the contract for the new kitchen equipment; it should be done by Friday. And some news: the mayor is to visit the site on May 12, so the press office will be contacting some of you about photos. Finally, a question for the program leads: will any of you be needing the hall for evening events in June? Let me know by Thursday. That's all. Thanks, everyone.

Answers: 1 were going to reopen · 2 will be working · 3 will have finished · 4 will have been running · 5 are ('re) about to sign · 6 is to visit · 7 Will … be needing. Extra to notice: we're now aiming for (present continuous for a current plan) · will be contacting (future continuous: in the natural course of things). Gist: behind schedule (April → end of May); the mayor's visit.

1.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. By the time the auditors arrive, we'll have checked every invoice. · 2. Will you be using the conference room this afternoon? · 3. The launch was to have taken place in March. · 4. The two companies are on the verge of signing a deal.

Check: arrive (not will arrive) and the weak we'll have /wiːl əv/ in 1; was to have taken in 3 (learners often write "was to take"); on the verge of signing in 4.

1.2-A · The Tuesday slump (Workbook 1.2, Ex 1 · two voices)

Nadia: Tuesdays again. Sales are down forty percent. I don't get it. · Leo: It can't be the coffee. We're using the same beans, and Monday was busy. · Nadia: True. Could it be the new place on Fifth Street? · Leo: It might be, I suppose. But they opened in January, and the slump only started in March. So it can't possibly have started with them. · Nadia: What about Mia? She's been doing the Tuesday shift since March. · Leo: Mia? The customers love her. No, she won't be the reason. · Nadia: OK, so what changed in March? · Leo: Well, Tom at the print shop says the parking lot across the street is half empty on Tuesdays now. The insurance company over there may well have switched to working from home on Tuesdays. · Nadia: That would explain it. Their staff would always order ten lattes at once. · Leo: Then it must be that. If they're remote on Tuesdays, we're bound to be quieter. · Nadia: So what do we do? Close on Tuesdays? · Leo: We might as well try a Tuesday deal first, something for the students at the college. It should bring some people in. · Nadia: It could conceivably work. Let's try it for a month.

Answers: 1 can't be (R) · 2 might be (P) · 3 can't possibly have started (R) · 4 may well have switched (L) · 5 would always order (H) · 6 must be (C) · 7 are ('re) bound to be (C) · 8 might as well try (D). Extra: she won't be the reason (deduction) · It should bring some people in (expectation) · It could conceivably work (a slim chance). Follow-up: "How sure is Leo at the start, and at the end?"

1.2-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. She may well have missed the train, given the traffic. · 2. We might as well leave now; the meeting's been canceled. · 3. They can't possibly have known about the changes. · 4. He must have been waiting for hours.

Check: have after the modals (heard as /əv/; watch for "of") and been in 4. Ask: "Which sentence is a decision, not a guess?" (2)

1.3-A · The volunteer program: the committee's recommendations (Workbook 1.3, Ex 1)

Good evening, and thank you all for coming. On behalf of the board of the Riverside Community Center, I'd like to present our recommendations on the volunteer program. First, it is essential that the volunteer handbook be updated before the spring program begins. Second, we recommend that every new volunteer attend an orientation session before their first shift. Third, we propose that volunteers not be asked to work more than two shifts a week. Fourth, it is vital that each team leader have a key to the storage room, so that no shift starts late. We are aware of the cost. If the budget were larger, we would hire a part-time coordinator, but for now we must work with what we have. Be that as it may, the board is united on one point. For too long, we have treated training as though it were optional. It is high time we took this seriously. Thank you.

Answers: 1 be updated · 2 attend · 3 not be asked · 4 have · 5 were · 6 were · 7 took. Extra to notice: the fixed phrase Be that as it may. Ask: "Which recommendation is negative? Which are passive?" (3 negative; 1 and 3 passive.)

1.3-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. The committee insists that every application be submitted by Friday. · 2. It's essential that he not be told before the meeting. · 3. Were I in your position, I'd think very carefully. · 4. She spoke as though she had witnessed the whole thing herself.

Check: be submitted (not is) in 1; not be told in 2; Were I in 3; had witnessed (unreal, earlier) in 4.
