B · New Identity Cards (2.1 · 2.2 · 2.3)

Any learner may use a card instead of their own life, in any task, with no reason needed. In 2.1 they use a decision and its what-ifs (drill, journal, homework project). In 2.2 they use an occasion for a speech (drill, homework set piece). In 2.3 they use a misconception about my work (drill, journal).

C · The Case Study Panel — Case Files (2.1)

All four companies are invented. Give each group of 3–4 one case: learner A gets "The story," learner B gets "The numbers & hinge factors." They share orally (no showing), then prepare a three-minute analysis on the workbook frame. A model analysis of Case 1 is in the answer key.

Case 1 · A · The story

Harbor Fresh — grocery delivery

  • Founded in Riverside in 2019 by Tomás Reyes; delivers fresh groceries in 60 minutes.
  • Grew fast in 2020–21.
  • In 2022 the board discussed building its own warehouse. Tomás said no; the company kept buying everything from one wholesaler.
  • In 2023 the wholesaler went bankrupt. For three weeks the app showed empty shelves.
  • Harbor Fresh lost about 30% of its customers but survived. Today it is stable, but smaller than its rival, GreenCart.
Case 1 · B · The numbers & hinge factors

Harbor Fresh — grocery delivery

  • The warehouse would have cost $400,000 (a bank loan was available).
  • Tomás takes big risks in marketing but hates debt; he has never borrowed money.
  • GreenCart built its warehouse in 2022 and is now the market leader.
  • The lucky break: the Riverside Farmers' Co-op supplied Harbor Fresh free of charge for two weeks during the crisis.
  • On the table now: the co-op offers a partnership, on condition of a three-year contract and a minimum weekly order.
Case 2 · A · The story

Kestrel Software — clinic scheduling app

  • Founded in 2020 by Aiko Mori; the app books appointments for dental and physio clinics.
  • In 2021 Aiko, who speaks fluent Japanese, pitched the app to a clinic chain in Tokyo and won the contract.
  • In 2023 a power failure at Kestrel's data center took the app offline for six hours.
  • No data was lost: a backup system took over.
  • Customers stayed loyal, and Kestrel is now profitable.
Case 2 · B · The numbers & hinge factors

Kestrel Software — clinic scheduling app

  • The Tokyo chain now brings in 40% of Kestrel's revenue.
  • Only two of Kestrel's competitors have any clients outside their home country.
  • The backup system cost $20,000 and was installed only three months before the outage, because the lead engineer, Sam Okoro, insisted on it.
  • Aiko is famously persuasive but impatient with technical details.
  • On the table now: a clinic group in Seoul wants the app, provided that Kestrel hires Korean-speaking support staff.
Case 3 · A · The story

Maple & Stone — family furniture maker

  • A family workshop that has made wooden tables and chairs for 60 years.
  • For decades the company refused to sell online.
  • In 2021 the founder's granddaughter, Nell Stone, built a website in six weeks.
  • A customer posted a video of the workshop that was watched two million times.
  • Sales rose by 50% in a year. The workshop now has an eight-month waiting list.
Case 3 · B · The numbers & hinge factors

Maple & Stone — family furniture maker

  • Its main competitor went online in 2015 and doubled in size by 2020.
  • The grandfather, Arthur Stone, is a traditionalist: he believes customers must touch the wood before they buy.
  • The viral video was pure luck; the company paid nothing for it.
  • Two of the eight carpenters are retiring next year.
  • On the table now: a department store will stock Maple & Stone furniture, on condition that production doubles within a year.
Case 4 · A · The story

Northgate Textiles — fabric manufacturer

  • A mid-sized fabric factory in Northgate, founded in 1988.
  • In 2020 the CEO, Ruth Okafor, met a sportswear designer at a trade fair by chance, over coffee.
  • Months later, Northgate switched half its production to recycled fabric.
  • It lost two long-standing clients who didn't want to pay more, but gained five new ones.
  • Today it is the region's leading producer of recycled fabric.
Case 4 · B · The numbers & hinge factors

Northgate Textiles — fabric manufacturer

  • The new machines cost $1.2 million; a regional green-industry grant paid for half.
  • Ruth is curious and open to new ideas; her previous CEO was not.
  • Recycled fabric costs 15% more to produce, but sells for 30% more.
  • The trade fair meeting happened only because Ruth's flight home was canceled.
  • On the table now: a large retailer offers a five-year contract, provided that Northgate sells recycled fabric to nobody else (exclusivity).

D · The Speechwriter — Briefs (2.2)

One brief per pair. The underlined lines are strong candidates for inversion, but the team decides. Target: 3–5 inversions, three different triggers, one conditional inversion, one so / such, and one inversion cut by the editor. The small gray model at the foot of each card is for the teacher; fold it under before handing the card out.

Brief 1 · Tribute · 2 minutes

Walter Chen retires

Speaker: the principal of Riverside High · Audience: staff, students and Walter's family at his retirement party

Walter Chen has worked at Riverside High for twenty-eight years. I have never met anyone who knows a building so well. He fixed the boiler during the blizzard of 2011. He didn't tell anyone. We only found out when the heating bills came. He was so modest that most students didn't know his name. But they all knew his whistle. If he hadn't been here, the school would have closed many times. He never missed a graduation. He never complained. We didn't understand how much he did until he took a vacation last year. If you ever need a door fixed, don't call Walter. He's retired now. Please raise your glasses to Walter.

Model: For twenty-eight years, Walter Chen has kept Riverside High running. Never have I met anyone who knows a building so well. During the blizzard of 2011 he fixed the boiler, and told no one. Only when the heating bills arrived did we find out. So modest was he that most students never learned his name, though every one of them knew his whistle. Had it not been for Walter, this school would have closed more times than I can count. He never missed a graduation, and he never once complained. Should you ever need a door fixed, please don't call Walter: he's retired. Please raise your glasses: to Walter. (5 inversions; the "vacation" line was cut.)
Brief 2 · Formal statement · 90 seconds

Kestrel Software: after the outage

Speaker: Aiko Mori, CEO · Audience: clinic customers, in a recorded video message

Yesterday our booking system was offline for six hours. We are very sorry. Your data was not at risk at any time. The problem was a power failure at our data center. We have not had an outage like this before. We restored the system only after we had checked every account. We will not compromise on safety under any circumstances. The outage was so serious that we called every clinic personally. We have not only fixed the problem; we have also added a second power supply. If you notice anything unusual, please contact us. We know how much you rely on us. Thank you for your patience.

Model: Yesterday, our booking system was offline for six hours, and we are deeply sorry. At no time was your data at risk. The cause was a power failure at our data center, the first outage of its kind in our history. Only after every account had been checked did we restore the system: under no circumstances will we compromise on safety. So serious was the disruption that we called every clinic personally. We have fixed the problem and installed a second power supply. Should you notice anything unusual, please contact us directly. We know how much you rely on us, and we thank you for your patience. (5 inversions; "never before" and "not only" were cut and rewritten plainly.)
Brief 3 · Opening address · 90 seconds

The tenth Riverside Community Science Fair

Speaker: the fair's organizer · Audience: families, young scientists and judges in the library hall

Welcome to the tenth Riverside Science Fair. We have never had so many entries. There are 140 projects this year. The youngest scientist is seven. The oldest is eighty-two. The projects are so good that the judges asked for an extra day. You will not find a better way to spend a Saturday anywhere in the county. We didn't know, ten years ago, that the fair would grow like this. The library didn't just give us the hall. It also gave us three volunteers. If the library hadn't helped, there would be no fair. Please don't touch the volcano. Enjoy the day.

Model: Welcome to the tenth Riverside Science Fair. Never have we had so many entries: 140 projects, from scientists aged seven to eighty-two. So good are the projects this year that the judges asked for an extra day. Ten years ago, we had no idea the fair would grow like this. Not only did the library give us this hall, but it also gave us three wonderful volunteers. Had it not been for them, there would be no fair at all. And one request: under no circumstances should anyone touch the volcano. Enjoy the day! (5 inversions; "Little did we know" and "Nowhere will you find" were cut.)
Brief 4 · Safety briefing · 90 seconds

New volunteers at the Harborview food pantry warehouse

Speaker: the warehouse supervisor · Audience: twelve new volunteers on their first morning

Welcome to the warehouse. Please listen carefully. You must not use the forklift under any circumstances. Only trained staff may use it. You should wear gloves at all times. If there is a fire alarm, leave by the nearest exit. Do not stop to collect your things. The floor near the freezer is very slippery. Several people have fallen there. We have not had a serious accident even once in five years. We want to keep it that way. If you are unsure about anything, ask a supervisor.

Model: Welcome to the warehouse. Please listen carefully. Under no circumstances are you to use the forklift; only trained staff may operate it. Gloves must be worn at all times. Should the fire alarm sound, leave by the nearest exit, and on no account should you stop to collect your belongings. So slippery is the floor near the freezer that several people have fallen there. Not once in five years have we had a serious accident, and we intend to keep it that way. If you're unsure about anything, just ask a supervisor. (5 inversions; the last line stays deliberately plain and friendly.)

E · The Editing Desk — Articles B & C (2.3)

Article A (Tallis Print Works) is printed in the workbook. The task for every article: keep every fact; use at least one it-cleft, one what-cleft (or another frame) and two instances of fronting or extraposition; no more than three clefts; given information first, new information last. Model versions are in the answer key.

Article B · 13 sentences

Nap pods at Eastgate Distribution Center

Eastgate Distribution Center is a warehouse near the airport. It runs 24 hours a day. Last year it installed two nap pods for night-shift workers. Some managers laughed at the idea. The results were not funny. Accidents on the night shift fell by a third. The pods did not produce this result alone. A new rule produced it too. Workers must now take a twenty-minute break after four hours. The workers suggested the rule. Management did not suggest it. Other warehouses are now asking Eastgate for advice. Eastgate has no plans to remove the pods.

Facts to keep (fact-checker ticks): ☐ near the airport ☐ 24 hours ☐ two pods, last year ☐ night shift ☐ managers laughed ☐ accidents −⅓ ☐ new rule: 20 min after 4 h ☐ workers' idea ☐ other warehouses asking ☐ pods staying

Article C · 11 sentences

The Greenway Middle School garden

Greenway Middle School has a vegetable garden. The garden was a parent's idea. Nobody expected much from it. Attendance on Fridays improved. The garden club meets on Fridays. The science teacher uses the garden for lessons. Students measure plant growth every week. The cafeteria now serves the vegetables. Students eat more salad than before. Maintaining the garden in summer is difficult. Volunteers from the neighborhood water it.

Facts to keep (fact-checker ticks): ☐ vegetable garden ☐ a parent's idea ☐ low expectations ☐ Friday attendance up ☐ club meets Fridays ☐ science lessons ☐ weekly measuring ☐ cafeteria serves the vegetables ☐ more salad ☐ summer is hard ☐ neighborhood volunteers water it

G · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural C1 speed: do not slow down artificially, but pause at clause boundaries. Read each script twice: once for the gist, once for the task. For the speech, stand and give the inverted lines their weight. For the interview, use two voices or give Nadia's part to a confident learner. 2.1-A · "The Hinge": Brightwell Bikes (Workbook 2.1, Ex 1)

Welcome back to The Hinge, the podcast about the single decisions that shape a company. Today: Brightwell Bikes, a small e-bike maker in Riverside. In 2022, Brightwell was offered a three-year contract to supply two hundred bikes to Swift Deliveries. It turned the offer down. The founder, Lena Brightwell, worried that one big customer would end up owning the company. In hindsight, that was the turning point. If Brightwell had accepted the Swift Deliveries contract in 2022, it would be twice its current size today. Its rival, Cobalt Cycles, took the deal instead, and Cobalt has doubled. So why did Lena say no? Partly the numbers, but mostly character. If its founder weren't so cautious by nature, she would have taken the offer on the spot. And yet caution has its rewards. When a battery shortage hit in 2023, Cobalt's deliveries stopped for months. But for a loyal base of local customers, the company would have closed in 2023 as well: they waited, and many of them paid in advance. So what now? Analysts say Brightwell can still grow, provided that it finds a second factory. It has learned from the shortage, too: it now keeps a buffer of batteries in case a supplier lets it down. If the market were to recover next year, Brightwell would be well placed. But the clock is ticking. The city is about to choose a supplier for its new bike-share scheme, and unless it moves quickly, a rival will take the new city contract. That's The Hinge for this week.

Answers: 1 had accepted · would be · 2 weren't · would have taken · 3 But for · 4 provided that · 5 in case · 6 were to · 7 Unless. Follow-up: "Which conditional explains the present with the past? Which explains the past with a permanent trait?" (1 · 2)

2.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. If we had kept a buffer, we wouldn't be chasing suppliers now. · 2. If he weren't so cautious, he would have invested years ago. · 3. But for your warning, I would have signed the contract. · 4. Take a spare key in case you get locked out.

Check: had kept (learners hear "'d kept" and write "kept") and would have (not "would of") in 2. Ask: "Which one is a precaution?" (4)

2.2-A · Volunteer of the Year: the acceptance speech (Workbook 2.2, Ex 1)

Thank you. Thank you all. Never in my life have I been so completely lost for words, and those of you who know me know how rare that is. Little did I imagine, ten years ago, that one Saturday morning at the Harborview food pantry would change everything. I came to drop off a bag of rice. I stayed for a decade. This award isn't mine; it's yours. Last winter, when the storm cut the power for four days, not only did you keep the pantry open through the storm, but you also delivered meals to every door on Pier Street. So generous was the response from our neighbors that we had to borrow a second truck. And let me say this plainly: had it not been for Rosa's spreadsheets, we would have lost track of half the donations. Rosa, please stand up. Next winter will be harder. Prices are up, and the need is growing. But I know this: only by standing together will we be able to keep the doors open next winter. Thank you.

Answers: 1 have I been · 2 did I imagine · 3 did you keep · 4 was the response (from our neighbors) · 5 Had it not been · 6 will we be. Notice the plain sentences between the inversions ("I came to drop off a bag of rice. I stayed for a decade."): they give the inversions room to land. Discussion: six inversions in a short speech; which one would you cut?

2.2-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. Under no circumstances should the door be left open. · 2. No sooner had we arrived than the meeting was canceled. · 3. Should you require assistance, please ask at the front desk. · 4. So strong was the wind that the ferry stayed in port.

Check the order auxiliary + subject in every sentence, than (not when) after No sooner, and was the wind in 4. Ask: "Which one would you see on a hotel letter?" (3)

2.3-A · Radio interview: The Copper Kettle (Workbook 2.3, Ex 1 · two voices)

Host: With me in the studio is Nadia Brooks, who owns The Copper Kettle on Main Street. Nadia, this week you've been called "the café owner who hates laptops." Fair? · Nadia: Not at all. It wasn't the laptops themselves that were the problem. I use one every day. · Host: So what was? · Nadia: What we noticed was that forty people were sharing six tables between noon and two. Lunch customers would walk in, see no seats, and walk straight out again. · Host: How long did it take you to notice? · Nadia: Too long, honestly. It wasn't until we counted the customers walking out that we understood the scale of it. · Host: And your response was a ban. · Nadia: I'd call it a pause. All we did was ask people to close their laptops for two hours a day. The reason we tried it was that our lunch customers were leaving, and lunch pays the rent. · Host: Did you lose the morning crowd? · Nadia: The morning regulars, we didn't lose at all. They still work here every day, just not over lunch. · Host: Nadia Brooks, thank you.

Answers: 1 wasn't · that · 2 What we noticed · 3 wasn't until · 4 All we · 5 was that · 6 we didn't lose. Item 6 is fronting, not a cleft. Ask: "Which sentence corrects the host?" (1)

2.3-B · Which question does it answer? (read the statement, then the two questions; learners call out A or B)

1. "It was Maria who fixed the printer." A: Who fixed the printer? B: What did Maria fix? · 2. "What Maria fixed was the printer." A: Who fixed the printer? B: What did Maria fix? · 3. "It wasn't until Friday that the parts arrived." A: Did the parts arrive early? B: Where did the parts come from? · 4. "All I asked for was a receipt." A: Did you ask for a lot? B: Who did you ask? · 5. "The reason we moved is that the rent doubled." A: When did you move? B: Why did you move? · 6. "The second proposal, the board approved at once." A: What happened to the second proposal? B: Who wrote the second proposal?

Answers: 1 A · 2 B · 3 A (no: later than expected) · 4 A (no: very little) · 5 B · 6 A. Then ask learners to say 1 and 2 aloud with the stress on the focus: "It was Maria…" / "…was the printer."
