C · Register Workshop — Source Cards: Verbal → Academic (3.1)

Round 1. One card per group of 3. Each is a spoken update from a meeting (read it aloud in a relaxed voice first). Groups write a 4–5 sentence formal summary for a report. The small gray model at the bottom is for you: fold it back or cut it off before class. Any version that keeps the facts, hides the agents and fits the checklist is correct.

Source 1 · Team meeting

The four-day week

"So we tried the four-day week for three months. Honestly, people got more done: we closed 12% more support tickets. Fewer people called in sick, too. But customers complained that they couldn't reach us on Fridays, so we'll need to fix that before we decide anything."

A three-month trial of a four-day working week was carried out. The results showed a 12% increase in closed support tickets and a reduction in sick leave. However, there were complaints from customers about the lack of availability on Fridays. A solution to this problem will be required before a final decision is reached.
Source 2 · Clinic staff meeting

The walking group

"We started a lunchtime walking group for patients with high blood pressure. About forty people came regularly. Most of them lost a little weight, and their blood pressure went down. They said they liked meeting other people, and lots of them want to keep going."

The introduction of a lunchtime walking group for patients with high blood pressure attracted around forty regular participants. Most participants showed a modest reduction in weight and an improvement in blood pressure. The social contact was reported to be a major benefit, and there is strong demand for the continuation of the program.
Source 3 · Library board

Longer opening hours

"We kept the library open until 9 p.m. for six months. Lots more students came in the evenings, especially before exams. It cost us more because we had to pay two extra staff. We think it's worth it, and we want to do it again next year."

The extension of library opening hours to 9 p.m. for six months led to a significant rise in evening use by students, particularly before examinations. The extended hours required the recruitment of two additional staff, resulting in higher costs. Nevertheless, the trial is considered a success, and its repetition next year is recommended.
Source 4 · City transportation meeting

The bike-lane trial

"The city put a protected bike lane on Main Street for a year. Way more people cycled, nearly twice as many. Drivers complained at first because it took them longer to get through, but after a few months they stopped. Two shops said they got more customers."

The installation of a protected bike lane on Main Street for a one-year trial resulted in an almost twofold increase in cycling. Initial complaints from drivers about longer journey times declined after a few months. There was also an improvement in business reported by two local shops.
Source 5 · Office managers' meeting

Shared desks

"When we moved to shared desks, we saved a lot on rent. But people kept losing their things, and some couldn't find a quiet place to concentrate. So we put in lockers and made two quiet rooms, and now people seem much happier."

The move to shared desks produced considerable savings in rent. However, it also caused problems with the storage of personal belongings and a lack of quiet space for concentration. Following the installation of lockers and the creation of two quiet rooms, there has been a noticeable improvement in staff satisfaction.
Source 6 · School staff meeting

Phone-free lunch

"We tried banning phones at lunch for a term. Students talked to each other a lot more, and there were fewer arguments about things online. Some parents were worried they couldn't reach their kids, so we let students use the office phone."

A one-term ban on phone use at lunchtime was introduced. The ban led to an increase in face-to-face interaction among students and a reduction in online conflicts. In response to concerns from parents about contact with their children, access to the office phone was provided.

C · Register Workshop — Plain English Notices: Academic → Plain (3.1)

Round 2. One notice per group. Each was written for a real, busy reader but reads like a policy document. Groups rewrite it so the reader understands it in one read: short sentences, active verbs, you / we, still polite. Models are small and gray at the bottom: cut them off before class.

Notice 1 · To: all residents

Elevator repairs

"Please be advised that the completion of elevator maintenance is subject to the arrival of the replacement components. Resumption of elevator operation is anticipated upon receipt and installation of said components. Residents requiring assistance with the transportation of heavy items in the interim are requested to make contact with the management office."

We're still repairing the elevator. We'll finish as soon as the new parts arrive, and we'll let you know when it's working again. If you need help carrying anything heavy before then, please call the management office.
Notice 2 · To: a patient

Your appointment

"Due to the reorganization of clinic scheduling, the rescheduling of your appointment has been necessitated. Confirmation of your attendance at the revised time is required by telephone within five working days. Failure to provide confirmation may result in the cancellation of the appointment."

We've had to change the time of your appointment because we're reorganizing our schedule. Please call us within five working days to confirm that you can come at the new time. If we don't hear from you, we may have to cancel the appointment.
Notice 3 · To: parents and guardians

Pickup at the end of the day

"In light of the implementation of the new traffic arrangements, the collection of students is to take place exclusively at the Oak Street entrance. The utilization of the main entrance for the purpose of vehicle stopping is prohibited during the hours of dismissal."

Because of the new traffic plan, please pick up your child at the Oak Street entrance only. Please don't stop your car at the main entrance at the end of the school day.
Notice 4 · To: all staff

Expense claims

"With effect from March 1, the submission of all expense claims is required through the new online form. The utilization of paper forms will no longer result in the processing of reimbursement. Staff requiring guidance on the completion of the form should direct inquiries to the finance team."

From March 1, please use the new online form for all expense claims. We can't pay back expenses claimed on paper forms any more. If you need help filling in the form, please ask the finance team.

D · Repair the Report — Drafts & Device Checklist (3.2)

One draft per group of 3. Each is about 140 words and deliberately repetitive. Target: 90 words or fewer, every fact kept. Model repairs are in the answer key (Module 3, 3.2).

Report 1 · Facilities · The office kitchen

The office kitchen is too small for the number of staff. The office kitchen was designed for 20 staff, but 45 staff now use the office kitchen. At lunchtime, staff wait for the microwaves. Staff wait for the microwaves for up to 15 minutes. There are two microwaves. The two microwaves are old, and one of the two microwaves often breaks. The fridge is also a problem. The fridge is always full, and the fridge is not cleaned regularly. Staff have suggested buying two new microwaves. Staff have also suggested cleaning the fridge every Friday. We recommend buying two new microwaves. We also recommend cleaning the fridge every Friday. If the company buys two new microwaves and cleans the fridge every Friday, staff will be happier. Buying two new microwaves will cost about $400. We think that the cost of $400 is reasonable.

Report 2 · Tenants' association · The composting pilot

In April, the tenants' association started a composting pilot. The composting pilot ran for three months. In the composting pilot, 30 households put food waste in two compost bins. The two compost bins were in the back yard. The 30 households produced 900 pounds of compost. The 900 pounds of compost were used in the community garden. Some tenants complained about the smell. The tenants complained about the smell in June, when the weather was hot. The association moved the compost bins away from the windows. After the association moved the compost bins, the tenants did not complain about the smell. The association wants to continue the composting pilot. The association also wants to add two more compost bins. If the landlord agrees to the association adding two more compost bins, the association will add two more compost bins in September.

Report 3 · Small business · Customer survey summary

In May, Spoke & Chain Bike Repair surveyed 120 customers. The survey asked customers about prices, speed and service. Most customers were happy with the prices. Most customers were also happy with the service. However, many customers were not happy with the speed. Many customers said that repairs took too long. Customers said that repairs took an average of six days. Customers want repairs to take two or three days. Some customers suggested an online booking system. An online booking system would let customers book a time for a repair. Other customers suggested longer opening hours. The owner thinks an online booking system is a good idea. The owner does not think longer opening hours are a good idea, because longer opening hours would cost too much. The owner will introduce an online booking system in the summer.

E · Two-Minute Talk — Topic Cards (3.2)

Face down in the middle of each group. The speaker takes one, gets 30 seconds to think, then talks for two minutes. Any card can be answered from real life, a new identity card or imagination. The tally-keeper counts repeats and devices.

Talk · 1

A place I know well

Describe it, what happens there, and how it has changed.

Talk · 2

How a workplace or school has changed

Compare five years ago with today. What's better? What's worse?

Talk · 3

A skill I learned as an adult

How did you learn it? What was hard? Would you recommend it?

Talk · 4

A tool or app I use every day

What does it do? What would you do without it?

Talk · 5

The best way to learn a language

Compare two or three methods. Which one works best, and for whom?

Talk · 6

What makes a good neighborhood

Give three features and an example of each.

Talk · 7

How I organize a busy week

Lists, apps, calendars, habits: what works and what doesn't?

Talk · 8

A job I'd like to try for one day

Why that job? What do you think the hardest part would be?

F · Diplomatic Role-Play Series — A/B Cards (3.3)

Groups of 3: A delivers the message, B receives it, the Observer uses the checklist below. Four rounds, one per scenario, roles rotate each round. Give neighboring groups different versions (v1 / v2). All positions are assigned: learners play the card. Each round: 1 min plan · 2½ min conversation · 1½ min observer feedback.

① Giving bad news · v1 · A

Project lead, print & design company

Your client ordered 5,000 brochures for an event. Because your paper supplier is late, the brochures will arrive on the 18th, not the 4th. You can offer 500 copies by the 4th from a local printer at no extra cost, and the rest on the 18th.

Tell the client clearly and kindly. Use preface → soften → reason → alternative.

① Giving bad news · v1 · B

Client, event manager

Your event is on the 10th. You're already stressed. You want to know exactly what you'll get and when. You'll accept a reasonable solution if it's explained clearly, but you won't accept vague promises.

At the end, say in one sentence what you now understand.

① Giving bad news · v2 · A

Office manager

The team asked for six standing desks. They're not in this year's budget: the money went on the new heating system. You can offer two desk converters now (cheaper) and add standing desks to next year's budget request.

Tell the team representative clearly and kindly.

① Giving bad news · v2 · B

Team representative

Several colleagues have back problems, and you collected twelve signatures for the request. You're disappointed and want something now, not only next year. You're willing to compromise.

At the end, say in one sentence what you now understand.

② Disagreeing with a manager · v1 · A

Customer service lead

Your manager wants to launch the new booking app to customers next week. In the test version you saw double bookings. You think it needs two more weeks of testing with 50 real customers. Disagree respectfully and propose the alternative.

② Disagreeing with a manager · v1 · B

Manager

You promised the director a launch next week, and a competitor launched its app last month. You're attached to the date, but you're open to a good argument, especially a compromise (for example, a small launch first).

At the end, say in one sentence what you now understand.

② Disagreeing with a manager · v2 · A

Team member

Your manager proposes moving the weekly team meeting to Monday at 7:30 a.m. Several people have school drop-offs or long commutes, and you think attendance will drop. Suggest 9:30 a.m. or a 20-minute online stand-up instead.

② Disagreeing with a manager · v2 · B

Manager

You want the meeting to finish before the phones open at 9:00, and you think early starts show commitment. You'll accept an alternative that keeps the phones covered.

At the end, say in one sentence what you now understand.

③ Declining a request · v1 · A

Customer service desk, supermarket

A colleague asks you to cover their Saturday shift. You can't: you have a commitment that day (you don't have to say what it is). Say no warmly. Offer to swap a shift next week or suggest asking Ahmed, who wants extra hours.

③ Declining a request · v1 · B

Colleague

Your sister is visiting from out of town on Saturday, and you've already asked three people. You really hope A says yes, and you're a little disappointed if not. You'd welcome a helpful alternative.

At the end, say in one sentence what you now understand.

③ Declining a request · v2 · A

Data analyst

Your manager asks you to take on a third project. You're already on two, and the Harris report is due on Friday. Say "not now" clearly. Offer to start in two weeks, or to do one small part (the data cleaning) if another deadline can move.

③ Declining a request · v2 · B

Manager

The director wants the new project started this week, and you think A is the best person for it. You'll accept a clear alternative, but you need a concrete plan to take back to the director.

At the end, say in one sentence what you now understand.

④ Softening criticism · v1 · A

Team lead

Your colleague's slides for Thursday's client presentation have strong content and a great chart on slide 3, but they're overcrowded (up to 150 words per slide, small font). Ask for 30 words or fewer per slide and the details moved to a handout. Start with a real strength.

④ Softening criticism · v1 · B

Colleague

You worked all weekend on these slides and you're proud of how thorough they are. You're a little defensive at first, but you want the presentation to go well.

At the end, say in one sentence what you now understand.

④ Softening criticism · v2 · A

Supervisor

A new team member's emails to customers are fast and friendly (customers like the speed), but too informal: "Hey!", "no worries", emojis, no sign-off. Ask for "Hello + name," a clear sign-off and no emojis with new customers. Offer to share the email template.

④ Softening criticism · v2 · B

New team member (three weeks in)

You think your friendly style is why customers like you. You're nervous about doing badly in your first month, so criticism feels bigger than it is.

At the end, say in one sentence what you now understand.

H · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural speed. Read each script twice: once for the main idea, once to check. Change your voice for each speaker, or give the lines to confident learners. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

3.1-A · The electric vans: the spoken update and the written summary (Workbook 3.1, Ex 1)

Read part 1 in a relaxed, chatty voice; read part 2 slowly and formally. Learners complete the gaps in part 2.

Part 1, Marcus at the team meeting: OK, quick update on the electric vans. So, we brought in two electric vans back in March, and we looked at how they did for three months. Costs went up at first, because we had to buy chargers, and, annoyingly, one of the chargers failed in May. But fuel costs dropped a lot, so overall, it's really worked. And the board said yes last week, so we're going to roll out ten more by the end of the year. · Part 2, the written summary: The introduction of two electric vans in March was followed by a three-month assessment of their performance. Despite an initial increase in costs for chargers and the failure of one charger in May, the trial resulted in a significant reduction in fuel costs. Following the board's approval last week, the implementation of a ten-van rollout is planned by the end of the year.

Answers: 1 introduction · 2 assessment · 3 increase · 4 failure · 5 reduction · 6 approval · 7 implementation. Verb → noun: brought in → introduction · looked at how they did → assessment · went up → increase · failed → failure · dropped → reduction · said yes → approval · roll out → implementation (rollout).

3.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. The introduction of the new policy led to a marked reduction in waste. · 2. An investigation was carried out into the cause of the delay. · 3. Poor lighting poses a serious threat to cyclists. · 4. To reduce emissions, we must use renewable resources.

Check the prepositions (reduction in, investigation into, threat to) and the stress on introDUCtion, reDUCtion, investiGAtion. Ask: "Which sentence is the plain-English one? Is it less serious?" (4; no.)

3.3-B · What do they really mean? (C9 Diplomatic stage · read each once, with the intonation)

1. "The food wasn't bad at all." · 2. "I'm not entirely sure that's wise." · 3. "It's not the most exciting job in the world." · 4. "We might have a slight issue with the budget." · 5. "She's not exactly a morning person." · 6. "I'd have thought we could do a little better."

Learners say the real meaning: 1 It was good. · 2 I think it's a bad idea. · 3 It's boring. · 4 There's a real (possibly serious) problem. · 5 She's tired or grumpy in the mornings. · 6 I'm disappointed; we should do better. Ask: "Is it the same in your language, or more direct?"
