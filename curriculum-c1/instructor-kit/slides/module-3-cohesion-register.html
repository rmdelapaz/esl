3.1 · And back again · when NOT to nominalize

Dense is not the same as clever

Over-nominalizedThe implementation of the utilization of renewable resources is a requirement for the achievement of a reduction in emissions.
RebalancedTo reduce emissions, we must use renewable resources.
For a noticeConfirmation of attendance is required prior to commencement. → Please confirm that you'll come before it starts.
The test: Who does what? Can you tell in three seconds? If not, turn a noun back into a verb. Is the short version less serious? (No, just clearer.)

3.2 · Cohesion · the thread between sentences

Point to it · swap it · drop it

✘ The committee rejected the proposal. The committee felt the proposal was too expensive. The committee will review a new proposal next month, and the committee hopes the new proposal will be cheaper.

✔ The committee rejected the proposal; they felt it was too expensive. They'll review a new one next month and hope it will be cheaper.

Same information? (Yes.) Which one sounds like a person thinking? (The second.) What did the speaker do? (Pointed back, swapped in "one", left words out.)
3.2 · Reference · point to it

Make every arrow land in one place

this / that + nounHouse prices rose sharply. This increase surprised economists.
such (a) + nounHe was rude to a customer. Such behavior is unacceptable.
the former / the latterWe can fly or take the train; the former is faster, the latter cheaper.
the above / as followsFor the above reasons, … · The steps are as follows: …
VAGUE Sales fell and the manager resigned. It was unexpected. → The resignation was unexpected.

3.2 · Model · count the devices

Two reports

Anna: Did you finish the two reports?

Ben: The short one, yes; the long one, not yet.

Anna: Will it be ready by Friday?

Ben: I think so. I meant to start it yesterday, but I didn't get to.

Anna: Can you send me the figures?

Ben: I'll do so this afternoon. Well, I'll try to. Such requests always take longer than they look.

Which device sounds a little formal for two colleagues? ("I'll do so" · "Such requests")

3.3 · Academic hedging · match the claim to the evidence

proves → shows → suggests → may suggest

Modalsmay · might · could · tend to
Verbsseem · appear · suggest · indicate
Adverbialsarguably · largely · to some extent
FramesIt would appear that… · There is some evidence that…
"These results suggest caffeine may improve memory." Proven? (No.) Weak or careful? (Careful.) Easier to attack: this or "proves"? ("Proves.")
Six hedges in one sentence? (Too many: the claim disappears.)
3.3 · Diplomatic softening · distance = politeness

The distance ladder

Can you send it today?
Could you send it today?
Would it be possible to send it today?
I was wondering if you could send it today.
"I was wondering…": past or present request? (Present: the past tense is softer.)
"It's not the cheapest option." (= It's expensive.)
"I'm not entirely convinced." (= I disagree.)
"Wouldn't it be safer to wait?" (= Let's wait.)
ALSO a slight problem · not exactly ideal · With respect, … · That's a bit risky, isn't it?
3.3 · Bad news & refusals · model

Preface → soften → reason → alternative

Carla: Would it be possible for you to present our results on Thursday?

Omar: As much as I'd like to, I'm afraid Thursday's not going to work. The thing is, I'm with a client all afternoon.

Omar: Perhaps we could ask Jin instead? Or couldn't we record a short video?

Yes or no? (No.) Clear? (Yes.) Reason? (A client.) What does Carla get instead? (Two options.) "Hmm, that might be a bit tricky…" and stop? (Too soft: she might hear a yes.)
