8.1 · What fluency really is · check the meaning

Fluency = keep going, not go fast

Freeze: "The reason is… um… [long silence] … sorry, I don't know how to say it."
Flow: "The reason is, well, how can I put this? It's partly about cost, and partly, I suppose, about habit."
Was the second speaker faster? (No.) Why does she sound more fluent? (No dead silence; the phrase filled the gap.) Is pausing wrong? (No. Frozen, silent pauses are the problem.)

The word won't come? Describe it.

the thing you use to…

"It's the thing you use to open a wine bottle." Does she know the word? (Not right now.) Did the conversation stop? (No.) What often happens next? (The listener says it: "A corkscrew!") · Also: it's when… (a commute) · the, oh, whatever it's called…
8.1 · Self-correction · repair on the move

Fix it without stopping

✘ We arrived on Tuesday. No wait, sorry, that's wrong, um, it was Wednesday, sorry.

✔ We arrived on Tuesday, or rather, Wednesday, and got straight to work.

Which day was it? (Wednesday.) Did she apologize? (No.) Do we trust her more or less? (More: she's monitoring her own speech.)
Also: what I mean is… · let me rephrase that · to put it another way…
SAY IT the repair is light and quick; stress the corrected word: "Tuesday, (or rather) WEDnesday"

8.2 · Why edit? · one pass

"I didn't have time to write a short letter, so I wrote a long one."

First draft (35 words): Due to the fact that we did not have a sufficient amount of time, we made the decision to postpone the meeting until a later date in order to be able to prepare more fully.
Edited (16 words): Because we lacked time, we decided to postpone the meeting so we could prepare more fully.
ASK Which ideas were lost? (None.) What exactly was cut? Name four cuts.

8.2 · Cut 4 · double negatives & tangles

Can you say it in one breath?

TangledClear
It is not uncommon for delays to occur in the event that staff are absent.Delays are common when staff are absent.

DON'T OVER-CUT "Committee will review proposal" ✘ (headline, not English) · keep numbers, names, sources, hedges
8.2 · Freer task · 26 min

✂️ The 30% Cut Challenge

  • Round 1 · before/after cards: match them and name every cut
  • Round 2 · groups of 3 · one text (~150 words) → cut at least 30%
  • Editor (pen) · word-counter · meaning-keeper (veto)
  • Round 3 · read it aloud: before / after / percent
How much do you cut? (At least 30%.)
Can you cut an idea? (Never.)
A number or a source? (No.)
Who can say "No, that loses meaning"? (The meaning-keeper.)
Shortest wins? (No, the clearest.)
8.3 · The position paper · six parts · about 500 words

Take a stand, face the objection

1 ThesisThis paper argues that…4 Counter-argumentAdmittedly, it might be objected that…
2 Argument AThe primary reason is…5 RebuttalThis concern, while valid, overlooks…
3 Argument BA related consideration is…6 ConclusionOn balance, the weight of evidence…
Topics: a later school start · a four-day week · AI tools for homework · cashless stores · portfolios instead of exams · paid trial days instead of interviews · … Choose one, or draw one. Draw a side card if you prefer: you argue the card, not your opinion.
8.3 · Model · a defense under questioning

Concede without collapsing

Q1: What about residents with disabilities, or older people who can't walk far? Isn't a blanket ban unfair to them?

Priya: That's a fair point, and I don't want to brush it aside. You're right that a blanket ban would be unfair. That said, the proposal I'd defend isn't a blanket one: it exempts accessible transportation and permit holders. So the principle stands, provided the exemptions are properly designed.

What does she concede? (A blanket ban would be unfair.) What does she hold? (The principle.) Did she give a reason? (Yes: the exemptions.)
8.3 · The viva · questions and answers · check the meaning

Concede → hold → hedge

Examiners ask…
🔍 clarify: What exactly do you mean by…?
📊 evidence: How do you know that…?
⚖️ objection: What about people who…?
🔮 consequence: If this were adopted, what might go wrong?
📏 scope: Where do you draw the line?
"Yes, you're right. My idea is bad." (Collapse ✘)
"No, that's wrong." (Stonewall ✘)
"That's a fair point. That said, … So I'd put it slightly more cautiously: …" (Poised ✔)
"This proves that…" (Overclaim → "This strongly suggests…")
Unsure of the question? "If I've understood your question, you're asking whether…"
