1.1 · The future continuous · in progress & tact

This time tomorrow…

I'll be flying
I'll have packed
I'll have answered 50 emails
I'll have been waiting for an hour
"Will you use the car tonight?" / "Will you be using the car tonight?" Which could sound like a challenge? (The first.) Why is the second more polite? (It asks about the plan; no pressure to decide now.)
1.1 · Simple or continuous? · check the meaning

Result or activity?

By Friday she'll have written three chapters.
By Friday she'll have been writing for a month.
By the launch we'll have tested every screen.
By the launch we'll have been testing for six months.
Which ones count results? (1 and 3: simple.)
Which measure an activity? (2 and 4: continuous.)
"In August they'll have been running the business for 20 years." Will they stop in August? (Probably not.)
1.1 · The past looking forward · formal & imminent futures

Plans, near misses and announcements

was going toWe were going to fly, but the strike changed everything.
was about toI was about to sign when the phone rang.
was to have + ppThe launch was to have taken place in March. (it didn't)
be toThe mayor is to open the site on May 12. · If we are to meet the deadline, …
verge · due · setThey're on the verge of signing. · The fix is due to arrive on the 20th. · The firm is set to expand.
WATCH We're about to sign next week ❌ · about to = any moment now

1.2 · Certainty is a dial, not a switch

How sure are you, exactly?

near-certainmust (have) · is bound toShe must be exhausted. · It's bound to happen again.
expectedshould · ought toThey should be home by now.
quite likelymay / might / could wellThe update may well be the cause.
possiblemay · might · couldPrices might fall.
slim chancemight just · could conceivablyIt could conceivably be the software.
ruled outcan't · can't possibly (have)They can't possibly have known.
1.2 · False twins · check the meaning

may well ≠ may as well

"The train may well be late."
Probability or decision? (Probability: quite likely.)
Stress? (may well)
"The train's late, so we may as well get a coffee."
Probability or decision? (Decision: no better option.)
Any feeling? (A little shrug.)
TEST "It's probably going to…" → may well · "We might as well just…" → may as well
1.2 · The hidden jobs of will & would

Not always about the future

deduction · nowThere's the doorbell. That'll be the courier.
refusalHe won't listen. · The lock wouldn't turn.
typical behaviorShe'll sit there for hours. · She will keep interrupting!
past habitEvery summer we'd drive to the coast. (actions only)
criticismYou would say that!
politenessI'd suggest a short break. · I wouldn't worry.
1.2 · Model · speculating about now and the past

The dark office

Sam: The office is dark and Priya's coat's gone. She must have left early.

Alex: She may well have, though she might just be in the server room.

Sam: She won't answer her phone when she's busy, so we might as well email her.

Alex: She should reply by morning. She could have been planning this all week!

now → must be joking · must be waiting  |  past → must have left · must have been waiting · must of ❌

Verbsrecommend · insist · propose · request · require · demandWe recommend that she chair the meeting.

Nounsour recommendation / proposal / requirement that…a requirement that each applicant submit references

1.3 · Three registers · the lookalike · check the meaning

Formal, softer, informal

I suggest that she be promoted.most formal · standard in US
…that she should be promoted.British · softer
…that she is promoted.informal
"The data suggests that he is right." Demand or fact? (Fact: suggest = show.)
"She insisted that he leave." (Demand.)
"She insisted that she was innocent." (Fact: she asserted it.)
1.3 · Unreal forms · check the meaning

Stepping outside the real

If I were you… · Were we to delay…
I wish it were simpler.
Supposing the budget were cut…
It's high time we reviewed this.
I'd rather you didn't mention it.
"He talks as if he owned the company." Does he own it? (No.) Past time? (No, now: past = unreal.)
"It looks as if it's going to snow." Might it? (Yes: present = possibly true.)
Was I in charge… ❌ → Were I in charge…
