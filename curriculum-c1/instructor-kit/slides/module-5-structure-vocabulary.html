Drop the conjunction. Drop the repeated subject.

Full clauseParticiple clauseLink the reader supplies

After they had signed the contract, they celebrated.Having signed the contract, they celebrated.sequence
The novel was written in 1920, and it…Written in 1920, the novel…background

5.1 · Choose by voice, not by tense

Does the subject do it, or receive it?

FormMeaning
-ingdoes it · same time, reason, result
having + p.p.did it · earlier
having been + p.p.had it done to it · earlier
p.p.has it done to it · passive
"Building in 1920, the museum…" Can a museum build? (No! Built in 1920.)
"The storm hit the coast, flattening homes." What flattened them? (The storm: a result.)
"Having been warned twice, the driver slowed down." Did the driver warn anyone? (No, he was warned.)
5.1 · Absolute clauses · the different-subject fix

Give the participle its own subject

Two clauses, two subjectsAbsolute
Because the road was blocked, the drivers turned back.The road blocked, the drivers turned back.
The meeting had ended, so the staff went home.The meeting over, the staff went home.
Because the funds had run out, the project stopped.The funds having run out, the project stopped.
She waited at the gate while her heart was pounding.She waited at the gate, her heart pounding.
"The weather being fine, we set off." Was we fine? (No, the weather.) How many subjects? (Two.) "Being fine, we set off"? (Dangles!) Formal or casual? (Formal, written.)

5.1 · The one-question test

Who is doing this -ing?

"Walking into the room, the noise was overwhelming."
Who is walking, grammatically? (The noise!)
Repair ①: change the subject ("…, I found the noise overwhelming.")
Repair ②: its own subject / a full clause ("As I walked into the room, …")
"Having finished the exam, the results came quickly."
Who finished the exam? (The students, not the results.)
Fix? ("…, the students got their results quickly." / "The exam finished, the results came quickly.")
"Having said that, …" (Fixed phrase: fine.)
CHECK the answer to "who is doing this?" must be the very next subject

5.2 · Model · find the chunks

The quarterly figures

Manager: With regard to the quarterly figures, how do you account for the drop in sales?

Analyst: It largely results from the supply delays. The dip is consistent with last winter's.

Manager: Are we still reliant on a single supplier?

Analyst: For now. We need an alternative to them. In the event of another delay, we have no buffer.

Manager: Draft a proposal, in accordance with the procurement policy.

Does "results from" name the cause or the effect first? (Effect first: the drop.) Which phrase would you drop in a hallway chat? ("With regard to" → "About")

"We got a lot of data and found something big." → "We obtained substantial data and identified a significant finding." Did the grammar change? (Hardly: the words did.)
5.3 · Stance verbs · don't over-claim, don't over-formalize

How strongly does the source say it?

StrengthVerbs
treated as provendemonstrate · show
a positionargue · claim · maintain
tentativesuggest · indicate · imply
neutral factnote · observe
admits a limitacknowledge · concede

Ana: We looked at the survey and found out a lot of people got put off by the price.

Report: An examination of the survey indicated that a substantial proportion of respondents were deterred by the price.

USE / UTILIZE TEST Does the long word add meaning, or only length? · I shall endeavor to commence → I'll start
