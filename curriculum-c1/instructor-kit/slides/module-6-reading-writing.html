6.1 · The toolkit · the skeleton of an argument

Evidence —(warrant)→ Claim

Factcan be checkedComplaints rose by 11 percent.
Opiniona judgment, openly signaledFrankly, it was a waste of time.
Opinion in disguisea judgment in the grammar of factIt is clearly the future of work.
Warrantthe unstated link"The pilot, and nothing else, caused the fall."
"Sick days fell by 18%, so the pilot should be permanent." Claim or evidence: the 18%? (Evidence.) What must we assume? (The pilot caused it.) Could the figure be true and the argument still weak? (Yes: winter, flu season…)

6.2 · Lead-in · topic sentences only

What's the question? What's the position?

  • …employers should set clear limits on out-of-hours email, because doing so protects employee well-being and… improves the quality of the work itself.
  • The most compelling reason for limiting after-hours email is its effect on recovery.
  • A second, less obvious benefit is that limits force clearer communication during the working day.
  • Admittedly, critics contend that rigid rules ignore the reality of modern work…
  • Limiting after-hours email, then, is not an attack on flexibility but a defense of…
SHAPE hook → context → thesis · PEEL · PEEL · concede → rebut · restate → synthesize → implication
6.2 · Thesis clinic · check the meaning

Could a reasonable person disagree?

WeakWhy
"This essay is about training at work."T topic only
"Training is important for employees."O obvious
"There are good arguments on both sides."F on the fence
"Employees deserve higher salaries."Q a different question
Strong: "Although paid training time carries short-term costs, employers who offer it gain more skilled and more loyal staff, and should treat it as an investment." What does Although do? (Concedes.) Specific? Arguable? On the question? (Yes · yes · yes.)
6.2 · Cohesion & the fair rebuttal

Match the connector to the logic

JobConnector
addFurthermore · Moreover
resultConsequently · Therefore
compareBy contrast · Whereas
concedeAdmittedly · Granted
turn backNevertheless · Yet
exampleFor instance · A case in point
Concede: "Admittedly, critics contend that rigid rules ignore emergencies."
Rebut: "Yet this objection targets a blanket ban, not the limits proposed here."
"It saved money. Consequently, staff disliked it." Is that a result? (No → Nevertheless.)
"Some people think workers should never learn anything." Does anyone argue that? (No: a straw man.)
6.2 · Model · a peer review that helps

Honest, specific, kind

Mei: Can I check I've understood your thesis? You're arguing that open-plan offices cost more in concentration than they save in space.

Karim: Yes, exactly.

Mei: The strongest sentence, for me, is the one about "renting silence back by the hour." But I wasn't entirely sure how the second paragraph connects to the thesis. You might consider explaining why noise matters for your kind of work.

Karim: So, finish the PEEL. What about the counter-argument?

Mei: I'd question the rebuttal. You concede that new staff learn faster in open-plan offices, then just say it isn't important. That reads a little like dismissing it.

ORDER check the thesis → strongest sentence → one question · the writer listens, then decides

6.3 · Lead-in · the register ladder

Same request. Which reader?

A"hey can we chat about that proposal thing sometime? thx"
B"Dear Professor Lin, I am writing to inquire whether it might be possible to arrange a brief meeting to discuss the research proposal."
C"I hereby humbly beseech you to grant me the honor of your esteemed attention at your earliest possible convenience."
D"Hi Sara, could we find twenty minutes this week to go over the proposal? I'd really value your input."
A senior stranger? (B.) A colleague you know? (D.) Anybody? (Not A or C.) Is the ask clearer in B or D? (Equally clear: only the framing changes.)
6.3 · The email · purpose first

Direct is not rude. Vague is not polite.

Subject: Request for Q3 overtime figures — report due Friday

Dear Mr. Nguyen,

I am writing to request the overtime figures for the third quarter, which I need to complete the staffing report due this Friday.

Could you please send the spreadsheet by the end of the day on Wednesday? If that timeline is difficult, please let me know.

Thank you for your help.
Best regards,
Leila Haddad

  • Clarity: one idea per sentence
  • Concision: no throat-clearing
  • Register: matched to the reader
  • Reader-focus: the ask is easy to find
6.3 · The report · navigable, objective, actionable

Findings → conclusions → recommendations

PurposeThis report examines staff satisfaction with the shift-swap system.
MethodA survey was distributed to all 240 staff; 172 responses were received.
FindingsSwaps were found to take an average of four days to approve.
ConclusionsThese findings suggest that the system is slow and perceived as unfair.
RecommendationsIt is recommended that the paper form be replaced.
"Sadly, 58% of staff…" Can an opinion go in Findings? (No.) "…that the form be replaced": which lesson? (1.3, the subjunctive.)
6.3 · The cover letter · evidence + relevance

Let the achievement speak

Boastful"I am an outstanding communicator and a natural leader."
Over-humble"I don't have much experience, but I hope I might be suitable."
Confident"I introduced a troubleshooting guide that reduced repeat calls by 15 percent, and I now train every new hire to use it. Your posting emphasizes coaching…"
Can the reader check "outstanding"? (No: empty.) Can they check "15 percent"? (Yes: that's why it's confident.) "I'm sorry if my English is not perfect": keep it? (No: delete it.)
US Dear Ms. Carter, · Dear Hiring Manager, · Sincerely, · résumé · no photo, age or marital status
