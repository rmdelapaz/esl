2.1 · Mixed conditionals · diagnose each clause

The condition and the result live in different times

DirectionFormExample
past → presentif + had done → would doIf we had accepted the offer, we'd be twice the size now.
present → pastif + did / were → would have doneIf she weren't so cautious, she would have invested years ago.
"If she weren't so cautious…" Is she cautious now? (Yes, it's her character.) Did she invest? (No.) · "If I had studied medicine, I would have been a doctor now." Fix it? (I'd be: the result is now.)
2.1 · Same event, three shades · check the meaning

fails · should fail · were to fail

If-clauseProbability · register
If the merger fails…real · we're planning for it
If the merger should fail…unlikely · tentative
If the merger were to fail…hypothesis · boardroom formal
Which is least likely? (should / were to)
Which would you hear in a formal report? (were to)
Does the event change? (No: only the distance and the formality.)
Coming in 2.2: Should it fail… · Were it to fail…
2.1 · Alternatives to "if" · each adds a twist

Choose the exact condition

provided (that) · as long asonly on this conditionWe'll sign provided that the date holds.
on condition thatformal stipulationFunds released on condition that the audit continues.
unlessexcept ifWe'll leave at six unless you object.
supposing · what ifa spoken hypothesisSupposing the client says no?
otherwise · even ifor else · regardlessEven if it rains, we'll go.
TEST unless only if "except if" fits · I'd be amazed unless she won → if she didn't win
2.1 · in case ≠ if · before or when?

A precaution, taken now

in case it rains
a charger, in case the battery dies
a copy, in case it's lost
a spare key, in case…
"I'll take an umbrella in case it rains." When do I take it? (Now, before.) · "…if it rains." (Only when it rains.) · "Call me in case you need help"? (Really a condition → if you need help.)
2.1 · The decisive factor · + a NOUN

But for one detail…

FrameExample
but for (formal)But for your help, I would have failed.
if it weren't for (now)If it weren't for the traffic, the commute would be pleasant.
if it hadn't been for (then)If it hadn't been for one witness, the case would have collapsed.
Did you help? (Yes.) Did I fail? (No.)
Is there traffic now? (Yes.)
"But for you helped me"? (No: a noun → your help.)

2.2 · Inversion · front the trigger, flip the auxiliary

Never have I…

TriggerPlainInverted
NeverI had never seen…Never had I seen…
Little (no auxiliary)He little knew…Little did he know… (knew)
Not onlyShe not only resigned…Not only did she resign, but she also…
Only whenWe realized only when it ended.Only when it ended did we realize.
Under no circumstancesYou should not sign it.Under no circumstances should you sign it.
NO INVERSION I have never… (not fronted) · Only Sarah knew (a subject) · Here it comes · That book, I've never read
2.2 · Dropping "if" · so & such

Had · Were · Should

If…Inverted
If I had known…Had I known…
If she were to accept…Were she to accept…
If you should require…Should you require…
If it had not been for…Had it not been for…
"Hadn't I known…" (A question! → Had I not known.)
So great was the demand that the servers crashed.
Such was his anger that no one spoke.
In the doorway stood a stranger. (literary)
2.2 · Model · the speechwriter's edit

Weight or pomp?

Manager: "I have never worked with anyone like Grace."

Writer: "Never have I worked with anyone quite like Grace."

Manager: And "So calm was she that nobody panicked"?

Writer: That's three in a row. Keep two, and let the rest breathe.

A toast: "Rarely does a team…" (WEIGHT) · A text: "Never have I been so hungry." (POMP, unless it's a joke) · Four inversions in one paragraph? (POMP: one well-placed inversion beats three.)

2.3 · One fact, three shapes · check the meaning

Her attitude impressed me.

StructureSentenceAnswers the question…
it-cleftIt was her attitude that impressed me.Was it her CV? (spotlight, contrast)
what-cleftWhat impressed me was her attitude.What impressed you? (focus at the end)
frontingHer attitude, I could not fault.And her attitude? (links back)
SAY IT It was her attitude that impressed me · What impressed me ↗ | was her attitude ↘
2.3 · Frames · focus, correction, explanation

It was X, not Y, that…

Correct politelyActually, it was the timing, not the price, that worried us.
Later than expectedIt wasn't until Monday that we noticed.
ExplainWhat happened was (that) the server overheated.
MinimizeAll I want is a straight answer.
Give the reasonThe reason I called is that the invoice is wrong. (is because)
AGREE What I need is a break · are answers · What surprised us was the silence
2.3 · Information flow · the two principles

Given → new · light → heavy

Fronting links back:
We tried three strategies. The first we dismissed at once. The second looked promising but far too costly.

Extraposition moves the weight:
That you missed it is a pity. → It's a pity (that) you missed it.
Is "the first" new or known? (Known: we just heard "three strategies.")
Where does the heavy clause go? (To the end.)
"That book have I never read"? (No: fronting a topic doesn't invert.)
Three clefts in a row? (Mannered: restraint.)
2.3 · Model · setting the record straight

A calm, courteous correction

Reporter: So the tool library closed because it was losing money?

Spokesperson: Actually, it wasn't the money that closed it. It was space.

Reporter: But people say the tools were never returned.

Spokesperson: What the figures show is a return rate of ninety-six percent. All we're doing is moving them.

Which sentence corrects the reporter? (it wasn't the money that…) · Which words carry the stress? (money · space · ninety-six percent)
