Welcome to Advanced English

Welcome, and thank you for joining us. You already use English confidently at work, in your studies, and in daily life. This course is about the next layer: precision, nuance, and control. You'll learn to say exactly what you mean, with the right degree of certainty; to write essays, reports, and professional emails that readers take seriously; to read arguments critically; and to hold your own in a seminar or a meeting, choosing your register as deliberately as your words.

Some of you have just finished our B2 class, and some of you are joining us for the first time, often with years of professional experience behind you. That range of experience is one of this class's greatest resources. Everyone is welcome here.

⚖️ How we run seminars and discussions

Much of this course happens in discussion: seminars, debates, and a position-paper defense at the end. These ground rules keep them rigorous and respectful:

  • 👂Listen to the end, then build on what was said: "Building on Ana's point…"
  • 💛Challenge the idea, not the person. "I'd question that assumption," never "That's ridiculous."
  • 🤝Acknowledge, soften, then challenge. "I take your point, but I'm not sure the evidence supports it."
  • ⚖️Share the floor. If you've spoken a lot, hand over by name: "What's your view, Tomás?"
  • 🎭Assigned sides are normal. You may argue a position you don't hold. It's excellent practice, and nobody has to reveal a personal view.
  • 🚪Some topics stay outside. We argue about work, study, technology, health, and community life, not party politics or religion.

✍️ How we give peer feedback on writing

In our writing labs you'll often read a classmate's draft, and they'll read yours. Good peer review makes both writers better:

  • 🌟Start with a genuine strength, and be specific: "Your thesis is clear by the second sentence."
  • ❓Ask questions rather than issue verdicts. "What's the evidence for this claim?" works better than "This is wrong."
  • 🧭Big picture first, then detail. Comment on structure and argument before grammar and spelling.
  • ✌️Two suggestions are enough. Choose the two changes that would improve the text most.
  • 📝Comment on the writing, not the writer. "This paragraph could be tighter," not "You always write too much."
  • 🙏Receive feedback graciously. Say thank you, ask for clarification if you need it, and decide later which suggestions to use.

🏠 At home: free online lessons

📱 Advanced English (C1): free

Every class has a matching online lesson with the same grammar, vocabulary, model texts, and dialogues. It's your homework, and it lets you catch up if you miss a class. It works on a phone, a tablet, or a computer.

rays-esl.netlify.app/curriculum-c1/
  1. Open the website, or scan the QR code with your phone camera. 📷
  2. Choose the lesson your teacher gives you (for example, Lesson 2.2).
  3. Press the 🔊 buttons to hear the model sentences, dialogues, and talks. Then shadow them: say each line along with the audio, copying its stress, rhythm, and intonation. (No sound? Turn up the volume, or try the Chrome browser. The 🎧 Audio control lets you choose a US or UK voice and slow it down.)
  4. Do the exercises and the quiz, then the lesson project. Record your spoken projects, listen back, and write one thing to refine in your learning journal.

⏱ Aim for 40–60 minutes, 2–3 times a week, plus a little reading in English every day: quality journalism, a professional publication in your field, or a book slightly above your comfort level.

The online lessons use British spelling and some British vocabulary (analyse, organisation, pavement, flat). In class we use American English. Both are correct; the important thing at C1 is to choose one and use it consistently in a piece of writing.

🌟 You're ready for this

At B2 you learned to argue, persuade, and adapt. Over the next 13 weeks you'll learn to be precise: to place an event exactly in time, to calibrate how certain you sound, to structure an argument that stands up to a critical reader, and to shift register on purpose. The gains at this level can feel quiet, but they are exactly what makes people listen. By the end, you'll write a position paper and defend it in front of the class, in English. We're glad you're here. Welcome.
