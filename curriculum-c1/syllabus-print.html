Instructor-Led Course Syllabus

Advanced English (C1 Advanced)

A 13-week, 26-class English course for adults whose English is already fluent and who now need precision, nuance, and control, for university, professional life, or their own satisfaction. Twice a week for 90 minutes, you refine advanced grammar and vocabulary, read arguments critically, write essays, reports, and professional emails, follow lectures, lead seminars, and finish by writing and defending a position paper. Between classes, the free online lessons (with audio) are your homework.

Class Schedule

Two classes a week, 90 minutes each. Every class has one lesson from the online course, except the two assessment classes. The skills lessons (critical reading, essay and report writing, lectures and note-taking, seminars) run as workshops. Lessons 4.3 and 8.3 are review projects: a persuasive talk with a register shift at the halfway point, and a position paper and its defense at the end. ✓ = the can-do goal for that class.

Course Details

Prerequisites

  • CEFR B2. You can tell a layered story, argue a case and handle disagreement, switch between formal and informal English, follow fast natural speech, and write a formal email and a balanced argument.
  • Finished Confident English (B2)? You’re ready. New to our program? Take the shared placement test first.
  • Learners whose reading and writing lag behind their speaking are welcome. Every lesson has support options, and the tests have an adapted version.
  • No computer needed in class. A phone or computer helps for homework, but it isn’t required.

What’s Provided

  • A Student Workbook for all 24 lessons, in natural C1 English (the learner keeps it)
  • A welcome handout with seminar and peer-review ground rules, what to bring, and QR codes to the online lessons
  • For the teacher: a Teacher’s Guide (a 90-minute plan for every class), slides, flashcards, role-play and seminar cards, lecture scripts, games, and answer keys
  • Free access to the companion online course: Advanced English (C1), with 🔊 audio
  • A free learning-tools page that shows how to translate lessons into any language

Assessment

  • Every class: an “I can…” checklist and feedback on the main task, with no grades
  • Class 13 · Progress Test 1 (Modules 1–4): listening, vocabulary, grammar rewrites, reading, a formal recommendation, and an 8-minute pair speaking task
  • Class 26 · Final Test (all 8 modules): a mini-lecture with note-taking, critical reading, use of English, a structured essay or report (about 250 words), and a 10-minute individual interview with a long turn, a discussion with push-back, and a diplomatic register task
  • Rubrics follow the CEFR C1 descriptors. Results decide the next class, never who gets to celebrate
  • Tasks about your life can always use a “new identity” or an imagined example, and in debates you may argue an assigned side instead of your own view

Homework & the Online Course

  • After each class, do the matching online lesson at home: about 40–60 minutes, with 🔊 Listen buttons for model sentences, dialogues, and talks
  • Plus one workbook page, and a little reading in English every day
  • Missed a class? The online lesson covers the same language, so you can catch up
  • After the course: Mastery English (C2) completes the same free ladder. IELTS Academic Preparation and Business English can run alongside

What You’ll Be Able to Do

By the end of the course, learners reach CEFR C1 and can use English flexibly and effectively for social, academic, and professional purposes:

Place events precisely in time and signal exactly how certain you are
Make formal proposals with the subjunctive and analyze what-ifs across time
Use inversion, clefts, and fronting for emphasis and cohesion
Write in an academic register without losing clarity, and link a text smoothly
Soften claims, requests, and bad news diplomatically, and choose words by register
Compress clauses and use prepositions and academic vocabulary accurately
Read arguments critically and write structured essays, reports, and professional emails
Follow a lecture, take structured notes, and use stress and intonation to carry meaning
Take part in and steer a seminar, and keep talking coherently under pressure
Edit your own writing for concision, and write and defend a position paper
