B · New Identity Cards (6.1 · 6.2 · 6.3)

Any learner may use a card instead of their own life, in any task: no reason needed. In 6.1 they describe the place in Describe & Draw. In 6.2 they use the habits / plans / memory lines in Find Someone Who and the two-minute talk. In 6.3 the too / enough line gives an idea for the role-play or the review.

C · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

Read at a natural speed with pauses, twice: once for the main idea, once to check. Use weak articles (/ə/, /ðə/) and weak to /tə/: that is what learners hear outside class, and hearing the small words is today's skill. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give extra listening at home.

6.1-A · My new neighborhood (Workbook 6.1, Ex 1: write a, an, the or —)

I moved to a new neighborhood last month. It's a quiet place near the river. There's a small park at the end of my street, and the park has an old wooden bridge. Every morning I see an elderly man with a dog. The man always says hello, and the dog always wants to play. I usually walk to work, but when it rains, I go by bus. Life here is slower, and I love it.

Answers: 1 a · 2 a · 3 the · 4 a · 5 the · 6 an · 7 an · 8 a · 9 The · 10 the · 11 — (work) · 12 — (bus) · 13 — (life).

6.2-A · Which one did you hear? (Workbook 6.2, Ex 4: circle a or b)

Read the main sentence. For a second round, read the alternative in parentheses; the answer flips.

1. On the way to work, I stopped to buy a coffee. [alt: I stopped buying coffee.] → b

2. My dad stopped working last year. [alt: My dad stopped to work on his car.] → a

3. I remember turning off the stove. [alt: Remember to turn off the stove.] → a

4. Remember to call the dentist. [alt: I remember calling the dentist.] → b

5. I'll never forget visiting that beach. [alt: I forgot to visit that beach.] → a

6. I tried to fix the sink, but it was too difficult. [alt: Try fixing the sink with this tool.] → a

7. Your phone is slow? Try closing some apps. [alt: Try to close the apps. It's hard, I know.] → b

8. She forgot to bring her keys. [alt: She'll never forget bringing her keys to the new house.] → b

6.3-A · A restaurant review (Workbook 6.3, Ex 4: take notes)

I had dinner at Rosa's Kitchen on Saturday night with a few friends. Let me start with the good news. The food was so delicious that we stayed for three hours! The chicken soup was perfect, and the bread was fresh. Our waiter was such a friendly person. He remembered all of our names. Now, the problems. The restaurant was too small for a busy Saturday night. There were too many tables and not enough space to walk. We waited forty minutes for a table, and there was too much noise to have a real conversation. Also, most of the desserts were sold out when we ordered. Only a few were left. Would I go back? Yes, but on a quiet Tuesday! Three stars out of five.

Answers: Saturday night, a few friends · good: so delicious that they stayed three hours; such a friendly waiter · problems: too small; too many tables / not enough space; too much noise (+ 40-minute wait) · most of the desserts sold out, only a few left · 3 stars; a quiet Tuesday.

6.3-B · Happy or sad? (learners hold up 🙂 or 🙁)

1. I have a few friends at work. (🙂)

2. There's little milk left. We need to go shopping. (🙁)

3. Few people came to the party. (🙁)

4. Don't worry, we have a little time. (🙂)

5. I speak a little Spanish. (🙂)

6. He has little patience with slow drivers. (🙁)

7. A few students stayed after class to help. (🙂)

8. Very few buses run on Sundays. (🙁)

Say a few and a little naturally; don't stress the a. Hearing the tiny /ə/ is the skill. If learners struggle, read each sentence a third time.

H · Game — Find Someone Who… (C18)

Photocopy one per learner. Turn each line into a question — the -ing or to stays the same: "enjoys cooking for other people" → "Do you enjoy cooking for other people?" If the answer is yes, write the name and ask the extra question. One name per line. You can answer as your new identity card person, or say "Pass."

Find someone who…NameExtra question
1. enjoys cooking for other peopleWhat do you like cooking?
2. is learning to do something newWhat? Is it difficult?
3. has given up something (sugar, soda, TV…)Was it hard to stop?
4. can't stand waiting in lineWhere do you wait the most?
5. is good at fixing thingsWhat did you fix last?
6. is interested in learning another languageWhich one? Why?
7. is looking forward to something next monthWhat are you looking forward to?
8. remembers learning to ride a bike (or to swim)How old were you?
9. often forgets to charge their phoneWhat happens then?
10. would like to learn to drive (or drive better)Why?
11. avoids drinking coffee in the eveningWhat do you drink instead?
12. once stopped to help a strangerWhat happened?
Teacher note

Drill two questions on the board before the mingle: "Do you enjoy cooking…?" and "Do you remember learning…?" Line 3 uses the present perfect ("Have you given up…?"), which B1 learners know; line 12 uses the past ("Did you ever stop to help…?"). Line 8 is a childhood memory: remind the class that "Pass" is always fine. Report back with he/she: "Amir can't stand waiting in line. He avoids going to the bank on Fridays."

J · Complaints & Compliments — Role-Play Cards (C19)

Cut A from B. A is the customer; B is the staff member. A has three problems as prompts: turn each into a precise sentence (too… / not… enough / too many / too much). B apologizes and offers what is on their card. When the problem is fixed, A gives a compliment with so or such. These are roles: nobody needs to use a real complaint. Skip any card that feels uncomfortable.

K · Problem-Solving — Fix the Community Center (C19)

Groups of 3–4, 12–15 minutes. Read the members' complaints. You have $1,500. Choose the fixes; the total must be $1,500 or less. Then one person reports to the class: "Most of the complaints are about… The room is too cold, so we'll… There aren't enough…, so we're buying… We have a little money left for…"

What members say
  1. "There aren't enough chairs for the Friday meetings."
  2. "The big room is too cold in winter."
  3. "There are too many people in the Saturday English class."
  4. "There isn't enough parking, and I ride my bike: there's nowhere to lock it."
  5. "The computers are too old and too slow."
  6. "The lights in the hallway are so dark that I almost fell."
  7. "We have too little space for the kids' homework club."
  8. "The coffee is terrible!"

Teacher note

There is no single right answer: the discussion is the task. Listen for too / enough in the reasons, most / some / none of in the summary, and verb patterns (We decided to…, We suggest adding…, We can't afford to…). Safety (the lights) and "free" fixes are good talking points. This is a natural bridge to Module 7's discussion language.
