B · How It's Made Quiz — Inventions & Processes (4.1)

Rules (read them to the class)
  1. Teams of 3–4. The teacher reads a card and the three answers, twice.
  2. Teams talk for 30 seconds, in English.
  3. This round's speaker says the answer as a full passive sentence: "Paper was invented in China." The speaker changes every round.
  4. 1 point for the right fact · +1 point for a correct passive sentence (right be + past participle).
  5. After each answer the teacher reads the extra fact (small gray line); a learner repeats it.
  6. Last round: each team writes one question for the others: "Where is ___ grown?" "When was ___ built?" (any place or product).
1
Paper was first made in ___ .
A) Egypt · B) China · C) Greece
It was invented in China about 2,000 years ago.
2
Most of the world's coffee is grown in ___ .
A) Brazil · B) Canada · C) Norway
It is also grown in Vietnam, Colombia and Ethiopia.
3
The World Wide Web was invented by ___ in 1989.
A) Bill Gates · B) Tim Berners-Lee · C) Steve Jobs
It was invented at a science lab in Switzerland.
4
Cheese is made from ___ .
A) eggs · B) milk · C) rice
More than 1,000 kinds of cheese are made around the world.
5
The Eiffel Tower was built for ___ .
A) a world's fair · B) a king's wedding · C) the Olympics
It was finished in 1889.
6
Penicillin was discovered by ___ in 1928.
A) Marie Curie · B) Alexander Fleming · C) Albert Einstein
It is still used to treat infections today.
7
Potatoes were first grown in ___ .
A) South America · B) Ireland · C) Russia
They were grown in the Andes thousands of years ago.
8
Glass is made from ___ .
A) sand · B) salt · C) plastic
The sand is heated to a very high temperature.
9
The Mona Lisa was painted by ___ .
A) Picasso · B) Leonardo da Vinci · C) Van Gogh
It is kept in the Louvre in Paris.
10
The first modern Olympic Games were held in ___ in 1896.
A) Athens · B) Paris · C) London
They are held every four years.
11
Most of the world's maple syrup is made in ___ .
A) Mexico · B) Canada · C) Australia
Most of it is produced in Quebec.
12
Pizza as we know it today was first made in ___ .
A) Naples, Italy · B) New York · C) Athens
Today it is eaten all over the world.
13
Blue jeans were first made in ___ in the 1870s.
A) the US · B) Japan · C) Brazil
They were made for workers.
14
Chocolate is made from ___ .
A) coffee beans · B) cacao beans · C) sugar cane
Most cacao is grown in West Africa.
15
Silk was first made in ___ .
A) China · B) Italy · C) India
It is made by silkworms.
16
The ballpoint pen was invented by László Bíró, from ___ .
A) Hungary · B) Spain · C) Japan

C · Process Strips — How It's Made (4.1)

The strips are printed in the correct order (this is your key). Cut them, mix them, and put them in an envelope, one set per pair. Pairs put the steps in order, then take turns saying them with First… Then… After that… Finally…, without reading if they can. Stretch: add one relative clause ("The beans are roasted, which gives them their color.").

C1 · How chocolate is made (8 strips)

The cacao pods are picked from the trees.
The beans are taken out and left for a few days.
The beans are dried in the sun.
The dried beans are roasted.
The beans are ground into a thick paste.
Sugar and milk are added.
The chocolate is poured into molds and cooled.
The bars are wrapped and sold.

C2 · How tea is made (6 strips)

The young tea leaves are picked by hand.
The leaves are spread out and dried a little.
The leaves are rolled.
The rolled leaves are dried again.
The tea is packed into boxes or bags.
The tea is exported all over the world.

F · Detective Cards — 12 Cases (4.2)

Groups of 3–4, cards face down. A player reads a case aloud. Each other player makes one deduction at a different level (must · might · can't) and says the clue: "There are three newspapers at the door, so they can't be home." The reader adds one piece of advice: "We should…". Rule: deductions are about the card people, never about people in the room.

G · "Whose Bag?" — a Detective Case (4.2)

A bag was found on the bus. Put the four owner cards face up. Put the five clue cards face down in order. Turn over one clue at a time. After each clue, every player makes one deduction about one person: "It can't be Grace's, because…" After clue 5, the group writes: "It must be ___'s bag, because…" The best reasons win, not the fastest group.

L · Game — Module 1–4 Review Race (C12 warmer · C13 station)

Rules
  1. Groups of 3–4, one board, one die, a counter for each player (a coin, an eraser). Start on square 1.
  2. Roll and move. Follow the path: 1–6 left to right, 7–12 right to left, and so on (follow the numbers).
  3. Answer the square in a full sentence. The group says "Good!" or helps you fix it. If the group helps, stay on the square this time.
  4. Personal squares: answer about you, a friend, a card person, or invent. Anyone can say "Pass!" and move back 1.
  5. First to FINISH, or the player nearest to it when the teacher stops the game, wins.

Read at a natural but slow speed, with pauses. Read each script twice: once for the main idea, once to check. Use weak forms and contractions (it's made, it was built /wəz/, she must be), because that is what learners hear outside class. For dialogues, change your voice or ask a confident learner to read one part. The 🔊 buttons in the online lessons give learners extra listening at home.

4.1-A · How coffee is made (Workbook 4.1, Ex 1a: number the steps)

Coffee is one of the most popular drinks in the world. Most of it is grown in Brazil, but it is also grown in Vietnam, Colombia and Ethiopia. So, how is it made? First, the coffee cherries are picked. On many small farms, they are picked by hand. Then the skins are removed, and the beans are dried in the sun for about two weeks. After that, the dried beans are roasted at a very high temperature. This gives them their dark color and their smell. Next, the roasted beans are ground into a powder. Finally, the coffee is packed into bags, and it is sold in stores all over the world.

Answers: 1 picked · 2 skins removed · 3 dried · 4 roasted · 5 ground · 6 packed and sold (workbook order: 4 · 1 · 6 · 3 · 2 · 5).

4.1-B · News in brief (Workbook 4.1, Ex 1b: write the six passive verbs)

Good evening. Here is the local news. A new library was opened downtown yesterday. It was designed by students from the city college, and more than two thousand books were given by local families. In other news, a delivery truck was stolen on Main Street last night. It was found this morning near the river. Nobody was hurt.

Answers: was opened · was designed · were given · was stolen · was found · was hurt. Then ask: "Who stole the truck?" (We don't know: that's why it's passive.) "Who found it?" (Probably the police: obvious.)

4.2-A · Where's Sam? (Workbook 4.2, Ex 1: the clue and the modal)

Rosa: It's nine-thirty, and Sam's still not here. That's not like him. · Ken: Maybe he's stuck in traffic. · Rosa: No, he can't be stuck in traffic. He rides his bike to work. · Ken: True. Hmm… he might be still asleep. Maybe his alarm didn't ring. · Rosa: Or he may be sick. He looked really pale yesterday. · Ken: Wait, look at my phone. There's a message from him at nine-fifteen: "Leaving now! Sorry!" · Rosa: Oh good. So he must be on his way. Let's give him ten minutes.

Answers: stuck in traffic: he rides his bike → can't · still asleep: not like him, maybe the alarm → might · sick: he looked pale → may · on his way: the message "Leaving now!" → must. Follow-up: "What made Rosa sure at the end?" (New evidence.)

4.2-B · Dictation (C11 end or C12 warmer; read each sentence 3 times)

1. The lights are off, so they must be out. · 2. It can't be true. · 3. You don't have to pay. It's free. · 4. You look tired. You should go to bed early.

Then ask: "Which ones are guesses? (1, 2) Which one is a choice? (3) Which one is advice? (4)"

4.3-A · Is social media good for us? (Workbook 4.3, Ex 2: check the phrases)

Maya: If you ask me, social media has done more harm than good. · Dan: Really? I'm not sure I agree. What's the main reason you say that? · Maya: Well, the main reason is that people compare their lives to everyone else's. For example, my sister feels worse every time she scrolls. · Dan: I see what you mean, but on the other hand, it keeps families in touch. If I didn't have it, I'd never talk to my cousins in other countries. · Maya: That's a good point. Maybe it depends on how you use it. · Dan: Exactly. It seems to me that it's a tool. It isn't good or bad on its own. · Maya: I take your point. I'd still say we spend too much time on it, though!

Heard: If you ask me · I'm not sure I agree · the main reason is · For example · I see what you mean, but · on the other hand · That's a good point · Exactly · It seems to me that · I take your point. Not heard: In my opinion · I couldn't agree more. Maya changes her mind a little ("Maybe it depends…").

4.3-B · The bag on the train: the model story (Workbook 4.3, Ex 3a: find the structures)

When I was younger, I used to take the train to work every day. I didn't like it very much, because it was always crowded. One evening, a woman who was sitting across from me got off at her stop, but she left her bag on the seat. I looked inside. There was a laptop and a notebook, which had a name and a phone number on the first page. "She must be really worried," I thought. I called the number, and she answered right away. She told me that she was on her way to a job interview the next morning and that she needed the laptop. I took the bag to the station office, and it was picked up an hour later. A week later, a card was delivered to my house. It said, "I got the job! Thank you!" I've taken that train hundreds of times since then, but I've never found anything else. If I found a bag again, I'd do exactly the same thing. And I wish more stories were like that one.

Structures: used to take · a woman who was sitting / a notebook, which had · She told me that she was… · She must be really worried · it was picked up / a card was delivered · I've taken… since then / I've never found · If I found…, I'd do… · I wish more stories were… Use it again on slide 17: one sentence per step of the story road.
