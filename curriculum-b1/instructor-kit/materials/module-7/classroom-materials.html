B · Discussion Topic Cards (7.1)

One card per group per round. Every card has two sides and a few ideas, so nobody has to invent a topic. All topics are everyday and community-level. Keep politics, religion and immigration out of this activity. Read the card aloud to groups with literacy learners.

Topic 1 · public transport

"Buses and trains in our town should be free for everyone."

For: less traffic · cleaner air · helps people with low incomes

Against: who pays? · buses could get too crowded · money for roads and schools

Topic 2 · work

"Working from home is better than working in an office."

For: no commute · quiet · more time with family

Against: lonely · hard to stop working · some jobs can't be done at home

Topic 3 · schools

"Phones should not be allowed in schools."

For: fewer distractions · more talking with friends · less cyberbullying

Against: parents need to contact children · phones can help learning · safety

Topic 4 · where to live

"Life in the city is better than life in the country."

For: more jobs · public transport · stores and hospitals nearby

Against: expensive · noisy · less nature and fresh air

Topic 5 · shopping

"Online shopping is better than shopping in stores."

For: fast · cheaper · you can shop at night

Against: you can't try things on · returns are difficult · local stores close

Topic 6 · community

"Stores should stay open 24 hours a day."

For: good for night-shift workers · more jobs · convenient

Against: night work is hard for staff · expensive for stores · safety

Topic 7 · food

"Every school should teach students to cook."

For: healthier food · saves money · a skill for life

Against: not enough time · families can teach it · kitchens are expensive

Topic 8 · home

"Children should help with chores at home every day."

For: they learn responsibility · helps busy parents · teamwork

Against: they need time for homework and play · it can cause arguments

C · Discussion Role Cards, Talking Tokens & Phrase Strip (7.1)

One role set per group. Give the observer or chair role to your most talkative learners in round 1. Rotate roles clockwise in round 2. Keep the tokens: they return in Module 8 (8.3 panel discussion).

Role · Chair 🎙️

You make the discussion fair.

  • Start: "Today's question is… Let's go around. What do you think, ___?"
  • Ask quiet people: "___, how do you feel about this?"
  • Stop interruptions: "Hold that thought. Let's hear from ___ first."
  • Finish: "Thank you, everyone."
Role · Timekeeper ⏱️

You watch the clock.

  • Round: 2 minutes. Discussion: 6 minutes.
  • Say: "Two minutes left." · "One more comment, please." · "Time's up!"
  • You also give your opinion.
Role · Observer 👀

You listen. You don't talk.

  • Play Discussion Phrase Bingo: tick a phrase when you hear it.
  • Notice: Did everyone speak? Did anyone interrupt?
  • At the end, read your bingo lines to the group.
Role · Summarizer 📝

You report to the class.

  • Take short notes: each person's opinion.
  • Report: "Most of us think… because… But ___ doesn't agree. She thinks…"
  • Or: "We agreed to disagree!"

Talking tokens — cut out (3 per learner). Or use coins, paper clips or beans. Put one in the middle each time you speak. No tokens left? Wait until everyone has used theirs, then take 3 back.

D · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

7.1-A · Phones at school: a parents' meeting (Workbook 7.1, Ex 1: change your voice for each speaker, or use 2–3 confident learners)

Ms. Lopez: Thanks for coming, everyone. Tonight's question is simple: should students be allowed to use phones at school? What do you think, David? · David: In my opinion, phones should stay in students' bags during class. They're a big distraction. · Grace: I couldn't agree more. My son checks his phone every five minutes. · Amir: I see your point, but I'm not so sure a total ban is a good idea. Parents sometimes need to contact their children, for example if the bus is late. · David: That's a good point. Maybe phones could be allowed at lunch, then? · Grace: Hmm. To be honest, I think lunch is a time to talk to friends, not to look at a screen. · Ms. Lopez: How do you feel about that, Amir? · Amir: I suppose it depends on the age. Little kids don't need phones, but teenagers are different. · Ms. Lopez: So it seems to me that we all agree on one thing: no phones in class. · David: Exactly. · Grace: Absolutely. · Amir: Yes, I agree with that.

Answers: 1 David · 2 Grace, "I couldn't agree more." · 3 parents need to contact their children (e.g. the bus is late) · 4 age · 5 no phones in class · softeners: maybe, to be honest, I suppose. Follow-up: "Find one phrase for each board column."

7.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. In my opinion, the buses are too slow. · 2. I see your point, but I'm not so sure. · 3. I couldn't agree more. · 4. What do you think about working from home?

7.2-A · Ana calls the building manager (Workbook 7.2, Ex 1, Call A)

Mr. Park: Maple Street Apartments, Tom Park speaking. · Ana: Hello, this is Ana Silva in apartment 4B. I'm sorry to bother you, but I'm afraid there's a problem with my kitchen sink. It's been leaking since Monday. · Mr. Park: Oh, I'm sorry to hear that. Is there water on the floor? · Ana: Yes, a little. I've put a bucket under it, but it's getting worse. · Mr. Park: OK. I'll send someone to look at it. Would Thursday morning work? · Ana: I'm afraid I'm at work then. Would it be possible to come on Thursday after five, or on Saturday? · Mr. Park: Let me check… Saturday at ten is fine. · Ana: Great, thank you. Could you give me a reference number, please? · Mr. Park: Sure. It's R-2-5-7. · Ana: R-2-5-7. Thank you. So someone is coming on Saturday at ten. · Mr. Park: That's right. Have a good day.

Answers: 4B · the kitchen sink is leaking · since Monday · a bucket · Saturday at 10 · R-257. Point out Ana's final line: she confirms the arrangement.

7.2-B · Mr. Osei calls the power company (Workbook 7.2, Ex 1, Call B)

Kevin: Thank you for calling City Power. My name is Kevin. How can I help you? · Mr. Osei: Hi, Kevin. I'm calling about my electricity bill. I think I was charged twice this month. · Kevin: I'm sorry to hear that. Could I have your account number, please? · Mr. Osei: Yes, it's four-four-oh-nine-one-eight. · Kevin: Thank you. Yes, I can see two payments of eighty-six dollars. But I'm afraid I can't give refunds. · Mr. Osei: I understand, but the second payment was a mistake. Would it be possible to speak to a supervisor, please? · Kevin: Of course. Please hold for a moment. · Maria: Hello, Mr. Osei. This is Maria, the supervisor. I've looked at your account, and you're right. You were charged twice. I do apologize. We'll refund the eighty-six dollars in five to seven days. · Mr. Osei: Thank you, I appreciate it. Could you send me an email to confirm that? · Maria: Absolutely. You'll get it today.

Answers: 1 charged twice · 2 $86 · 3 Kevin couldn't give refunds · 4 in 5–7 days · 5 an email to confirm. Ask: "Was Mr. Osei angry with Kevin?" (No. "I understand, but…")

7.2-C · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

7.3-A · Samuel's interview (Workbook 7.3, Ex 1: a learner can read Ms. Grant)

Ms. Grant: Thanks for coming in, Samuel. Tell me a little about yourself. · Samuel: Thank you for having me. I'm currently working as a delivery driver, and I've been doing that for about two years. Before that, I worked in a supermarket, so I have experience in stocking shelves and dealing with customers. Now I'm looking for a full-time position with more responsibility, which is why I applied for this job. · Ms. Grant: Why do you want to work in our warehouse? · Samuel: Well, I like being active, and I'm good at organizing things. I've also heard that your company offers forklift training, and I'd love to learn. · Ms. Grant: Can you tell me about a time you solved a problem at work? · Samuel: Let me think for a moment. Yes. Last winter, a snowstorm closed a road on my route. I had fifteen deliveries to finish before six. So I called my supervisor, checked a map app and planned a new route, and I phoned my customers to tell them I'd be a little late. In the end, I delivered every package that day, and two customers wrote nice reviews. · Ms. Grant: Great. And what's your biggest weakness? · Samuel: I used to get nervous speaking in meetings. But I've been taking an English class, and I've been practicing speaking up. It's getting easier. · Ms. Grant: That's good to hear. Do you have any questions for us? · Samuel: Yes. What does the training look like for new staff?

Answers: 1 delivery driver, about two years · 2 a supermarket · 3 likes being active · good at organizing · forklift training · 4 S snowstorm closed a road · T 15 deliveries before 6 · A called supervisor, planned a new route, phoned customers · R delivered every package, two nice reviews · 5 nervous speaking in meetings; English class, practicing speaking up · 6 what the training looks like for new staff. Filler: "Let me think for a moment."

7.3-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

E · Complaint Role-Play Cards — Counter & Phone (7.2)

Cut A (customer) from B (staff). All names and numbers are fictional. B's card says how helpful to be. On cards marked ⬆️, B first refuses; A escalates politely; then B reads the Flip part and plays the manager. Swap roles after each card. Put cards 1, 2 and 6 at the 🏪 counter and cards 3, 4 and 5 at the 📞 phone station (chairs back to back).

1 · 🏪 Counter · A · customer

A clothing store

You bought a jacket here last Saturday ($65). Today the zipper broke. You have the receipt. You want a refund, not store credit.

1 · 🏪 Counter · B · sales assistant ⬆️

A clothing store

Apologize. Ask for the receipt. Then say: "I'm afraid I can only give you store credit."

Flip → Manager: Apologize. Look at the jacket. Agree it's faulty. Give a full refund.
2 · 🏪 Counter · A · customer

A restaurant

You ordered chicken with rice 30 minutes ago. The food arrived cold, and it's the wrong side dish (fries, not rice). You're in a hurry: your lunch break ends in 20 minutes.

2 · 🏪 Counter · B · server

A restaurant

Be very helpful. Apologize, offer to bring a new hot plate with rice in 5 minutes, and offer a free drink or dessert.

3 · 📞 Phone · A · tenant

Your building manager

Apartment 12C. There's been no hot water since yesterday morning. You have two small children. You need it fixed today or tomorrow. Ask for a reference number. Confirm the day and time.

3 · 📞 Phone · B · building manager

Maple Street Apartments

Answer: "Maple Street Apartments, how can I help?" Apologize. First offer "next Tuesday." If A asks politely for sooner, find a plumber for tomorrow at 9. Give reference number H-418.

4 · 📞 Phone · A · customer

Your internet company

Account 55-2071. Your bill is $30 higher this month. It says "Movie Package," but you never ordered it. You want the charge removed and the $30 refunded.

4 · 📞 Phone · B · agent ⬆️

FastNet Internet

Ask for the account number and the name (ask A to spell it). Say: "I'm afraid I can't remove charges."

Flip → Supervisor: Apologize. Remove the package. Refund $30 on the next bill. Offer an email confirmation.
5 · 📞 Phone · A · customer

A delivery company

Tracking number TK-7730. The website says your package was delivered yesterday, but it's not at your door, and your neighbors don't have it. It's a birthday present.

5 · 📞 Phone · B · customer service

Speedy Parcel

Apologize. Ask for the tracking number and the address. Say you'll check with the driver today and call back by 5 p.m. If it's lost, you'll send a replacement.

6 · 🏪 Counter · A · customer

A supermarket customer service desk

Look at your receipt: you were charged twice for a bag of rice ($12.99). You want the extra money back.

6 · 🏪 Counter · B · cashier ⬆️

A supermarket

Look at the receipt. Say: "I'm afraid I can't give refunds at this register."

Flip → Store manager: Apologize. Agree the rice was scanned twice. Give $12.99 back in cash.

Teacher note — good tips vs. unhelpful tips

Model feedback before round 1. Helpful tips are specific and doable: "Add a Result to your story." · "Say 'for three years,' not 'since three years.'" · "Look up from your notes." Unhelpful tips are vague or personal: "Speak better." · "Your accent…" Start with the great thing. Feedback is about the answer, never the person.

H · Game — Discussion Phrase Bingo (C20)

The observer in each discussion group plays. Tick a square when you hear the phrase (any speaker). A line (across, down or diagonal) = "Bingo!", but say it quietly and wait until the discussion ends. Cards A and B have different squares; give different cards to observers in neighboring groups. Also good as a C21 warmer: pairs discuss an annoyance statement while a third learner plays.
