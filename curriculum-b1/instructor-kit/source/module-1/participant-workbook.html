⏱️ Time words — which tense do they go with?

🔚 Finished time → past simple➡️● Connected to now → present perfect
yesterday · last week · in 2019 · two days ago · when I was a child · on Fridayever · never · today · this week · so far · just · already · yet · still · for · since
Grammar box
🔚 Past simplea finished action at a finished timeI saw that movie last Friday.
➡️● Present perfecta past action connected to now (experience, news, result, still true)I've seen that movie three times.
⚠️ Golden ruleIf you say when (a finished time), use the past simple.I've seen him yesterday. ❌ → I saw him yesterday. ✅
for+ a length of timeI've known her for ten years / for a long time.
since+ a start pointI've known her since 2014 / since I was a child.
justa very short time agoI've just finished lunch.
alreadysooner than expectedShe's already left.
yetquestions and negatives, at the endHave you eaten yet? · I haven't eaten yet.
stillnot happened (and I'm surprised)I still haven't packed.

Tell a story like this: open with the present perfect (no time) → give the details in the past simple.

"I've been to Japan. I went in 2019 with two friends. We visited Tokyo and Kyoto. It was amazing."

been = went and came back. gone = went and is still there. "She's gone to Japan." = She is in Japan now.

🔚 Finished time (past simple)➡️● Connected to now (present perfect)

My story. Write 4–5 sentences about an experience or some news. Open with the present perfect (no time). Then give the details in the past simple: when, where, who with, how it was. Real, a new identity card, or imagined: your choice.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

A: Have you ever run a marathon?

B: Yes, I have! I ran one last year in Chicago.

A: Wow. How was it?

B: Really hard, but great. I trained for six months before it.

A: I've never done anything like that. I've just started going to the gym, though!

B: That's a good start. How long have you been a member?

A: Since Monday. I went twice this week, and I haven't missed a class yet!

🗣️ Read it twice, then change the experience (climb a mountain, cook for 50 people, sing in public…) and read it again.

👀 Clues: out of breath · exhausted · covered in flour · dirty hands · wet hair · red eyes · muddy boots

latelyrecentlyall dayall weekfor agessince JanuaryWhat have you been up to?
Grammar box
✅ YesI / you / we / they have been + -ing · he / she / it has been + -ingI've been working. · She's been studying.
❌ Nohaven't / hasn't been + -ingThey haven't been sleeping well lately.
❓ QuestionHave / Has + subject + been + -ing?Have you been waiting long? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
⏳ How long?How long have you been + -ing?How long have you been learning to drive?
⏳ Use 1: still goingstarted in the past, still happening nowI've been studying English for three years.
👀 Use 2: a result nowa recent activity; we can see the resultHis hands are dirty. He's been fixing the car.
🔢 How many? → simple⏳ How long? → continuous
I've written three emails.I've been writing emails all morning.
We've painted the kitchen. (the job is done)We've been painting the kitchen. (that's why it's messy)

⚠️ No -ing with these verbs (states, not activities): know · like · love · hate · want · need · believe · understand · be · have (= own) · belong.
I've known her for years. ✅   I've been knowing her for years. ❌

B: A little, about ten minutes. So, what have you been doing lately?

A: Oh, I've been painting my apartment. That's why I look exhausted!

B: Nice! I've been learning to cook, actually. I've made about six new dishes this month.

A: Six? That's impressive. How long have you been doing that?

Used to & Would — How Life Has Changed

Class C3
📖 🗣️ New words — then and now

  • send emails and messages
  • a cell phone
  • watch TV together
  • go to the bank
  • shop at the market
  • the supermarket
  • a paper ticket
  • write by hand
  • take the train
back thenin the pastwhen I was youngerevery summeron Sundaysthese daysnowadaysnot … anymore
Grammar box
✅ Yessubject + used to + base verbPeople used to write letters. · There used to be a bakery here.
❌ Nodidn't use to + base verbI didn't use to like coffee.
❓ QuestionDid + subject + use to + base verb?Did you use to have a bike? — Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
🔁 wouldsubject + would + base verb (repeated actions)Every summer we would visit my grandparents.

✏️ Spelling: after did / didn't, no d: didn't use to · Did you use to…?   🗣️ Say: used to = /ˈjustə/ ("YOO-stuh")

used towould
🔁 Actions (visit, walk, cook, watch…)✅ We used to visit them.✅ We would visit them.
🏠 States (live, like, have, be, know)✅ I used to live in Rome.❌ I would live in Rome.

💡 Not sure? used to is always safe. Use would when the past time is already clear ("When I was a child… every Sunday…").

✏️ Exercise 5 — Then and now

Choose a topic: 📱 phones · 🛒 shopping · 💼 work · 🚌 getting around · 📺 free time · 🍲 food · 💵 money · 📚 learning. Write 5 sentences: two with used to, one with would, one about now, and your opinion. You can write about people in general, a place you know, or your own life.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

A: Do you remember the old neighborhood? There used to be a bakery right on the corner.

B: Of course! We would go there every Saturday after soccer. I used to love their cinnamon rolls.

A: And old Mr. Alvarez — he would always give us the broken cookies for free.

B: He did! I didn't use to appreciate it back then. The whole street used to feel like family.

A: It's all apartments now. It doesn't feel the same, does it?
