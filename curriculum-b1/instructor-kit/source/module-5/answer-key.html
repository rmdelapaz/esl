Module 5 — Narrating Past & Future: Answers

Answers to every Module 5 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, notes for the classroom materials (What Had Happened?, the Worst Day Ever story chain, Life in 2040), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 5.1–5.3Classes C14–C169 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At B1, mark meaning first, then form. Tense choice in stories and futures often has more than one good answer. Mark a choice wrong only when it changes or blurs the meaning: the past simple where the order becomes unclear ("When I arrived, the train left" = I saw it leave), a continuous for a sudden event, or will in a time clause. Contractions and full forms are both correct (had left / 'd left; will be / 'll be). A small spelling slip in a participle ("forgoten") is a success with a note. American English is the model; British forms (film, cinema, holiday, lift, cancelled, practise, at the weekend) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

My goal after Progress Test 1

Personal; not marked. Read the goals after C14 (if learners are happy to share the workbook) so you can support them. A good goal names one area and one small, regular action ("I will write three sentences with if every Sunday"). If a goal is very big ("speak perfect English"), help the learner make it smaller in a private moment.

5.1 · Past Perfect — The Earlier Past (C14)

Exercise 1 — Why was Sam late? (Script 5.1-A)
He forgot to charge his phone.He woke up at 7:30.He left his ID card at home.He got to the bus stop.He waited twenty minutes.He arrived at the office.
123456

So the numbers next to the workbook lines (in workbook order) are: 5 · 1 · 6 · 2 · 4 · 3. The trap: Sam says the ID card last ("I realized I'd left my ID card at home"), but it happened before he left the house. Spoken sentence: "When he got to the bus stop, the bus had already left." (Also correct: had left, 'd gone.)

Exercise 2 — Make the past perfect
  1. had already started
  2. hadn't slept (had not slept)
  3. had gone
  4. had never eaten
  5. had lost
  6. Had you met him before the party? — No, I hadn't.

All positive forms may use 'd (the show'd already started is heard but rarely written; I'd, she'd, they'd are normal). Wrong: "had already start" · "hadn't sleep" · "had went" · "had never ate" · "Did you had met…?" · "No, I didn't."

Exercise 3 — Which came first?
  1. woke up … had made
  2. couldn't … had locked
  3. had read … called (also correct: read … called, because after already shows the order)
  4. had started … found
  5. was … had never driven

Item 4 is the one pairs argue about: the movie started first (earlier → had started), we found our seats later. Wrong: "had woken up … made" (the order is reversed), "hadn't could," "had never drove."

Exercise 4 — Join the two events
  1. By the time I got there, the store had closed. / The store had closed by the time I got there.
  2. After I had finished my work, I went home. / I went home after I'd finished my work. (Also accept: After I finished my work, I went home.)
  3. She had never seen the ocean before she visited Florida.
  4. He couldn't log in because he had forgotten his password.

Either clause order is correct. Wrong: both verbs in the past perfect ("By the time I had got there, the store had closed"); "had forgot"; "She never had seen" (word order: had never seen).

Exercise 5 — Do we need the past perfect?

1 A (a simple list in order: no jump back) · 2 B (the bank closed before I got there; A means I saw it close) · 3 B (not eating = the earlier cause) · 4 B (no later past moment; "born" is just the past simple).

Item 2 A is not "wrong English," it's a different story: it means the bank closed at the moment I arrived. Use it for a good CCQ.

Dialogue & speaking

Past perfects in the dialogue (6): had already left · 'd booked · had canceled · 'd saved · 'd never had to · had closed. Why B missed the flight: "Because the taxi never came: the app had canceled it."

My story (open). Look for: the moment in the past simple, 6 past perfect sentences for the earlier actions, by the time, already or just between had and the participle, never … before, and one because … had …. Model: When I got to the café, my friends had already ordered. I'd told them I was coming at six, but I'd written the wrong time in my phone. By the time I sat down, they'd eaten all the fries! I was embarrassed because I had never been late for Ana's birthday before. She'd just opened her presents. In the end, we all laughed.

Self-check
Q1
"When I got home, my sister ___ dinner." (cooking before I got home)
A) cooked
B) had cooked
C) was cooking
Why: the earlier, finished action takes the past perfect. A suggests she cooked after I arrived; C means she was in the middle of cooking.
Q2
Which sentence is correct?
A) The bus had already leave when we arrived.
B) The bus didn't had left when we arrived.
C) The bus had already left when we arrived.
Why: had + past participle (left), with already between them. A uses the base verb; B mixes did with had.
Q3 · new
Lena was nervous on the plane. She ___ before.
A) has never flown
B) had never flown
C) had never fly
Why: a first-time experience before a past moment → had never + participle. A (present perfect) connects to now, but the story is in the past; C uses the base verb.
Errors you will hear most (5.1)

"When I arrived, the train already left." · "She had forget / had went." · "Had you did…?" / "Did you had…?" · "When I had arrived, the train had left." (both verbs in the past perfect) · "I had been born in…" (no later past moment) · "Yes, I'd." Correct the participle and the did mix-ups every time; let a past simple + already go in fluent speech if the meaning is clear, and recast.

5.2 · Narrative Tenses — Telling a Story (C15)

Exercise 1 — Dev's worst day (Script 5.2-A)
  1. At ten o'clock (10 a.m.).
  2. It was sunny: the sun was shining (and the birds were singing).
  3. It broke down: it stopped suddenly.
  4. His umbrella.
  5. He was soaked (completely wet), and he had coffee on his new shirt.
  6. The manager laughed, they talked for an hour, and Dev got the job.

Verbs: background: was shining, were singing, was feeling, was drinking, was running · action: stopped, got off, started, went, laughed, said, talked, got · flashback: had prepared, had bought, had broken down, 'd left. Any correct one is fine.

Exercise 2 — Background, action or flashback?
  1. was snowing … left
  2. was cooking … called
  3. had cut
  4. were sitting … ran off
  5. had finished
  6. opened … found

Also correct: 1 snowed … left is possible but loses the background feel (accept with a note). 4 ran away. Wrong: "While Tom cooked…" is understood but weak (keep while + continuous); "a dog was running off" for the sudden event; "she cut her hair" in 3 (it happened before; accept only if the learner explains a same-day sequence).

Exercise 3 — Add the connectors

1 First · 2 Then · 3 While · 4 Suddenly · 5 By the time · 6 In the end.

Accept After that in 2 if a learner uses it. Wrong: "At the end" in 6 (→ In the end); "When" in 3 is possible English, but the box asks for while.

Exercise 4 — Label the verbs

was falling C · sat S · had had P · was reading C · rang S · opened S · found S · had left P · turned out S.

had had surprises learners: the first had is the helper, the second is the participle of have. Say it naturally: "She'd had a long day."

Exercise 5 — Order, then write

Workbook order: we finally found the movie theater 3 · we decided to see a movie 1 · the movie had already started 4 · we were driving around, looking for parking 2 · in the end we still enjoyed it 5.

Model story: First, we decided to see a movie. We were driving around for ages, looking for parking, and by the time we finally found the movie theater, the movie had already started. In the end, though, we still enjoyed it. Accept any story with the three tenses doing their jobs and two or more connectors.

Speaking — story chain & story map

Open. Check each step's tense: ① scene = past continuous (+ It was…) · ② flashback = past perfect · ③ problem = suddenly + past simple · ④ then + past simple, or while + past continuous · ⑤ by the time + past simple, then past perfect · ⑥ in the end + past simple. Listen for listener reactions and one follow-up question. A story that ends well or funny is the target; don't push for "worse" events.

Self-check
Q1
"I ___ home when I ___ a loud noise."
A) was walking … heard
B) walked … heard
C) was walking … was hearing
Why: a long background action (past continuous) interrupted by a short event (past simple). B loses the "in progress" meaning; C makes the sudden noise continuous.
Q2
"By the time the guests arrived, she ___ the cake."
A) baked
B) was baking
C) had baked
Why: by the time points to something already finished before the later moment → past perfect.
Q3 · new
We were watching TV. Suddenly, the lights ___ out.
A) were going
B) had gone
C) went
Why: suddenly + a short main event = past simple. A makes it a long background; B would mean the lights were already out before we started watching.
Errors you will hear most (5.2)

"Suddenly the phone was ringing." · "While I walked home, …" · "I walking home when…" (no was) · "I was knowing him." · the past perfect for every verb in the story · "At the end, everything was fine." · he/she mix-ups with named characters · "Actually" meaning "now." In the story chain, note these and correct later; in Ex 2–4, correct straight away.

5.3 · Future Forms & Future Continuous (C16)

Exercise 1 — Nora and Sam plan the week (Script 5.3-A)
  1. A small party for Leo's birthday, after work (at about six, in the break room).
  2. He's seeing the dentist.
  3. To pick up the cake from the bakery.
  4. As soon as she gets the order number.
  5. At home: she'll be working from home.
📅 arrangementI'm having a small party · I'm seeing the dentist at four
🧠 planI'm going to order a cake
🤝 offerI'll pick it up for you!
will be + -ingWill you be using the meeting room? · I'll be working from home · we'll be eating cake
time clauseas soon as I get it · until the party starts
Exercise 2 — Choose the best future form
  1. 'll close (will close)
  2. 're flying (are flying)
  3. 's going to fall
  4. 'll be lying
  5. 'm going to sign up
  6. will be ('ll be)

Also correct: 2 're going to fly (a plan; less "fixed," but fine) · 5 'm signing up if the learner means it's already booked · 6 is going to be (acceptable as a prediction). Wrong: 1 "I'm going to close" is correct English but not a decision made now (discuss, don't mark wrong in speech) · 3 "will fall" is weaker with visible evidence · 4 "will lie" / "'ll lying" · "I sign up next month."

Exercise 3 — Present or will?
  1. 'll text … land
  2. calls
  3. go
  4. finishes … 'll go
  5. won't buy … saves

Also correct: 1 'm going to text · 4 're going to go. Wrong: any will in the time clause ("as soon as I will land," "until she will save") · missing -s: "until the doctor call," "when the class finish."

Exercise 4 — Priya's day tomorrow
  1. At 10:00, she'll be teaching a computer class.
  2. At 1:00, she'll be having lunch with Ana.
  3. At 3:00, she'll be meeting a client.
  4. At 6:30, she'll be cooking dinner.
  5. "Not really. She'll be watching a movie."

Accept Priya will be… and full forms. Wrong: "she'll teaching," "she will be teach," "she is teaching at 10:00" (fine as an arrangement but not the target here: praise, then ask for will be -ing). "This time tomorrow, I'll be…" sentences are open.

Exercise 5 — Make it polite
  1. Will you be coming to the meeting on Monday?
  2. Will you be staying for dinner?
  3. Will you be working late on Friday?

Wrong: "Will you be come…?" · "Are you will be…?" · "Will be you…?"

Dialogue & speaking

Dialogue find: arrangements I'm having a few people over · I'm meeting a client · decisions with I'll: I'll text you · I'll make a dessert (also I'll call you) · plan I'm going to try that new shop · will be + -ing we'll all be sitting · time clause as soon as I finish work.

Life in 2040 (open). Look for: 3+ different future forms, one will be + -ing, one time clause with the present, a reason for each prediction, and polite agreeing / disagreeing. Model: In 2040, most people will probably be working from home three days a week. I don't think offices will disappear, though, because people like to meet. When cars drive themselves, teenagers won't need a license. Cash might disappear, but I'm not sure. My grandma still pays for everything with cash!

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) I'll phone you when I will get home.
B) I'll phone you when I get home.
C) I phone you when I get home.
Why: present simple in the time clause, will in the main clause. A has will after when; C has no future in the main clause.
Q2
"This time tomorrow, I ___ across the ocean."
A) will fly
B) will be flying
C) am flying
Why: the flight is in progress at that future moment → future continuous. A suggests the flight starts then. C is heard in casual speech for a booked trip, but it doesn't show "in the middle of it" (accept in conversation, not in this item).
Q3 · new
Don't call me at 9 tonight. I ___ my English homework.
A) will do
B) 'll be doing
C) do
Why: at 9 I'll already be in the middle of the homework → will be + -ing. A means I'll start at 9; C is not future.
Errors you will hear most (5.3)

"I'll call you when I will arrive." · "I call you tomorrow." · "I'll flying / I'll be fly." · "After ten years, cars will fly." (→ in ten years) · "I will a new phone." (German/Dutch want) · "Will you be come…?" · "In 2040 years." (→ in 2040). In the discussion, correct only what blocks meaning; save the rest for feedback.
