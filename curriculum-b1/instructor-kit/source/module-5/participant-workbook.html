5.1

The Earlier Past — "It had already left"

Class C14
📖 🗣️ New words — what had happened?
  • the train had left
  • lose your keys
  • forget your passport
  • leave your umbrella at home
  • the battery had died
  • the store had closed
  • miss a flight
  • 💡 realize / find out

📌 Past participles (the same as the present perfect):

go → goneleave → leftlose → lostforget → forgotteneat → eatensee → seentake → takenwrite → writtenclose → closedstart → started

⏪ Signal words: by the time · before · after · because · already · just · never … before

Grammar box — the past perfect
✅ YesI / You / He / She / We / They had ('d) + past participleShe'd gone home. · They'd left.
❌ Nohadn't + past participleWe hadn't met before.
❓ QuestionHad + you + past participle?Had you seen the email?
💬 AnswerYes, I had. / No, I hadn't.(not "Yes, I'd.")
📍 Where?had + already / just / never + participleThe bus had already gone.

Two past actions? The earlier one → past perfect. The later one → past simple.

① earlier② later③ now
the train had leftI arrivedI'm telling you the story

When I arrived, the train had already left. = The train had already left when I arrived.

⚠️ A simple list in order does not need it: I got up, had breakfast and left. ✅

6. you (meet) him before the party? — No, I .

✏️ Exercise 3 — Which came first?

Write both verbs. The earlier action → past perfect. The later action → past simple.

1. By the time I (wake up), my kids (make) breakfast.

2. We (not / can) get in because someone (lock) the door.

3. After she (read) the message, she (call) me.

4. The movie (start) when we (find) our seats.

5. He (be) nervous because he (never / drive) in the snow.

✏️ Exercise 4 — Join the two events

Use the word in parentheses. Put the earlier action in the past perfect.

Example: The concert ended. Then we arrived. (by the time) → By the time we arrived, the concert had ended.

1. The store closed. Then I got there. (by the time)

2. I finished my work. Then I went home. (after)

3. She never saw the ocean. Then she visited Florida. (before)

4. He forgot his password. So he couldn't log in. (because)

📖 Exercise 5 — Do we need the past perfect?

Circle the better sentence: A or B.

1. A) I got up, had a shower and made coffee.   B) I got up, had had a shower and had made coffee.

2. A) When I got to the bank, it closed.   B) When I got to the bank, it had closed.

3. A) I was hungry because I didn't eat all day.   B) I was hungry because I hadn't eaten all day.

4. A) My grandmother had been born in 1950.   B) My grandmother was born in 1950.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

A: You look exhausted. What happened last night?

B: Oh, it was a disaster. By the time I got to the airport, my flight had already left.

A: No! Why were you so late?

B: The taxi never came. I'd booked it for six, but the app had canceled it, and nobody told me.

A: So what did you do?

B: Luckily, I'd saved the airline's number, so I called them. They found me a seat on a later flight. I'd never had to do that before. I was so nervous!

A: At least you got there in the end.

B: Just! When I finally landed, the hotel had closed the front desk, so I had to wait outside for an hour.

✏️ Underline every past perfect. 🗣️ Why did B miss the flight? Tell your partner in one sentence.

5.2

Telling a Story — Three Tenses, One Story

Class C15
📖 🗣️ New words — small disasters
  • it was pouring · get soaked
  • the car broke down
  • spill coffee (on your shirt)
  • lock yourself out
  • oversleep
  • miss the bus
  • 😱 a disaster / a nightmare
  • 🤯 a coincidence · it turned out that…

🔗 Story connectors:

First, …Then …After that, …While I was …Suddenly, …A few minutes later, …By the time …In the end, …

👂 Good listeners say:

No way!Oh no!You're kidding!Then what happened?What a nightmare!How did it end?
Grammar box — the story camera 🎥
🎬 Actionpast simplethe main events, in orderShe opened the door and walked in.
🌧️ Backgroundwas / were + -ingthe scene · an action in progressIt was raining. People were talking.
⏪ Flashbackhad + past participlewhat had happened earlierShe'd left her umbrella at home.
while+ was / were -ing (a long action)While we were eating, the lights went out.
when+ past simple (a short action)I was walking home when I saw an old friend.
by the time+ past perfectBy the time we arrived, it had closed.

Most of a story = past simple. A little background. One or two flashbacks.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

A: You'll never believe what happened to me yesterday.

B: Go on, tell me!

A: So, I was walking to the station, and it was raining really hard. I'd forgotten my umbrella at home, of course.

B: Typical! What did you do?

A: Well, while I was standing under a tree, a car suddenly pulled up next to me. It was my old teacher! I hadn't seen her for years.

B: No way! What a coincidence.

A: Exactly. She offered me a ride, so I got in. We talked the whole way, and in the end she drove me right to my door.

B: That's such a nice story.

The Future — Plans, Predictions & "I'll Be…"

Class C16
📖 🗣️ New words — talking about the future
  • an appointment (I'm seeing the doctor at 3.)
  • a booking (We're flying on Friday.)
  • getting around in 2040
  • shopping in the future
  • schools in 2040
  • 🔮 a prediction · an intention · an arrangement
this time tomorrowthis time next weekin ten yearsin 2040as soon asuntilprobablyI don't think… will…

in ten years = ten years from now (not "after ten years").

Grammar box — which future?
⚡ willdecide now · I think · offer · promiseIt's cold. I'll close the window. · I think it'll rain.
🧠 going toa plan I already have · I can see itWe're going to paint the kitchen. · Look! It's going to rain.
📅 -ingin my diary (time / place)I'm meeting Sara at six.

Future continuous — in the middle of an action at a future time:

✅will be + -ingThis time tomorrow, I'll be flying to Chicago.
❌won't be + -ingDon't call at noon. I won't be working then.
❓ politeWill you be + -ing?Will you be using the car tonight?

Time clauses — after when, as soon as, before, after, until, by the time: present simple, not will.

❌I'll call you when I will arrive.
✅I'll call you when I arrive. · When I get home, I'll cook.
