Real-World Communication

Share your opinion and disagree politely. Get a problem fixed in a store, with a landlord or on the phone. Talk about your experience in a job interview. In all three, the secret is the same: calm, polite and clear gets results.

📖 🗣️ Useful phrases — the four moves

💬 Give an opinion

I think / I believe…In my opinion, …It seems to me that…As far as I'm concerned, …If you ask me, …Personally, I feel…

👍 Agree

Exactly. / Absolutely.I couldn't agree more.That's a good point.You're right.I know what you mean.So do I. / Neither do I.

🤝 Disagree politely

I see your point, but…I'm not so sure (about that).I'm afraid I disagree.Actually, I think…Yes, but don't you think…?That may be true, but…

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read in threes

Nadia: So, do you two prefer working from home or going into the office? Personally, I feel much happier at home.

Tom: Really? I'm not so sure. I see your point about the commute, but to be honest, I get a bit lonely at home.

Nadia: That's a good point. But don't you think you concentrate better without all the noise?

Tom: Maybe. It depends on the day, I suppose.

Priya: As far as I'm concerned, the best answer is a mix: a few days at home, a few in the office.

Nadia: Exactly! I couldn't agree more.

Tom: Actually, that does sound sensible. What do you think the boss will say, Priya?

Complaints & Getting Things Fixed

Class C21
📖 🗣️ New words — what's the problem?
  • It doesn't work.
  • It's faulty. (the zipper is broken)
  • They're the wrong size.
  • I was charged twice.
  • The sink is leaking.
  • There's no hot water.
  • The fridge has stopped working.
  • It's cold. / It's not what I ordered.
  • 📦 My package hasn't arrived yet.
  • 📶 The internet isn't working.

More words: a refund (your money back) · a replacement (a new one) · a receipt · a landlord / building manager · a manager / supervisor · a reference number

Grammar box — a complaint in 3 parts
1 🧸 Soft openerExcuse me, I'm sorry to bother you, but… · I'm afraid there's been a mistake. · I'm calling about… · I'm writing to complain about… (email)
2 🔎 The problemThere's a problem with my bill. · The heating doesn't work. · The sink has been leaking since Monday.
3 🙏 Ask for actionCould you…? · Would it be possible to…? · Would you mind + -ing…? · I'd appreciate it if you could… · I'd like a refund, please.

🪜 The polite ladder (top = most polite): Would it be possible to send a new one? → Would you mind sending…? → Could you send…? → Can you send…? → Send it!

😐 Blames a personYou charged me twice. · You didn't clean the room.
🙂 No blame (passive)I was charged twice. · The room wasn't cleaned.
🧑‍💼 Staff: sorry + actionI do apologize. I'll sort that out right away. · We'll give you a refund.
⬆️ Still no help?I understand, but I'd like to speak to a manager, please.

📞 On the phone: spell your name, say numbers one by one, and at the end ask for a reference number or the person's name. Write down the date. Then check: "So someone is coming on Saturday at ten?"

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner (in a store)

Customer: Excuse me, I'm sorry to bother you, but I'm afraid there's a problem with this jacket. I bought it last week, and the zipper is broken.

Assistant: Oh, I'm sorry about that. Do you have your receipt?

Customer: Yes, here it is. Would it be possible to get a refund?

Assistant: I'm afraid I can only give you store credit.

Customer: I understand, but I'd really prefer a refund. Could I speak to a manager, please?

Manager: Hello, I'm the manager. I can see the jacket is faulty. I do apologize. We'll give you a full refund.

Customer: Thank you. I really appreciate your help.

💡 Getting things fixed — a practical tip

Keep a record: the date, the name of the person you spoke to, and the reference number. Keep receipts and emails. If you have a serious problem with housing, a bill or safety, ask your teacher or program staff where to get free help. (This class is about English, not the law.)

Grammar box — talking about your experience
How long (still true)I've been working as a cook for three years / since 2021.
Experience up to nowI've worked in two restaurants. · I've trained five new colleagues.
My job nowI work as a… · I'm responsible for + -ing · My job involves + -ing
My skillsI have experience in + noun / -ing · I'm good at + noun / -ing

⚠️ After for / in / at: -ing. "I'm good at cooking." ✅ "I'm good at to cook." ❌

⚠️ for + a length of time (for six months) · since + a starting point (since May). You apply for a job, but to a company.

⭐ Strong interview answers
"Tell me about yourself."Present → Past → Future: I'm currently… → I've… / Before that, I… → Now I'm looking for… That's why I applied for this position.
"What are your strengths?"Strength + example: I'm good at working under pressure. For example, …
"Tell me about a time when…"STAR: Situation (when, where) → Task (what you had to do) → Action (what you did) → Result (what happened, what you learned)
"What's your biggest weakness?"A small, real weakness + how you're improving: I used to…, but I've learned to…
"Do you have any questions?"Always say yes! Could you tell me what a typical day looks like?

🕐 Need time to think? "That's a good question." · "Let me think for a moment." · "Well, I'd say…"

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Interviewer: Thanks for coming in, Marco. So, to start, tell me a little about yourself.

Marco: Thank you for having me. Well, I'm currently working as a customer-service assistant, and I've been in that role for about three years. I'm responsible for handling calls and solving customer problems.

Interviewer: What would you say your main strengths are?

Marco: I'm good at staying calm under pressure. For example, in my last job I often dealt with difficult calls, and I always tried to find a solution.

Interviewer: And a weakness?

Marco: Let me think for a moment. I used to take on too much work myself, but I've learned to ask my colleagues for help.

Interviewer: That's good to hear. Do you have any questions for us?

Marco: Yes. Could you tell me what a typical day in this role looks like?
