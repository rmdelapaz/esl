Module 4 — Structures for Fluency: Answers

Answers to every Module 4 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (How It's Made quiz, fact files, "Whose bag?", detective cards, rules-or-advice cards), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Marking principle

At B1, mark meaning and form together. In the passive, the be verb (tense + agreement) and the past participle are the two things to check; a spelling slip in a correct participle ("recieved") is a success with a note. In deduction, the choice of modal is the meaning: might for must is a small shift, but mustn't for can't changes the meaning and is always wrong. may and could are always acceptable for might. Opinion tasks are open: mark the phrase and the support, never the opinion itself. American English is the model; British forms (city centre, parcel, postman, have got to, practise) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

4.1 · The Passive Voice — How It's Made (C10)

Exercise 1a — How is coffee made? (Script 4.1-A)
Workbook lineStep
The beans are roasted.4
The coffee cherries are picked.1
The coffee is packed and sold.6
The beans are dried in the sun.3
The skins are removed.2
The roasted beans are ground.5

Order to say aloud: picked → skins removed → dried → roasted → ground → packed and sold. If a learner puts "dried" before "skins removed," accept it with praise: some farms dry the whole cherry first. The point is the passive, not coffee science.

Exercise 1b — News in brief (Script 4.1-B)

1 was opened · 2 was designed · 3 were given · 4 was stolen · 5 was found · 6 was hurt

Why no doer? opened (not important: the city) · designed by students (the agent is kept because it is interesting) · given by local families (kept: it's the story) · stolen (unknown) · found (obvious: the police) · hurt (the point is "nobody").

Exercise 2 — Make the passive

1 is spoken · 2 was built · 3 are made · 4 were sent · 5 isn't served (is not served) · 6 Were … taken · 7 is grown · 8 were held

Wrong: "The Eiffel Tower is built in 1889" (past date → past be) · "These shoes is made" (plural → are) · "Was the photos taken" · "was hold / was helded." In 7, rice is uncountable → is.

Exercise 3 — Active → passive
  1. My phone was stolen. (drop "by someone")
  2. The office is cleaned every evening. (drop "by them")
  3. The Mona Lisa was painted by Leonardo da Vinci. (keep the agent: it's the interesting part)
  4. Spanish is spoken in Mexico. (drop "by people")
  5. A new phone was launched last year. (agent optional: "…by the company")
  6. Plastic isn't recycled here. (drop "by them")

Also correct: full negatives (is not recycled). Accept an agent in 5; mark "by someone / by them / by people" in 1, 2, 4 and 6 with a note ("correct, but the passive's job is to drop it"), not as wrong grammar.

Exercise 4 — Find and fix
  1. English is spoken here. (no passive without be)
  2. The bridge was built in 1890. (past date → past be)
  3. These cars are made in Japan. (participle, not base verb)
  4. My bag was stolen. (empty agent)
  5. An accident happened on Main Street. (happen has no object → no passive)
Exercise 5 — made of / from / in / by, then "My 6 facts"

1 of · 2 from · 3 in · 4 by · 5 from

In 5, from is best (the trees are changed into paper and you can't see wood). If a learner says "made of wood" about paper, praise the logic and show the difference with cheese (from milk) vs a table (of wood).

My 6 facts (open). Look for: the right be + participle every time, 3 present and 2 past, one useful by. Model (tea): Tea is grown in China, India and Kenya. The leaves are picked by hand. Then they are dried and packed. Tea was first drunk in China thousands of years ago. It was brought to Europe in the 1600s. Today, billions of cups are drunk every day. A process about bread, cheese, a local product, a building in town or a dish from anywhere is equally good.

Both files are invented, so the facts can't be looked up: success = the question form. Listen for Wh- + was / is / are + subject + participle. "When the bridge was built?" (no inversion) is the most common error; recast it. Active questions ("When did they build it?") are correct English; praise them, then ask for the passive version.

Self-check
Q1
"The letters ___ yesterday."
A) are sent
B) were sent
C) was sent
Why: yesterday → past be; letters is plural → were. A is present; C is singular.
Q2
Which is the best passive of "They built the school in 1985"?
A) The school was built in 1985.
B) The school is built in 1985.
C) The school was built by them in 1985.
Why: same tense as the active (past), and the vague doer they is dropped. B has the wrong tense; C is grammatical but "by them" adds nothing.
Q3 · new
Which sentence is correct?
A) An accident was happened.
B) My bag was stolen.
C) Rice is grown by people in Asia.
Why: happen has no object, so it has no passive (A). C is an empty agent: "Rice is grown in Asia."
Errors you will hear most (4.1)

"English spoken here." (no be) · "The bridge is built in 1890." (wrong tense of be) · "are make / was builded / was stealed" · "An accident was happened." · "It was stolen by someone." · "When the bridge was built?" · "Here speaks English." (Spanish se) · "made of milk." Correct the missing be and the participle every time; treat the empty agent as style, not grammar.

4.2 · Modals of Deduction & Obligation (C11)

Exercise 1 — Where's Sam? (Script 4.2-A)
IdeaThe clueModal
He's stuck in traffic.He rides his bike to work.can't
He's still asleep.It's not like him; maybe his alarm didn't ring (not sure)might
He's sick.He looked pale yesterday.may (might / could)
He's on his way.A message at 9:15: "Leaving now!"must

Accept any clue in the learner's own words. Point out the new evidence at the end: the speakers move up the certainty scale when they find a better clue. That is how deduction works.

Exercise 2 — must, can't or might?

1 can't · 2 must · 3 might (may / could) · 4 must · 5 can't · 6 must

Also acceptable: 4 might / could (he could just feel cold) if the learner explains; 6 might (it might be free bread!) with a reason. Wrong: mustn't in 1 or 5; can in 3.

Exercise 3 — Fix the deduction
  1. That can't be her brother. She doesn't have one.
  2. The store can't be open. All the lights are off.
  3. He can't be Italian. He doesn't speak a word of it.
  4. This can't be the right address. There's no number 40.

Why: for "I'm sure it's not true," the opposite of must is can't. mustn't means "not allowed." Also acceptable: couldn't be (rare at B1). "It isn't her brother" is correct English but not a deduction; ask for the modal.

Exercise 4 — Rules and advice

1 don't have to · 2 mustn't · 3 should · 4 have to · 5 have to · 6 don't have to

Also correct: 2 can't (the most natural US form) · 4 and 5 must. Wrong: 1 mustn't (it would mean paying is forbidden!) · 6 mustn't (a suit isn't forbidden, just not necessary) · 3 have to (it's advice, not a rule).

Exercise 5 — "must": rule or deduction?

1 R · 2 D · 3 R · 4 D · 5 D

Clue for learners: must be / must know + a reason in the situation = deduction; must + an action someone requires (wear, sign in) = rule.

Speaking — My 3 deductions (open)

Check: a clue for each, so, modal + base verb, can't for the negative. Model: The window is wet, so it must be raining. There's a cup on the table, so someone might be home. The TV is off and it's quiet, so my brother can't be watching the game.

"Whose bag?" case (Set G)

It must be Mei's bag.

ClueWhat the group can say

2 · a plane ticket to TorontoIt can't be Grace's (she's going to Miami). It might be Mei's or Luis's.
3 · a passportIt can't be Omar's (he doesn't have a passport yet).

Detective cards (Set F) — model deductions

Open task; any logical deduction with the right modal is correct. One model per card: 1 They must be away. · 2 She might be busy / Her phone could be off. · 3 He must be going somewhere special. · 4 It must be raining. · 5 The food can't be very good / It might be new. · 6 She must be going somewhere cold. · 7 He must be tired. · 8 She must have good news. (accept: she might be happy about something) · 9 Someone must be looking for it. · 10 That can't be right. It must be a mistake. · 11 The bread must be really good. · 12 They can't be at home. Advice lines: any should / shouldn't + base verb.

Rules or advice? cards (Set H)
have to / mustwear a seatbelt (car) · show your passport (airport) · buy a ticket before you get on (train) · wash your hands before you cook (restaurant job)
don't have topay (free museum) · go to work (office closed on Sunday) · bring food (host said no) · turn on your camera (teacher says it's your choice)
mustn't / can'trun (pool) · talk loudly (library) · take more medicine than it says
shoulddrink lots of water (a cold) · arrive early (job interview) · go to bed early (tired)

Accept should for "wash your hands" and "arrive early" as advice if the learner explains; accept must / have to for "arrive early" if they say it's the company's rule. The discussion is the point.

Self-check
Q1
"The whole house is dark and quiet. They ___ be asleep."
A) must
B) mustn't
C) don't have to
Why: a positive deduction from clues → must. B means "not allowed"; C means "not necessary." Neither is a deduction.
Q2
The sign says the entrance is free. Which sentence is correct?
A) You don't have to pay.
B) You mustn't pay.
C) You can't be paying.
Why: free = not necessary → don't have to. B says paying is forbidden; C is a deduction, not a rule.
Q3 · new
"His car isn't here and his lights are off. He ___ be at home."
A) mustn't
B) can't
C) must
Why: the clues say "sure it's not true" → can't. mustn't is never a negative deduction; C says the opposite of the clues.
Errors you will hear most (4.2)

"He mustn't be at home." (→ can't) · "She must to be tired." · "It must is broken." · "It can be the mail carrier." (→ might / could) · "You mustn't come, it's optional." (→ don't have to; German speakers especially) · "can't" said like "can" · "Maybe he is sick." (fine, but ask for might). Correct mustn't for can't and mustn't for don't have to every time: both reverse the meaning.

4.3 · Review Project — Opinions & Storytelling (C12)

Exercise 1 — What is the phrase doing?

1 O · 2 D · 3 A · 4 O · 5 D · 6 D

5 I'm afraid I disagree is polite because of I'm afraid; it is more direct than 2 and 6. Good discussion point.

Exercise 2 — Is social media good for us? (Script 4.3-A)

Heard (10): If you ask me · I'm not sure I agree · The main reason is · For example · I see what you mean, but · On the other hand · That's a good point · Exactly · It seems to me that · I take your point. Not heard (2): In my opinion · I couldn't agree more.

Does Maya (A) change her mind? A little. She says "Maybe it depends on how you use it," but she still thinks we spend too much time on it. Accept "yes, partly" or "no, not really" with a reason.

Exercise 3a — The bag on the train (Script 4.3-B)
used toI used to take the train to work every day.
relative clausea woman who was sitting across from me · a notebook, which had a name and a phone number
reported speechShe told me that she was on her way to a job interview… and that she needed the laptop.
must / might / can'tShe must be really worried.
the passiveit was picked up an hour later · a card was delivered to my house
present perfectI've taken that train hundreds of times since then · I've never found anything else
If… would / I wishIf I found a bag again, I'd do exactly the same thing. · I wish more stories were like that one.
Exercise 3b — Complete with the right form

1 had · 2 said · 3 was making (or had made) · 4 which · 5 must

3: the teacher said "You're making real progress" → was making; or "You've made real progress" → had made (past perfect, recognition only until 5.1). Accept is making if the learner means it's still true (US speakers often don't backshift). 4: that is wrong here (no that after a comma). 5: might is a meaning error with the cue "almost sure."

Exercise 4 — So do I / Neither do I

1 So do I. · 2 Neither do I. · 3 So have I. · 4 Neither can I. · 5 Neither did I. · 6 So am I.

Also correct in speech: Me too. (1, 3, 6) and Me neither. (2, 4, 5). Wrong: "I don't too," "So don't I," "Neither I do." Nor do I is correct but more formal.

Exercise 5 — Turn a fact into an opinion (models)
  1. If you ask me, learning a language is hard at first, but it gets easier. For example, I couldn't order a coffee in English two years ago.
  2. It seems to me that social media is popular because it keeps us connected, which is why so many people use it every day.
  3. As far as I'm concerned, working from home is great for some jobs. The main reason is that people don't have to travel.

Mark: an opinion phrase + a reason or an example. Any opinion is fine.

Project Parts A and B — the mini debate and the story

Open. Score with the C12 speaking checklist (classroom materials, Set K). Debate planner: look for an opener, 2 reasons, an example and one If + past, would sentence. Story frame: notes (not sentences) and one target structure per step. The model story (Script 4.3-B) shows all five steps.

Self-check
Q1
Your friend says, "I think exams are useless." You disagree, but you want to be polite.
A) No, that's completely wrong.
B) I see what you mean, but I think they do measure some skills.
C) Exactly, I couldn't agree more.
Why: acknowledge first, then disagree with a reason. A is blunt; C agrees.
Q2
Which sentence is a correct second conditional (an imaginary idea)?
A) If we ban cars, the air will be cleaner.
B) If we banned cars, the air would be cleaner.
C) If we will ban cars, the air would be cleaner.
Why: If + past simple, would + base verb. A is a correct first conditional (a real possibility), not imaginary; C has will after if.
Q3 · new
"I don't like crowds." You feel the same. You say:
A) So do I.
B) Neither do I.
C) I don't too.
Why: agree with a negative → Neither + helper verb + I. A agrees with a positive; C is not English ("Me neither" is fine in speech).
Errors you will hear most (4.3)

"I am agree." · "According to me…" · "I think no." · "In my opinion, I think…" · "We discussed about…" · "You're wrong." (a tone problem) · "So do I" after a negative · "If I will have time…" · "I use to take the train." (used to: the d is hard to hear, so check it in writing) · "She told me that she is…" (accept in US speech if still true). In C12, note errors for delayed feedback only; never stop a debate or a story to correct.
