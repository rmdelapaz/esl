Module 3 — Reporting & Connecting Ideas: Answers

Answers to every Module 3 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (Gossip Chain, Survey Report, Message Relay, definitions game and bingo, sentence strips), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 3.1–3.3Classes C7–C99 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At B1, mark meaning first, then form. A report is correct if a listener gets the right message and the structure is right: the backshift after said / told, tell + person, statement word order in questions, (not) to after told / asked, and the right relative pronoun. Be generous about optional things: that can be used or dropped; backshift is optional when the fact is still true ("She said she lives in Rome"); if and whether are both correct; that and which are both correct in defining clauses about things; an object pronoun can be kept or dropped. Contractions (he'd, she'd) are fine. American English is the model; British forms (film, flat, mum, holiday, neighbour, practise) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

3.1 · Reported Speech — Statements (C7)

Exercise 1 — Lina's voicemail (Script 3.1-A)
1234567
was startinghad foundwerecouldwould finishhad seenhad to

Lina's exact words: "I'm starting… · I've found… · The hours are good · I can walk… · I'll finish… · I saw the manager yesterday… · I must buy…". Also accept: 1 was going to start (meaning OK) · 5 'd finish. Not accepted: 1 "is starting" (no backshift is only OK if you report it the same day and the plan is still true, which is not the task here) · 7 "must" (must → had to is the target).

Exercise 2 — Report it
  1. They said (that) they were watching a movie.
  2. She said (that) she had lost her phone.
  3. He said (that) he had seen the accident.
  4. She told me (that) she would pay me back.
  5. He told me (that) I could borrow his car.
  6. She said (that) she had to leave early.
  7. He said (that) he might be late.
  8. She said (that) she would love to come. (no change: would is already "back")

Also correct: contractions (she'd lost, he'd seen, she'd pay); 1 film. Wrong: 2 "she has lost" · 3 "he saw" is acceptable in speech (past simple often stays in informal reports), but the target is had seen: mark with a note · 4 "she would pay you back" (the pronoun must be me) · 5 "I can borrow" · 6 "she must leave" · 8 "she would loved".

Exercise 3 — Say or tell?

1 told (person: me) · 2 said · 3 told (person: us) · 4 said · 5 tell (person: him) · 6 said · 7 tell (person: you) · 8 tell (a fixed phrase).

Note on 8: tell also goes with some nouns without a person: tell the truth, tell a story, tell a joke, tell a lie. Teach these as chunks. In 6, "The message told me the train was late" is also correct (a person is added).

Exercise 4 — Time and place words (Monday → Thursday, café → home)
  1. Ben said (that) he would call me the next day. (or: the following day)
  2. Ben said (that) he had bought that jacket the day before. (or: the previous day)
  3. Ben said (that) he loved the coffee there.
  4. Ben said (that) he had started his new job two weeks before. (or: two weeks earlier)

Also correct: 3 the coffee at the café; 4 he started (past simple often stays). Wrong: "tomorrow," "yesterday," "here," "ago" left unchanged (the time and place are different now) · "the next day" for yesterday (direction mix-up).

Exercise 5 — Find the mistake
  1. She told me that she was tired. / She said that she was tired.
  2. He told me he would help. (no to)
  3. Maria said that she was tired.
  4. They said they would arrive on Monday.
  5. He said he had finished the report.

In 4, will is acceptable only if Monday is still in the future and you are reporting soon after. Accept with a note if a learner explains this; the safe answer is would.

Dialogue — Sam's exact words

Reported verbs: was moving, 'd found (had found), had, would start, could visit, lived, would show. Sam's exact words (any two): "I'm moving to Chicago in June." · "I've found a great apartment near the lake." · "I have a new job at a bank." · "I'll start on June 1st." · "You can visit any time." · "My sister lives there too. She'll show you around." Note: "in June" doesn't change because June is still in the future.

Speaking — Gossip Chain (news cards) & "Things people said to me"

The model report is printed small on the back of each news card (classroom materials, set C). Score 1 point per correct backshifted verb in the last learner's written report. Example (card 1): "Mrs. Lee said she was moving to Florida the next month. She said she had sold her car and her son would drive her there." (3 points.) "Things people said to me" is open: check said vs told + person, the backshift and the pronoun.

Self-check
Q1
Direct: "I have finished my homework." Which is the correct report?
A) He said he has finished his homework.
B) He said he had finished his homework.
C) He said he had finished my homework.
Why: present perfect → past perfect (had finished), and my → his. A has no backshift; C forgets the pronoun change.
Q2
Which sentence is correct?
A) She said me that she was late.
B) She told to me that she was late.
C) She told me that she was late.
Why: tell + person, with no to. say cannot take a person directly.
Q3 · new
On Monday Tom said, "I'll call you tomorrow." It is now Friday.
A) Tom said he will call me tomorrow.
B) Tom said he would call me the next day.
C) Tom told he would call me the next day.
Why: will → would, and "tomorrow" was Tuesday, so it becomes the next day. C has told with no person.
Errors you will hear most (3.1)

"She said me…" · "He told to me…" · "She told that…" (no person) · "He said he is tired." (no backshift) · "She said she will come." · "My sister said he was tired." (he / she) · "Maria said I was tired." (pronoun not changed) · "He said he had went." Correct say / tell and the pronoun every time (they change the meaning or sound very wrong); treat a missing backshift gently when the meaning is clear.

3.2 · Reported Questions, Requests & Commands (C8)

Exercise 1 — Report the questions
  1. He asked what time the movie started.
  2. They asked if / whether I was hungry.
  3. She asked if / whether I had called my mother.
  4. He asked if / whether I could drive.
  5. She wanted to know when the bus would come.
  6. He asked me why I was laughing.

Also correct: asked me in any item; 3 whether I called (past simple can stay in speech). Wrong: "what time did the movie start" · "if was I hungry" · "if I have called" · "when would the bus come" · any question mark.

Exercise 2 — Fix the mistake
  1. She asked where I lived. (remove did and "?"; statement order; backshift)
  2. He asked what time the store opened. (remove does; backshift)
  3. They asked if / whether I was ready. (add if / whether; statement order)
  4. I asked him why he was angry. (subject before verb)
  5. She asked me if / whether I liked coffee. (remove did; add if / whether)
  6. The teacher asked us if we had finished. (remove "?")

Accept shop (British) in 2.

Exercise 3 — At the clinic (Script 3.2-A)
  1. She asked Mr. Kim if / whether he had an appointment.
  2. She asked him what time his appointment was.
  3. She asked if he had been there before.
  4. She asked him to spell his last name.
  5. She asked whether / if he had his insurance card.
  6. She told him to fill out the form. (also: to fill in, British; to complete)
  7. She told him not to forget to sign it.

Item 4 is a trap: "Can you spell your last name?" is a question in form, but it is a polite request, so asked him to spell is best. Accept "asked if he could spell his last name" as correct grammar with a note. Wrong: 2 "what time was his appointment" · 3 "if he has been" · 7 "to not forget" / "don't forget".

Exercise 4 — Commands and requests
  1. The teacher told us to open our books.
  2. My friend asked me to wait for him / her.
  3. The doctor told me not to eat before the test.
  4. Mom asked me not to be late.
  5. The guide told us to stay together.
  6. My neighbor asked me to water his / her plants.

Also correct: told in 2, 4 and 6 is understood but sounds less polite than the original; mark with a note. Mum (British) in 4. Wrong: "told to us" · "told me don't eat" · "asked me to not be late" · "asked me that I wait."

Exercise 5 — reminded · warned · advised · ordered

1 reminded · 2 warned · 3 advised · 4 ordered (also accept told).

Dialogue — the doctor's exact questions

1 "Are you sleeping well?" · 2 "How much water do you drink?" Also accept "Do you sleep well?" for 1.

Speaking — Survey Report, Message Relay & "My interview report"

Survey (open). Check the choice between the wh- word and if / whether, statement word order, and the backshift. Model: I asked Omar what he usually ate for breakfast. He said he ate eggs and bread. Lina asked me whether I liked spicy food. I told her I loved it.

Message Relay. The model reports and to-dos for all 8 cards are in the classroom materials (set E). A note that has the right action is correct even if the spoken report had small errors.

My interview report (open). Look for 6+ reports with asked / wanted to know / told / asked + to, one if / whether, one not to, no do / did and no "?". Model (a job interview): The manager asked me where I had worked before. She wanted to know if I could work on weekends. She asked me whether I had a driver's license. She told me to bring my references. She asked me not to be late on my first day. She said she would call me on Friday.

Self-check
Q1
Which is the correct reported question for "Where do you live?"
A) She asked where did I live?
B) She asked where I lived.
C) She asked where lived I.
Why: statement word order (subject + verb), no did, no "?", and live → lived.
Q2
Report this command: "Don't touch the paint."
A) He told me not to touch the paint.
B) He told me to not touching the paint.
C) He told that I didn't touch the paint.
Why: told + person + not to + base verb. B has the wrong order and -ing; C has no person and changes the meaning.
Q3 · new
Report this question: "Do you have a car?"
A) He asked if I had a car.
B) He asked did I have a car.
C) He asked if did I have a car.
Why: a yes/no question → if / whether + statement order; have → had. B has no if; C keeps did.
Errors you will hear most (3.2)

"She asked where did I live." · "He asked what time does it start." · "They asked was I ready." · "She asked me if I was ready?" · "He told me don't worry." · "She told me to not worry." · "She said me to wait." · "He asked to me…" · "askt" said as "ask." Correct the word order and the extra do / did every time: this is the focus of the lesson. Recast "told me don't" with not to.

3.3 · Relative Clauses (C9)

Exercise 1 — Choose the pronoun

1 who (also that) · 2 where · 3 whose · 4 which (also that, or nothing: "the laptop I bought") · 5 when (also that or nothing in speech) · 6 who (not that: there are commas).

Exercise 2 — Who am I? What is it?

1 d (mechanic) · 2 c (fridge) · 3 f (airport) · 4 a (chef) · 5 e (passport) · 6 b (post office).

7 (open): It's a person who helps doctors / takes care of sick people in a hospital. · 8 (open): It's a place where you keep your money / where you can get cash. Check who for the person and where + subject + verb for the place ("where you…", not "where keeps money").

Exercise 3 — Make one sentence
  1. I have a friend who speaks five languages. (also: that)
  2. This is the book that / which changed my life.
  3. We visited a village where my mother grew up. (also: which my mother grew up in)
  4. I know a man whose car is worth a fortune.
  5. My brother, who lives in Chicago, is a nurse. (also: My brother, who is a nurse, lives in Chicago.)
  6. Our new apartment, which we moved into last month, has a big kitchen. (also: Our new apartment, which has a big kitchen, … is less natural; accept)

Wrong: 1 "a friend which" · 2 "the book which it changed" · 3 "a village where my mother grew up there" · 4 "who's car" / "who his car" · 5 missing the second comma · 6 "which we moved in" is common in speech; accept, but moved into is the careful form.

Exercise 4 — Commas or not? Drop the pronoun?
  1. Non-defining (one car): My car, which is very old, broke down again. 🔒 must stay.
  2. Defining (which people? the ones we met): no commas. who is the object → ✂️ can drop: "The people we met on vacation…"
  3. Defining: no commas. who is the subject (she won) → 🔒 must stay.
  4. Non-defining (we know which city): Venice, which is full of canals, is sinking slowly. 🔒 must stay.
  5. Defining: no commas. that is the object (we watched it) → ✂️ can drop: "The movie we watched last night…"

In 2, whom is also possible in very formal English; don't teach it at B1. holiday (British) = vacation.

Exercise 5 — The commas change the meaning

1 a (no commas: "which brother? the one in Texas," so there are others) · 2 d (commas: all the students finished early and all went home; in c, only the early finishers went home).

Dialogue — the dropped pronoun

"The bread they make is amazing." (= the bread that / which they make). Other clauses in the dialogue: who just came in, who's wearing, whose daughter, who opened, which opened last month (non-defining), where I bought, who works.

Speaking — "Who am I? / What is it?" game, bingo, sentence strips, "my people, places & things"

Game: any clear definition with the right pronoun wins. Each card (classroom materials, set F) shows the word and two "Don't say" words; the bingo definitions in Script 3.3-A are good models. Bingo (Script 3.3-A): nurse, chef, mechanic, waiter, airport, post office, bank, station, fridge, umbrella, passport, key (doctor, supermarket, remote and hotel are not called). Sentence strips: answers in the classroom materials (set G). My people, places & things (open): 6 sentences with who / which / where / when / whose, at least one non-defining clause with two commas and no that. Model: My best friend is someone who always listens. I have a neighbor whose cat visits me every day. The town where I grew up is next to a river. My kitchen is the place where I relax. My phone, which is five years old, still works well. I'll never forget the day when I passed my driving test.

Self-check
Q1
"I know a man ___ car is worth a fortune."
A) who
B) whose
C) which
Why: the car belongs to the man (= his car), so whose.
Q2
Which sentence has the correct commas?
A) My father that is retired plays golf.
B) My father, who is retired, plays golf.
C) My father who is retired, plays golf.
Why: you have one father, so the fact is extra → non-defining: two commas and who (never that). C has only one comma.
Q3 · new
Which sentence is NOT correct?
A) The movie I watched was great.
B) The man called me was angry.
C) The house where I live is old.
Why: in B the pronoun is the subject (he called me), so it must stay: "The man who called me was angry." In A the dropped pronoun is the object, which is fine.
Errors you will hear most (3.3)

"The book which I read it." · "The man which…" / "the car who…" · "London, that is huge, …" · "The man called me was angry." · commas around a defining clause (German, Russian, Polish speakers) · only one comma · "who's" for "whose" · "the town where I was born there." Correct the extra pronoun and the missing subject pronoun every time. Treat commas as a writing focus: in speech, listen for the pause.
