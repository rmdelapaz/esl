Module 2 — If & Imagining (Conditionals): Answers

Answers to every Module 2 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (card sort, dilemmas, Small Wishes, Wish Mingle), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 2.1–2.3Classes C4–C69 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

At B1, mark the verb pair first: present + present (zero), present + will (first), past + would (second), wish + past / were / could / would. A wrong pair changes the meaning ("If I had time, I'll call") and is an error. Be generous with the choice between zero and first where both make sense (If you heat water, it boils / it'll boil), and accept was for were in speaking (informal but common), while noting were as the standard for writing. Contractions and full forms are both correct. American English is the model; British forms (flat, holiday, neighbour, football, apologise) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

Also accept "won't boil over" (1) and "will get soft" (2): meaning is the same. Discussion answer: the superstitions use will because people tell them like predictions of one future result.

Exercise 2 — Verb forms
  1. heat … melts (zero: a fact)
  2. rains … won't go
  3. get … 'll call (will call)
  4. study … 'll pass (will pass)
  5. press … turns (zero: how it always works)
  6. leave … 'll miss (will miss)
  7. see … tell (imperative)

Also correct: 1 "will melt" (a prediction; accept, but the fact reads better with melts) · 4 "can pass / might pass". Wrong: "If it will rain" · "When I will get" · "Unless you don't leave" · 7 "you will tell him" (grammatical but not the order the prompt asks for).

Exercise 3 — Match

1 e (we'll miss the train) · 2 a (I'll lend you my umbrella) · 3 b (I'll make you a sandwich) · 4 c (my parents will celebrate) · 5 d (she'll be really happy).

Exercise 4 — if / when / unless + commas

1 When (sure) · 2 If (maybe) · 3 unless · 4 Unless · 5 when · 6 unless.

Commas: a) If I see Anna, I'll tell her. · b) no comma · c) When you're ready, call me.

In 3, "You'll be cold if you don't wear a coat" is also correct if a learner rewrites it. Wrong: "unless you don't wear a coat."

Exercise 5 — One sentence + "My six predictions"
  1. If I have time, I'll help you.
  2. If you see Anna, give her this book.
  3. If we leave early, we won't hit traffic.
  4. I'll be late unless I leave now.
  5. You won't succeed unless you try.

Also correct: the clauses in the other order (no comma): "I'll help you if I have time." · 2 "Please give her this book if you see her." · 4 "Unless I leave now, I'll be late."

My six predictions (open). Look for: present after if / when / unless, will / won't (or can / might) in the result, one unless, one when, commas. Model: If I finish work early on Friday, I'll cook something nice. If it's sunny on Saturday, we'll go to the park. When I get my paycheck, I'll buy new shoes. I won't be tired on Monday unless I stay up late. If the bus is late, I'll walk. If I practice every day, my English will get better.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) If it will rain, I'll stay home.
B) If it rains, I'll stay home.
C) If it will rain, I will stay home.
Why: present simple after if; will goes in the result only.
Q2
"I'll miss the bus ___ I run." (= if I don't run)
A) unless
B) when
C) if
Why: unless I run = if I don't run. When means it's certain; if I run says the opposite.
Q3 · new
When I ___ home tonight, I'll call you.
A) will get
B) get
C) got
Why: when follows the same rule as if: present simple for a future meaning. got is past.
Errors you will hear most (2.1)

"If it will rain, I'll…" · "When I will get home…" · "If it rains tomorrow, I stay home." (present in the result for one future) · "Unless you don't hurry…" · "If you heat ice, it will melts." · no comma after a first if-clause (writing only). Correct the first two every time; the others with a recast.

2.2 · Second Conditional (C5)

Exercise 1 — Sam and Rosa (Script 2.2-A)

1 F — Rosa would travel for a whole year; Sam would buy a little house by the sea. · 2 T · 3 T · 4 F — "If I were you, I'd take it." · 5 T · 6 T ("I'm going to go on Saturday").

"I'd" sentences: I'd quit my job · I'd travel for a whole year · I'd buy a little house by the sea · I'd take it · I'd say yes right away. Also "I might visit the city first" (might in the result).

Exercise 2 — Second conditional
  1. had … would learn ('d learn)
  2. knew … would call ('d call)
  3. would travel ('d travel) … didn't have
  4. were … wouldn't say
  5. would … do … met

Also correct: 4 "was" (informal; note were as standard) · 1 "could learn". Wrong: "If I would have" · "we would travel more if we wouldn't have" · "What you would do".

Exercise 3 — First or second?
  1. rains … will take ('ll take) — first
  2. were … would change ('d change) — second
  3. go … will feel ('ll feel) — first
  4. could … wouldn't need — second
  5. pass … will celebrate ('ll celebrate) — first
  6. were … would sleep ('d sleep) — second

Also correct: 1 "might take" · 3 "might feel". Wrong: mixed pairs ("If I were the president, I'll change…").

Exercise 4 — Advice (sample answers)
  1. If I were you, I'd go to bed earlier. / …I wouldn't drink coffee at night.
  2. If I were you, I'd buy a new one. / …I'd delete some old apps.
  3. If I were you, I'd watch movies in English every day.
  4. If I were you, I'd call her / him / them and apologize. / I wouldn't wait too long, if I were you.
  5. If I were you, I'd put a small bowl by the door for your keys.
  6. If I were you, I'd buy a travel mug. / …I'd make coffee at work.

Any sensible advice is correct. Check: If I were you + I'd / I wouldn't + base verb. "If I was you" = informal (note it). Wrong: "If I were you, I will…" · "If I am you…" · "I'd to go".

Exercise 5 — What would you do if…? (open)

Check: If I + past, I'd + base verb in every answer, one If I were…, one could / might. Model: If I had a free day tomorrow, I'd sleep late and cook a big breakfast. If I found $50 on the bus, I'd give it to the driver. If I could have any job for one day, I'd be a pilot. If I could speak every language, I'd work as a tour guide. If I met my favorite singer, I might ask for a photo. If I were on a free vacation, I'd go to the mountains.

Dilemma cards & advice role-play (classroom materials)

Open answers: there is no right answer to a dilemma. Check the form (If I…, I'd… because…) and the reactions (I'd do the same. / Really? I wouldn't.). Advice role-play: any If I were you, I'd… that fits.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence uses the second conditional correctly?
A) If I will have money, I would travel.
B) If I had money, I would travel.
C) If I would have money, I travel.
Why: If + past simple (had), would + base verb. No will or would after if.
Q2
You really think rain is possible this afternoon. Which is best?
A) If it rains, I'll stay home.
B) If it rained, I'd stay home.
C) If it rained, I'll stay home.
Why: real and likely → first conditional. B is for an unlikely or imagined rain; C mixes past + will.
Q3 · new
Your friend says, "My phone is so slow." Which advice is best?
A) If I am you, I'll buy a new one.
B) If I were you, I'd buy a new one.
C) If I was you, I will buy a new one.
Why: the fixed advice phrase is If I were you, I'd…. A uses the present; C mixes was with will.
Errors you will hear most (2.2)

"If I would have money, I'd…" · "If I have a million dollars, I'd…" · "If I had time, I'll call you." · "I buy a house" (dropped 'd) · "If I am you…" · "What you would do if…?" Accept "If I was you" in speech.

Exercise 2 — Complete the wish

1 had · 2 were (was OK in casual speech) · 3 knew · 4 were · 5 didn't live · 6 weren't (wasn't informal).

Exercise 3 — Past, could or would?

1 could (ability) · 2 would (someone else's annoying behavior) · 3 had (past) · 4 would (annoying) · 5 were (about yourself → never would).

Exercise 4 — Complaint → wish (sample answers)
  1. I wish my job weren't boring. / I wish I had a more interesting job.
  2. I wish I could drive.
  3. I wish my phone weren't so slow. / I wish I had a faster phone.
  4. I wish you would close the door. / I wish you wouldn't leave the door open.
  5. If only it were warm! / If only it were warmer!

Wrong: "I wish I can drive" · "I wish my phone isn't so slow" · "I wish you will close the door."

Exercise 5 — hope or wish?

1 hope · 2 wish · 3 hope · 4 wish · 5 hope · 6 wish.

Rule to repeat: possible → hope + present; not real now → wish + past.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct (and standard)?
A) I wish I am on vacation.
B) I wish I were on vacation.
C) I wish I will be on vacation.
Why: a present wish uses a past form, and were is standard with I.
Q2
Your neighbor plays loud music every night. It annoys you. Which is best?
A) I wish my neighbor would turn the music down.
B) I wish my neighbor could turn the music down.
C) I wish my neighbor turned the music down.
Why: wish + would = someone else's annoying behavior we want to change. could is about ability; C is possible but weaker.
Q3 · new
The forecast says sun tomorrow. Which is correct?
A) I wish it's sunny tomorrow.
B) I hope it's sunny tomorrow.
C) I wish it will be sunny tomorrow.
Why: sun tomorrow is possible → hope + present. wish is for things that are not real now.
Errors you will hear most (2.3)

"I wish I can swim." · "I wish I have a car." · "I wish I would be taller." · "I wish it will stop raining." · "I wish you come to my party." (→ I hope you can come) · "She wish she could…" (missing -es). Accept "I wish I was…" in speech.
