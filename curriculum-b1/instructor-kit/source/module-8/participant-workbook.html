8.1

Question Tags & Keeping a Conversation Going

Class C23
📖 🗣️ New phrases — keeping the conversation alive

Question tags (a little question at the end):

…, isn't it?…, aren't you?…, don't you?…, doesn't she?…, didn't they?…, haven't you?…, can you?…, won't you?…, aren't I?…, shall we?

React (show interest):

Oh really?That's interesting!How nice!Oh no!You're kidding!That sounds great.

Echo questions (copy the helper verb): "I went to Chicago." → "Did you?" · "She's moved." → "Has she?"

Follow-up questions (ask for more):

What was it like?How long for?Who did you go with?How did it go?Tell me more!

👂 Backchanneling (small sounds that say "I'm listening"): mm-hm · uh-huh · right · yeah · really? · no way!

Grammar box
➕ → ➖You're coming, aren't you? · They've left, haven't they? · She'll help, won't she?
➖ → ➕He isn't ready, is he? · We can't park here, can we? · You don't mind, do you?
No helper verbpresent → do / does: You live here, don't you? · She works late, doesn't she?
past → did: They arrived late, didn't they?
SpecialI'm late, aren't I? · Close the door, will you? · Come in, won't you? · Let's go, shall we?

Two questions every time: 1. Is my sentence positive or negative? → flip it. 2. Which verb do I copy? → the helper verb, or do / does / did.

↘ Voice goes downI'm sure. I want you to agree.Lovely day, isn't it? ↘
↗ Voice goes upI'm not sure. I'm really asking.You haven't seen my keys, have you? ↗

Answer the fact, not the tag: "You don't drive, do you?" → "No, I don't." (if you don't drive)

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Priya: You went to the new café on Bridge Street, didn't you?

Tom: I did, yeah. We had breakfast there on Sunday.

Priya: Oh really? What was it like?

Tom: Honestly, it was fantastic. The coffee's amazing.

Priya: Is it? I love a good coffee. It's not too expensive, is it?

Tom: Not really. You'd like it. You'll come with me next time, won't you?

Priya: Of course! Let's go on Saturday, shall we?

Tom: Perfect. See you there!

👥 Speaking — the small-talk mingle

1. Take a small-talk card. Start the conversation with its tag (voice down ↘).

2. Keep it going for 2 minutes: React 😮 → Ask ❓ → Share 🙋. Listen with mm-hm, right, really?

3. Bell! Say "Nice talking to you!" Swap cards. Find a new partner.

4. Guess & check: guess one small thing about your partner. Check it with your voice up ↗: "You like spicy food, don't you? ↗"

8.2

Phrasal Verbs — A Practical Toolkit

Class C24
📖 🗣️ New words — phrasal verbs by topic

A phrasal verb = verb + a small word (a particle): get + up, look + after, find + out. Together they have one meaning.

🏠 Home & daily life

  • wake up / get up
  • turn on / turn off
  • turn up / turn down (the volume)
  • put away (clothes)
  • look for (my keys)
  • 🗑️ throw away (old food)
  • clean up (the kitchen)

💼 Work

fill out (a form)call back (a customer)find out (= discover)sort out (= solve)deal with (a problem)put off (= do it later)hand in (a report)carry on (= continue)

❤️ Relationships

get along with (UK: get on with)look after (= take care of)catch up with (an old friend)run into (= meet by chance)count on (= trust)get together (= meet)bring up (children)look forward to

✈️ Travel (from the online lesson): set off · check in · get on / get off (the bus) · give up

Grammar box
✂️ SplitTurn on the TV. = Turn the TV on.pronoun in the middle: Turn it on. ✅ Turn on it. ❌
🔗 StickI look after the kids.always together: I look after them. ✅ I look them after. ❌
🚫 No objectI get up at seven. · Let's get together.nothing after the verb
+ -ingI'm looking forward to seeing you. (not "to see")

💡 Many verbs with after, for, with, into, on (= trust) are 🔗 Stick: look after, look for, deal with, run into, count on.

📖 🗣️ Dialogue — read with a partner

Nadia: Morning! You look tired. Didn't you sleep well?

Leo: I woke up at five and couldn't get back to sleep. Then I had to set off early for the airport.

Nadia: Oh no. Did you check in OK?

Leo: Eventually. The machine wouldn't turn on, so a staff member had to sort it out.

Nadia: Typical! And who's looking after the dog while you're away?

Leo: My neighbor. I get along really well with her, luckily. I'm looking forward to a real rest, though.

Nadia: You deserve it. If anything comes up, just call and I'll deal with it.

Leo: Thanks. I'll find out the hotel's number and send it to you.

Review Project — Presentation & Panel Discussion

Class C25
📖 Your B1 toolkit — use it in your talk!
Pastpast perfect, narrative tenses, used to / wouldBefore I started, I had never tried it.
If…first & second conditional, wishIf more people cycled, the city would be quieter.
Reportingreported speechMy friend told me she had stopped.
Passive & modalsthe passive, must / might / have toThe park was built in 1990. It might help.
Wordsarticles, -ing / to, too / enough, relative clausesLearning a skill takes time. · the place where I relax
Talkopinions, question tags, phrasal verbsIn my opinion… · We should sort it out, shouldn't we?
📖 Signposting — road signs for your listener
1 · StartToday I'd like to talk about… · I chose this topic because…
2 · PointsFirst of all… · Another point is… · What's more… · For example… · Finally…
3 · EndTo sum up… · In conclusion… · Thank you for listening. Does anyone have a question?

In the panel: Agree — That's a really good point. · I completely agree. · Disagree politely — I see what you mean, but… · I'm not sure I agree, to be honest. · Bring others in — What do you think? · What's your view on that?
