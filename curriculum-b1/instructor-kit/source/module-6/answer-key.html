Module 6 — Precision & Detail: Answers

Answers to every Module 6 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, the classroom-materials answers (Editor B, verb sort, meaning-change cards, role-play language, Community Center task), and the errors you can expect to hear most.

Lessons 6.1–6.3Classes C17–C199 self-check items15 exercises
Marking principle

This module is about precision, so in the controlled exercises mark the form exactly. But in open writing and speaking, mark generously: an article slip rarely blocks meaning, and learners from article-free languages need many months of noticing. Count a missing or extra article in open work as a note, not a fail. A choice is wrong when it changes the meaning (stopped to smoke for someone who quit; few friends for someone who is happy; too good as praise). American English is the model; British forms (film, holiday, favourite, practise, go to hospital, post a letter, at the weekend) are acceptable when a learner uses them.

6.1 · Articles — a / an, the & No Article (C17)

Exercise 1 — My new neighborhood (Script 6.1-A)
12345678910111213
aatheatheananaThethe———

Reasons: 1, 2, 4, 8 new (first mention) · 3 the river: the local one everybody knows (accept a if the learner explains "one river, new to the listener" — the script says the) · 5, 9, 10 second mention · 6 an + vowel sound "old" · 7 an + "elderly" · 11 go to work (fixed) · 12 by bus (fixed) · 13 life in general.

Exercise 2 — a, an, the or — ?
  1. an honest person (silent h) · a nurse (job)
  2. — music (general) · the songs (specific: the ones my grandmother taught me)
  3. — dinner (meal) · the news (fixed with the)
  4. a phone (first mention) · the phone (second mention)
  5. — Italy (country) · the sea (the one near them / unique in context)
  6. — school (for its purpose) · — bus (by + transport)
  7. the piano (instrument) · — tennis (sport)
  8. — bed (go to bed = to sleep)
  9. an hour (silent h) · the doctor (the one we were waiting to see)
  10. The Earth · the sun (only one)

Also correct: 7 "plays piano" is common in casual American English; accept it but teach the. 9 "a doctor" is possible if it's any doctor in a big clinic; accept with a good reason. Wrong: "a hour," "the Italy," "the dinner" (the meal in general), "go to the bed" (unless the learner means the object).

Exercise 3 — You are the Editor (12 mistakes)

Welcome to our community garden! Last spring, a group of neighbors decided to turn an empty lot on the Maple Street into a garden. Today, the garden has more than forty vegetable beds. Most beds belong to families, but there is also a bed for the elementary school next door. The children come every Friday with their teacher, who is also a volunteer here. We grow the tomatoes, beans, and herbs, and we give the extra vegetables to a food bank. The gardening is good for your health! Water is free, but please bring your own tools. We meet on the first Saturday of every month at 10 a.m. New members are always welcome — you don't need to be an expert. Just bring a hat and a smile!

TypeMistake → correctionReason
Missing (6)^a group · ^an empty lot · into ^a garden · also ^a bed · also ^a volunteer · on ^the first Saturdayfirst mention ×4 · a role/job · ordinal (first)
Extra (3)the Maple Street · the tomatoes · The gardeningstreet name · general plural · general activity
Wrong (3)a garden → the garden · a expert → an expert · the hat → a hatsecond mention · vowel sound · any hat (new)

Traps (correct as written): our community garden (possessive, no article) · Most beds belong to families (general) · the elementary school next door (defined by what follows) · The children (the school's children: specific) · the extra vegetables (the ones from this garden) · a food bank (also OK: the local food bank) · Water is free (The water is also acceptable: the garden's water) · a smile. If a learner changes a trap and gives a sensible reason, give credit for the thinking and explain why the original works.

Exercise 4 — One mistake in each sentence
  1. My brother wants to be a doctor. (job)
  2. I think life is beautiful. (general: no the)
  3. She plays the guitar in a band. (instrument)
  4. We went to France on vacation. (country: no the)
  5. Can you close the window? (we both know which)
  6. I don't really like dogs. (general)

Also correct: 4 "on holiday" (British). 3 "She plays guitar" (casual US) — accept but it doesn't fix the mistake the learner was asked to find; the target is the.

Exercise 5 — One noun, three ways (open)

Look for: a / an for one portion or one example, the + a reason it's specific (here, this, the one I bought), and a general sentence with no article. Model with bread: Can I buy a loaf of bread? (note: bread is uncountable, so "a bread" is wrong: a strong learner will spot this) · The bread at this bakery is amazing. · I eat bread every day. Model with soccer: I bought a soccer ball. · The soccer game last night was exciting. · I play soccer on Sundays.

Speaking — Describe & Draw (open) · Editor B (classroom materials)

Describe & Draw: check the pattern There's a … → The … is …, general sentences with no article, and one fixed phrase if it fits. The drawing should match the description; mismatches usually come from a missing the ("There is table" → B draws a second table).

Editor B (10 mistakes: 4 missing, 5 extra, 1 wrong): Hi Sam, I have good news: I got a job! I'm a cook at a new restaurant downtown. The restaurant (not A) is small, but the kitchen is modern and the owner is very kind. I start the work at six in the morning, so I go to the bed early now. I take the bus to work — it's the fastest way. The best part? The lunch is free for the staff! The life is busy, but I'm happy. Let's have dinner next week. I'll send you a message on the Monday. Talk soon, Li

Missing: a job · a cook · the fastest · a message. Extra: the work · the bed · The lunch · The life · the Monday. Wrong: A restaurant → The restaurant. Traps: the kitchen, the owner (the restaurant's: clear from the situation), the bus (the usual route; by bus also fine), The best part, the staff.

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) She's teacher at a big school.
B) She's a teacher at a big school.
C) She's the teacher at big school.
Why: jobs take a / an, and a big school is new information. A drops the job article; C uses the for a first mention and drops the article before big school.
Q2
"I really enjoy ___, especially ___ that plays in this restaurant."
A) the music … the music
B) music … the music
C) music … music
Why: first music in general (no article), then specific music (defined by "that plays in this restaurant").
Q3 · new
The bus was late. We waited for ___ hour!
A) a
B) an
C) the
Why: the h in hour is silent, so the first sound is a vowel: an. The hour would need a specific hour we both know.
Errors you will hear most (6.1)

"I'm engineer." / "She's doctor." · "I bought phone. Phone is broken." · "The life is hard." / "I like the animals." · "the France" · "a hour" / "an university" · "have the breakfast" · "go to the bed" (for sleeping) · "play guitar" (accept in speech). Correct the job article and the extra the in general statements every time in controlled work; in speaking, note them for delayed feedback.

6.2 · Gerunds & Infinitives — Verb Patterns (C18)

Exercise 1 — -ing or to?
  1. reading (enjoy + -ing)
  2. to stay (decide + to)
  3. waiting (mind + -ing)
  4. cooking (after the preposition at)
  5. to pay (offer + to)
  6. seeing (to is a preposition here: "looking forward to the weekend")
  7. Learning (-ing as the subject)
  8. to buy (can't afford + to)

Wrong: "looking forward to see" (the most common), "good at cook," "To learn a language takes time" (grammatical but formal and unusual; accept with a note, the target is Learning).

Exercise 2 — Choose the form for the meaning
  1. to have (stopped in order to eat)
  2. eating (end the habit)
  3. to mail (a future task)
  4. meeting (a past memory)
  5. restarting (tested it as a solution)
  6. to buy (a task I didn't do)
  7. to open (a big effort)

Also correct: 3 "to post" (British). 5 "tried to restart it" is not wrong English, but it means "made an effort" (maybe it was hard to restart), so it doesn't match the meaning given.

Exercise 3 — Verb + person + to
  1. He asked me to help him.
  2. The doctor advised her to rest.
  3. They invited us to come to their party.
  4. She warned him not to touch the wire.
  5. My mom reminded me not to forget my passport. / My mom reminded me to take (bring) my passport.

Also correct: 3 "They invited us to their party" (invite + to + noun). Wrong: "He asked to help him" (now he helps) · "She warned him to not touch" (accept in speech; standard is not to) · "reminded me don't forget."

Exercise 4 — Which one did you hear? (Script 6.2-A)

1 b · 2 a · 3 a · 4 b · 5 a · 6 a · 7 b · 8 b

If you read the alternative sentences in the script (for a second round), the answers flip: 1 a · 2 b · 3 b · 4 a · 5 b · 6 b · 7 a · 8 a.

Exercise 5 — My verb collection (open)

Check each verb is in the right column and each sentence uses the pattern. Model: I enjoy walking in the park. · I avoid eating late. · I keep losing my pens. · I can't stand waiting in line. · I miss cooking with my sister. | I want to get a better job. · I decided to join a gym. · I hope to visit Canada. · I promised to call my mom every Sunday. · I need to renew my license. Meaning-change pair: I stopped drinking soda. / I stopped to buy water on the way. Watch for like / love / hate / start / begin in either column: both are correct.

Classroom materials — verb sort & meaning-change cards

Verb sort. + -ing: enjoy, avoid, finish, suggest, keep, mind, can't stand, feel like, give up, miss. + to: want, decide, hope, promise, learn, offer, refuse, manage, agree, can't afford. Both, meaning changes: stop, remember, forget, try.

Meaning-change cards. S1 She stopped buying coffee. → M: She doesn't buy coffee anymore. · S2 She stopped to buy a coffee. → M: She paused on her way so she could buy one. · S3 He stopped talking. → M: He was quiet after that. · S4 He stopped to talk to his neighbor. → M: He was walking, and he paused to chat. · S5 I remember locking the door. → M: I have a memory: I did it. · S6 Remember to lock the door. → M: Don't forget. It's a task for later. · S7 I'll never forget meeting you. → M: It's a special memory. · S8 I forgot to call my mom. → M: I didn't call her. Oops! · S9 Try turning it off and on. → M: Test this idea. Maybe it will fix the problem. · S10 I tried to open the jar, but it was too tight. → M: I made a big effort, but I couldn't. · S11 Try adding a little salt. → M: An experiment: taste it and see. · S12 She tried to stay awake. → M: It was difficult, but she made an effort.

Find Someone Who and the two-minute talk: answers are personal. Check the question keeps the pattern ("Do you enjoy cooking…?", "Do you remember learning…?") and the report uses he/she + -s ("She can't stand waiting.").

Self-check
Q1
Which sentence is correct?
A) I enjoy to swim in the sea.
B) I enjoy swimming in the sea.
C) I enjoy swim in the sea.
Why: enjoy takes the -ing form. A uses to; C uses the base form.
Q2
"We were driving all day, so we ___ lunch." (= we paused in order to eat)
A) stopped having
B) stopped to have
C) stopped have
Why: stop to do = pause in order to do. A means they don't have lunch anymore; C is not a pattern.
Q3 · new
I'll never forget ___ the ocean for the first time.
A) to see
B) seeing
C) see
Why: it's a memory of something that happened, so forget + -ing. Forget to see would mean "not do a task."
Errors you will hear most (6.2)

"I'm looking forward to see you." · "I enjoy to cook." · "interested in learn" / "before to leave" · "She told to wait." / "I want that you listen." · "He suggested to go." · "I stopped to smoke" (meaning "I quit") · "I enjoy cook" (no -ing). Correct looking forward to + -ing and the missing person after tell / ask every time: both can change or blur the meaning.

6.3 · Degree & Quantity — Complaints & Compliments (C19)

Exercise 1 — too, enough, too much or too many?

1 too (before the adjective) · 2 too much (uncountable work) · 3 enough (after the adjective) · 4 too many (countable people) · 5 enough (before the noun) · 6 enough (after tall) · 7 too much (uncountable sugar)

Also correct: 1 "very heavy" is grammatical but loses the "problem" meaning with for me to lift; mark as a note. Wrong: "too many work," "enough old," "too much people."

Exercise 2 — so, such or such a?

1 so (adjective alone) · 2 such a (singular noun person) · 3 such (plural noun stories) · 4 so (adjective alone) · 5 such (uncountable weather)

  1. The movie was so funny that we laughed for hours. / It was such a funny movie that we laughed for hours.
  2. It was such a long meeting that I almost fell asleep. / The meeting was so long that I almost fell asleep.

Also correct: that can be dropped in speech ("It was so funny we laughed for hours"). Wrong: "such nice weather" → "such a nice weather" (weather is uncountable) · "so a long meeting."

Exercise 3 — How much? How many?

A. 1 a little (positive, uncountable) · 2 a few (positive, countable) · 3 few (negative, countable) · 4 a little (uncountable, a small amount) · 5 little (negative, uncountable)

B. 6 All people (general: no of) · 7 Most of my friends (my → of) · 8 none of them (pronoun → of) · 9 Some of the students (the → of; Most of is also correct: "a few didn't" suggests most passed) · 10 all of the cake / all the cake (both fine)

Also correct: 5 "very little time." 3 "not many people" (same meaning, less formal). Wrong: "most of people," "the most of my friends," "none them."

Exercise 4 — A restaurant review (Script 6.3-A)
  1. On Saturday (night), with a few friends.
  2. The food was so delicious that they stayed for three hours · the waiter was such a friendly person (he remembered all of their names). Accept also: the chicken soup was perfect; the bread was fresh.
  3. The restaurant was too small for a busy Saturday night · there were too many tables and not enough space to walk · there was too much noise to have a real conversation. Accept also: they waited forty minutes for a table.
  4. Most of the desserts were sold out; only a few were left.
  5. Three stars (out of five). She'd go back on a quiet Tuesday.
Exercise 5 — Make it precise (sample answers)
  1. The soup has too much salt. / The soup is too salty.
  2. The jacket is too small. / The jacket isn't big enough.
  3. There isn't enough hot water. / The water isn't hot enough.
  4. There are too many people on the bus.
  5. The nurse was so kind that I felt calm. (any logical result)
  6. It was such a great party that we stayed until midnight. (any logical result)
Speaking — Complaints & Compliments · Community Center (classroom materials)

Role-play (open). A strong performance has all five moves: a soft start (Excuse me, I'm sorry to bother you, but…), 2–3 exact problems with too / not … enough / too many / too much, a polite request (Could you…? / Would it be possible to…?), B's apology + offer (I'm so sorry. I'll… / We have a few…), and a compliment with so / such. Expected problem sentences per card: 1 Hotel The room is too cold / isn't warm enough · there aren't enough towels · there's too much noise. 2 Restaurant The soup is too salty / has too much salt · we waited too long · the table is too small for four. 3 Store The jacket is too small / the sleeves aren't long enough · the zipper is broken · the receipt price is too high (it was on sale). 4 Internet The internet is too slow to watch videos · the bill is too high · there are too many ads / calls. 5 Delivery The food was so late that it was cold · there wasn't enough rice · some of the drinks were missing. 6 Class The room is too crowded · there aren't enough computers · the class is too early for people who work nights.

Community Center (open). Any set of fixes that stays within $1,500 is correct. Check the report language: Most of the complaints are about…, There aren't enough…, so we'll…, We have a little money left for…, None of us thought … was important. One efficient answer: chairs ($400) + heater ($350) + second Saturday class time (volunteer, $0) + a bike rack ($250) + fix the lights ($300) = $1,300, with $200 left.

Self-check
Q1
Maya is 15. In her state you must be 16 to drive. Which sentence is correct?
A) She isn't enough old to drive.
B) She isn't old enough to drive.
C) She isn't too old to drive.
Why: enough comes after the adjective. A has the wrong word order. C is grammatical but means "her age is not a problem," which is the opposite of the situation. (The workbook adds Maya's age because the online quiz asks "Which is correct?" without context, and C is correct English.)
Q2
"It was ___ interesting book ___ I read it in one day."
A) so … that
B) such an … that
C) such an … so
Why: there's a noun (book), so such; singular + vowel sound, so an; the result starts with that.
Q3 · new
"Hurry up! We have very ___ time." (not much: a problem)
A) a little
B) little
C) few
Why: time is uncountable (little, not few), and the meaning is negative (no a). A little time would mean "don't worry, there's some."
Errors you will hear most (6.3)

"The food was too delicious!" (praise) · "enough big" · "so a nice day" / "such nice" · "too much people" · "I have few friends" (meaning a few, happy) · "most of people" / "the most of my friends" · "such a nice weather." Correct too as praise and enough word order every time; in the role-play, praise precise complaints even if a small form slips.
