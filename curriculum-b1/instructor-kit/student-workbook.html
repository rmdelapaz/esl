⏱️ Time words — which tense do they go with?

🔚 Finished time → past simple➡️● Connected to now → present perfect
yesterday · last week · in 2019 · two days ago · when I was a child · on Fridayever · never · today · this week · so far · just · already · yet · still · for · since
Grammar box
🔚 Past simplea finished action at a finished timeI saw that movie last Friday.
➡️● Present perfecta past action connected to now (experience, news, result, still true)I've seen that movie three times.
⚠️ Golden ruleIf you say when (a finished time), use the past simple.I've seen him yesterday. ❌ → I saw him yesterday. ✅
for+ a length of timeI've known her for ten years / for a long time.
since+ a start pointI've known her since 2014 / since I was a child.
justa very short time agoI've just finished lunch.
alreadysooner than expectedShe's already left.
yetquestions and negatives, at the endHave you eaten yet? · I haven't eaten yet.
stillnot happened (and I'm surprised)I still haven't packed.

Tell a story like this: open with the present perfect (no time) → give the details in the past simple.

"I've been to Japan. I went in 2019 with two friends. We visited Tokyo and Kyoto. It was amazing."

been = went and came back. gone = went and is still there. "She's gone to Japan." = She is in Japan now.

Grammar box
✅ YesI / you / we / they have been + -ing · he / she / it has been + -ingI've been working. · She's been studying.
❌ Nohaven't / hasn't been + -ingThey haven't been sleeping well lately.
❓ QuestionHave / Has + subject + been + -ing?Have you been waiting long? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
⏳ How long?How long have you been + -ing?How long have you been learning to drive?
⏳ Use 1: still goingstarted in the past, still happening nowI've been studying English for three years.
👀 Use 2: a result nowa recent activity; we can see the resultHis hands are dirty. He's been fixing the car.
🔢 How many? → simple⏳ How long? → continuous
I've written three emails.I've been writing emails all morning.
We've painted the kitchen. (the job is done)We've been painting the kitchen. (that's why it's messy)

⚠️ No -ing with these verbs (states, not activities): know · like · love · hate · want · need · believe · understand · be · have (= own) · belong.
I've known her for years. ✅   I've been knowing her for years. ❌

Class C4
📖 🗣️ New words — life hacks and superstitions

A life hack 🔧 = a clever, simple trick for everyday life. A superstition 🍀 = an old belief about good luck or bad luck.

  • boil (water boils at 212°F)
  • freeze / melt
  • go hard / get soft
  • charge (a phone)
  • open an umbrella indoors
  • break a mirror
  • spill salt
  • my hand itches
  • miss the bus
  • can't sleep
good luck / bad luckbelieve (in)a warningalways truelikelywhen (I'm sure)if (maybe)unless (= if … not)
Grammar box
🔧 ZeroIf / When + present, presentIf you heat ice, it melts. · When I'm tired, I drink coffee.
📅 FirstIf + present, will / won't + verbIf it rains this afternoon, I'll take the bus. · If we leave now, we won't be late.
➕ Resultcan / might + verb, or an orderIf you finish early, you can go home. · If you see John, tell him hi.

🔧 Zero = always true (a fact, a habit, a life hack). 📅 First = one real, likely future.

⛔ Never put will after if or when. If it will rain rains, I'll stay home. · When I will get get home, I'll call you.

whenI'm sure it will happen.When I get home, I'll call you.
ifMaybe it will happen.If I get the job, I'll celebrate.
unless= if … not (+ a positive verb)I'll be late unless I leave now.

✏️ Comma: If first → comma: If I see her, I'll tell her. · If second → no comma: I'll tell her if I see her.

Phrasal Verbs — A Practical Toolkit

Class C24
📖 🗣️ New words — phrasal verbs by topic

A phrasal verb = verb + a small word (a particle): get + up, look + after, find + out. Together they have one meaning.

🏠 Home & daily life

  • wake up / get up
  • turn on / turn off
  • turn up / turn down (the volume)
  • put away (clothes)
  • look for (my keys)
  • 🗑️ throw away (old food)
  • clean up (the kitchen)

💼 Work

fill out (a form)call back (a customer)find out (= discover)sort out (= solve)deal with (a problem)put off (= do it later)hand in (a report)carry on (= continue)

❤️ Relationships

get along with (UK: get on with)look after (= take care of)catch up with (an old friend)run into (= meet by chance)count on (= trust)get together (= meet)bring up (children)look forward to

✈️ Travel (from the online lesson): set off · check in · get on / get off (the bus) · give up

Grammar box
✂️ SplitTurn on the TV. = Turn the TV on.pronoun in the middle: Turn it on. ✅ Turn on it. ❌
🔗 StickI look after the kids.always together: I look after them. ✅ I look them after. ❌
🚫 No objectI get up at seven. · Let's get together.nothing after the verb
+ -ingI'm looking forward to seeing you. (not "to see")

💡 Many verbs with after, for, with, into, on (= trust) are 🔗 Stick: look after, look for, deal with, run into, count on.
