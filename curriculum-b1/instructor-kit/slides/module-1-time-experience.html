1.1 · The choice

Finished time or connected to now?

🔚 Past simple · finished time➡️● Present perfect · connected to now
I lost my keys yesterday.I've lost my keys. (I can't find them now.)
She lived there in 2015. (not now)She's lived here for ten years. (still here)
Did you eat sushi at the party?Have you ever eaten sushi?
We arrived at 8 o'clock.We've just arrived. (we're here now)
GOLDEN RULE You say WHEN (yesterday, in 2019, ago) → past simple · I've seen him yesterday ❌
1.1 · Check the meaning

What does it mean?

"I've lost my keys."
Do I have them now? (No.)
Do we know when? (No. The result is important.)
"She has lived here for ten years."
Does she live here now? (Yes.)
"She lived there for ten years." Now? (No.)
"I've been to Japan." Am I in Japan now? (No, I came back.)
"She's gone to Japan." Where is she? (In Japan.)
"I still haven't finished." Did I finish? (No.)
Happy or a bit unhappy? (Unhappy / surprised.)

1.1 · Model dialogue · open → details

Have you ever run a marathon?

A: Have you ever run a marathon?

B: Yes, I have! I ran one last year in Chicago.

A: Wow. How was it?

B: Really hard, but great. I trained for six months before it.

A: I've never done anything like that. I've just started going to the gym, though!

SHAPE Open: present perfect (no time) → Details: past simple (when, where, how)

1.2 · have / has + been + -ing

Three pieces, always in this order

✅I've been working. · She's been studying.
❌They haven't been sleeping well lately.
❓Have you been waiting long? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
⏳How long have you been learning English? — For three years. / Since 2023.
👀His hands are dirty. He's been fixing the car.
SAY IT been = "bin" /bɪn/ · I've been WAIT-ing · How LONG have you been WORK-ing?
1.2 · Simple or continuous? · check the meaning

How many? or How long?

🔢 How many? → simple⏳ How long? → continuous
I've written three emails.I've been writing emails all morning.
We've painted the kitchen. (done)We've been painting the kitchen. (that's why it's messy)
"I've painted the kitchen." Finished? (Yes.) · "I've been painting the kitchen." Finished? (Maybe not.)
"I've been knowing her for years"? (No! know = a state → I've known her.)
NO -ING know · like · love · want · need · believe · understand · be · have (= own)

1.3 · used to + base verb

True then — not true now

✅People used to write letters. · There used to be a bakery here.
❌I didn't use to like coffee.
❓Did you use to have a bike? — Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
"I used to smoke." Do I smoke now? (No.) Once or many times? (Many times.)
"There used to be a movie theater here." Is it here now? (No, it's gone.)
SPELLING after did → no d: didn't use to · Did you use to…? · used to went ❌ · SAY IT /ˈjustə/ "YOO-stuh"
1.3 · would · the state rule

Every Sunday we would…

used towould
🔁 Actions: visit, walk, cook, tell✅ We used to visit them.✅ We would visit them.
🏠 States: live, like, have, be, know✅ I used to live in Rome.❌ I would live in Rome.
"When I was a child, every Sunday my father would cook a big breakfast." Future? (No.) One time or many? (Many.)
"We would have a dog." OK? (No! have = a state → We used to have a dog.)
TIP Set the past scene first. Not sure? used to is always safe.
