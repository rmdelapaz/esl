Small words, big difference

  • 6.2 Which form? I enjoy cooking. I want to learn. I stopped to buy a coffee.

NOTE Your real life, a new identity card, or an invented place: all are OK. You can say "Pass."
6.1 · Articles · one question

Does my listener already know which one?

YES → theI saw a dog. The dog was huge. · the sun · Close the door. · the best day · the woman who called
NO + one thing → a / anI bought a phone. · Pass me a pen. · She's an engineer.
NO + general → —I like cats. · Water is important. · Life is hard.
WATCH She's doctor ❌ · The life is hard ❌ · the France ❌ · a hour ❌
6.1 · One noun, three ways · check the meaning

☕ a coffee · the coffee · coffee

a / anI need a coffee. (any cup)
theThe coffee here is great. (this coffee)
—I don't drink coffee. (in general)
"I saw a dog." Do you know this dog? (No. It's new.)
"The dog was huge." Which dog? (The one I told you about.)
"I love music." One song or all music? (All music, in general.)
"The sun is hot." How many suns? (One.)
STEP BY STEP 1 Specific? → the · 2 One countable thing? → a / an · 3 Otherwise → no article

6.2 · Verb patterns · check your sort

-ing or to?

+ -ingenjoy · avoid · finish · suggest · keep · mind · can't stand · feel like · give up · missI enjoy cooking.
+ towant · decide · hope · promise · learn · offer · refuse · manage · agree · can't affordWe decided to stay home.
both!stop · remember · forget · tryThe meaning changes →
HINT many -ing verbs = experiences · many to verbs look forward (want, hope, plan, promise)
6.2 · Three more patterns

told him to · good at -ing · Swimming is…

verb + person + toShe told him to wait. · The doctor advised her to rest. · She warned him not to touch it.
preposition + -inggood at drawing · interested in learning · instead of taking the bus
"to" trapI'm looking forward to seeing you. (looking forward to the weekend ✔ → preposition)
-ing = subjectLearning a language takes time.
WATCH She told to wait ❌ · I want that you listen ❌ · looking forward to see ❌ · before to leave ❌
6.2 · The meaning changes · check it

stop · remember · forget · try

+ -ing+ to
stopstopped buying coffee (no more)stopped to buy a coffee (pause → in order to)
rememberremember locking it (memory ←)Remember to lock it (task →)
forgetnever forget meeting herforgot to call him (didn't do it)
tryTry restarting it (a test)tried to open the jar (an effort)
"He stopped working." Does he work now? (No.)
"He stopped to buy bread." Why did he stop? (To buy bread.)
"Remember to lock the door." Did you lock it? (Not yet. A task.)
"Try restarting it." Easy or difficult? (Easy. A test.)

6.3 · too · enough · too much / many

"too" = a problem

too + adjectiveThe coffee is too hot (to drink).
adjective + enoughShe's old enough to drive. · The room isn't warm enough.
enough + nounWe don't have enough chairs.
count / no counttoo many cars · too much noise · You worry too much.
WATCH enough big ❌ · too much people ❌ · "The food was too good!" (praise) ❌ → so good ✔
6.3 · so / such … that

Is there a noun after it?

NO → soIt was so hot that we left early.
YES → such aIt was such a good movie that I watched it twice.
YES → suchThey're such kind people. · such nice weather

Priya: How was the concert last night?

Dan: Amazing! The band was so good that we didn't want it to end.

Priya: Really? I heard it was such a long show, though.

Dan: It was, three hours! But the time went so quickly that I hardly noticed.

6.3 · a few / few · a little / little · most of

🙂 a few friends · 🙁 few friends

countno count
🙂 somea few friendsa little time
🙁 not many / muchfew friendslittle time
generalMost people agree.
specificMost of the people here · Some of my friends · None of them
"I have a few friends here." Am I OK? (Yes 🙂.)
"I have few friends here." (Lonely 🙁.)
"We have little time." Relax or hurry? (Hurry!)
"Most of people"? (No! Most people.)
6.3 · Model dialogue · a polite complaint

"There's a problem with my room."

Guest: Excuse me. I'm sorry to bother you, but there's a problem with my room.

Clerk: Oh, I'm sorry to hear that. What's wrong?

Guest: It's too cold. The heater is on, but it isn't warm enough. And there are only a few towels. That's not enough for three people.

Clerk: I'm so sorry. I'll send more towels right away. We have a few rooms free on the fifth floor. Would you like to move?

Guest: Yes, please. Thank you. You've been so helpful!

5 MOVES soft start → exact problem → request → sorry + offer → compliment
6.3 · Role-play · 17 min

Complaints & Compliments

  • A = customer · B = staff · plan 1 minute, don't write it all
  • A: "Excuse me, I'm sorry to bother you, but… It's too… / There aren't enough…"
  • B: "I'm so sorry. I'll… / Let me see what I can do."
  • Fixed? A gives a compliment: "You've been so helpful!" Swap cards.
Is A the customer? (Yes.)
Write the whole dialogue? (No.)
"The room is bad." Enough? (No! Say exactly what.)
What does A say at the end? (A compliment.)
A real complaint? (No. They're roles.)
