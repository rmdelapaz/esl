Instructor-Led Course Syllabus

Independent English (B1 Intermediate)

A 13-week, 26-class English course for adults who can handle everyday situations and want to go further. Twice a week for 90 minutes, you learn to tell detailed stories, give and defend your opinions, report what people said, and deal with real situations at work: discussions, complaints, and job interviews. Between classes, the free online lessons (with audio) are your homework.

Level

Class Schedule

Two classes a week, 90 minutes each. Every class has one lesson from the online course, except the two assessment classes. Lessons 4.3 and 8.3 are review projects: a story-and-opinion task at the halfway point, and a short presentation with a panel discussion at the end. Each class shows its lesson, its focus, and the ✓ can-do goal learners reach by the end.

Every Class, the Same Shape

A steady routine helps learners relax and speak. Timings flex a little from lesson to lesson.

0:10—PresentationA dialogue, story, or discussion · meaning, form & pronunciation
0:30—DrillListen and repeat: all together, then one by one
0:40—Controlled practiceWorkbook exercises, then check with a partner
0:55—Break (5 min)
1:00—SpeakingDiscussions, storytelling, role-plays, interviews, and presentations in pairs and groups
1:20—Feedback & “I can…”Fluency first, then kind error correction · tick the can-do checklist

Course Details

Prerequisites

  • CEFR A2. You can tell simple stories about the past, talk about your plans and experiences, and handle everyday situations such as shopping, travel, and the doctor’s office.
  • Finished Everyday English (A2)? You’re ready. New to our program? Take the shared placement test first.
  • Learners who are still building basic reading and writing are welcome. Every lesson has literacy-support options, and the tests have an adapted version.
  • No computer needed in class. A phone helps for homework, but it isn’t required.

What’s Provided

  • A Student Workbook for all 24 lessons, in clear B1 English (the learner keeps it)
  • A welcome handout with class and discussion ground rules, what to bring, and QR codes to the online lessons
  • For the teacher: a Teacher’s Guide (a 90-minute plan for every class), slides, flashcards, role-play and discussion cards, listening scripts, games, and answer keys
  • Free access to the companion online course: Independent English (B1), with 🔊 audio
  • A free learning-tools page that shows how to translate lessons into any language

Assessment

  • Every class: an “I can…” checklist and short speaking checks, with no grades
  • Class 13 · Progress Test 1 (Modules 1–4): listening, vocabulary, grammar, reading, a short opinion paragraph, and a 7-minute pair speaking task
  • Class 26 · Final Test (all 8 modules): the same skills, a complaint email and an opinion essay, plus an individual speaking interview with a story, a short talk, a discussion, and a role-play
  • Rubrics follow the CEFR B1 descriptors. Results decide the next class, never who gets to celebrate
  • Tasks about your life can always use a “new identity” or an imagined story instead of real details

Homework & the Online Course

  • After each class, do the matching online lesson at home: about 30 minutes, with 🔊 Listen buttons for model sentences and dialogues
  • Plus one workbook page to review the class
  • Missed a class? The online lesson covers the same language, so you can catch up
  • After the course: Confident English (B2) continues the same free ladder, from A1 all the way to C2

What You’ll Be Able to Do

By the end of the course, learners reach CEFR B1 and can use English independently, at work and in daily life:

Tell a detailed story with the right past tenses, in a clear order
Talk about experiences, and how long you have been doing things
Describe how life used to be, and how it has changed
Talk about real and imaginary situations, and what you wish were different
Report what other people said, asked, and told you to do
Describe people, places, processes, and facts in detail
Give, support, and defend an opinion, and disagree politely
Make a complaint that gets results, and handle one politely
Describe your work experience and answer job-interview questions
Give a short, well-organized presentation and take part in a discussion
