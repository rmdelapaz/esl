What IELTS Is (and Which One)

IELTS — the International English Language Testing System — measures your English across four skills for study, work, or migration. There are two versions, and choosing the right one matters:

VersionWho it's forKey difference
AcademicUniversity study; professional registrationReading & Writing use academic texts and data description (Task 1 = a graph/chart/process)
General TrainingMigration; work; secondary schoolReading & Writing use everyday/workplace texts (Task 1 = a letter)

💡 This course is for IELTS Academic

The Listening and Speaking sections are identical in both versions, so most of this course serves you either way. Only Reading and Writing differ — and we focus on the Academic form. If you need General Training, everything here still helps; just adapt Writing Task 1 to letter-writing.

You can take IELTS on paper or on a computer — the content and scoring are the same; only the interface differs. Speaking is always a face-to-face interview with a real examiner (in person or by video call), never with a machine.

The Four Sections

The whole test takes about 2 hours 45 minutes. Listening, Reading, and Writing are completed in one sitting with no breaks; Speaking may be the same day or up to a week apart.

SectionTimeWhat you do
Listening~30 min (+ transfer time on paper)4 recordings, 40 questions — conversations and monologues, everyday and academic
Reading60 min3 long academic passages, 40 questions — no extra transfer time
Writing60 minTask 1 (describe data, 150+ words, ~20 min) + Task 2 (essay, 250+ words, ~40 min)
Speaking11–14 minPart 1 (interview) + Part 2 (long turn from a cue card) + Part 3 (discussion)

⚠️ Reading has no transfer time. Unlike Listening (on paper), you get no extra minutes to copy answers in Reading — you write directly on the answer sheet as you go. Budget your 60 minutes as roughly 20 minutes per passage and move on ruthlessly.

The 9-Band Scoring System

IELTS is not pass/fail. You receive a band score from 1 to 9 (in half-band steps: 6.0, 6.5, 7.0…) for each of the four skills, plus an Overall Band Score — the average of the four, rounded to the nearest half band.

BandDescriptorRough meaning
9ExpertFully operational command; native-like
8Very goodFully operational with occasional lapses
7GoodOperational command; occasional inaccuracies
6CompetentEffective command despite some errors
5ModestPartial command; copes with overall meaning
4 & belowLimited → non-userFrequent breakdowns in communication

✅ How the overall is rounded

Average the four skills, then round to the nearest half band. Example: L 6.5, R 6.0, W 6.0, S 7.0 → average 6.375 → Overall 6.5. An average ending in .25 rounds up to the next half band; .75 rounds up to the next whole band. Every half-band on a single skill can swing your overall — which is why lifting your weakest skill is usually the fastest route up.

For Listening and Reading, a raw score out of 40 converts to a band. As a rough guide, about 30/40 is around Band 7 and 23/40 around Band 6 (the exact conversion varies slightly by test). Writing and Speaking are marked by trained examiners against four criteria each — the subject of Lessons 3.3 and 4.2.

Setting a Realistic Target

Your target isn't "as high as possible" — it's the band your institution requires, reached efficiently. Find the exact requirement before you plan.

Typical requirementCommon target
Undergraduate studyOverall 6.0–6.5
Postgraduate studyOverall 6.5–7.0
Competitive programmes / some professionsOverall 7.0–7.5+
⚠️ Watch the sub-scores. Many universities require not just an overall band but a minimum in each skill (e.g. "Overall 6.5 with no band below 6.0"). A brilliant Reading score can't rescue a Writing 5.5 if the rule says "nothing below 6.0." Check both numbers.

🧠 Mindset

IELTS is a skill you train, not a verdict on your worth. Two learners with identical English can score a band apart purely on technique — time management, question-type awareness, and knowing the mark scheme. That's the good news: much of your improvement here comes from strategy you can learn quickly, not just from years of language growth. Treat it as a game whose rules reward preparation. 🌱

The Strategic Mindset

Three principles separate candidates who score at their true level from those who score below it:

✅ 1. Manage the clock, always

IELTS is as much a test of time management as of English. In Reading, a hard question is worth exactly one mark — the same as an easy one — so never let it eat minutes you need elsewhere. Guess, mark it, move on.

✅ 2. Never leave a blank

There is no penalty for a wrong answer. An unanswered question scores zero; a guess has a chance. Before time is called, fill every gap — even a random letter on a multiple-choice item.

✅ 3. Answer the question they actually asked

Marks are lost less often to weak English than to not following instructions: exceeding the word limit ("NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS"), misreading the task, or writing off-topic in Task 2. The mark scheme rewards precision — give it exactly what it asks.

⚠️ The word-limit trap

Completion questions often say "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER." Write three words and you score zero for that item — even if the meaning is right. Count your words, and note that a hyphenated word (e.g. "well-known") counts as one, while a contraction is discouraged. Precision is the skill being tested.

🔊 Hear It & Repeat — Model Sentences

Listen to each model sentence, then say it back. Use the 🎧 Audio control (bottom-right) to choose a UK or US voice and slow the speed down. These sentences model the calm, clear English that scores well across the test.

  • The test is challenging, but with a clear strategy and steady practice, a high band is well within reach.
  • Rather than aiming for perfection, I focus on communicating my ideas clearly and accurately.
  • In my view, consistent daily practice matters far more than occasional intensive study.
  • I always read the question twice to make sure I understand exactly what is being asked.
  • By managing my time carefully in each section, I avoid rushing the final questions.

Diagnose & Plan

🏋️ Exercise 1: Format check

Objective: Lock in the essentials from memory.

  1. How many questions are in the Listening section? In the Reading section?
  2. How long is Writing, and how should you split it between the two tasks?
  3. Which section gives you no transfer time?
  4. How is the Overall Band Score calculated?
✅ Answer

1. 40 questions each. · 2. 60 minutes — about 20 on Task 1, 40 on Task 2. · 3. Reading. · 4. Average the four skill bands, rounded to the nearest half band.

🏋️ Exercise 2: Overall band maths

Objective: Practise the rounding rule.

Calculate the Overall Band Score for: Listening 7.0, Reading 6.5, Writing 6.0, Speaking 6.5.

✅ Answer

Average = (7.0 + 6.5 + 6.0 + 6.5) ÷ 4 = 26 ÷ 4 = 6.5. Overall Band 6.5. (Notice Writing 6.0 is the weak link — lifting it to 6.5 would push the average to 6.625 → Overall 6.5 still, but any further gain then tips to 7.0.)

🎯 Your Task: Write Your IELTS Plan

  1. (3 min) Find and note your exact requirement — the overall band and any per-skill minimum your institution demands.
  2. (3 min) Set a test date (real or provisional) and count the weeks you have.
  3. (3 min) Honestly rank your four skills from strongest to weakest. Your weakest skill is your priority — it moves the overall most.
  4. (3 min) Draft a one-line weekly commitment (e.g. "one full Reading practice + one Task 2 essay per week").

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Confirm your exact required bands (overall and per-skill) before you build a study plan.
  • Put your weakest skill first — it's usually the cheapest half-band to gain.
  • Practise under real time limits from early on; IELTS punishes the unprepared clock.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • Studying "English" instead of "IELTS": the test rewards specific techniques; general study alone leaves marks on the table.
  • Ignoring the mark scheme: not reading instructions, over-writing word limits, and going off-topic cost more than grammar slips.
  • Neglecting the weak skill: pouring time into a skill you're already strong at yields little; the overall is an average.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • IELTS has four sections (Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking); this course targets the Academic version.
  • Scoring runs 1–9 in half bands; your Overall is the average of the four, rounded to the nearest half band.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

How long does it take to raise my band by 0.5–1.0?

It varies, but a common rule of thumb is roughly 12 weeks of focused study per half-band at higher levels, less at lower ones. Strategy gains (technique, timing) can come faster than raw language gains (vocabulary, accuracy), which is why we front-load technique.

Is the computer test easier than paper?

Neither is easier — the content and scoring are identical. Choose by comfort: computer gives you a word count and easy editing in Writing but requires reading passages on screen; paper lets you annotate freely. Practise in your chosen format.

Do IELTS scores expire?

Results are typically valid for two years. Time your test so your score is still valid when you apply, and check your institution's own recency rules.
