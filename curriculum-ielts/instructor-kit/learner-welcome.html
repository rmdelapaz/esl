Welcome to IELTS Academic Preparation

Welcome, and thank you for joining us. Most of you are here because a number matters: a band score for a university place, for professional registration, or for a visa application. This course is designed to help you show the English you already have, under exam conditions. Over eight weeks you'll learn exactly how each part of the test works, practice every major question type, write and self-mark both Writing tasks, rehearse the Speaking interview, and sit two full practice tests so that nothing on test day is a surprise.

Some of you are engineers, nurses, teachers, or students; some of you have taken IELTS before and some have never seen a test paper. Everyone starts from a different place, and everyone is measured against their own starting point, not against the person sitting next to them.

⏱ When the clock is running

Many classes include timed practice, and three classes are full test sessions. These ground rules keep the room fair and calm for everyone, and they are close to the rules of the real test:

  • 🤫Silence once the clock starts. If you need something, raise your hand and wait for your teacher.
  • 📱Phones face down and away, and no dictionaries or translation apps during timed work.
  • ✏️Pencils down when time is called, even in the middle of a sentence. Honest timing gives you an honest result.
  • 🔁Finished early? Check your spelling, your word limits, and any blanks. Stay quiet until everyone has finished.
  • 🎲Never leave a blank. A wrong answer loses nothing, so an educated guess is always worth writing.
  • ✅Mark honestly. When we self-mark, a generous score today only hides the problem we need to fix before test day.

⏱ Aim for 45–60 minutes, 2–3 times a week, plus 15 minutes of English every day: a news article or a science feature, a podcast or a lecture, or one paragraph of writing.

The online lessons use British spelling (practise, analyse, centre, programme). In class we use American English. IELTS accepts both; the important thing is to choose one and use it consistently in a piece of writing.

📈 What to expect: honest numbers

We want you to plan with real information, so here it is:

  • With regular homework, a gain of about half a band over eight weeks is common. Some learners gain more, especially when their main problem was technique (timing, word limits, the Writing task structure). A bigger jump is possible, but it is not guaranteed, and anyone who promises one is guessing.
  • Progress is usually uneven. Listening and Reading often move first; Writing and Speaking usually move more slowly because they depend on habits built over time.
  • Scores sometimes dip for a week or two while you change your technique. That is normal, and it usually comes before a gain.
  • Our practice-test bands are estimates. They are useful for planning, but only the official test gives an official score.
  • If the gap between your diagnostic band and your target is large, your teacher will talk with you privately about a realistic test date, or about a general English course first. That is advice, not a judgment.

🌿 Keeping test nerves under control

Almost everyone feels nervous about a test that matters this much. A little tension helps you focus; too much steals your technique. These habits help:

Practice under real conditions. The more timed tests you sit here, the more ordinary the real one will feel.
Breathe slowly before each section. Breathe in for four counts and out for six, three times. It really does slow your heart rate.
Let one question go. Missed an answer in Listening? Leave it and catch the next one. One mark is only one mark.
Speaking is a conversation. The examiner wants you to talk. If you lose a word, describe it and keep going.
Sleep beats cramming. In the last few days, light review and a good night's sleep will help you more than new material.
Talk to your teacher. If worry is affecting your sleep or your practice, tell us. You are not the first, and we can help you plan.

🗓 Booking the official test

  • Check your requirement first. Ask your university, registration body, or immigration adviser for the exact score: the overall band and any minimum for each skill (for example, "6.5 overall, no skill below 6.0").
  • Choose the right version. This course prepares you for IELTS Academic. Some visa and work routes ask for General Training instead, so check before you book.
  • Book only through the official IELTS website or an authorized test center. You can usually choose a paper-based or computer-delivered test; the content and scoring are the same. Book early, because popular dates fill up.
  • Use the same ID to book and on test day, usually a passport. Check the name and date of birth carefully.
  • Be careful of scams. Nobody can sell you a guaranteed band or a certificate without the test. If an offer sounds too good to be true, it is.

This course is independent. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the IELTS partners, and fees, dates, and rules are set by them, so always check the official information before you book.
