Module 3 — Academic Writing: Answers

Answers to every Module 3 workbook exercise and self-check question, with the "why," acceptable alternatives, annotated band-7 model answers for the timed tasks (the bar-chart report, the table and process reports, the problem–solution essay), the Examiner Panel estimates with examiner-style commentary, and the errors you can expect to meet most.

Marking principle

Writing has criteria, not answers. For the closed items (trend words, prepositions, question types, which criterion), there is one best answer and the key gives it. For everything else, the models are examples of band-7 writing, not the answer. Accept any version that (1) does the job the task sets (an overview with no figures; a thesis with a position; one idea developed), (2) is accurate (figures read correctly from the chart, a rise in, rose not rised) and (3) stays in a formal, objective register. All bands in this key are estimates: say "about band 6.5," never "this is a 6.5." American English is the model; accept British spellings and forms (summarise, levelling off, programme, per cent, favour), which IELTS also accepts, as long as the learner is consistent. Contractions are not appropriate in either task.

3.1 · Writing Task 1 — Describing Data (C9)

Practice Test 1 — my results (tally)

No answers: each learner's grid is personal. Check two things as you circulate: every lost mark has a stroke somewhere (learners skip the painful ones), and the "one thing I will do" is concrete. ✘ "Practice more." ✔ "Check plurals: after each Listening section, read every answer and ask 'one or more than one?'" Raw scores convert to approximate bands with the table in assessment/answer-keys.html.

Exercise 1 — The jobs of the paragraphs (Chart 1 model)
  1. B · No.
  2. A · percentage → proportion · shows → compares · between 2004 and 2024 → at four-year intervals between 2004 and 2024 (accept any two genuine changes).
  3. By pattern, not by year: C = the starting point for all three plus Northport's rise; D = Riverton's later rise, contrasted with Eastvale's decline.
  4. Any two: declined slightly · rose steadily · climbing steeply · increased sharply · remained very low (accept remained stable as a verb + adjective pattern if the learner explains it).
  5. overtaking · drawing level with (also accept at 12% in 2012 described as "the same").
  6. No. It reports only what the chart shows. As a result in B links two facts from the chart, not a real-world cause.
Exercise 2 — Choose the accurate words

1 slightly (6 → 8) · 2 by (6 → 31 = 25 points) · 3 rise (noun after a steep) · 4 remained stable (10 → 10) · 5 rose (raise needs an object) · 6 three times (31 vs 10) · 7 respectively · 8 of (the amount; in would introduce the topic: a decline of 4 points in cycling)

Percent vs percentage points: strong learners may ask. 6% → 31% is a rise of 25 percentage points; "rose by 25%" would technically mean 6 × 1.25. Accept points or percentage points; don't penalize "by 25%" at this level, but mention it to stretch learners.

Exercise 3 — The overview (Chart 3, pie)

Model: "Overall, renewable sources generated the great majority of Lakeland's electricity in 2024, with hydroelectric power and wind together producing around three fifths of the total. Fossil fuels played a much smaller role, and coal accounted for only a small share."

Accept any overview that names the largest source(s) and the smallest or a clear contrast (renewable vs fossil), with no exact figures. Approximations (around three fifths, the majority) are acceptable in an overview; exact percentages are not. Watch for: "Overall, hydro is 34%…" (figure) · a list of all six sources · "which is good for the environment" (opinion).

Exercise 4 — Select and group (Chart 4, table)
  1. Elston (51 minutes) · Dunmore (19 minutes)
  2. Dunmore (52%)
  3. Brenton (45%)
  4. The larger the share of commuters who drove, the longer the average commute: Elston 70% / 51 min → Ashby 61% / 42 → Calder 48% / 35 → Brenton 35% / 28 → Dunmore 22% / 19. Report the pattern; don't claim the cars cause the long commutes.
  5. One good grouping: Body A Elston and Ashby (long commutes, car-dominated), with Calder as the middle case · Body B Dunmore and Brenton (short commutes; walking/cycling or public transportation as the main mode). Accept other logical groupings that are justified.

Model report (band 7, 173 words):

The table compares the average length of a one-way commute in five cities in 2024, together with the proportion of commuters who traveled by car, by public transportation and on foot or by bicycle.

Overall, the cities where driving was most common also had the longest commutes, whereas the shortest journeys were found where most people walked or cycled. Dunmore stood out as the only city in which active travel was the main mode.

Elston and Ashby had the longest average commutes, at 51 and 42 minutes respectively, and in both cities the car was by far the dominant mode, used by 70% and 61% of commuters. Walking and cycling were rare in these two cities, at 5% and 9%.

At the other end of the scale, the average journey in Dunmore took only 19 minutes, and just over half of its commuters (52%) walked or cycled. Brenton and Calder fell between these extremes, with commutes of 28 and 35 minutes; Brenton was the only city where public transportation was the most popular choice, at 45%.

Exercise 5 — A process (Chart 5)
  1. First, rain falls on the school roof and runs / flows into gutters and downpipes. (Active: rain falls and runs are intransitive, so no passive is possible. A good teaching point.)
  2. Next / Then, leaves and dirt are removed by a leaf screen.
  3. After that, the water is stored in an underground tank.
  4. Next / Then, it is pumped to a filter unit, where it is filtered and treated with UV (ultraviolet) light.
  5. Finally, the clean water is used to flush toilets and water the garden.
  6. "Overall, the process has seven stages, from rain falling on the roof to the treated water being used in the building. It is linear rather than cyclical, although surplus water leaves the system when the tank is full."

Model report (band 7, 164 words):

The diagram illustrates the process by which rainwater is collected from the roof of a school building, cleaned and then reused.

Overall, there are seven stages in the process, which begins with rain falling on the roof and ends with the treated water being used for the school's toilets and garden. It is a linear rather than a cyclical process, although excess water can leave the system at the storage stage.

First, rain falls on the school roof and runs into the gutters and downpipes. It then passes through a leaf screen, where leaves and dirt are removed. After that, the water is stored in an underground tank. If this tank becomes full, the overflow is directed into a storm drain.

At the next stage, the stored water is pumped to a filter unit, where it is filtered and then treated with ultraviolet light. Once it has been cleaned in this way, the water is finally used to flush the toilets and to water the school garden.

Watch for: is fall / is run (passive with intransitive verbs) · missing is ("the water stored in a tank") · every sentence beginning "Then" · adding what the diagram doesn't say ("so the school saves money").

Dialogue — checking the overview

Overview 1: "Overall, paid work increased from 6 to 12 hours, and lectures fell from 14 to 11." Overview 2: "Overall, students spent more time on paid work and online lessons, and less on lectures and independent study." The second is better because it gives the shape (what grew, what shrank) with no figures and groups the activities. Ravi's advice: don't give a reason ("because students need more money"); the chart doesn't show why. Report that paid work became the most time-consuming activity instead.

Chart Talk — model descriptions (Set C)

Pair 1 A (recycling): "Overall, the share of waste recycled in Hollin more than doubled. It rose gradually from 18% to 22% between 2010 and 2013, then jumped to 35% by 2016. After a small dip to 40% in 2022, it reached a high of 44% in 2024." · Pair 1 B (waste per person): "Overall, household waste per person fell steadily, from 11 kilograms a week to 8.4, with one very small rise in 2019." · Pair 2 A (news): "Phone apps were by far the most common source, used by just under half of adults. Television came second at about a fifth; websites and radio had the same share, 12%; printed newspapers were the least common, at 8%." · Pair 2 B (museum): "Visitor numbers were lowest in February, at 8,000, climbed through spring and peaked in August at 32,000, roughly four times the winter low. They then fell sharply in September, before a smaller rise in December."

Timed task — Chart 2, bar chart: annotated band-7 model (161 words)

[Introduction: paraphrase what, who, when] The bar chart compares the average number of hours per week that first-year students at one university spent on five activities in 2014 and 2024.

[Overview: grouped, no figures, the most striking feature] Overall, time spent on paid work and online video lessons rose considerably over the decade, while the hours devoted to lectures, seminars and independent study fell. The most striking change is that paid work, which had been only the third most time-consuming activity, became the largest in 2024.

[Body A: the falling group, with figures] In 2014, lectures and seminars took up the most time, at 14 hours a week, followed by independent study at 10 hours. Both declined over the period, to 11 and 8 hours respectively.

[Body B: the rising group, comparisons] By contrast, the time students spent in paid work doubled from 6 to 12 hours, overtaking lectures and seminars. The rise in online video lessons was even more dramatic in relative terms: a fivefold increase from just one hour to five. Group projects, meanwhile, changed very little, rising only slightly from 3 to 4 hours.

Why this is about band 7 for Task Achievement

The overview is explicit, grouped (rising vs falling) and figure-free, and it names the most striking feature (paid work overtaking lectures). The body paragraphs follow the overview's grouping, not the order of the bars. Every figure is accurate. Comparison language is varied (followed by, respectively, by contrast, overtaking, even more dramatic in relative terms, meanwhile) and the trend language is precise (doubled, a fivefold increase, rising only slightly). No cause is given for any change. To reach band 8, a writer might note more precisely that paid work (12 hours) exceeded lectures (11) by only one hour, and integrate the small change in group projects into the overview's pattern.

Common problems in learners' versions: overview with numbers · going bar by bar, 2014 then 2024 · "because students need money / because of the internet" (invented causes) · "paid work increased to 6 hours" (confusing to and by) · "online video lessons increased 400%" (possible, but many learners compute it wrongly; fivefold is safer) · "In 2024 year" · under 150 words because the introduction was copied.

Self-check
Q1
Which paragraph of a Task 1 report is the most important for your band, and why?
A) The introduction, because it comes first.
B) The overview: without a clear one, Task Achievement is capped around band 5.
C) The first body paragraph, because it has the most figures.
Why: the descriptors (paraphrased) link a clear overview to band 6 and above; a report that recounts details with no overview stays around band 5 for Task Achievement, however accurate the figures.
Q2
A value drops from 90 to 12 in a single year. Which is the most accurate description?
A) It declined gradually.
B) It fluctuated.
C) It plummeted / fell sharply.
Why: a very large drop in a short time needs a strong verb or adverb. Gradually is slow and small; fluctuated means up and down.
Q3 · new
Which sentence about Chart 2 is accurate and suitable for a Task 1 report?
A) Time spent on paid work rose from 6 to 12 hours, doubling over the period.
B) Paid work rised by 12 hours because students need more money.
C) I think paid work increased a lot, which is worrying.
Why: A is accurate and objective. B has a wrong verb form (rised), a wrong figure (by 6, not 12) and an invented cause. C gives an opinion and vague language.
Errors you will meet most (3.1)

No overview, or figures in it · "In conclusion" repeating the overview · describing every bar or year in order · invented causes · rised / was risen / raised · an increase of cycling · to vs by confused · the 60% of · The number of students were · present tense for past data · percent vs percentage · plural -s dropped (five activity, 12 hour) · copying the prompt word for word (copied words don't show the examiner your own language) · under 150 words. For range, praise overtook, drew level with, a fivefold increase, respectively, just under a third, and a clean past perfect (had overtaken by 2016).

Exercise 2 — Upgrade the thesis (models)
  1. I strongly agree that cooking should be a regular part of the secondary school timetable, as it gives young people a practical skill they will use every day.
  2. Although flexible hours can make teamwork harder to organize, I believe their benefits for both employees and employers are far greater.
  3. While better habits at home would help, I believe supermarkets and restaurants should carry most of the responsibility for reducing food waste.
  4. Adults are returning to education mainly because the job market is changing, and in my view this is a largely positive development.

Accept any thesis that answers the question with a position. For P1 a partial position is fine (I partly agree…, provided that…) if it is stated clearly. Watch for: "This essay will discuss…" with no position · a general statement about the topic · "I will agree" (future).

Exercise 4 — Develop a thin point (model)

[Topic sentence] One of the clearest benefits of flexible hours is that they allow working parents to balance their jobs and family responsibilities. [Why] When employees can start earlier or later, they are able to take children to school or collect them without asking for time off. [So what] As a result, they are likely to feel less stressed and more loyal to their employer, which means companies lose fewer experienced staff. [Example] A small accounting firm that lets staff choose to start at any time between 7 and 10 a.m., for instance, might find that parents who would otherwise have left stay with the company for years.

Accept a developed paragraph on any one of the four ideas (parents, less traffic, companies). The key: one idea with a why, a consequence and a specific example, not four ideas.

Exercise 5 — Model essay A
  1. "While educating households has clear value, I believe that the greater responsibility lies with supermarkets and restaurants, because their decisions shape what everyone else buys and throws away." Position: businesses should carry more of the responsibility.
  2. View A (households) = paragraph 2 · view B (businesses) = paragraph 3 · the writer's opinion = the thesis (paragraph 1) and the conclusion (paragraph 4); paragraph 3's topic sentence also signals it.
  3. "However, individual effort can only go so far when businesses encourage over-buying."
  4. "…they buy less and discard less, which also saves them money." (Also accept "a habit that may last for years" or "children who grow up in these households often learn the same habits.")
  5. No. It restates the position in new words.
  6. "While educating households has clear value…" or "teaching households to manage food better is worthwhile, but…"
Dialogue — two ways to plan

Ana forgot that the prompt has two questions ("Why is this happening?" and "Is it a positive development?"); she planned only the second. "Push it" = develop the point: ask why and so what, and add an example. Ana's thesis: "Mostly positive, as long as courses are affordable" (in an essay: …this is a largely positive development, provided that courses remain affordable).

Timed task — P3 (sitting at work): annotated band-7 model (259 words)

[Introduction: paraphrase + thesis covering both parts] In many modern jobs, employees spend seven or eight hours a day in a chair, often in front of a screen. This lifestyle creates problems for both physical and mental health, but a combination of workplace changes and personal habits could reduce its effects considerably.

[Body 1: problems. Topic sentence → why → so what → second, linked problem] The most serious problem is the impact on the body. When people sit for long periods, they burn very little energy, and over time this can contribute to weight gain, back pain and poor circulation. These conditions are not only uncomfortable for the individual; they also lead to more sick days, which affects employers as well. Long hours at a desk can also harm concentration and mood, since workers rarely move, go outside or take a genuine break from their tasks.

[Body 2: solutions. Topic sentence → two measures (employer, individual) → example → so what] Fortunately, several practical measures could address these issues. Employers could provide adjustable desks that allow staff to stand for part of the day, and they could encourage short walking breaks by holding some meetings on foot. Such changes cost relatively little but make movement a normal part of work. At the same time, individuals can take responsibility for their own routines, for instance by setting a reminder to stand up every half hour or by cycling or walking at least part of the way to work. Small habits like these, repeated daily, add up to a meaningful difference.

[Conclusion: restates both parts, no new idea] In conclusion, a largely seated working day can damage both physical and mental well-being. However, if companies make it easier to move and workers build activity into their routines, these problems can be significantly reduced.

Why this is about band 7 or above

TR: both parts of the prompt are answered in proportion, and the thesis previews both. CC: one idea per body, clear topic sentences, and cohesion carried by referencing (This lifestyle, These conditions, these issues, Such changes, Small habits like these, these problems) rather than a stack of linkers. LR: precise collocations (burn energy, poor circulation, take a genuine break, a meaningful difference). GRA: a range of complex forms (when…, not only… but also, which affects…, by setting…) with no errors. Hedging (can contribute to, could reduce) avoids overclaiming.

Timed task — P5 (flexible hours): plan and opening

Plan: Advantages–disadvantages · verdict: advantages outweigh · Body 1 (disadvantages first, then turn): harder to schedule meetings and cover customers → e.g. a team that can't meet until 11 a.m. → can be solved with "core hours" · Body 2 (advantages): staff work when they are most productive, parents balance family, less rush-hour travel → e.g. a firm that keeps experienced parents · Conclusion: with core hours, the benefits are greater.

Model introduction: A growing number of employers now allow staff to decide when they start and finish work. Although this flexibility can make teamwork harder to organize, I believe its advantages for both employees and companies clearly outweigh this drawback.

Accept either verdict, or a balanced one (the advantages outweigh the disadvantages only when…), if it is clear. Common fault: two lists and no verdict, so the question "Do the advantages outweigh…?" is never answered.

Self-check
Q1
A prompt says "Discuss both views and give your own opinion." Which approach best answers it?
A) Present both views fairly, and make your own opinion clear in the introduction and conclusion.
B) Argue strongly for the view you hold and ignore the other.
C) List as many arguments as possible from both sides and give no opinion.
Why: a discussion prompt has three jobs: view A, view B and your opinion. B misses a view; C misses the opinion. Either caps Task Response.
Q2
What is the main job of the thesis in your introduction?
A) To give a detailed example supporting one side.
B) To state your position or answer to the question clearly.
C) To copy the exam question word for word.
Why: the thesis is the answer to the whole question. Examples belong in body paragraphs; copied words from the prompt show the examiner none of your own language.
Q3 · new
"More adults are returning to education. Why is this happening? Is it a positive development?" Which plan fits best?
A) Body 1: advantages · Body 2: disadvantages · no opinion.
B) Three body paragraphs, all about why it is positive.
C) Body 1: the reasons · Body 2: whether it is positive, with a clear answer.
Why: a two-part question needs a direct answer to each question. A treats it as an advantages–disadvantages essay and gives no answer; B ignores "Why is this happening?"
Errors you will meet most (3.2)

Answering the topic, not the question (one part missing) · no position, or a position that changes · "This essay will discuss…" as the whole thesis · four listed ideas in one paragraph · a new idea in the conclusion · memorized openers ("In this modern era," "As the saying goes") · Although… but… · On the contrary for On the other hand · comma splices · people is · contractions and you in formal writing · invented statistics ("87% of experts agree") · under 250 words. For range, praise a qualified thesis (largely positive, provided that…), a concession and rebuttal (Admittedly… However…) and precise hedging (can contribute to, tend to).

3.3 · Coherence, Cohesion & the Band Criteria (C11)

Exercise 1 — Which criterion?

1 TR (no position) · 2 CC (mechanical over-linking) · 3 LR (repetition, no range) · 4 GRA (no range of structures) · 5 CC (no paragraphing) · 6 GRA (accuracy: articles) · 7 TR (a part of the task missing) · 8 LR (collocation and spelling)

Items 2 and 5 both hit CC: cohesion is about much more than "using linkers." Accept a learner's argument that 8 touches GRA (do a mistake is often discussed as grammar), but collocation and spelling belong to LR.

Exercise 2 — Fix the over-linked paragraph (model)

As more people shop online, the number of home deliveries is rising sharply. This trend has a visible cost: delivery vans increasingly fill residential streets, and much of the packaging they bring is thrown away after a single use. Companies therefore have a responsibility to find solutions, such as grouping deliveries or using reusable packaging.

Seven signposts became two or three (As, therefore, such as), with one summary noun (This trend) and ideas combined. Accept any version with 2–3 well-chosen links, at least one this / these + noun, and the empty sentences ("In conclusion, action is needed") cut or merged. Watch for: versions that simply delete the linkers and leave seven disconnected sentences.

Exercise 3 — Name it with a summary noun
  1. This trend (also This change, This development)
  2. This measure / This decision / This rule (also Such a ban)
  3. This scheme / This approach / Such a service
  4. This view / This idea / Such a proposal
  5. These trends / These changes / These developments (plural: the verb is affect)
  6. This risk / This possibility / Such a cut

Accept any noun that names the whole previous idea and agrees with the verb. ✘ "This" alone (vague) · ✘ "This measure" in 1 (nobody took an action) · ✘ "These change."

Exercise 4 — Make it coherent (model)

Order: c → d → a → b. Model: "Ready-made meals offer busy families clear savings, but at a cost. Many families now buy them to save time, and these meals are often cheaper than cooking from fresh ingredients, so the money saved can be used for other household costs. However, the food is not always as healthy as it looks."

Accept any logical topic sentence that signals the trade-off (convenience vs health). The reference (these meals / they) and one linker (However, or so) are the target.

Accept a panel's estimate within half a band of these if the panel quotes the script. The most common disagreements: (1) panels mark Script B's TR at 5 because of the blurred position and new idea in the conclusion (defensible; the commentary calls it "a low 6"); (2) panels mark Script A's TR at 6 because the counter-argument paragraph is short (the position is clear throughout and two ideas are developed, which points to 7); (3) panels over-reward Script B's CC for "using lots of linkers": this is exactly the misconception the lesson targets. Nobody should be marked "wrong" for a well-evidenced half-band difference.

My essay — four passes

No fixed answers. Check that each estimate has a quotation and each improvement is concrete: ✘ "better vocabulary" ✔ "replace three of my six importants with essential, significant, a priority" · ✘ "better grammar" ✔ "check every the number of… has a singular verb." Learners often overestimate LR and GRA and underestimate TR. When you read their grids before C12, write one line where your estimate differs by more than half a band.

Dialogue — comparing estimates

Disagreeing politely: "Why did we go lower?" · "It is short, but…" · "I'd argue the Coherence is a 5, not a 6." Accepting a point: "Fair point." · "OK, I'm convinced." · "Exactly."

Self-check
Q1
A writer begins every sentence with "Firstly, Moreover, In addition, Consequently, In conclusion." What is the likely effect on the Coherence & Cohesion band?
A) It raises the band: lots of linking words.
B) No effect: linkers aren't marked.
C) It lowers the band: the linking is mechanical and overused.
Why: the descriptors (paraphrased) reward linking that is natural and accurate and mark down overuse. Linkers are marked (B is wrong), but more is not better (A).
Q2
An essay answers a different question from the one in the prompt, but it is well organized and grammatically excellent. Which criterion is most affected?
A) Task Response
B) Coherence & Cohesion
C) Grammatical Range & Accuracy
Why: TR judges whether the essay answers this question. Organization and grammar may score well, but an off-topic response is heavily penalized under TR.
Q3 · new
"Cities face heavy traffic, poor air quality and crowded trains." Which next sentence has the clearest cohesion?
A) Moreover, this makes it essential.
B) These pressures make government action essential.
C) Cities face heavy traffic, poor air quality and crowded trains, so government action is essential.
Why: These pressures is a summary noun that points back to all three problems and names them. A uses a wrong linker (Moreover adds nothing) and two vague references (this, it). C repeats the whole sentence.
Errors you will meet most (3.3)

A linker on every sentence · Moreover / In addition used for contrast · Consequently / Therefore with no real result · a vague this / it pointing at a whole paragraph · the same noun repeated where a summary noun would do · one-block essays or very long paragraphs · no topic sentences · learners who judge the writer instead of the script · band estimates with no evidence · polishing the strongest criterion instead of the weakest. For range, praise flexible reference chains (remote work → this arrangement → such flexibility), a well-placed That said, and paragraphs that link by meaning without a signpost.
