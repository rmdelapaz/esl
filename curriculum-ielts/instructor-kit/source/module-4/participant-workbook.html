IELTS Academic Workbook · Module 4

Speaking & Test Mastery

Three lessons and a final practice test. Learn to give the examiner enough to reward: longer answers, a full two-minute talk, and a real argument in Part 3. Find out how Speaking is marked, and lift your pronunciation and vocabulary. Then plan your test day, from the clock to the nerves, and take Practice Test 2 to see how far you've come.

How this module works

Each exercise heading tells you what to do: Write, Listen, Say, Read or With a partner. In Speaking there's rarely one right answer. What matters is that you give the examiner enough English to reward, clearly and naturally.

When a task asks about your own life, you decide what to share. Use your real life, or a new identity card, or something you invent. In the real test you can invent details too: you're marked on your English, not on your life.

Read — Exercise 5: Order the Part 3 answer

The examiner asks: "Do you think people will still read printed books in the future?" Put the answer in order: opinion → reason → other view → resolution → future.

a) That said, I can see why e-readers appeal to people: you can carry hundreds of books in your pocket.

b) I'd say yes, printed books will survive, although probably in smaller numbers.

c) So perhaps the future isn't one or the other, but people choosing print for the books they really love.

d) The main reason is that a lot of readers still enjoy the feel of paper and a break from screens.

e) In the long run, I imagine printed books might even become a bit of a luxury, like vinyl records.

Order: → → → →

Now answer a different question aloud with the same five moves: "Should cities spend more money on parks?"

Read and say — Dialogue: the start of the test

Examiner: Good afternoon. My name is Ms. Rivera. Can you tell me your full name, please?

Leo: Good afternoon. It's Leonardo Costa, but everyone calls me Leo.

Examiner: Thank you. Now, in this first part, I'd like to ask you some questions about yourself. Do you work or are you a student?

Leo: I work, actually. I'm a lab assistant at a water-testing company. I check samples from the city's reservoirs, which sounds dull, but I find it surprisingly satisfying, because you can see exactly what you've achieved at the end of the day.

Examiner: Do you like the area where you live?

Leo: Mostly, yes. It's quite a close-knit neighborhood; people say hello in the street. The only drawback is the noise from the highway. If I could change one thing, I'd move that highway a mile away!

Examiner: Thank you. Now I'd like you to speak about a topic for up to two minutes. Here's your card. You have one minute to prepare first, and you can make notes.

Leo: Sorry, could I have something to write with?

Examiner: Of course. Here's some paper and a pencil. Your minute starts now.

Underline one example of each: a reason, a contrast, a hypothetical. Why is it fine for Leo to ask for something to write with?

Language box

The four Speaking criteria (each 25%), in our own words, paraphrased from the public IELTS band descriptors:

Fluency & CoherenceDo you keep going without long pauses, and are your ideas easy to follow?
Lexical ResourceHow wide and precise is your vocabulary? Do you use some less common and idiomatic words naturally? Can you paraphrase?
Grammatical Range & AccuracyDo you mix simple and complex sentences? How many errors, and do they block meaning?
PronunciationAre you easy to understand? Do you control stress, intonation and chunking? (Accent is not marked.)

Read and say — Dialogue: after the rating

Mina: OK, Tomás, I've got your bands. Want the good news first?

Tomás: Please. I'm a bit nervous about this.

Mina: Fluency, I'd say a 7. You kept going for the whole two minutes and you used "That said" really naturally.

Tomás: Really? It felt like I was rambling.

Mina: Not at all. Lexical Resource is more like a 6. You said "nice" three times, but "a heavy workload" was great.

Tomás: Fair enough. What about pronunciation?

Mina: Clear, easy to follow. Maybe a 6 or a 7. Your lists were a bit flat, though. Everything stayed at the same level.

Tomás: So the voice should go up, up, and then down at the end?

Mina: Exactly. And one target: swap "nice" for something more precise.

Tomás: OK. Let me try the first thirty seconds again.

How does Mina give her evidence? Underline one quote she uses for each criterion. Practice the dialogue, then use the same language when you rate a partner.
