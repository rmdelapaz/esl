C · Speaking-Sample Interviewer Card (C1)

Interviewer card · 3 minutes

How to run the diagnostic speaking sample

  1. Say: "This is a short practice conversation, about three minutes. There are no right or wrong answers. I'd just like to hear you speak."
  2. Use the questions on the Speaking page of the diagnostic paper (assessment/diagnostic.html), in order. About one minute of familiar questions, then the longer question.
  3. Neutral follow-ups only: "Why?" · "Can you give me an example?" · "Tell me a little more." Don't correct, don't nod "yes" to every answer, don't finish sentences.
  4. If the learner freezes: wait five seconds, then repeat the question once, more slowly. Then move on: "That's fine. Let's try another one."
  5. At three minutes: "Thank you. That's all." Smile. The learner goes back to the writing sample and gets the missed minutes.
  6. After the learner leaves (never in front of them), note one or two words for each area: Fluency & Coherence · Lexical Resource · Grammatical Range & Accuracy · Pronunciation. Estimate a band range with the paraphrased criteria in assessment/answer-keys.html.

At home option: a friend or family member reads the questions, or the learner reads each question aloud and then answers. Record on a phone and bring it to class.

D · Format Information Gap — A/B Cards (C2)

One A/B pair per two learners. A has the facts for Listening and Writing; B for Reading and Speaking. They ask and answer to complete Workbook 1.1, Exercise 2. No showing cards.

Student A

You know about Listening and Writing

Listening: about 30 minutes · 4 recordings (conversations and talks) · 40 questions · you hear each recording once · on paper there is extra time at the end to copy answers · spelling counts.

Writing: 60 minutes · 2 tasks · Task 1: describe a graph, chart, table, map or process in 150+ words (about 20 min) · Task 2: an essay of 250+ words (about 40 min) · Task 2 counts twice as much · too few words lose marks.

Ask B about: Reading and Speaking: How long? How many parts or questions? What do you do? Any warning?

Student B

You know about Reading and Speaking

Reading: 60 minutes · 3 long academic passages · 40 questions · many question types · no extra time to copy answers · plan about 20 minutes per passage.

Speaking: 11–14 minutes · 3 parts: Part 1 interview about familiar topics · Part 2 a talk from a cue card (1 minute to prepare, up to 2 minutes to speak) · Part 3 a discussion of more abstract questions · always with a real examiner · don't memorize answers.

Ask A about: Listening and Writing: How long? How many parts or questions? What do you do? Any warning?

D · Myth or Fact? Cards (C2)

One set of 12 per group of 3. Groups sort into MYTH / FACT / NOT SURE. The small gray answer on each card is for you: fold it back or cut it off before class. The full explanations are in the Module 1 answer key.

Card 1

IELTS is pass or fail.

MYTH: a band for each skill + an overall band; institutions set their own requirement.
Card 2

A wrong answer in Listening or Reading loses you a mark.

MYTH: no penalty. Never leave a blank.
Card 3

Spelling counts in Listening and Reading answers.

FACT: misspelled = wrong. British or American spelling both fine.
Card 4

The computer test is easier than the paper test.

MYTH: same content, same scoring.
Card 5

Writing Task 2 is worth more than Task 1.

FACT: twice as much. About 20 + 40 minutes.
Card 6

If you write fewer than 150 / 250 words, you can lose marks.

FACT: under-length answers lose marks for the task criterion.
Card 7

You need a British or American accent to get a high Speaking band.

MYTH: being easy to understand matters, not a particular accent.
Card 8

Memorized answers are a good way to get a high Speaking band.

MYTH: examiners notice rehearsed answers. Prepare ideas and phrases, not scripts.
Card 9

Using as many long, rare words as possible raises your band.

MYTH: precise, natural vocabulary scores. A misused rare word costs marks.
Card 10

The examiner gives marks for having the "right" opinion in Task 2.

MYTH: any clear, well-supported position can score well.
Card 11

The overall band is the average of the four skills, rounded to the nearest half band.

FACT: .25 → up to .5 · .75 → up to the next whole band.
Card 12

A hyphenated word such as "well-known" counts as one word.

FACT: one word in completion answers.

E · Paraphrase Relay — Prompt Cards (C3)

One set per pair. A reads the prompt aloud; B has 30 seconds, then restates it in one or two sentences. A checks the three don't repeat words. All prompts are original and written for this course. The gray model is for you: fold it back before class.

Relay · 1

Cycle lanes

"Some people think that city streets should give more space to bicycles, even if this means less space for cars. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"

Don't repeat: streets · space · cars

It is sometimes argued that urban roads ought to devote more room to cyclists, even at the expense of drivers.
Relay · 2

School gardens

"Many schools now have a vegetable garden. Some people believe every school should have one. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"

Don't repeat: schools · garden · every

A growing number of educational institutions grow their own produce, and it is sometimes suggested that this should become standard for all of them.
Relay · 3

Online degrees

"Some people believe that a degree completed entirely online is as valuable as a degree from a traditional university. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"

Don't repeat: degree · online · valuable

There is some debate about whether a qualification earned entirely over the internet is worth as much as one from a traditional campus.
Relay · 4

The four-day week

"A number of companies are trying a four-day working week. What are the benefits and drawbacks of this for employees and employers?"

Don't repeat: companies · four-day · benefits

Several firms are experimenting with a shorter working week, which raises the question of what advantages and disadvantages it brings for staff and businesses.
Relay · 5

Museum fees

"Some people think that public museums should be free for everyone to visit. To what extent do you agree or disagree?"

Don't repeat: museums · free · visit

Many people believe that there should be no admission charge for publicly funded galleries and exhibitions.
Relay · 6

Screen time

"Children today spend more time on screens than ever before. What problems does this cause, and what can be done about them?"

Don't repeat: children · screens · problems

Young people now devote more time to digital devices than any previous generation, which raises concerns about the consequences and possible solutions.
Relay · 7

Shared workspaces

"More and more people work in shared offices instead of at home or in a company building. Why is this happening? Is it a positive development?"

Don't repeat: people · shared · offices

A growing number of workers are choosing co-working spaces over both their homes and traditional company premises, and it is worth considering why and whether this trend is beneficial.
Relay · 8

Car-free weekends

"Some cities have closed their centers to cars on weekends. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?"

Don't repeat: cities · centers · cars

Several urban areas now ban private vehicles from their downtown districts on Saturdays and Sundays, a policy whose benefits and drawbacks deserve examination.

E · No-Echo Examiner Cards (C3)

A is the examiner. B answers in 2–3 sentences without repeating the question's key words. A taps the table once for each echo. A new identity card's hometown is always fine.

Examiner card · 1

Part 1 questions

  1. Do you work, or are you a student?
  2. What do you like most about your job or your studies?
  3. Do you enjoy cooking?
  4. How often do you use public transport?

Part 1 questions

  4. Which apps do you use most on your phone?

H · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher only)

Read at a natural speed. For the exam-style scripts (1.2-A), read once for the first listening, as in the real test. Where there are two speakers, change your voice a little or read with a confident learner. All scripts are original.

1.1-A · The results conversation (Workbook 1.1, Ex 1 · read twice)

Adviser: Hi, Amal, come in. I've got your practice-test results here. Shall we go through them? · Amal: Yes, please. I'm a bit nervous. · Adviser: There's some good news. Listening first: seven point oh. That's a strong result. · Amal: Really? Seven? · Adviser: Seven. Now, Reading. · Amal: I think it was six point five. · Adviser: Let me check… no, it's six this time. Six point oh. You left four questions blank at the end of the last passage, so the problem there is time, not your English. · Amal: Oh. And Writing? · Adviser: Writing was five and a half. Your ideas were good, but your Task 2 essay was short, only about two hundred words. · Amal: And Speaking? · Adviser: Six point five. The examiner's notes say you were fluent and easy to understand. · Amal: So what's my overall? My friend got seven overall last month. · Adviser: You can work that out yourself: you know the rule. What does your university need? · Amal: Six point five overall, and no band below six. · Adviser: Then you know which skill comes first. · Amal: Writing. And finishing Reading on time.

Answers: L 7.0 · R 6.0 · W 5.5 · S 6.5 · average 6.25 → overall 6.5 · requirement 6.5, no band below 6.0 · priority: Writing. Traps: Amal's "six point five" for Reading (corrected) and the friend's "seven overall." Ask: "Is Amal's Reading problem language or technique?" (Technique: time and blanks.)

1.1-B · Dictation: numbers and scores (C2 bands stage · read each sentence 3 times)

1. The whole test takes about two hours and forty-five minutes. · 2. My target is seven point five overall, with no band below seven. · 3. My test is on the thirteenth, not the thirtieth. · 4. The workshop costs fifteen dollars, not fifty. · 5. Thirty out of forty is roughly band seven.

Check: learners write numbers as figures (2 hours 45 minutes · 7.5 · 7 · 13th / 30th · $15 / $50 · 30/40). Point out the stress: thirTEEN / THIRty, fifTEEN / FIFty. Ask: "Which ones would be a trap in a Listening form?" (3 and 4.)

1.2-A · The workshop registration (Workbook 1.2, Ex 1 · read ONCE, then again for the traps)

Receptionist: Student Learning Center, how can I help? · Student: Hi, I'd like to sign up for the note-taking workshop, please. · Receptionist: "Note-taking for Lectures"? Sure. We had two sessions planned, Tuesday and Thursday, but I'm afraid the Tuesday session won't be going ahead: not enough people signed up. So it's Thursday. · Student: Thursday's fine. What time does it start? · Receptionist: It used to start at four, but there's a class in the room before it now, so it starts at half past four. · Student: Four thirty. And where is it? · Receptionist: In the Harper Building, Room 21… sorry, no, that was last term. It's Room 12. · Student: Room 12. Do I need to bring anything? A notebook? · Receptionist: You don't need a notebook, because we'll give you paper for the practice, but please do bring a laptop. Part of the session is about typing your notes. · Student: OK. Is there a charge? · Receptionist: The full price is twenty dollars, but for students it's fifteen. · Student: Great, I'm a student here. · Receptionist: Can I have your family name? · Student: It's Okafor. O-K-A-F-O-R. · Receptionist: O-K-A-F-O-R. Thank you. You're registered.

Answers: 1 Thursday · 2 4:30 / half past four · 3 Room 12 · 4 (a) laptop · 5 $15 · 6 Okafor. Traps: Tuesday ("won't be going ahead" = canceled) · four ("used to") · Room 21 · notebook · $20 (full price). Ask: "Which paraphrase told you Tuesday was canceled?" ("won't be going ahead.") "Did you hear the word canceled?" (No!)

1.2-B · Dictation: academic chunks (C3 word-families stage or homework check · read each sentence 3 times)

1. A growing number of students acquire new skills online. · 2. Traffic noise has a detrimental effect on sleep. · 3. The graph illustrates a substantial increase in sales. · 4. A poor diet can give rise to a range of health problems.

Check the partner words (acquire skills, a detrimental effect on, illustrates… an increase in, give rise to) and the stress on de-tri-MEN-tal, sub-STAN-tial, IL-lus-trates. Then pairs paraphrase one sentence aloud, climbing two rungs (for example, 2 → "Sleep is often disturbed by the sound of vehicles.").

1.3-A · Three answers to one Part 3 question (Workbook 1.3, Ex 1 · read each answer once)

Examiner: Why do you think more people are choosing to live in cities? · Candidate A: Many people move to cities. There are more jobs there. Cities have good hospitals and schools. Young people like the entertainment. I think this will continue. · Candidate B: Although cities have more jobs, but the living cost is very high, which make many people to think twice. If people will not find a good job, they would go back to their village. Nowadays the number of families who moves to the city are increasing because of they want better education for children. · Candidate C: I'd say the main reason is work. Because most well-paid jobs are concentrated in cities, young people in particular tend to move there after graduating. Cities also offer better hospitals and universities, which matters a lot to families. That said, if housing were cheaper, I think even more people would make the move, since rents are far higher in cities than in the countryside.

Answer: C (subordinate clauses with because, since; non-defining relative which; second conditional; comparative far higher than; no errors). A: accurate, simple. B: ambitious, with errors that blur meaning ("Although…, but," "make… to think," "If people will not… would," "the number of families who moves… are," "because of they want"). Ask: "Which candidate would you rather be next month: A or B?" (Either can reach C. A needs range; B needs accuracy.)

1.3-B · Dictation: complex frames (C4 drill stage or C5 warmer · read each sentence 3 times)

1. Although online learning is flexible, it can feel isolating. · 2. Students who work part-time often manage their time well. · 3. If fees were lower, more people would enroll. · 4. What is clear is that demand will keep growing.

Ask: "Which structure is in each sentence?" (1 subordinate clause · 2 defining relative clause · 3 second conditional · 4 noun clause.) Check that learners hear the pause after the although clause, and that nobody wrote "…, but it can feel isolating."
