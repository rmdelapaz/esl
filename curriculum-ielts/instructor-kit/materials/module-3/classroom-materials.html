D · Plan Pitch — Prompt Cards & Listener Checklist (3.2)

All prompts are original practice prompts on neutral topics. P1–P5 match Workbook 3.2, Exercise 1; P6–P8 are new. Plan for 2 minutes, pitch for 1 minute, then swap.

Prompt P1 · opinion

Some people believe that all high school students should learn to cook as part of their regular schedule. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Prompt P2 · discussion

Some people think the best way to reduce food waste is to teach households how to plan, store and cook food better. Others believe supermarkets and restaurants should be made responsible for cutting waste. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Prompt P3 · problem–solution

Many people now spend most of their working day sitting down. What problems does this cause, and what measures could be taken to solve them?

Prompt P4 · two-part

More and more adults are returning to education in their thirties and forties. Why is this happening? Is it a positive development?

Prompt P5 · advantages–disadvantages

In some companies, employees can choose their own working hours. Do the advantages of this outweigh the disadvantages?

Prompt P6 · opinion

Some people say that museums and galleries should be free for everyone to enter. To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Prompt P7 · problem–solution

In many cities, the number of people cycling is increasing, but cycling accidents are also becoming more common. What are the causes of this, and what can be done to make cycling safer?

Prompt P8 · discussion

Some people think that children learn best when they spend most of their day outdoors. Others believe that classroom learning is more effective. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.

Cut the type label off the P-cards for a harder version: the speaker must name the type from the instruction words.

Listener checklist
  • ☐ The type is right (check the instruction words).
  • ☐ Every part of the prompt is answered.
  • ☐ The position is clear (for a discussion: their own view too).
  • ☐ One idea per body paragraph.
  • ☐ Each idea has a because and a specific example.
  • ☐ The conclusion adds no new idea.

Ask "So what?" once, about the weakest point.

E · Examiner Panel — Sample Scripts (3.3)

Three anonymous, invented scripts written for this workshop. All answer the same prompt. Copy one set per learner. Errors in the scripts are deliberate: do not correct them. Word counts are given, as an examiner would check length.

The prompt (original practice prompt)

In many towns and cities, public libraries now offer services such as job-search support, quiet study spaces and loans of laptops, and they spend less of their budget on books. Is this a positive or negative development? Write at least 250 words.

Script A · 254 words

Nowadays, many public libraries are offering new services like help with finding a job, quiet places to study and lending laptops, while they spend less money on books. In my opinion, this is a positive development, although it has some disadvantages.

Firstly, these services help people who cannot afford technology at home. For example, a person who is looking for a job need a computer and internet to apply online, and many companies only accept online applications now. If the library can lend a laptop and a staff member can check their CV, this person has a better chance to find work. Also, students who live in small or crowded homes can use the quiet study rooms, which helps them to concentrate and make a research for their assignments.

Secondly, libraries must change because people read in a different way today. Many young people prefer to read on their phones or listen to audiobooks. Therefore, if libraries only buy books, less people will visit them and they may close. By offering modern services, they attract new visitors, and these visitors may borrow books too.

On the other hand, some people, especially elderly people, may be disappointed because there are fewer new books. However, I think this problem is not so serious, because libraries can still share books between branches.

In conclusion, I believe that the new services are a positive change because they help people with jobs and study and keep libraries useful in the modern world. Libraries should find a balance, but the change is mostly good.

Script B · 257 words

In this modern era of technology, libraries are changing a lot in many different ways. Nowadays many libraries offer services like job search help, study space and borrowing laptops, and they buy less books than before. In my opinion this is a positive development but it also has some negative sides.

Firstly, the library is very important for people who dont have a computer at home. For example, if people want to find a job, they need internet to apply. Many people cannot pay for internet at home because it is expensive. Moreover, the library can help them to write their CV. In addition, students need a quiet place for studying because their house is noisy. Furthermore, borrowing a laptop is very useful for students. So the library is very important for the society.

On the other hand, books are also very important. Books are good for relax and many people enjoy to read in a quiet place. Moreover, if the library buys less books, people can not read the new books. Children need to read books to improve their english and imagination. In addition, some old people dont like technology and they only want to read books. Consequently, they will stop to go to the library and this is a big problem for them.

In conclusion, I think the new services are good because they help many people, but libraries should not forget books because books are important too. The goverment should give more money to libraries so they can do both things and help everybody in the community.

Script C · 272 words

Public libraries were once defined almost entirely by their collections, but many are now reinventing themselves as community hubs that offer job-search support, study space and loans of laptops or tablets. In my view, this shift is largely positive, provided that libraries do not abandon the reading altogether.

The strongest argument for the change is that it directs public money toward the people who need it most. A job seeker without a home computer, for instance, is at a serious disadvantage when almost every vacancy requires an online application, and a librarian who can help them draft a résumé may make the difference between an interview and silence. Similarly, a teenager sharing a small apartment with younger siblings may have nowhere quiet to study. For both, the library offers something a bookstore never could: free, trusted support in a calm setting.

Moreover, these services are likely to strengthen, rather than weaken, the library's traditional role. Visitors who come in to print a form or use a study booth often passes the new-arrivals shelf on their way out, and some will leave with a novel they had not planned to borrow. Seen this way, the new services act as a doorway to reading, not a replacement for it.

That said, the trend carries a real risk. If book budgets shrink too far, collections could become outdated, readers who depend on printed material, particularly older people and young children, would lose out. Libraries therefore need to protect a minimum level of spending on books.

Overall, I believe the benefits of this development clearly outweigh its drawbacks, as long as reading remains at the heart of what libraries do.

E · Examiner Panel — Role Cards & Commentary

Groups of 4. Rotate roles for each script. Use the criteria table in Workbook 3.3. Report a whole band (5, 6, 7 or 8) and one quotation as evidence.

Role · TR examiner

Task Response

  • Does it answer this question (positive or negative)?
  • Is the position clear from the introduction to the conclusion?
  • Are the ideas developed (why, so what, example) or only listed?
  • Any new idea in the conclusion? Is it 250+ words?
Role · CC examiner

Coherence & Cohesion

  • One clear idea per paragraph? Topic sentences?
  • Circle every linker at the start of a sentence. Too many? Used correctly?
  • Underline reference words (this, these + noun, it). Clear, or vague?
  • Any repetition that referencing could fix?
Role · LR examiner

Lexical Resource

  • Range: precise and less common words? Natural collocations?
  • Repetition: which words appear again and again?
  • Errors: spelling, word form, wrong collocation. Do they make the reader work?
  • Any memorized phrases that say nothing?
Role · GRA examiner

Grammatical Range & Accuracy

  • Range: complex sentences (if, which, although, participle clauses)?
  • Count the sentences with no errors. Most, many, or few?
  • Errors: agreement, articles, verb forms, punctuation (comma splices).
  • Do the errors ever hide the meaning?

Keep face down until every panel has committed. One copy per group. The bands are estimates by an experienced reader, not official scores; two trained examiners can differ by half a band. Panels may disagree if they can quote the script.

Examiner commentary · Script A · overall about band 6.5

Task Response: 7. The script answers the question directly and states a position early ("In my opinion, this is a positive development, although it has some disadvantages"), which is maintained through to the conclusion. The two main ideas (access to technology and study space; libraries needing to adapt) are extended with relevant examples, such as the job seeker who must apply online. The disadvantage is raised but dismissed quickly ("libraries can still share books between branches"), and this under-developed paragraph keeps the response from band 8.

Coherence & Cohesion: 6. The essay is clearly organized, with one idea per body paragraph and a logical progression. Cohesive devices are used, but somewhat mechanically (Firstly … Also … Secondly … Therefore … On the other hand … However), and "Therefore" in paragraph 3 signals a result that doesn't follow directly from the previous sentence. Referencing is attempted and mostly clear ("these services," "this person," "these visitors").

Lexical Resource: 6. The vocabulary is adequate for the task, with some less common items ("afford," "crowded homes," "attract new visitors"). There are errors in collocation and word form ("make a research," "less people," "a better chance to find work"), and some repetition ("people," "services"), though meaning is always clear.

Grammatical Range & Accuracy: 7. A variety of complex structures is used with good control: conditionals ("If the library can lend a laptop…, this person has a better chance…"), relative clauses ("which helps them to concentrate") and a participle phrase ("By offering modern services"). Most sentences are error-free; the main slip is subject–verb agreement ("a person who is looking for a job need").

(7 + 6 + 6 + 7) ÷ 4 = 6.5. Next step: replace the list of signposts with a topic sentence plus referencing, and develop the counter-argument with a reason and an example.

Examiner commentary · Script B · overall about band 5.5

Task Response: 6. The script addresses the question and considers both the positive and the negative sides, and a position is given ("this is a positive development but it also has some negative sides"). However, the position is blurred by the end, and the conclusion introduces a new idea ("The goverment should give more money to libraries"). Ideas are mostly listed rather than developed; only one example is given, and it is general. This is a low 6; a second examiner could reasonably give 5.

Coherence & Cohesion: 5. There is paragraphing and some overall organization, but cohesive devices are overused and sometimes inaccurate: almost every sentence in paragraphs 2 and 3 begins with a linker, "Moreover" follows "On the other hand" without adding to it, and "Consequently" introduces something that is not a result. A lack of referencing leads to repetition ("the library … the library …," "books … books").

Lexical Resource: 5. The range is limited and repetitive ("important" appears five times, "very" often), and the opener "In this modern era of technology" is a memorized phrase that adds nothing. There are noticeable errors in spelling ("dont," "goverment," "english") and word choice ("less books," "negative sides"). They do not stop the reader understanding, but they are frequent.

Grammatical Range & Accuracy: 6. Sentences are mostly simple or compound, with some complex forms ("if the library buys less books, people can not read…," "because their house is noisy"). There are errors, especially with verb patterns ("stop to go," "enjoy to read," "good for relax") and articles ("for the society"), but meaning is generally clear.

(6 + 5 + 5 + 6) ÷ 4 = 5.5. Next step: cut most of the linkers, choose one idea per body paragraph and develop it with a why and an example, and keep the conclusion to the position already stated.

Examiner commentary · Script C · overall about band 7.5

Task Response: 8. The script answers the question fully with a clear, qualified position ("largely positive, provided that…"), maintained to the conclusion. The main ideas are well developed and supported with specific, relevant examples (the job seeker without a computer, the teenager in a small apartment, visitors who pass the new-arrivals shelf). The risk is acknowledged and answered with a concrete limit. The risk paragraph could be developed a little further.

Coherence & Cohesion: 7. Ideas progress logically, and each paragraph has one clear central idea introduced by a topic sentence. Referencing is flexible and precise ("For both," "Seen this way," "the trend," "these services"). Paragraph-initial linkers ("Moreover," "That said," "Overall") are appropriate but slightly formulaic, and the comma splice in paragraph 4 weakens the link between two ideas.

Lexical Resource: 8. A wide range of vocabulary is used fluently and precisely: "reinventing themselves as community hubs," "directs public money toward," "at a serious disadvantage," "a doorway to reading, not a replacement for it," "lose out." Collocations are natural, and there are no noticeable lexical errors.

Grammatical Range & Accuracy: 7. A wide variety of complex structures is used (conditionals, participle clauses, "provided that," "rather than," embedded relative clauses), and most sentences are error-free. There are, however, a few errors: an unnecessary article ("abandon the reading"), subject–verb agreement ("Visitors … often passes") and a comma splice ("collections could become outdated, readers who depend…").

(8 + 7 + 8 + 7) ÷ 4 = 7.5. Next step: proofread for agreement and sentence boundaries in the last five minutes; for CC 8, let some paragraphs begin with a topic sentence rather than a linker.

G · Listening & Dictation Scripts (teacher reads aloud)

3.1-A · The cycling graph (C9 guided practice · learners look at Chart 1 in Workbook 3.1)

Listen and look at Chart 1. I'm going to describe it, but I'll make two mistakes with the figures. When you hear a mistake, raise your hand. · The graph shows the percentage of commuters who cycled to work in three cities between 2004 and 2024. Overall, cycling became much more popular in Northport and Riverton, while it fell slightly in Eastvale. In 2004, Eastvale had the highest rate, at 14 percent. Northport started at just 6 percent, but its figure rose steadily and then steeply, reaching 35 percent in 2024. Riverton remained very low until 2012, then rose sharply, drawing level with Eastvale at 11 percent in 2016. Eastvale's rate declined gradually to 8 percent in 2020 and then remained stable.

Mistakes: Northport reached 31% (not 35%) · Eastvale fell to 10% (not 8%). Ask: "Which sentence was the overview? Did it have a number?" (The second sentence; no.)

3.1-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. Overall, cycling became far more common in two of the three cities. · 2. There was a sharp rise in paid work between 2014 and 2024. · 3. Hydroelectric power accounted for roughly a third of the total. · 4. After that, the water is stored in an underground tank.

Check: far more common (not "more commoner"), a sharp rise in, accounted for, the passive is stored. Stress: an INcrease vs to inCREASE if a learner asks.

3.2-A · Two students planning (C10 guided practice · two voices, or with a confident learner)

Tomás: OK, the prompt says: "Many people now spend most of their working day sitting down. What problems does this cause, and what measures could be taken to solve them?" · Aiko: So what's your plan? · Tomás: I'm going to write about health problems. Back pain, weight gain, stress. Three paragraphs, one for each. · Aiko: That's a lot of problems. What about the second question? · Tomás: Which second question? · Aiko: "What measures could be taken to solve them?" If you only write about problems, you've answered half the prompt. · Tomás: Oh no, you're right. So one body paragraph for problems and one for solutions? · Aiko: Exactly. And pick the most serious problem and develop it properly, instead of listing three. · Tomás: Physical health, then. Because we burn very little energy when we sit, which leads to back pain and more sick days. · Aiko: Good. And a solution? · Tomás: Adjustable desks, and short walking meetings. For example, a small office that holds its Monday meeting on foot. · Aiko: That's a plan.

Ask: "What did Tomás forget?" (The second part: the solutions.) "What was wrong with three problem paragraphs?" (Listing; no room to develop; half the question missing.) "What's the type?" (Problem–solution.)

3.2-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. While educating households has clear value, I believe businesses bear greater responsibility. · 2. The most serious problem is the effect on physical health. · 3. This is because the body burns very little energy when we sit. · 4. As a result, workers may take more sick days.

Ask which sentence is a thesis (1), a topic sentence (2), a "why" (3), a "so what" (4). Check while (not "while that"), and effect (noun) vs affect (verb).

3.3-A · One paragraph, two versions (C11 strategy stage · read Version 1 in a flat, list-like voice, Version 2 naturally)

Version 1: Firstly, cycling to work saves money. Moreover, it is good for health. In addition, it reduces traffic. Furthermore, it causes no pollution. Consequently, many cities support it. Therefore, it is a positive trend. In conclusion, cycling has many advantages. · Version 2: Cycling to work offers several practical benefits. It costs almost nothing once a bicycle has been bought, and regular riders tend to be fitter as a result. These advantages extend beyond the individual: every commuter who cycles means one less car in the morning traffic and no exhaust fumes. It is not surprising, then, that many cities now actively support this trend.

Ask: "How many sentence-initial linkers in Version 1?" (Seven.) "In Version 2?" (Almost none.) "Which sounds more skilled? What does Version 2 use instead?" (Referencing: These advantages, this trend; combined ideas; one idea developed.)

3.3-B · Dictation (read each sentence 3 times)

1. Governments could raise fuel taxes. This measure would reduce car use. · 2. These pressures make government action essential. · 3. Such an approach would cost relatively little. · 4. However, the trend carries a real risk.

Ask learners to circle the summary noun in each sentence (measure, pressures, approach, trend) and say what it could refer to.
