1.1 · Strategy · where marks really go

Three principles

  • 1 Manage the clock. A hard question = 1 mark = an easy question. Guess, mark it, move on.
  • 2 Never leave a blank. No penalty for a wrong answer.
  • 3 Answer what they asked. Word limits, the task, the topic.
NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER. "the old library"? (3 words = zero → "old library".) "15 May"? (OK.) "well-known"? (One word.)

1.2 · Lead-in · find the answer

Question: "The museum's income fell after the entry fee was removed."

A"The museum removed several paintings after its income fell."
B"Once admission became free, the gallery saw its revenue decline."
C"Entry fees at the museum rose sharply last year."
Which one matches? (B.) Which words repeat the question? (None!) Why are A and C tempting? (They share words: museum, removed, income, fell, entry fee.)

1.3 · Lead-in · rank them for grammar

Which paragraph scores higher?

AMany people use buses. Buses are cheap. They reduce traffic. Cities should buy more buses.
BAlthough buses are cheap, but many people prefer drive their cars. The number of passengers are falling in many cities. Buses, which are cheaper than cars, reduce traffic. If cities will buy more buses, people would use them.
CAlthough buses are cheaper than cars, passenger numbers are falling in many cities. If cities invested in faster, more frequent services, more people would leave their cars at home, which would reduce traffic.
A: range? (Low.) Accuracy? (High.) · B: how many error-free sentences? (1 of 4.) · C: range and control. That's the target.
