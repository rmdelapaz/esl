3.1 · Lead-in · five seconds only

That one sentence is the overview. "Overall, …" · the big picture · no numbers.
3.1 · Strategy · 150+ words · about 20 minutes

Four paragraphs, four jobs

1 Introductionparaphrase the task: what · where · whenThe line graph compares the proportion of…
2 Overviewthe 1–2 biggest features · no figuresOverall, cycling became far more common in…
3 Body Afigures, groupedIn 2004, 14% … compared with just 6% …
4 Body Bfigures, grouped + comparisonsRiverton followed a similar but later pattern…
CAREFUL No opinions · no reasons the chart doesn't show · no conclusion (the overview does that job)

3.2 · Lead-in · same topic, same essay?

What's the job?

  • People work longer hours than before. To what extent do you agree that this is a problem?
  • Some say long hours help careers; others say they harm health. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
  • Why do people work long hours, and what can be done about it?
  • Do the advantages of long hours outweigh the disadvantages?
3.2 · Strategy · read the instruction words · count the parts

Five question types

"Discuss both views…": how many jobs? (Three.) Only the view you agree with? (Task Response capped.) "Outweigh": two lists enough? (No: a verdict.)

3.2 · Language · paragraph architecture

One idea, pushed: "So what?"

✘ Thin: Working from home is convenient. It saves time. People like it. Companies benefit.
(four claims · no proof)
Topic sentenceThe most serious problem is the effect on health.
Why?This is because the body burns very little energy…
So what?…, which means more sick days.
ExampleA call-center worker, for example, …
"…which means workers start the day rested." Why or so what? (So what: a consequence.) Must the example be a real statistic? (No: specific and plausible. Never invent exact data.)

3.2 · Plan Pitch · then Timed: 5 min plan + 15 min writing

Say the plan. Then write the opening.

Ana: I'm going to write about why it's good.
Ben: "Why is this happening? Is it positive?" That's two questions.
Ana: Reasons: jobs are changing, so people need new skills.
Ben: Push it. Why? So what?
Ana: Technology replaces some tasks, which means a second career. For example, a bank clerk retraining as a nurse.

3.3 · Coherence = order · Cohesion = links

The over-linking trap

✘ Mechanical
Firstly, cycling saves money. Moreover, it is healthy. In addition, it reduces traffic. Furthermore, it causes no pollution. Consequently, cities support it.
✔ Natural
Cycling to work offers several practical benefits. It costs almost nothing, and regular riders tend to be fitter. These advantages extend beyond the individual: … Many cities now support this trend.
More linkers = higher band? (No: overuse is marked down.) What does version 2 use instead? (Reference: summary nouns; combined ideas.) No paragraphs: which criterion? (CC, capped around 5.)
3.3 · The most useful cohesive move

this / these + summary noun

✘ RepeatsGovernments could raise fuel taxes. Raising fuel taxes would reduce car use.
✔ RefersGovernments could raise fuel taxes. This measure would reduce car use.
Nounstrend · measure · approach · problem · change · view · risk · pressures
"Cities face traffic, pollution and crowded trains. This makes action essential." Clear? (Vague.) Better? (These pressures make action essential.)
3.3 · Examiner Panel · 30 min · groups of 4

Judge the script, not the writer

  • Three anonymous scripts · one prompt (public libraries)
  • One criterion each · rotate every script
  • A band (5–8) + one quotation as evidence
  • Average → overall estimate · then the reveal
How many criteria per person? (One.)
What goes with every band? (A quotation.)
Is the commentary the "right answer"? (An expert estimate: disagree with evidence.)
When do we turn it over? (After every panel commits.)
