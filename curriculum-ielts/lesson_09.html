🔗 Lesson 3.3: Writing — Coherence, Cohesion & Band Descriptors

You've learned to structure a Task 2 essay and build an argument. Now let's open the examiner's marking sheet. IELTS Writing is scored on four criteria, each worth a quarter of your band. Understand what each one rewards — and you stop writing in the dark and start writing to the mark scheme. This lesson demystifies marking, zooms in on Coherence & Cohesion, and hands you a rubric to score your own essays.

The Four Marking Criteria

Every IELTS Writing task is marked by a trained examiner against four criteria, each contributing 25% of your band for that task. Your Writing band is not a single global impression — it's the average of four separate judgements. Knowing them turns vague ambition ("write better") into a checklist you can actually act on.

Criterion (25% each)What it meansHow to lift it
Task Achievement (Task 1) / Task Response (Task 2)Did you fully answer the whole question, with a clear position and developed, relevant ideas? (Task 1: accurate overview + key features.)Address every part of the prompt; state a clear position and hold it; develop each idea with explanation and example. See Lessons 3.1 and 3.2.
Coherence & CohesionIs the writing logically organised and easy to follow — clear paragraphs, ideas that flow, and appropriate linking?One idea per paragraph with a topic sentence; reference and link naturally, not mechanically. This lesson's focus.
Lexical ResourceRange, accuracy, and appropriacy of your vocabulary — including less common words and collocation.Use precise, topic-appropriate words; paraphrase; avoid repetition and misused "big" words. See Lesson 1.2.
Grammatical Range & AccuracyRange of sentence structures and how error-free they are.Mix simple and complex sentences; control tense, articles, and punctuation. See Lesson 1.3.

✅ Why this matters strategically

Because each criterion is 25%, your band is often held down by your weakest one. A candidate with dazzling vocabulary but no paragraphing, or flawless grammar but who half-answers the question, cannot reach Band 7 overall. The fastest route up is usually to find the criterion capping you and fix that — not to polish the one you're already good at.

Coherence & Cohesion in Depth

These two words are often lumped together, but they're different things — and separating them is the key to the criterion.

🧩 Coherence = the writing makes sense as a whole

Coherence is the big-picture logic. Do the ideas flow in a sensible order? Can the reader follow your argument without backtracking? It lives at the level of paragraphs and structure:

  • One central idea per paragraph. Each body paragraph makes a single main point and develops it — not three half-points crammed together.
  • Topic sentences. The first sentence signals what the paragraph is about, so the reader knows where they are before the detail arrives.
  • Logical progression. Ideas build in an order that makes sense (general → specific, cause → effect, point → example → so-what).

🔗 Cohesion = the surface links that hold it together

Cohesion is the connective tissue — the words and grammar that stitch sentences and ideas to one another so the reader glides between them. It has three main tools:

  • Linking words / connectors — however, therefore, in addition, for instance.
  • Referencing — pronouns and pointers (it, they, this problem, these measures, such changes) that refer back so you don't repeat yourself.
  • Substitution — replacing a repeated word with a synonym or a shorter stand-in (the government … the authorities … it).
💡 The one-line difference. Coherence is whether your ideas are in a sensible order; cohesion is whether your words connect smoothly. A high band needs both — you can have perfect connectors gluing together a jumble of ideas (cohesive but incoherent), or beautifully logical ideas that read as disconnected islands (coherent but not cohesive).

Linking Devices & Referencing

Cohesion is easiest to control with a bank of linkers organised by function. Reach for the one that matches the relationship between your ideas — not just any connector.

FunctionLinking devices
Addingfurthermore, in addition, moreover, additionally
Contrastinghowever, on the other hand, whereas, nevertheless, in contrast
Cause / resulttherefore, consequently, as a result, thus, for this reason
Giving examplesfor instance, for example, such as, to illustrate
Sequencingfirstly, subsequently, then, finally
Concludingin conclusion, to sum up, overall, in short
Furthermore, … However, … As a result, … Whereas … For instance, … In conclusion, …

🔁 Referencing — the underrated half of cohesion

Most candidates over-invest in connectors and ignore referencing, yet examiners weigh it just as heavily. Referencing lets you avoid clumsy repetition by pointing back to something already mentioned:

this trend these measures such an approach the former / the latter it / they / this doing so

Notice the pattern "this/these + noun" — it's the single most useful cohesive move in academic writing. Instead of repeating a whole idea, you compress it:

Repetitive: Governments could raise fuel taxes. Raising fuel taxes would reduce car use. ✗

Referenced: Governments could raise fuel taxes. This measure would reduce car use. ✓

Good referencing quietly improves three criteria at once: it strengthens Cohesion, reduces repetition (Lexical Resource), and often tightens your grammar too.

⚠️ The Over-Linking Trap

Here is the insight that separates a Band 6 from a Band 7 on this criterion, and almost nobody is told it:

⚠️ More connectors is NOT better

Stuffing every sentence with Firstly… Moreover… In addition… Consequently… In conclusion… is mechanical, and the band descriptor explicitly penalises the overuse or inaccurate use of cohesive devices. A paragraph where every sentence opens with a signpost reads like a template, not like thinking. Cohesion is supposed to be varied and natural — and much of it comes from referencing and paragraphing, not connectors at all.

Compare these two versions of the same paragraph:

❌ Over-linked (mechanical) — caps the CC band:

Firstly, working from home saves time. Moreover, it saves money. In addition, it reduces stress. Furthermore, it is good for the environment. Consequently, many people prefer it. Therefore, it is a positive trend. In conclusion, working from home has many advantages.

✅ Smoothly cohesive — natural links + referencing:

Working from home offers several practical benefits. It saves employees the time and cost of commuting, and many report lower stress as a result. These advantages extend beyond the individual: fewer daily journeys also mean lower carbon emissions. For all these reasons, remote work has become an increasingly popular choice.

The second version uses fewer connectors, but it flows better — because it leans on referencing ("These advantages", "It"), keeps one idea developing through the paragraph, and lets some sentences connect by meaning rather than a bolted-on signpost. That's what "varied and natural" cohesion looks like.

Rule of thumb: if you can start a sentence without a connector and it still reads clearly, do. Save your linkers for the moments where the relationship genuinely needs signalling — a real contrast, a real result.

🗂️ Paragraphing is cohesion too

The most powerful cohesive device isn't a word — it's white space. Clear paragraphs, each with one idea and a topic sentence, do more for your CC band than any connector. An essay written as one giant block cannot score above about Band 5 on this criterion, no matter how many "howevers" it contains.

Self-Assessment & Band Descriptors

You can't award yourself an official band, but you can diagnose roughly where you sit. Below is a simplified, learner-friendly version of the descriptors for each criterion at Bands 5, 6, and 7 — enough to spot what's capping you.

CriterionBand 5 (modest)Band 6 (competent)Band 7 (good)
Task Response Partly addresses the task; position unclear; ideas thin or repeated. Addresses all parts; position present but development may be uneven. Fully addresses the task; clear, well-developed position throughout.
Coherence & Cohesion Some organisation; linking faulty, repetitive, or mechanical; weak paragraphing. Coherent overall; linking used but sometimes mechanical or over-used; paragraphs mostly clear. Logically organised; cohesion varied and natural; clear central topic per paragraph.
Lexical Resource Limited range; noticeable errors that may impede meaning. Adequate range for the task; some less common words; errors don't impede meaning. Sufficient range with flexibility; some less common/idiomatic items; occasional slips.
Grammatical Range & Accuracy Limited structures; frequent errors that can cause difficulty. Mix of simple and complex forms; errors present but meaning usually clear. Variety of complex structures; frequently error-free sentences; good control.

To self-assess, read your essay once per criterion — four passes, each with one question in mind. This stops the "it feels fine" global impression that hides specific weaknesses.

💡 You can't fully mark your own accuracy

Self-assessment is excellent for Task Response and Coherence & Cohesion — you can honestly judge whether you answered the question and organised it well. But you are the worst judge of your own grammar and vocabulary errors, because if you could see the mistake you wouldn't have made it. So pair self-marking with outside feedback wherever you can: a teacher, a study partner, a writing group, or at minimum reading your essay aloud (your ear catches errors your eye skips). Self-assessment finds structural problems; other people find accuracy problems.

📏 Other band-limiters — the quick checklist

⚠️ Things that cap your band before content is even judged

  • Under length — below 150 words (Task 1) or 250 words (Task 2) is penalised under Task Response. Aim slightly over, never under.
  • Off-topic — answering a different question than the one asked, however well-written, is heavily penalised.
  • No paragraphs — one undivided block caps Coherence & Cohesion near Band 5.
  • Memorised chunks — pre-learned sentences the examiner recognises are discounted and can lower your score.
  • Illegible handwriting (paper test) — if the examiner can't read it, it can't earn marks. Legibility is part of the deal.

Practice & Your Task

🏋️ Exercise 1: Fix an over-linked paragraph

Objective: Make mechanical cohesion natural by cutting redundant connectors and adding referencing.

Rewrite this so the cohesion is varied and natural (aim for two or three well-chosen links, plus referencing):

Firstly, cities are becoming overcrowded. Moreover, traffic is increasing. In addition, pollution is rising. Furthermore, public transport is overstretched. Therefore, governments must act. Consequently, they should invest in infrastructure. In conclusion, action is needed.

✅ Answer (model — judge by the CC descriptor)

Model rewrite: "As cities grow more crowded, they face mounting traffic, rising pollution, and an overstretched public transport network. These pressures make government action essential. One effective response would be sustained investment in infrastructure, which could ease congestion and improve air quality at the same time."

Why it's better: the four cramped points are combined into one flowing sentence; "These pressures" references them all at once; connectors appear only where a real relationship needs signalling. Notice we went from seven signposts to essentially none — and it reads more sophisticated, not less.

🏋️ Exercise 2: Improve coherence — reorder, add topic sentences & referencing

Objective: Turn a jumbled paragraph into a coherent one.

These sentences are out of order and lack a topic sentence. Reorder them and add referencing so the paragraph flows:

  1. Some students take part-time jobs while studying.
  2. Their grades can suffer as a result.
  3. The extra income helps cover living costs.
  4. However, working long hours leaves less time for study.
✅ Answer (model)

Model paragraph: "Working while studying is a double-edged decision for many students. Some take part-time jobs, and the extra income helps cover living costs. However, working long hours leaves less time for study, and as a result their grades can suffer."

What changed: a topic sentence now signals the paragraph's point (the trade-off) before the detail; the ideas move in logical order (do it → benefit → cost → consequence); "as a result" links the cost to the outcome; and the whole thing reads as one developed idea rather than four loose statements.

🏋️ Exercise 3: Which criterion does it affect?

Objective: Diagnose which of the four criteria each feature helps or hurts.

Match each essay feature to the criterion it most affects: TR (Task Response), CC (Coherence & Cohesion), LR (Lexical Resource), GRA (Grammatical Range & Accuracy).

  1. The essay never states a clear position on the question.
  2. Every paragraph opens with "Firstly / Moreover / In conclusion."
  3. The same word "important" appears eleven times.
  4. Every sentence is a short, simple subject-verb-object.
  5. The writing is one long block with no paragraph breaks.
  6. Articles (a/the) are missing throughout.
✅ Answer

1. TR (no clear position). · 2. CC (mechanical over-linking). · 3. LR (no range; repetition). · 4. GRA (no range of structures). · 5. CC (no paragraphing). · 6. GRA (accuracy of articles).

Notice items 2 and 5 both hit CC — proving cohesion is about far more than "using connectors."

🎯 Your Task: Mark an essay, then mark your own

Work against realistic timing and use the band-descriptor table above as your rubric.

  1. (10 min) Take any sample Band-6-ish Task 2 essay (one from a practice book, or the over-/under-developed model you critiqued earlier). Read it four times, once per criterion, and give each criterion a band (5, 6, or 7) with a one-line comment justifying it.
  2. (15 min) Now take your own Task 2 essay from Lesson 3.2. Run the same four-pass process. Be honest — pretend it's a stranger's.
  3. (5 min) Identify your lowest criterion. That's what's capping your band.
  4. (10 min) Write one concrete improvement per criterion — e.g. "CC: cut half my connectors and add two 'this + noun' references"; "GRA: add two complex sentences and fix my articles."

✅ Task Completion Checklist

  • ☐ I marked the sample essay on all four criteria with justifying comments
  • ☐ I re-marked my own Lesson 3.2 essay the same way
  • ☐ I identified the single criterion capping my band
  • ☐ I wrote one specific, actionable improvement for each of the four criteria
  • ☐ I got (or scheduled) outside feedback on my grammar/vocabulary accuracy

Partner/tutor option: swap essays with a study partner and mark each other's blind, then compare bands. Gaps between your two scores show exactly where your self-perception drifts from a reader's — priceless information.

🧠 Mindset

IELTS is a trainable skill, not a verdict. The band descriptors can feel like a judgement, but they're really a map: they tell you precisely what to do next. A learner who knows "my CC is stuck at 5 because I don't paragraph" has a five-minute fix that a learner writing blindly might miss for months. Self-assessment turns anxiety ("am I good enough?") into engineering ("which lever do I pull?"). Every criterion you can name is a criterion you can raise. 🌱

🎯 Quick Quiz

Question 1: A candidate begins every single sentence with "Firstly, Moreover, In addition, Consequently, In conclusion." What is the likely effect on their Coherence & Cohesion band?

Question 2: An essay answers a completely different question than the one on the prompt, but is beautifully organised and grammatically flawless. Which criterion is most affected?

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Read your essay four times — once per criterion. Separate passes catch what a single "does it feel okay" read misses.
  • Lead each body paragraph with a topic sentence and keep it to one central idea — the cheapest CC gain there is.
  • Use "this/these + noun" referencing to link and de-repeat at the same time.
  • Get outside feedback on accuracy — you can't reliably see your own grammar and vocabulary errors.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • Treating cohesion as "add connectors": over-linking is mechanical and lowers the CC band. Referencing and paragraphing matter as much as linkers.
  • Polishing your strongest criterion: your band is held down by your weakest of the four — fix that one first.
  • Writing one big block: no paragraphs caps Coherence & Cohesion around Band 5 regardless of everything else.
  • Trusting your own accuracy check: if you could see the error, you wouldn't have made it — always seek a second reader.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • IELTS Writing is marked on four criteria, each 25%: Task Achievement/Response, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range & Accuracy.
  • Coherence = logical ideas and paragraphing; cohesion = the surface links — connectors, referencing, and substitution.
  • Over-linking lowers your band — the descriptor penalises mechanical, overused cohesive devices. Aim for varied, natural cohesion, and remember paragraphing and referencing count too.
  • Self-assess criterion by criterion, fix your weakest one, and get outside feedback for the accuracy you can't see yourself.

🎉 What You've Accomplished

You've read the examiner's marking sheet. You can now name what each of the four criteria rewards, tell coherence from cohesion, dodge the over-linking trap that quietly caps so many essays, and run a four-pass self-assessment that turns "write better" into a specific to-do list. That's the difference between hoping for a band and engineering one. 🎉

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

How many linking words should a good essay actually use?

There's no magic number — the descriptor rewards appropriate and varied use, not quantity. A strong Band 7 essay might use only a handful of connectors, leaning on referencing and clear paragraphing for the rest. If a linker isn't signalling a genuine relationship, cut it.

Can I really guess my own band accurately?

Partly. You can judge Task Response and Coherence & Cohesion quite reliably — you know whether you answered the question and organised it. But you'll systematically underestimate your grammar and vocabulary errors, so treat your self-mark as a good diagnosis for structure and pair it with a second reader for accuracy.

Is Coherence & Cohesion marked the same way in Task 1 and Task 2?

The criterion is the same idea in both — logical organisation, clear paragraphing, and natural cohesion — but Task 1 also expects a clear overview and logically grouped data, while Task 2 expects a progression of argument. The core skill you built here transfers to both.
