📊 Lesson 3.1: Writing Task 1 — Describing Data

Writing Task 1 asks you to become a clear, objective reporter: you're handed a graph, a chart, a table, a process diagram, or a map, and in about 20 minutes you must describe what it shows in at least 150 words — no opinions, no invented reasons, just the key facts, well organised and precisely worded. This lesson gives you a reliable four-paragraph structure, the language toolkit examiners reward, and a full annotated model so you know exactly what a Band 7+ report looks like.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Structure a Task 1 report as introduction → overview → body with a clear, band-critical overview
  • Select and group the key features — not every number — and compare them meaningfully
  • Use accurate trend, comparison, and process language with the right verbs, adverbs, and approximation
  • Handle each visual type (line/bar/pie/table, process diagram, map) and hit the 150-word / 20-minute requirement

⏱️ Estimated Time: ~60 minutes

🎯 Task: Write a full ~150-word Task 1 report from a described chart in under 20 minutes, then self-mark it against a Task Achievement checklist.

What Task 1 Really Is

Academic Writing Task 1 asks you to describe and summarise visual information — a line graph, bar chart, pie chart, table, process diagram, or map — in your own words. It is objective reporting only: you say what the data shows, never what you think about it, and never a cause you've invented.

RequirementDetail
LengthAt least 150 words
TimeAbout 20 minutes (leave 40 for Task 2)
WeightingTask 1 counts for less than Task 2 — don't overspend time on it
RegisterFormal, impersonal — no "I think", no contractions

⚠️ Two instant band-limiters

  • Under 150 words = automatic penalty. The examiner counts, and a short answer is scored down for Task Achievement regardless of quality. Learn to feel where 150 words lands in your handwriting.
  • Opinions or invented reasons = off-task. Writing "this is because the economy improved" (when the chart never says so) is guessing. Report the figure, not the cause.

Because Task 1 is worth less and comes first, treat it as a controlled warm-up: get in, report the key features accurately, hit the word count, and get out — protecting the 40 minutes Task 2 deserves.

The Four-Paragraph Structure

Almost every high-scoring Task 1 report uses the same reliable shape. Learn it once and you never face a blank page again.

ParagraphJobWhat goes in it
1. IntroductionParaphrase the questionSay what the visual shows, where, and when — in your own words, not copied from the prompt
2. OverviewThe big pictureThe 1–2 most important features or main trends — no specific numbers here
3. Body ADetail group 1Specific data with figures, logically grouped
4. Body BDetail group 2The remaining key data with figures and comparisons

The overview is the single most band-critical element of the whole task. It is the sentence (or two) that answers, "If you could only tell me one thing about this chart, what would it be?"

⚠️ No overview caps you at Band 5

A report that lists numbers with no clear overview cannot reach Band 6 for Task Achievement, no matter how accurate the details. Always signal it: begin the paragraph "Overall, …" or "It is clear that …". Write it early — many strong candidates write the overview second, right after the introduction, so it's never forgotten.

graph TD
    A["1. Introduction (paraphrase what, where, when)"] --> B["2. Overview (the 1-2 biggest features, no numbers)"]
    B --> C["3. Body A (key data + figures, grouped)"]
    C --> D["4. Body B (remaining key data + comparisons)"]

💡 Where the numbers live

Keep specific figures out of the overview and save them for the body. The overview is the shape of the story ("upward overall, with X far ahead of Y"); the body is where you back it up with data ("X rose from 20% to 65%").

Selecting & Grouping the Key Features

You cannot — and must not — describe every number. Band 7+ writers select the significant features and group related data; Band 5 writers list everything mechanically, left to right, and run out of time and words.

✅ What counts as a "key feature"

  • The highest and lowest values
  • The biggest change (steepest rise or fall) over time
  • A striking comparison (one category far above the rest; two that are nearly equal)
  • A turning point, peak, or crossover where two lines meet
  • Anything stable or unchanging when everything else moves

💡 Group, don't list

Instead of describing each line in isolation, bundle related data: put the rising categories together and the falling ones together; pair the two highest; contrast the fastest-growing with the flattest. Grouping is what makes a report read like analysis rather than a caption, and it directly lifts your Coherence & Cohesion and Task Achievement scores.

Rule of thumb: if a detail doesn't support the overview or a meaningful comparison, it probably doesn't belong. Report the story, not the spreadsheet.

The Language Toolkit

The right vocabulary is what separates a flat report from a fluent one. Build these phrase banks into muscle memory — they carry your Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range marks.

📈 Trends — for changes over time (line graphs, dated bar charts)

Every movement can be a verb or a noun. Master both so you can vary your grammar:

MovementVerbsNouns
Uprise, increase, grow, climb, surge, soara rise, an increase, growth, a climb, a surge
Downfall, decline, drop, decrease, plummet, dipa fall, a decline, a drop, a decrease
No changelevel off, remain stable, stay constant, plateaua plateau, stability
Up & downfluctuate, vary, oscillatea fluctuation, variation
Peak / lowpeak (at), reach a high, hit a low, bottom outa peak, a high point, a low point, a trough

Add an adverb of degree (with a verb) or an adjective (with a noun) to show how much and how fast:

Size / speedAdverbs (with verbs)Adjectives (with nouns)
Large / fastsharply, dramatically, rapidly, steeply, significantlya sharp / dramatic / rapid / significant …
Steadysteadily, gradually, consistentlya steady / gradual …
Small / slowslightly, marginally, moderatelya slight / marginal / moderate …
sales rose sharply (verb + adverb) there was a sharp rise in sales (adj + noun) the figure climbed steadily to 60% numbers fell dramatically before levelling off a gradual decline over the decade

💡 The powerhouse pattern

"There was a + [adjective] + [noun] + in X." → "There was a dramatic increase in tourism." It lets you front the noun form and vary your sentence openings — a quick, reliable way to show grammatical range beyond "X increased."

⚖️ Comparison & proportion — for charts without time (pie charts, single-year bar charts, tables)

the largest / smallest proportion the majority / a minority of… X accounts for 40% of… twice as many / three times as many as… significantly higher / lower than… by far the most / least common roughly equal to / on a par with…

⚙️ Process diagrams — passive + sequencing

Processes have no numbers and no time trend: describe the stages in order, usually in the passive voice (the actor doesn't matter — the step does).

First / To begin, the raw material is collected Next / Then, it is heated After that / Subsequently, … Once this has been done, … Finally, the product is packaged

🗺️ Maps — change over time, position & direction

Most map tasks show a place then and now. Use the passive past for what changed and clear location language:

the forest was replaced by housing a bridge was constructed across the river to the north / south / east / west of the town in the north-eastern corner the factory was demolished / converted into…

🎯 Approximation — report figures accurately

You don't need exact numbers if the graph is unclear, but you must be accurate. Round sensibly and hedge:

approximately 500 just over 60% just under a third roughly / around 200 well over half nearly double

Handling Each Visual Type

The structure stays the same; only your language focus shifts with the visual.

Line graphTrends over time (verbs + adverbs)The general direction; which line ends highest/lowest
Bar chart (dated)Trends + comparisonOverall movement and the biggest gap between categories
Bar chart / pie (single time)Proportion & comparisonThe largest and smallest categories
TableComparison; spot the extremesHighest/lowest overall; any clear pattern

💡 Two charts in one question

Sometimes Task 1 shows two visuals (e.g. a pie chart and a table). Cover both — a report that ignores one is incomplete for Task Achievement. Your overview should name the key feature of each, and you can devote one body paragraph to each visual.

Annotated Model Report

Here is a described chart and a full Band 7+ model. Study how the four paragraphs do their jobs and how the trend language is deployed.

📋 The task

The line graph below shows the percentage of households with internet access in three countries — Country A, Country B, and Country C — between 2000 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

The described data: In 2000, all three countries were low: Country A at about 20%, Country B at 10%, Country C at just 5%. By 2020, Country A had risen steeply to around 90%; Country B had climbed steadily to roughly 75%; Country C had grown more slowly to about 40%. Country A was highest throughout; Country C remained lowest throughout. The steepest growth for all three occurred between 2005 and 2015.

[Introduction] The line graph illustrates changes in the proportion of households with internet access in three countries, labelled A, B and C, over a twenty-year period from 2000 to 2020.

[Overview] Overall, internet access rose markedly in all three countries across the period, yet a clear gap remained between them: Country A led throughout while Country C stayed consistently the lowest. The most rapid growth for each country took place in the middle decade.

[Body A] In 2000, household internet access was low everywhere, standing at approximately 20% in Country A, 10% in Country B and just 5% in Country C. From 2005 onwards, all three figures climbed sharply, with Country A surging to about 90% by 2020 — by far the highest of the three.

[Body B] Country B followed a similar upward path, rising steadily to roughly 75% by the end of the period. Country C, by contrast, grew far more gradually, reaching only around 40% in 2020 and thus remaining well below the other two nations throughout.

Word count: ~150. Notice the highlighted toolkit in action: rose markedly, climbed sharply, surging, rising steadily, grew gradually (verbs + adverbs); approximately, about, roughly, around (approximation); by far the highest, well below, consistently the lowest (comparison). No opinions, no invented causes — just the data.

✅ Why this scores well

  • Introduction paraphrases ("percentage" → "proportion", "shows" → "illustrates") instead of copying.
  • Overview is explicit ("Overall, …"), gives the big picture, and contains no specific figures.
  • Body groups data logically (the starting point together; then A, then the B/C contrast) rather than year by year.
  • Range of trend language and accurate approximation throughout, in a formal, impersonal register.

Practice & Your Task

🏋️ Exercise 1: Write the overview sentence

Objective: Produce the single most band-critical sentence.

Described bar chart: A single-year bar chart shows how five age groups spent their leisure time. "Watching TV" was by far the most popular (chosen by about 70%), followed by "socialising" (around 45%). "Reading" and "sport" were similar and moderate (roughly 30% each). "Gaming" was the least common (about 15%).

Write one or two sentences of overview — the big picture, no exact figures.

✅ Answer (model)

"Overall, watching television was clearly the most popular leisure activity, while gaming was the least common; reading and sport attracted broadly similar, mid-range levels of participation." Note: it names the extremes and a comparison, signals with "Overall," and keeps the numbers back for the body.

🏋️ Exercise 2: Choose accurate trend language

Objective: Match the movement to the right verb + adverb.

  1. A figure that goes from 10% to 12% over ten years → rose ______
  2. A figure that crashes from 80 to 15 in one year → ______ ______
  3. A figure that stays at 50 for five years → ______ ______
  4. A figure that goes up and down repeatedly with no clear direction → ______
✅ Answer (models)

1. rose slightly / marginally (a small change). · 2. plummeted / fell sharply (dramatically) (a large, fast fall). · 3. remained stable / levelled off (no change). · 4. fluctuated (irregular up and down). Any accurate near-synonym is fine — the key is that the degree matches the movement.

🏋️ Exercise 3: Fix the flawed report

Objective: Spot the three classic faults — an opinion, no overview, and mechanical listing.

A weak response to the internet-access graph above:

"In 2000 Country A was 20% and Country B was 10% and Country C was 5%. In 2005 they all went up a bit. In 2010 they went up more. In 2015 they were higher. In 2020 Country A was 90%, Country B was 75%, Country C was 40%. I think Country A did best because it has a better economy."

Name the three problems and say how you'd fix each.

✅ Answer
  • An opinion / invented cause: "I think… because it has a better economy" is off-task — the graph says nothing about the economy. Fix: delete it; report only the data.
  • No overview: there is no "Overall…" sentence giving the big picture, which caps Task Achievement at Band 5. Fix: add an overview naming the general upward trend and the persistent gap (A highest, C lowest).
  • Mechanical listing: it walks year-by-year with no grouping or comparison. Fix: group the data (all low in 2000; sharp rise in the middle decade; the A-vs-C contrast) and use trend verbs instead of "went up a bit."

🎯 Your Task: Write a Full Task 1 Report Under Time

Work to real exam timing — 20 minutes total.

The task: A pie chart shows how an average household's monthly budget is divided: housing 35%, food 20%, transport 15%, savings 12%, utilities 10%, and "other" 8%. Summarise the main features and make comparisons where relevant.

  1. (2 min) Plan: underline the largest and smallest slices; decide your two body groups.
  2. (2 min) Write the introduction (paraphrase the prompt) and the overview (biggest and smallest, no exact numbers).
  3. (12 min) Write the two body paragraphs with figures and comparisons — group the larger categories together and the smaller ones together.
  4. (4 min) Count your words (aim for 150–180) and check: is there a clear overview? Any opinions to delete? Any repeated verbs to vary?

✅ Task Completion Checklist (Task Achievement focus)

  • ☐ At least 150 words, written in ~20 minutes
  • ☐ A paraphrased introduction — not copied from the prompt
  • ☐ A clear, signalled overview with the key features and no specific figures
  • ☐ Body paragraphs report accurate figures and group related data
  • ☐ At least one meaningful comparison (largest vs smallest)
  • ☐ No opinions and no invented causes
  • ☐ Formal register; varied comparison/proportion language; accurate approximation

Solo: write it, then self-mark against the checklist and the four Writing criteria (Task Achievement, Coherence & Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy). Partner/tutor: swap reports and each mark the other's overview — does it capture the biggest feature in one glance?

Tips & Common Mistakes

✅ Do's

  • Write the overview early and signal it ("Overall,") so the examiner can't miss it.
  • Paraphrase the prompt in your introduction — change the verbs and nouns, keep the meaning.
  • Vary your verbs — don't let "increase" appear five times; rotate rise/climb/grow/surge and use noun forms.
  • Watch the clock: ~20 minutes, then move to Task 2, which is worth more.

❌ Common Mistakes

⚠️ Watch Out

  • No overview (or numbers in it): the fastest way to cap your band — always include one, and keep specific figures out of it.
  • Adding opinions or causes: "because of the recession" is off-task unless the visual states it. Report, don't explain.
  • Listing every data point: select and group; a wall of numbers with no comparison reads as a caption, not a report.
  • Copying the prompt wording: copied sentences aren't counted toward your 150 words and hurt Lexical Resource.
  • Under 150 words: an automatic penalty — learn where 150 words falls in your own handwriting.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Task 1 = objective description of a visual in ≥150 words / ~20 minutes — no opinions, no invented causes.
  • Use the four-paragraph structure: introduction, overview, and two body paragraphs.
  • The overview is band-critical — without a clear one, Task Achievement is capped at Band 5, and it should contain no specific figures.
  • Select and group the key features; deploy accurate trend, comparison, process, and approximation language.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Do I need a conclusion in Task 1?

No. Task 1 has no separate conclusion — the overview does that job at the top. Adding a "In conclusion…" that repeats the overview just wastes words. Spend those words on accurate body detail instead.

What if I genuinely can't read an exact number off the graph?

Use approximation — "approximately," "just over," "around," "well over half." Examiners expect sensible rounding; what they penalise is a figure that's clearly wrong. Accuracy of the trend matters more than the last digit.

How many words should I aim for — is more always better?

Aim for roughly 150–180. Below 150 is penalised; far above it eats time you need for Task 2 (worth more) and often adds repetition or irrelevant detail. Hit the target, then stop and move on.
